# Business Types: Storefront, Service-Area, Hybrid

Verified 2026-08-31. Getting the type wrong is a top suspension cause, so this decision comes before any optimization.

## Which type is the client?

| Type | Customers come to you? | You go to them? | Address on profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storefront | Yes | No | Shown |
| Service-area business (SAB) | No | Yes | **Hidden (required)** |
| Hybrid | Yes | Yes | Shown + service areas set |

## Storefront

**Do**
- Show the real address. "Physical address in city of search" is factor #4 and "address showing (not SAB)" is #7: a visible address in the searched city is a real advantage.
- Permanent on-site signage year-round, even for seasonal businesses. Google checks.
- Staff the location during every listed open hour; mismatched staffed-hours is now an explicit eligibility check, not a suggestion.
- One profile per physical location. Suite/floor numbers are fine.
- Photos of the actual premises, exterior and interior, so Maps/Street View confirmation is easy.

**Don't**
- Don't list a coworking desk as a storefront unless it has visible signage, staffed reception, and walk-in capability. Unstaffed coworking = virtual office = suspension.
- Don't leave "open" hours live when nobody is there.

## Service-area business (SAB)

The rules:

- **Address must be hidden** if customers are not served at the location. The verification address must still be real (home is fine); no P.O. boxes, no virtual offices.
- **Service areas: up to 20**, set by city or postal code, not radius. Google's guidance says the territory shouldn't extend past ~2 hours of driving from the base. Sterling Sky nuance (Dec 2025): the 2-hour rule governs the size of one profile's territory; the actual verification/suspension trigger for a second location is **overlapping service territory** with an existing profile, not raw distance.
- Home-based is fully permitted; hide the address, and video verification can use neighborhood landmarks instead of a commercial front.

**The hard truth about ranking:** the service-area setting has little to no direct ranking effect. Proximity is computed from the hidden verified address. Listing 20 cities does not make you rank in them; you rank strongest around the pin you cannot see, and fade outward. Set service areas honestly for eligibility and coverage display, not as a ranking play.

**How a SAB legitimately reaches past its radius:**
1. **Website location pages.** City-level relevance lives on the site, not the pin. Cap at ~10-15 highest-value cities, each genuinely unique (local landmarks, jobs done there, local proof), rolled out 1-2/week. Thin city-swap templates get demoted. Prioritize smaller surrounding towns over the big metro (nobody targets them, and you rarely outrank a real local's homepage without dedicated links). One service per satellite page; the home base can carry one page per service. Structure: Locations hub in nav, hub links every location page, new pages also linked from the homepage.
2. **Prominence in target cities:** reviews from customers there, citations and links from local orgs there, local press.
3. **Local Services Ads.** Pay-per-lead, covers the whole service area, not bound by organic proximity mechanics. The honest paid answer to "we want the whole metro."
4. A real second location with its own staff and non-overlapping territory (the expensive answer; see `donts.md` for what NOT to fake).
- "Near me" is never a target keyword, in copy or URLs. Google resolves proximity itself.

**Citations for SABs:** buildable with a hidden/suppressed address. GBP, Yelp (city/state/zip still show), and Nextdoor all support hiding; BBB and some structured directories require a real address on file even if unlisted, and a few industry directories require a public one (skip those or accept the tradeoff). Keep everything else (name, phone, city, zip, URL) identical everywhere; consistency substitutes for the missing storefront anchor.

## Hybrid (storefront + service area)

- Qualifies only with real storefront eligibility: staffed listed hours plus signage. Then show the address AND set service areas (same 20-area interface).
- Typical fits: HVAC with a showroom, restaurant that caters, salon that does on-site events.
- If walk-ins are theoretical and the location is really a dispatch office, run it as a SAB with hidden address instead of stretching hybrid eligibility.

## Verification (2025-2026 reality, all types)

- **Video is the standard path.** Live in-app (Google Maps app, the managing account); desktop and pre-recorded uploads are rejected. Minimum 30 seconds, one unbroken take, no people or voices, no sensitive documents in frame. Review up to 5 business days.
- What the video must show: nearby street signs/building numbers, then walk continuously to signage tying the business name to the location, then proof of operation: branded vehicle, tools/equipment, workspace, or a document (permit, invoice, utility bill) matching the profile name exactly.
- SAB/home-based: neighborhood landmarks near the home replace the commercial storefront shots; still show the tools/vehicle/paperwork.
- Re-verification triggers (practitioner consensus, not official policy text): editing business name, primary address, or primary category, especially within ~2 weeks of verifying; adding a manager account previously flagged for spam. Plan edits accordingly: batch nothing, change core fields slowly, never right after verification.

## Practitioner listings (the legal multiple-listing exception)

Doctors, lawyers, agents: multiple listings at one address ARE allowed when each practitioner is a distinct public-facing professional with their own clients and contact info. The practice/firm gets its own listing too. When a practitioner leaves, update their listing to the new location rather than marking it closed (unless retired, which can now be removed entirely). This is the one sanctioned way to have several profiles at one address; everything else is a duplicate.
