## 1. The Core Weekly Workflow: Question → Draft → Publish

This exact loop is the spine of every session (065, 080, 087, 091, 095) and is the mandatory weekly exercise ("work-session format": host pauses the recording, gives everyone ~30 min to research/write/publish independently, then resumes for Q&A — 080).

### 1.1 Find a real question (free tools first)

1. Go to **answerthepublic.com** (no subscription needed). Type in what you sell/do (demoed live with "hot sauce" 065, "meta ads" 080, "SEO" 095, "web hosting" 091).
2. Click **Search Engines** (left nav) → **Questions**. A wheel/list of real user questions loads.
3. Don't take page 1. Scroll to **page 2, 3, or 4** — competitors rarely click past page 1, so later pages are less contested and easier to rank for (065, 080, 087, 091, 095 — repeated in every call as the single most emphasized tactic).
4. Scroll further down the results page for a **second, hidden question set sourced from Bing** — most people miss this and only look at the Google-sourced set (091). Bing carries roughly **4% of global search volume** — not negligible — and ChatGPT pulls from *both* Google and Bing, so Bing-only questions are still worth targeting (091).
5. Set region to **United States** when checking keyword volume (087).
6. If your niche term returns nothing (e.g., "ColdFusion web hosting" 080, "motivational therapy tattoos" 087, a narrow term generally 091, 095): broaden it ("web hosting," "motivational tattoos," then just "tattoos," plain "SEO" instead of "marketing for service businesses") to surface real questions, then fold your specific niche back into the answer body (e.g., "actually, ColdFusion is the best way to do this"). This builds ranking/authority on the broader term first, making the ultra-specific term easier to rank later (080). Only narrow the search if the narrower version still returns real results (091).
7. Volume doesn't need to be high: **~10 searches/month is an acceptable target** (065, 080, 095) — don't dismiss low-volume questions; that traffic is highly qualified/high-intent (095: "those 10 people are looking to work with an SEO agency"). Zero or negative-scored volume on a tool is not a reason to skip a question — negative/zero just means "as low as the tool goes" (091). **~15% of all daily searches have never been searched before** — zero historical volume doesn't mean zero future volume (091). Even near-zero volume can be worth writing if other proof of demand exists, e.g. Reddit or another site already ranking for that exact question (087, 091).
8. Higher-volume keywords are, per the host's estimate, **"9 or 9.9 times out of 10" harder to rank for** than low-volume long-tail questions, even when the tool shows no explicit difficulty score (095).
9. Long-tail keywords don't have to be phrased as a question — a plain statement ("how long can I keep my LED lights on for," a "top 10 X" list) works just as well (091, citing Manny/LED lighting).
10. Google's **People Also Ask** section is a fully equivalent substitute for AnswerThePublic — same method, same result quality (065, 080, 087: confirmed live as "Yep. Okay. Yeah, 100%").
11. Paid keyword tools (SEMrush, Ubersuggest) are optional cross-checks, never required — the explicit goal is to keep SEO "as accessible as possible," and members are getting real traffic/sales from free tools alone (080, 087).

### 1.2 The AI interview-drafting method (ChatGPT/Claude)

Core idea, stated every call: paste your chosen question into ChatGPT (or Claude) with a prompt asking it to interview you first, answer its questions from your own expertise/experience (typed or, preferably, dictated via voice mode), then have it draft the post from your answers — "although it is written by AI, it's all you" (080, said to Varuna).

**Exact prompt variants** (the host does not keep a fixed prompt library — he tweaks wording every session and keeps whatever produces better output in the moment, 080; treat these as interchangeable, not versioned):
- (065, reconstructed from garbled audio): *"I'm writing a blog post answering [paste question]. Ask me 10 questions you'd need to know the answers to in order to write a blog post answering this, etc. Ask me one at a time."*
- (080, clarifying-questions prompt): *"I'm writing a blog post about [your chosen question/topic]. Ask me any questions you'd need to know the answer to in order to write me a helpful and unique blog post answering that question. It should use my expertise, experience, etc. Keep it limited [to] 10 questions."* Followed by a separate voice-mode instruction: *"I'm going to answer these in voice mode. Ask me one at a time and leave room for me to breathe. Don't write the blog post. I will prompt you after."*
- (087): *"Blog post about [topic]. Can you ask me 10 questions about my experience, expertise, case studies, opinions, etc.? Keep it limited to 10 questions and I'm going to use voice mode to answer these. Ask me one question [at a time]."* One participant ("V") runs a longer variant that also tells the AI to keep the site's goals/audience in mind and not write the full post until told to.
- (091): *"Write me blog posts using my experience, expertise, real stories and insights. Ask me questions you need to know the answers to in order to write a blog post that includes my expertise, experience, personal stories, opinions, etc. Keep it to 10 questions. It should be helpful and original. I'm going to answer this in voice mode, so just remember everything I say."*
- (095): *"I'm writing a blog post answering this question: [QUESTION]. Ask me any questions you'd need to know the answer to in order to write a helpful blog post using my expertise, experience, case studies, opinions. Keep it limited to 10 questions."*

Mechanics common to all versions:
- Cap clarifying questions at **10**.
- Answer via ChatGPT's **voice mode** (click the voice icon, not "dictate") one question at a time — it auto-transcribes and remembers the whole conversation (080, 087, 091, 095).
- If screen-sharing (e.g., on a live call), **stop sharing first** to avoid exposing client data before pasting real answers (091).
- Once all questions are answered, prompt it separately to write the full post from those answers.
- **Topic-ideation fallback when you're out of ideas** (Jorg's method, 087): tell ChatGPT what you've already written, your expertise, your life experience, and what you sell, then ask for a set number of new SEO-optimized topic ideas (Jorg asked for 5).
- Don't paste an already-finished, question-answering post back into ChatGPT and ask it to "make this SEO friendly" — it can derail the topic entirely (065: Morvi's "goat milk/eczema in pets" post got its topic changed; resolved by judging individual suggestions on merit rather than accepting a wholesale rewrite, e.g. naming cats/dogs specifically under "pets" was a fine addition). Directly answering the question already makes a post SEO-friendly — "we're not talking about using the keyword a certain amount of times" (065).
- **Claude vs. ChatGPT**: the host runs essentially all agency content, and the Hey Tony Blog Post Writer tool itself, through **Claude** — "writes like a person," needs fewer edits, whereas ChatGPT defaults to bullet points/summarizing (065). Same price point, roughly $20–30/month. He still falls back to ChatGPT for specific tasks (e.g. newsletter writing) when Claude's output goes "long and convoluted" (065). Full personal AI stack: **Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Manus, Gemini** — "paying a fucking fortune in AI subscription fees" (065).
- ChatGPT/Claude are useful for ideation but rated less reliable than AnswerThePublic/PAA/Reddit for confirming real search demand, since the AI "can sometimes make things up" about what people actually search (087). Treat ChatGPT's suggested keywords as a starting point to re-verify (e.g. in Ubersuggest), not literal gospel (095, Aaron's case).

### 1.3 Structuring for direct-answer + AEO/AI citation

- **Direct-answer-first structure**: restate the question as a subheading/lead-in, then answer it immediately and plainly in the first paragraph — LLMs/AI Overviews scan for direct Q→A pairs, so put the pairing at the very top and repeat the keyword/question near the top more than once (091). Exact rewrite instruction used live: *"The first paragraph should directly answer the question posed in the title / main keyword."* (091)
- Model direct-answer examples, quoted live:
  - (065) *"Do backlinks help ranking on Google? The answer is yes, backlinks do help ranking on Google. However, it depends on which kind of backlinks you're getting and how many you have."*
  - (091) *"Yes, ChatGPT can help with SEO, but it can't do it on its own."* (answering "Can ChatGPT do SEO?")
- **Key takeaways block**: add a bullet-point "key takeaways" section at the end of every post — 4–5 bullets covering everything (065, 091). Exact prompt used: *"write me key takeaways for the end."* (091) Worth doing regardless of post length (091).
- **TL;DR placement**: only add a top-of-post TL;DR for *longer* content; if the direct answer is already in paragraph one, a separate TL;DR isn't necessary (091). This is an open, ongoing experiment for the host personally — he's actively A/B testing "conclusion" vs. "key takeaways" as the section label (080).
- **FAQs are not a free SEO win**: stacking ~10 FAQs at the bottom of every post after the CTA does **not** itself help SEO/AI-visibility — the bar is "is this actually helpful to a human being? Probably not" for a bolted-on generic block (069). An FAQ-*style* piece of content overall can genuinely help (069). If used at all, **cap at 3–4 questions maximum** (091). The host personally avoids FAQs in blog posts (dislikes the toggle/accordion UX), reserving them for landing pages only if at all — flagged explicitly as personal preference, "your mileage may vary" (091).
- Meta description should contain the target keyword (080); one live audit caught a post missing this (080, V's case).
- Don't let a title/H1 render with a trailing "…" (three-dot truncation) — Google literally can't read what's past the cutoff; this is a real, fixable technical bug, not cosmetic (080, caught on V's Shopify site).
- Title tag vs. on-page heading vs. platform snippet are three different layers commonly conflated when debugging a "truncated title" complaint: the on-page title shown in a platform's rendered "search-style" preview (e.g. inside Shopify) is likely the H1/heading, not the actual `<title>` tag — the real title tag only shows on Google's results page, browser tabs, or social shares. Fixing the wrong layer won't solve a truncation complaint (080).
- **Title/H1 search-intent audit method**: for every existing title, ask "what would a real person actually Google to find this piece of content?" Editorial/poetic titles that don't map to a real search query need reworking toward real search phrasing, even when the underlying content is strong (080). Live example (V's tattoo brand): "When your strength tattoo feels like a lie" → reworked toward "strength tattoo pros and cons" or "strength tattoo meanings"; "Soft is not the opposite of strong" and "A gentle reminder after [baby]… why keep going" were flagged as non-searches. Titles that *did* work: "Are temporary tattoos safe?", "How to stay motivated on low energy days" (sits inside the "motivation" PAA cluster), "Can small anxiety tattoos help you breathe easier?" Reframe: "It's not that the titles suck… it's the intent of this piece of content" (080).
- **Differentiate near-duplicate topics** by changing the angle/audience in the title, not just the wording, so two posts target genuinely different keywords — e.g. "what to do when my wife has sexual avoidance" vs. "…when my husband has sexual avoidance" (095).
- Schema markup: don't bother with complicated/custom schema — the host uses none beyond whatever WordPress adds by default (080). Caveat/counter-data point: his own in-progress AI page-analyzer tool found that on the Hey Tony site, pages with **no ranking/traffic at all actually had more schema markup** than pages that do rank — flagged as true for that one site only so far, not yet validated across client sites, actively being tested rather than treated as a firm rule (080).
- **Table of contents layout**: rebuild a stacked/crowded TOC as a slim, anchored **side column (~1/4–1/3 page width)** running alongside the main content (2/3–3/4 width) instead of stacked above it, so content is visible immediately on landing; make it sticky so it follows the reader down the page (080, fixing Jennifer's/Bertha's crowded stacked TOCs against the host's own site as the positive example). One participant built a TOC specifically to help AI crawlers parse content — group feedback was negative on visual execution, not on the underlying idea (080).
- No fixed word-count target for question-based/long-tail posts — competition is already low from keyword specificity; if you want a number, check what's currently ranking for that exact keyword and match its length (065).

### 1.4 Featured & body images

- **Exact featured-image prompts** (interchangeable across sessions, run in the same ongoing chat thread as prior generations for style continuity — 095):
  - (087): *"Make me a featured image using this post. Using the attached images as style examples. The new image should not include elements from the examples, but create new ones. Do not include the title on the image."*
  - (091): *"Using the images attached as an example, create me a featured image for this post. It doesn't need to have the title on the image."*
  - (095): *"Using the images as a style inspiration, create me a featured image for this post. We don't need the title of the blog post on it. It should be something to do with [topic]."*
- **Reduce AI image friction**: generate only the *featured* image with AI; fill supporting in-body images with **real screenshots** already in your files (SEMrush screenshots, Looker Studio client-report screenshots) — deliberately pick screenshots showing **recent, visible date ranges** to prove recency of results (e.g. a December report shown on a Jan 19 call) (095).
- Clunky-pipeline pain point this solves (Nick, 095): saved ChatGPT prompt with brand/design particulars → ask for 3–5 images (cover + inline) → download PNG → convert to JPEG → compress via **tinyjpg.com** → upload → insert. Article itself takes 15–20 min to write; images take **45 min–1.5 hrs**. Fully custom designed images would otherwise run "at least 100 bucks, maybe 200 bucks" per image with a lot of back-and-forth (095).
- **PNG → WebP conversion**: open in Photoshop/Creative Cloud, export as WebP at **~70% quality** (barely any visible quality loss). No Photoshop → Google "JPEG or PNG to WebP converter" for a free online tool (095).
- **Platform gotcha**: GoHighLevel does **not** support WebP uploads for course/training video hosting — must be JPEG or PNG. Wix or Squarespace (one of the two) also lacks WebP support (095).
- **Avoiding repetitive real-photo blogs**: upload a couple of your own reference photos into ChatGPT or **Gemini** ("really, really good" for this) and ask it to generate "[subject] doing [action]" using those photos for likeness/style — or just take more photos on your phone; imperfect, off-the-cuff photography is fine and increasingly preferred over polished graphics (095, Dr. Karen's question).
- **Image reuse**: fine to reuse the same image across multiple pages if it's relevant to each page's content — just don't reuse it as the *featured* image more than once (069, Carol).
- **File naming / alt text**: name the file with a plain description of what's actually in the image; use that same description as the alt text (069, Carol).
- **Alt-text keyword rule**: use the exact target keyword phrase on roughly **half (or fewer) of the images** on a page — e.g. on a 12-photo page, "maybe half, not even." Never repeat the identical keyword phrase sequentially across every image (don't do "Newborn photographer Chicago 1/2/3/4"). Keep each page focused on one topic/keyword — don't stuff the same keyword into every image sitewide; differentiate per page (e.g. interior vs. exterior photography alt text on their respective pages) (065). Alt text/titles are valuable only where the underlying query has real search demand — optimizing for something nobody searches does nothing, and over-tagging every image with the same phrase dilutes each page's topical focus (065, Jason Bush spent a full day plus half of another redoing image titles/alt text/captions across his portfolio for "architectural photographer in Atlanta").

### 1.5 Internal linking rules

Numbers vary slightly call to call (same host, different sessions) — treat as a consistent range rather than one fixed rule:
- (065): 2–3 internal links to existing posts + 1 external authoritative link + at least 1 link to a product/service (money) page, per new post.
- (080): 3–5 outbound internal links to other site pages, per new post.
- (087): 1 link to the "money page" + roughly 2–3 links to other blog posts (restated moments later, same answer, as a wider 2–5 range). Do **not** send 3 links to the money page from one post — this was Aaron's proposed-technique mistake, corrected live.
- (091): "never forget to do your internal links" before publishing — no new number given, reinforcing it as non-negotiable.
- **After publishing**: go back into **3 different older/already-existing (ideally already-indexed) posts** and add a link from each pointing to the new post (065, 080). This signals to Google the new content is valuable, gives it context, and helps discovery/crawling faster if the linking pages are already indexed (080).
- Pages receiving the most internal links pointing at them are treated by Google as more important (087).
- Hyperlink UX: internal links should open in the **same window/tab**, not a new one (087).
- Live worked example of a full post's link order (host's own post, written live): external link → internal link to a "content ideas for your website" post → internal link to "writing compelling headlines" (originally published 2023 — still linked because still relevant, proving recency of the target post isn't required) → another external link ("how to do keyword research") → internal link to a service page at the very end. Order is flexible, not a required template (080).
- On a **young site with few posts** (Sandy's case): don't force internal links that don't exist yet. Publish content first (10–20 posts as a rough bar), link to what you naturally can as you go — linking opportunities accumulate as the library grows (095).
- **Internal links vs. backlinks — do not conflate**: internal links = links between your own pages/posts; backlinks = links pointing to your site from someone else's site (069).

### 1.6 Publish-day promotion checklist

Full checklist (merged from 065, 087, 095):
- Share as a **Google Business Profile post/update**.
- Include it in your **weekly customer/email newsletter** — only if it's genuinely helpful content, not a sales pitch ("hire us to do this") (087).
- Post to **Reddit** (find a relevant subreddit, including local/city-specific ones) — doubles as a promotion channel and a keyword-validity signal (if Reddit already ranks for/discusses a query, real demand exists even if a tool shows no volume) (087).
- Post a summary + link on **LinkedIn**.
- Post on **X**.
- Post on **Facebook**.
- If you have contacts in an **adjacent (non-competing) business**, ask them to link to it (087).
- If doing this for a client, get the **client to post the article on their own LinkedIn** too — adds validity (087).
- Cross-post to **Pinterest** as well (069).
- Point of all the promotion: prove to Google the content is getting traffic/links from *outside* your own site — "we're not just trying to game the algorithm" (087).
- **Facebook-group promotion tactic**: when someone in a relevant local/niche Facebook group asks a question you can answer, don't reply inline or drop your homepage link — write (or reuse) a blog post answering it and share *that* link as your answer (087, 095: essentially the same tactic, credited to Jackie in both). AI and Google now pull results from local Facebook groups since "they're like community chats… real people" (087).
- **Testing AI-answer visibility as a separate experiment**: after a post ranks organically, cross-post the link to LinkedIn and answer related questions on Quora/Reddit (with a link back) to test whether it increases odds of AI-answer citation — explicitly framed as an active experiment, not a proven fix, since ranking #2 organically didn't automatically get one post cited in AI results (095).

### 1.7 Speeding up indexing

- Google Search Console gives **10 free URL-inspection "Request Indexing" submissions per day** — use them; pages typically index/rank within **~2 hours** of requesting (065; Kenneth's case: "not in top 100" → "#1 or #2" two hours later).
- Reciprocal internal linking (3 older posts → new post) and manual GSC index requests are both offered as indexing accelerants — not mutually exclusive, use either or both (065).
- No GSC resubmission is needed after fixing on-page SEO issues surfaced by an audit — Google's own crawl picks up changes automatically (069).

## 2. The Golden Rule: Never Touch a Ranking Page

The single most repeated rule across every call:
- "Any page that is starting to rank like that, don't touch it. Don't do anything on that page." (065, told to David; reinforced to Shanae re: her golf-course pages)
- "If you're already ranking for [keywords], don't touch those pages unless you're fine with… losing that traffic." (065)
- "**Once you optimize a page, stop touching it.** Because… if you keep updating it every time you update it, Google's like, well, I need to restart from zero again." (069)
- "**If it's ranking, don't touch the text.** I don't care if it doesn't match your brand guidelines, whatever." (069) — applies explicitly even to Shopify product pages, even against an SEO tool's better-reading suggested copy.
- If a page is already ranking and someone wants its *format* changed (page → blog or vice versa), don't — unless you're fine losing that traffic (065, Shanae's boss wanted her ranking golf-course pages converted to blog format; advised against it).
- If a blog post and a service page are already ranking for the same keyword and it's working, **leave it alone** — don't "fix" a technically-cannibalizing setup that's actually delivering results (087).
- Give new location pages **3+ months** for Google to sort out rankings before touching/tweaking keywords again (069).
- Before any major site change (domain switch, redesign, restructure): keep any page that's already getting traffic as close to unchanged as possible — content and URL (065). "If any page is getting traffic, you need to keep that page content as similar as you possibly can." (065)

## 3. Keyword & Page Strategy

### 3.1 Cannibalization: diagnosis and fixes

- **Diagnosing "wrong page ranking" cannibalization**: run the target keyword through the Hey Tony **Money Page Tool**; if it reports a *different* page of yours (e.g. homepage) as the #1 ranker instead of the page you're optimizing, that's the cannibalization signal (065, William's case: a low-search-volume keyword was being won by his homepage, not his service page).
- **De-cannibalizing two competing pages**: paste the full content of both pages into Claude and ask it to compare them and recommend what to change on each so they stop competing (065, Kenneth's tip, confirmed working by William).
- **Keyword-to-page mapping rule**: near-duplicate/variant keywords with no location difference can share one page; adding a location term makes it "a whole new keyword" and needs its own page — otherwise the two pages cannibalize each other in rankings (069). "If you add a location, that is a whole new keyword" (069).
- Blog + service page cannibalizing for the same keyword while both already rank and convert: leave it, don't fix it (087, see Section 2).

### 3.2 Location / service page buildout

- Keep the **main/home site on your true hyperlocal base keyword** even if lower-volume; build **one dedicated location page per additional service area**, each with that area's name in the title tag, meta description, and H1 — not reused copy from the home base (069, Rebecca M.'s cleaning company: weighing "Ancaster," her true low-volume/less-competitive home base, vs. "Hamilton," higher-volume/far more competitive — resolved by splitting targets across home site + dedicated location page rather than picking one).
- Location/service page linking structure (080, reworking Manny's poorly-built pages):
  - Keep linking FROM blog posts TO service pages (already correct).
  - Location pages CAN cross-link to neighboring location pages ("if you're in [nearby city], we also service that area").
  - Only link **2–3 blog posts max** from any given location/service page — don't repeat the same blog link across every location page.
  - A money page about pricing (e.g. "how much does X cost") can and should link out to a relevant blog post on that topic, and vice versa.
- **Local business cross-linking** (to *other* businesses, not your own pages): fine to link to genuinely relevant nearby businesses, but don't link all of them and don't do it a lot — avoid looking like a coordinated link network; link only where it "makes sense" for a human reader (069, Christina's whitewater-rafting/river-tubing local guide). Alternative for reciprocal-link temptation: advertise other local businesses on your site instead of trading links with them (065, Louise's real-estate content site — advised against reciprocal guest-post exchanges with fellow agents).
- **Layer local SEO into broad/national-style content**: even when a post answers a broad question (e.g. "how does hair color work"), add a local CTA sentence with an anchor link inside it — e.g. "If you're looking for a hairstylist in Denver, [link] give us a call today" — keep NAP (name/address/phone) in the site footer, and link out to your Google Business Profile/reviews from posts (095, Jackie). Traffic from outside your service area still has value even though it can't convert directly: it costs nothing extra and builds engagement/trust signals with Google, so broad content is still worth publishing for a local-only business as long as local CTAs/NAP/GBP signals are layered in (095): "I can't service that person [out-of-area visitor]… they're helping me in a different way [building trust signals with Google]."
- **Annual/seasonal evergreen pages**: keep one evergreen URL with no year in it; each year, update just the title tag, meta description, and H1 to the new year (2026 → 2027 → 2028) instead of creating a new page — preserves the URL's accumulated equity/trust. Only fully retire an old dated page if it has no backlinks and no traffic; otherwise leave it live (069, Colleen's summer-camp pages).
- **Blog-now/service-page-later planning**: don't pre-plan a future 301 from a blog post to a not-yet-built geo-specific service page. First check what already ranks for the target keyword — if service pages dominate that SERP, a blog post targeting the same term won't rank well regardless. Wait until budget exists for the real service page; meanwhile write a *different* blog post (one you won't need to redirect later) and internally link it to the new service page once it's actually built (065, Leah's case). "Why are you doing extra work just to redirect later? That's going to confuse Google." (065)
- Check what format currently ranks (service page vs. blog post) for a keyword before choosing your own page format — writing the "wrong" format for that SERP won't rank regardless of quality (065).

### 3.3 Niche ecommerce & transactional keywords

- **Where transactional keywords should live**: put high-intent/transactional terms (e.g. "Scorpio candle") on **collection pages** (Shopify collection page = category/landing page) or specific product pages, not necessarily blog posts — you can't rank for a keyword without a page built around that exact keyword (087). "You can't rank for a keyword if you don't have a page about that keyword." (087)
- **Portfolio strategy when no single head term has enough volume**: build many small/medium transactional keyword variants (per zodiac sign, per birth month, per Chinese-zodiac year, etc.) instead of chasing one big saturated keyword (087, Todd/"Cosmos Within" candle brand). "Everybody's trying to get through that front door… I want to go through the back door where nobody else is going through… that's what these smaller keywords allow us to do." (087)
- **Blog angle for niche ecommerce**: write "best gifts for [segment]" posts linking to (a) a relevant product and (b) other complementary products for that segment, plus one **hub/master post** ("find your horoscope" — full breakdown of all signs/months) that links out to each individual segment post (087).
- **Seasonal/anticipatory publishing**: write a post for a keyword tied to a future calendar trigger (e.g. "gifts for a May birthday") months before that period's actual demand spike, so Google has time to rank it before seasonal search volume arrives (087, Todd had already independently started this and was confirmed to be doing it right).
- Don't replace regular blogging with transactional-keyword-only pages — do transactional targeting *additionally*, never *instead of* blogging: "Do not do that instead of writing blog posts, do that additionally." (087) The host was, at call time, personally testing a brand-new site built to target *only* transactional keywords, explicitly framed as an unproven parallel experiment, not a validated replacement strategy (087).
- **Blog-to-paid-product pipeline**: publish the free/shorter version as a blog post, then build a longer, more detailed version and sell that as a standalone paid guide (087, Jackie turned a curly-hair guide into a paid guide sold to people who never come in for the in-person service).
- **Turning a recurring support question into a reusable asset**: write the how-to once as a blog post, then send that link to every new client/customer instead of repeating the same walkthrough call each time — saves time now, can pick up organic traffic later (087, host's own case study: a team member wrote "how to add and remove users from Meta Business Manager" ~3–4 years ago; that link replaced a 30–60 min onboarding call for every new Meta-ads client and eventually earned organic traffic too). Aaron similarly wrote a post on a commonly-asked "vehicles" question purely as a link-out asset rather than a full secondary page (087).

### 3.4 One site, one keyword, one audience

- **Work on one website at a time.** Don't split SEO effort across multiple sites simultaneously — you end up half-assing all of them (weaker internal linking, thinner execution across the board) (095).
- If you own two sites/brands targeting the same audience/keyword, **pick ONE as the primary SEO/content focus**; use the other to link toward it (e.g. "if you'd rather do this yourself, check out our DIY course [link]") instead of publishing similar content on both and competing with your own name/authority across two domains (095, Jessa: a Washington-state in-person therapy site + a separate national/DIY course site — advised to primary-focus the course site and treat the therapy site as a secondary link-out). "Google's gonna be like, well, I'm not gonna let the same person rank twice. I don't care if they have two different websites." (095) Tempting ("ranking #1 and #2 would be fantastic") but usually backfires (095).

### 3.5 Multi-language sites

- Pick **one language for blog/general content**; rely on browser-level auto-translation for the rest. Only money/service pages need to be published in the language the customer actually understands (069, Melanie in Switzerland, corroborated by Jorg who runs German-market ecommerce entirely in English). Framed as a cost most small/solo operators shouldn't pay for — one language + auto-translate captures most of the value versus manually publishing in 2–3 languages (069).

## 4. Backlinks & Off-Site Signals

### 4.1 Rules

- **Never reciprocate links — one-directional only.** Even a single reciprocal swap with one or two people "destroys the link equity you're building." Getting 10–20 people to link to you while you link to none of them is fine (065). "You don't want reciprocating links… that's like two best friends being like, 'yeah, she's awesome,' and then the other friend says 'she's awesome.' But if it's just a stranger being like she's awesome, then that carries more weight." (065)
- **You only need ONE link from one domain** — additional links from that same domain lose value/diminishing returns (091). This directly overturns prior advice one member (Samantha) had received, that a separate blog domain would generate valuable backlinks to the main site (091).
- Backlink outreach is optional, not mandatory — the host personally skips formal outreach and prefers a lighter-touch ask to an adjacent, non-competing contact instead (087). Judged as a real lever whose time cost isn't worth the uncertain payoff, for him personally (087).
- Verify actual ranking keywords via **Google Search Console**, not manual self-searching — it shows the real queries you're appearing for, much of it driven by local relevance (087). For a national/competitive view, use Semrush or Ubersuggest and refresh stats — but coverage is incomplete, so GSC remains the more complete source of truth (087).

### 4.2 Directories

- Do sign up for **local directories** — chamber of commerce, local newspaper, local business directory — they send local relevance signals (065).
- National/global generic directories are lower-value ("lower level links") but not harmful (065). "Directories are lower level, like links anyways." (065)
- **Never sign up for directories based in a country you don't operate in** (065).
- **brightlocal.com** automates submission to a batch of business directories (not literally every directory an audit surfaces) (065).

### 4.3 Press-release → AI Overview tactic (AB Newswire)

Distinct, higher-risk growth tactic surfaced in 091 — treat as a discrete play, not baseline practice:
1. Buy a press release through **AB Newswire** (~$80 single-release tier, or a bulk package ~$901 that brings effective cost down to **~$8/release**).
2. Link the release to your best/target existing page (host linked his to his ranking blog post *and* his Amazon book listing).
3. Real "news" isn't required — a review or routine business update works: e.g., "[Company] receives raving review," then talk about the review and what you do (live example: "Adwise Creative receives raving review," for Nick).
4. Press releases officially aren't supposed to use superlative language like "best" — the host broke this rule and it still worked.
5. Outcome: even one press release can get you referenced inside Google AI Overviews/AI results for a broad, generic term, not just your brand name.
- **PR Newswire** is a pricier alternative that requires requesting a demo rather than self-serve checkout — flagged as "not ideal," not tested live.
- Case result: the host's own book "Get Found" — one AB Newswire release, linked to his ranking blog post (ranking #2 for a "best SEO book"-style query) and his Amazon listing (#1 under "best-selling search engine optimization books"). Result: the standalone term "get found" now surfaces inside Google AI Overviews; picked up by USA Today and, briefly, the Globe and Mail.
- **Backfire case**: Edward Sturm (a friend, informed beforehand) posted an Instagram video publicly calling the tactic "manipulating AI." The resulting attention led the Globe and Mail to take its article down — the AI-cited link now 404s. Publicizing a growth-hack can neutralize its own win.
- Stance: use your own judgment — the host doesn't believe this will harm a site long-term the way buying spammy backlinks would, but he isn't depending on it to rank and doesn't know if it will keep working ("is it going to work forever? Maybe, maybe not").
- Always add internal links to a new post before publishing regardless — "never forget to do your internal links" (091).

## 5. Technical Diagnostics

### 5.1 Orphan / unreachable pages

Two independent diagnostic methods, both valid:
- **Manual click-through**: start from the homepage and try to click through to the flagged page via on-site navigation; if you can't reach it that way, it's orphaned or buried too deep and needs a link from somewhere higher in the site structure (065, Linda's jewelry-ecommerce site — Audit Tool flagged a product page as "not accessible" despite it displaying fine when found manually).
- **Private/incognito test**: grab the direct URL and open it in a private browser window — if it loads, anyone with the link can still reach it (it's just unlinked); if it doesn't load, it's genuinely blocked (069, Ariel's orphan/QR-code pages). Intentionally-kept orphan/utility pages (QR-code event promos) don't need deleting just to chase a perfect audit score — "it's never going to be perfect and that's okay" (069).

### 5.2 Spam-looking backlink profiles blocking indexing

Full live diagnostic sequence (095, Thomas's site — Albania, ~6 months old, AI/web-hosting niche, only the homepage was indexed):
1. Pull the site up in **Semrush**, switch to **root domain** view.
2. Check **Authority Score** and **Referring Domains** (Thomas: 127 referring domains).
3. Check **Backlinks (all time)** — Thomas's count was in the hundreds of thousands (heard on the call as both 288,000 and 406,000 — audio unclear, but the point is an extreme, disproportionate count).
4. Site was also flagged as **"dangerous"** in the tool.
5. Identify the single referring site responsible for the bulk of links — in Thomas's case, one of his own client sites linking back via a disproportionate footer credit.
6. **Remove that footer link.** A concentrated, disproportionate backlink source from one domain reads as spam/gaming-the-system to Google and can suppress indexing of everything except the homepage.
- **Rule derived**: if any single external/client site is responsible for **more than 100 backlinks** to your domain, remove your link from that site (095).
- **Two-site duplicate-profile variant**: if a second site (same owner) also won't index, compare the two sites' backlink profiles — if the same backlinks point to both domains (near-identical referring-domain sets), Google may treat them as duplicate/spam and index only one. Fix: strip the shared/duplicate backlinks pointing at whichever site you care less about (095, Thomas's second site: reportedly 200,000+ shared backlinks).
- **Don't misdiagnose**: a keyword being objectively "difficult" is not, by itself, a reason a whole site fails to index beyond the homepage — Thomas had separately been told his difficult keyword targeting was the problem; the real root cause was the spam backlink profile, unrelated to keyword choice. Check backlinks before reworking keyword strategy (095).
- Parallel case: the host's own past domain previously carried roughly **300,000 backlinks** concentrated from one or two sites — same pattern, giving him instant recognition of Thomas's issue live (095).

### 5.3 Word count & keyword density: don't trust one tool

- Don't fully trust word-count or keyword-density numbers from any single tool — extension vs. extension vs. manual count can differ by hundreds of words on the same page (095).
- **Reconciliation process**: check the on-page count from at least two extensions plus a manual copy-paste into a word-counter site; treat the spread as a directional range, not a precise figure. If a tool's count is wildly higher than what you can visually count, view the page source — look for hidden pop-up text, mobile-only text, or plugin-injected duplicate text (095).
- Tools generally don't account for keyword variants (e.g. "web design" vs. "website design") when calculating density — expect reported counts to be inflated versus true literal usage (095).
- Live examples: one page reported ~2,000 words (team's own "page audit extension"), ~1,900 words (Detailed SEO extension), ~2,400 words (manual word-counter paste) — three different numbers for one page (095). A community member's density check on one of the host's own pieces reported the keyword used **35 times**; manual recount found it actually used **~5 times** literally, the rest coming from a closely related variant term being lumped in (095). One member's target density was ~1.5% but his own audit tool showed 0.8% for the same content (095). A prior client wasn't ranking despite "doing all the right stuff"; 3–4 months in, inspecting page source revealed a plugin injecting the same 2,000 words of hidden text onto every page — even though the plugin appeared disabled — making every page read as duplicate content to Google (095).
- On SEMrush specifically: trust **Keyword Difficulty** as the primary signal; don't weight "Competitive Density" heavily (065, Amanda's confusion resolved this way). Don't pay for both Yoast and SEMrush — do keyword research anywhere (e.g. Ubersuggest) and manually enter numbers where needed (065).

### 5.4 Domain moves & migrations

- **Domain forwarding** (old domain → new domain, homepage → homepage) is fine, even after the old domain's already been printed on business cards/QR codes (065, Marita: forwarded marisacomedy.com to her new personal-brand domain).
- **Full domain changes are high-risk**: expect to lose essentially all existing rankings and backlink equity even after notifying Google of the move — "it's almost like you're starting from scratch" (065). Case: Boomi changed her domain in January to match her brand; rankings collapsed afterward despite notifying Google — characterized as "six years down the drain" (six years of prior rankings/authority effectively reset). Reframed for her: "That's not down the drain. You're starting new." (065)
- **Migrating off a third-party blog add-on to a platform's native blog**: don't bother 301-redirecting the old posts — just republish the same content natively, then cancel/delete the old service; those old URLs were barely indexed/ranking anyway (065, Nelly migrating BigCommerce + DropInBlog to BigCommerce's native Storefront → Blog engine). Root technical issue that preceded this decision: connect **Cloudflare** to DropInBlog so blog URLs resolve as clean same-domain paths instead of exposing query strings/raw HTML; DNS needs to sit with Cloudflare rather than split between GoDaddy (registrar) and BigCommerce (065).
- **Consolidate a split-domain blog onto your main site's domain**: if your blog lives on a different domain/subdomain than your main site (e.g. a Wix blog next to a Shopify store), migrate it onto the main domain — don't keep it separate for editing convenience, even when the native blog editor is worse to work with (Shopify's "Blogs"/"Posts" section has limited image/formatting control vs. Wix) (091, Samantha: merged two product-line sites into one Shopify store but kept blog content on a separate Wix URL under prior advice it would build backlinks to the main site — corrected live to consolidate; ties directly to the "one link from one domain" rule in §4.1).
- Domain forwarding is generally safe but not risk-free: a domain carrying a lot of bad backlinks or a prior Google penalty could carry risk into the destination — tempered by the belief that Google is generally smart enough to discount deliberate negative-SEO link-dumping from competitors (065).

### 5.5 Friday "Why Doesn't It Rank" hot-seat method

Separate weekly call (see §10) using this comparison method (091): pick an underperforming page, then directly compare it to whoever currently ranks for that query — check their backlink count, how often/where they use the target keyword, whether their content is simply better, whether they use a top summary, whether they use key takeaways at the bottom, and how many internal links point to their page.

## 6. Content Structure Rules (Quick Reference)

- Cadence: **one published post/week is "fantastic"; two is "great"** (080) — but elsewhere capped explicitly at **no more than 2/week**, since going faster risks sloppiness or reusing recycled material ("oh, I already have something better") (095). At 2/week that's roughly **50–60 posts/year**, which is why teams run out of topic ideas and need multiple discovery tools (087). Don't overwhelm yourself — "if it takes you three weeks to do it… that is still fine. You're still making progress." (069)
- Pricing-page/blog hygiene: whenever prices change, update **every** page/post stating them, not just the main pricing page — clients will hold you to whatever number they found on any indexed page, including old blog posts (065, Carol's case: a client held her to an old, un-updated blog-post price after a price increase). State prices plainly ("these are our prices") rather than date-stamping them ("as of March 2026") — and still update every instance sitewide whenever pricing actually changes (065).
- Cornerstone content isn't something to deliberately build toward — treat it as something that emerges naturally, not a checklist item (065).
- Repurposing podcast episodes: don't publish raw transcripts as indexed pages — a podcast episode is "more of a shotgun… you're not going to hit anything," where "a blog is very laser focused" (065). Instead, write a blog post answering an actually-searched question, and reference the podcast/guest inside it as supporting authority (e.g. "Jim and I talked about this on our recent episode — here's what Jim had to say about ___"). For frequent guests, build a "Featured/Recent Guests" page with photo + short bio + link to their episode, to pick up "podcast with [Guest Name]"-type searches (065, Mike's podcast-site redesign).
- "Google likes experts. And if you can show that you're an expert, then Google will likely… promote you to the top." (069) — the framing to use when explaining "why blog at all" to a skeptic: Google needs context/proof of expertise beyond a bare services list.
- **Authenticity content trend**: film ~30-second real clips of an actual team member on site instead of faceless/stock content — e.g. someone named "Jim" on a roof cracking/bending a shingle to show granule loss and explain why a roof needs replacing, or a short piece testing how long "15-year shingles" actually last (095, Sandy/roofing client). "Where things are going… is more authenticity, more off the cuff, more real." (095) On faceless AI content: "Who wrote this? … that is telling who is writing the content on the internet." (095)

## 12. Tradeoffs & Open Questions

- **Claude vs. ChatGPT** isn't absolute: Claude is the default for SEO blog writing (more natural prose, fewer edits, powers Hey Tony's own Blog Post Writer), but the host still reaches for ChatGPT when Claude's output on a specific task (e.g. newsletter copy) comes out "long and convoluted" (065).
- **Domain forwarding** is generally safe but not risk-free: a domain with bad backlinks or a prior penalty could carry risk forward, tempered by belief that Google discounts deliberate negative-SEO link-dumping (065).
- **Podcast transcripts** look like they should help SEO (dense, keyword-rich) but don't — they don't answer one specific searched problem; the fix is extracting question-answering content and citing the episode, not publishing the transcript (065).
- **Reciprocal vs. one-directional links**: even limited reciprocal exchanges are treated as link-equity-destroying, while unlimited one-directional inbound links are fine — a deliberate asymmetry (065).
- **Reassigning a page to a bigger keyword** risks losing the smaller, already-secured ranking — a real tradeoff; the Claude-comparison de-cannibalization trick manages the transition but doesn't remove the risk (065).
- **AI answer-engine visibility panels** (WIX, similar tools) are informative but not fully trustworthy — they generate their own guessed prompts about a site rather than reflecting real queries, and there's no way to directly edit what an AI says about a business (065). "Did anybody even search that? They just kind of made it up." (065)
- **Authority Score rising** is a genuinely good signal (more traffic and/or backlinks) but explicitly separated from "confirmed ranking improvement" — it's a made-up metric, not a ranking factor (080). "The authority score is a made up metric that is not a hundred percent correlated with ranking." (080)
- **Paid keyword tools** sharpen volume/targeting confidence but are optional — the whole free-tools framing (AnswerThePublic/PAA + ChatGPT) is proven to produce real traffic/sales without them (080, 087).
- **Broadening a keyword deliberately** (ColdFusion → "web hosting") sacrifices exact-match relevance short-term to build topical authority that should make the ultra-specific term rankable later — a bet, not a guarantee (080, 087: Todd's zodiac-candle portfolio strategy is the ecommerce version of this same bet).
- **Schema markup**: skeptical/deprioritized stance, but the host's own supporting data point (non-ranking pages had *more* schema than ranking pages) is drawn from one site only and explicitly not yet confirmed across client sites (080).
- **Title tag vs. rendered heading vs. platform snippet** are three different layers that get conflated when debugging truncation — fixing the wrong layer won't fix the complaint (080).
- **Internal-link ordering** within a post is flexible — the host's own example order is illustrative, not a required template (080).
- **Location/service cross-linking** carries a light-touch cannibalization caution (cap 2–3 blog links, don't repeat everywhere) framed as a "don't overdo it" norm, not an enforced technical penalty (080).
- **FAQ blocks**: real AI-answer/SEO value exists in the right format, but a generic FAQ list bolted onto every post isn't worth it unless genuinely useful to a human reader (069, 091 — the host's own FAQ avoidance is flagged as personal UX preference, not hard rule).
- **Cost vs. access** on the toolkit: capping Blog Writer at 10 generations/account prevents runaway API cost for the host at the expense of a hard ceiling for every user, including a bug where failed runs still burn a slot (069).
- **Perfection vs. usefulness** on site audits: an audit/health score is directional, not a max-out target — orphan/utility pages are an acceptable permanent ding (069).
- **Multi-language publishing**: framed as a cost most small/solo operators shouldn't pay; one language + auto-translate captures most of the value, though transactional/money pages still need the customer's real language (069).
- **Blog+service cannibalization** is a technical risk in principle but the practical rule is: don't touch a setup that's already working (087).
- **Backlink outreach**: a real lever the host personally skips, judging time cost not worth uncertain payoff; substitutes a lighter adjacent-contact ask instead (087).
- **Rank-tracking tools** (Semrush/Ubersuggest) give useful competitive/national views but can't surface 100% of a site's actual ranking keywords; GSC is the more complete source of truth (087).
- **Zero/near-zero volume** isn't automatically disqualifying if other demand evidence exists (a subreddit already ranking for/discussing it) (087, 091).
- **Pinterest ROI is niche-dependent**: strong impressions but weak clicks for a marketing/SEO-adjacent account over 6 months of heavy posting, while reportedly much stronger for the hair industry — the same playbook doesn't generalize (087).
- **Transactional-only content** is being tested by the host himself as an unproven parallel strategy, explicitly additive to normal blogging, not a validated replacement (087).
- **The press-release/AI-Overview tactic** works right now and produced real pickup, but is explicitly framed as unproven long-term; publicizing a growth-hack can itself neutralize the win (Edward Sturm/Globe and Mail case) (091).
- **TL;DR vs. key-takeaways vs. neither** remains an open, ongoing personal experiment — key takeaways at the end are worth doing regardless of length; a top TL;DR is only worth it for longer content whose paragraph one doesn't already cover the direct answer (091).
- **Blog-domain consolidation** (Samantha's case): the SEO-correct move (merge onto the main domain) trades against real practical friction — a worse native editor vs. the external tool that prompted the original split. The host acknowledges the downside but still recommends migrating (091).
- **AI image generation** is far cheaper/faster than custom design work (~$100–200+/image otherwise) but format conversion, compression, and upload remain manual bottlenecks — genuinely more time-consuming than the writing itself in practice (Nick: 45 min–1.5 hrs on images vs. 15–20 min writing) (095).
- **Ranking organically vs. being cited in AI answers** are treated as two separate outcomes — ranking #2 didn't get one post mentioned in AI results, so social redistribution is being tested as a possible lever for AI visibility, not a proven fix (095).
- **Out-of-area traffic** still has value for a local business even though it can't convert directly — it costs nothing extra and builds trust/engagement signals with Google (095).
- **Owning two sites for the same audience** is tempting but usually backfires — better to pick one primary and cross-link from the secondary (095).
- **Keyword difficulty vs. indexing failure** are separate problems that get conflated — check backlink profile before reworking keyword strategy (095).
- A **Safari-specific rendering bug** on one Insiders keyword-tool page (content cut off/blank below the top) was reported and confirmed live, works fine in Chrome, left unresolved at call time (087).
