## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Avalanche technique** (why write a blog every Monday): post #1 might bring 2–3 visitors/week; post #2 the next week stacks on top so it's now ~6/week; then ~12; then ~24 — traffic compounds post over post, and it builds trust/topical authority with Google that helps the harder keywords rank later.
- **Diagnosing a CTR/impressions plateau**: go into Search Console, filter to one specific page, sort the keyword table by impressions, find the keyword(s) getting the most impressions but poor position, and rework/add that exact keyword into the page content (this is the method taught in the "10X Your Traffic For Free" training).
- **Diagnosing a ranking/traffic drop after edits**: open the page/post in WordPress → **Revisions** panel, compare to prior versions, cross-reference the date the drop started against your edit history, revert if a specific change lines up with the drop. Always save/keep the current version before reverting in case the revert doesn't help.
- **Protecting a high-traffic legacy post** (William's tarot example): do not rewrite or restructure a post that's already #1 and driving most of your site's traffic, even if its search intent doesn't match what you want to sell. Instead bolt on a popup or a nav-menu button ("get our course here") linking to the offer — leave the ranking post itself untouched. The post is functioning as topical-authority proof to Google; touching it risks the whole site.
- **Setting target word count for new content**: check what the current #1–3 ranking competitors have for word count on that exact keyword, then build that number into your AI prompt *before* it generates the piece (e.g., "my competitors have 1,500–2,000 words, aim for around 1,750") rather than defaulting to a fixed rule.
- **Fixing Kajabi's duplicate-H1 issue**: identify which H1 doesn't matter (e.g., a blog-index title like "Relationship Clarity Blog" sitting above every individual post's own H1) and convert that one to plain paragraph text (or at minimum H2) so the real post title is the first/only H1 on the page. Rule stated: the H1 should always be the first heading tag on the page.
- **Fixing Yoast auto-appended long titles**: Yoast SEO → Settings → Content Types → Posts (repeat for Pages) → find the title template field → remove the "Site title" variable that's auto-appended after the separator → Save. This is what was inflating title/H1 length past the audit-tool threshold.
- **Fixing WordPress location-page spam** (Elliot's case): the rule — "if I can't get to it from your homepage, Google's not going to index it." Unlinked pages built via Claude before Rank Week should be deleted since they do nothing. Going forward: build only 1–2 unique location pages a week, each linked into the site nav/footer, each written with genuinely different local detail/angle (e.g., mention oil-field workers for an Alberta-specific page vs. a different angle for Toronto) — not templated duplicate copy.
- **Large product catalogs with thin vendor data** (Kate's ~400 Shopify diamond SKUs, synced with no headers/text, just spec tables): do keyword research first (Semrush / hey-keywords.com / Ubersuggest) on the base term ("Canadian diamonds," "ethical diamonds," "engagement diamond," etc.) rather than assuming you already know the phrasing. Use the fields that are genuinely unique per SKU (carat weight, clarity, cut/grade) inside the title/H1 to make each product page a unique keyword. Split strategy: put the broad/competitive keyword ("ethically sourced Canadian diamonds") on **collection pages**; let individual **product pages** rely mainly on spec differentiators (carat/weight/clarity) without needing a full unique keyword phrase each. Roll out in small batches (settled on ~20/week) instead of doing all 400 at once, so lessons from the first batch can be applied before repeating the same approach 400 times.
- **Diagnosing "why am I not ranking at all" / over-optimization** (Shiloh's Costa Rica case): pull up the actual #1–3 ranking pages for your target keyword and compare word count and content depth directly — if your page has far more words/information than the top rankers, trim it down; you may only need a fraction of what you've written.
- **Pruning dead pages**: in Search Console, find pages with zero impressions / zero indexing / zero traffic — treat these as useless and remove them.
- **Making internal links visible**: underline them and give them a distinguishing color so they visually "pop" against body text (flagged when a member's blog had internal links but they weren't visually distinct).
- **GA4 user-journey / channel-attribution questions**: don't rely on memorizing GA4 — ask Claude or ChatGPT directly (e.g., "how do I see the user journey in GA4" or "how do I find which page ChatGPT-referred traffic lands on") and follow the specific click-path/filter instructions it returns.
- **Finding the exact Monday prompt text**: go to the Community tab inside Insiders and scroll to the post titled "Prompt for today" — screenshot it there rather than trying to read tiny on-screen text during a replay.
- **Locating the "10X Your Traffic For Free" training**: Video Training section → scroll to "5 Expert" → "10X Your Traffic for Free" (auto-unlocks at Level 5, but watchable by anyone at any level).
- **WebP image conversion workflow**: Canva cannot export WebP directly — export as PNG/JPG from Canva first, then run the file through a separate WebP converter tool before uploading.
- **External-link rule in practice**: on money pages/homepage, external-link to your own legitimacy profiles (Chamber of Commerce, Trustpilot, Glassdoor, or similar review platforms) rather than a competitor or unrelated site — and whenever you state a stat/claim ("50% of businesses do X"), hyperlink that exact phrase to the actual source/study making the claim, instead of a separate "References" list.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- SEO takes **3–6 months** of consistent work minimum; real inflection points can arrive as late as **month 12–14** even when monthly gains looked small/flat before that.
- Don't obsessively check rankings/analytics and react to daily noise — looking daily is fine as a habit, but do not take action based on day-to-day fluctuation. Same logic applies to credit scores and investment accounts.
- No industry-standard "healthy" click-through-rate or impressions-to-clicks ratio exists — don't benchmark against a mythical universal number.
- Top **3** organic positions capture roughly **90%** of clicks; the rest of page 1 splits the remaining ~10%; page 2 gets roughly **1–2%**, effectively zero. Ranking for more (lower-position) keywords will mathematically drag your average CTR down — that's expected, not a problem.
- A single page can rank for hundreds to 1,000+ keyword variations at once; most individual keyword rows will show zero clicks — normal, not a failure.
- Backlinks should point to the **specific page** you want ranked, matching what top-3 competitors have (e.g., if competitors have 5 backlinks to their money page, target ~5 backlinks to yours) — backlinks anywhere still lift the whole site, but page-specific links matter most for that page's own ranking.
- Put **one external outbound link per page**, including the homepage and money pages — this does not meaningfully cause visitor drop-off and builds trust signals.
- Location pages are a legitimate, effective tactic — but only if each one is genuinely unique to that location; duplicated boilerplate across pages is what fails.
- Stated baseline blog length in the program: **750–1,500 words** total (cited as roughly matching what Google prefers) — but the real rule is to size relative to current top-3 competitors for that keyword, not to force the fixed range.
- More content/more keyword repetition is **not** automatically better — over-optimization (excess length, excess keyword use) is called one of the most common SEO pitfalls and can actively hurt rankings.
- Category pages for **blog** content will basically never rank — don't bother adding meta descriptions/focus keyphrases to them. Exception: Shopify **collection** pages behave differently and can and do rank — worth optimizing those.
- A gallery/project-style page (mostly images, little text) showing impressions but zero keyword queries isn't necessarily broken — sometimes Google just doesn't pass query data through. Only add text (aim ~400–500 words) if there's real search demand for that specific project; otherwise leave it and give it time.
- Cap batch production of near-identical pages (product pages, location pages) — don't do all of them the same way in one go; learn from a small batch first (Kate settled on ~20/week rather than all 400 diamonds at once).
- Don't manually request indexing page-by-page as a priority — focus on making pages properly optimized (human-descriptive, internally linked) and let Google's natural crawl pick them up.
- New post indexing can take anywhere from **~2 hours to up to a week** — don't panic if it's not indexed at 24–48 hours.
- After a fix (e.g., adding alt text), wait roughly **a month** before re-checking/re-crawling — also account for possible caching delay before assuming a fix failed.
- AI-generated images (e.g., via Nano Banana) are fine to use, as long as you're not directly recreating someone else's specific photo.
- GoHighLevel (GHL) as a website platform is fine for SEO but sacrifices control over CSS and site speed versus something like WordPress.
- Only use **5–10 categories** total, treating each as a content "vertical"/topic. Avoid tags entirely — every tag spins up its own thin/near-empty archive page the moment you stop posting about that exact tag term.
- Don't touch sitemap plugin config beyond excluding tags — keep categories included in the sitemap, and otherwise leave whatever the plugin auto-generates alone.
- Don't insert filler words like "service" into a URL slug (e.g., prefer `/bathroom` over `/service-bathroom` style slugs) — it adds no ranking value.
- When both phrasings of a keyword ("wood chipper" vs. "wood chippers") show equal search volume in your keyword tool, Google is very likely treating them as the same keyword — just use the grammatically correct one and monitor.
- Homepage/navigation depth: content should be reachable in roughly 1–2 clicks from the homepage via the nav menu; anything buried 4–5 clicks deep is effectively invisible even if technically linked.
- Don't simplify or restructure a homepage based on generic advice heard elsewhere — check Search Console first: if the site/pages are already ranking, leave the homepage alone; only test changes if it's not ranking.
- Auto-rotating homepage carousels are discouraged — people generally don't interact with them manually.
- Google Analytics traffic includes every channel (social, ads, events, PR mentions), not just organic search — a "drastic" GA traffic drop doesn't necessarily mean an SEO problem; check Search Console specifically for the organic trend before worrying.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **White labeling**: upside is free client flow with zero marketing effort on your end; downside is concentration risk — a single large referral source can gain leverage over time and squeeze margins or dictate terms (Matt's own $250K/year client he had to fire).
- **Checking data daily**: harmless as passive observation, actively harmful the moment you react to normal day-to-day noise — applies equally to Search Console/GA, credit scores, and investment accounts per Matt and CJ's parallel examples.
- **Location pages**: can be a genuinely strong local-SEO tactic or pure spam that fails to rank and drags the whole site down — the entire difference is uniqueness and internal linking, not the tactic itself.
- **Blog word count**: the program's stated 750–1,500-word guideline is a rule of thumb, not gospel — real target should follow current top-3 competitor benchmarks, which is why blindly applying a fixed number (or letting an AI interview generate ~30,000 raw words) breaks down without a competitor-informed cutoff.
- **CTR appearing to "decline"**: a natural side effect of ranking for more (often lower-position) keywords as a site grows — the opposite of what it intuitively feels like when the number drops.
- **Homepage simplification**: not universally right or wrong; the deciding factor is current ranking performance in Search Console, not generic advice heard from elsewhere ("simplify your homepage") that may not apply to your specific site's situation.
- **Protecting a proven traffic asset vs. monetizing it**: William's #1 tarot post could theoretically convert better if rewritten toward buying intent, but risks losing the topical-authority traffic that supports the rest of the site — resolved by adding a non-invasive CTA (popup/nav link) instead of editing the core asset.
- **Manual indexing requests vs. natural crawl**: manual submission isn't the lever that matters; well-optimized, internally-linked pages get indexed regardless, so time is better spent on the page itself.
- **Batch-producing many similar pages fast** (400 product pages, or many location pages): trades short-term speed against the risk of having to redo everything once you learn the correct approach — capping batches (10–20/week) balances progress against rework risk.
- **GHL vs. WordPress as a platform**: GHL is fine for SEO fundamentals but sacrifices CSS/speed control that WordPress offers — a convenience-vs-control tradeoff.
- **Semrush vs. hey-keywords.com**: Semrush is fuller-featured but costly at scale (~$150/mo/user, ~$2–3K/mo for a team); hey-keywords.com is cheaper (~$30/mo) but deliberately more limited — full power at higher cost vs. streamlined at lower cost.
