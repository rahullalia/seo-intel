# Rank Week — Day 5 Replay (Matt Diamante, HeyTony)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **30-Day SEO Action Plan spreadsheet**, built live on the call: 4 weekly blocks, **same 4 tasks repeated every week** (new blog post + new money page each cycle):
  1. Publish 1 blog post that links to **2 other blog posts + 1 money page** (internal linking).
  2. Fix **5 "serious" (red) issues** flagged by the audit tool.
  3. Fix the title tag + meta description on 1 money page (~5 minutes; one prompt from earlier in Rank Week fixes both at once).
  4. Add **3 internal links** pointing to that money page from other pages on the site.
  - If one task already satisfies another (e.g. your new blog post already links to the money page), don't redo it — just check it off.
- **Blog-topic sourcing method** (this session deliberately skips AnswerThePublic "because I don't want to crash it again and I don't want you guys to get frustrated"): Google your core niche keyword → scroll to **"People also ask"** → click a question to surface more similar ones → also check **"People also search for."** Pull **4 different questions**, not near-duplicate angles (e.g. not two blood-pressure variants back to back). If the seed keyword returns nothing useful, type a full question instead of a bare term and see what else surfaces.
- **Money-pages tab**: go to your own site's services/products section, pick your **4 most important** service/product pages, paste them in.
- **Calendar-commitment step**: on the call, commit out loud to 4 calendar dates (one per week) for the 1-2 hour SEO session and paste the plan-spreadsheet link into each calendar invite. Matt's own example dates: Dec 15, 22, 29, Jan 5 (all Mondays — "you can choose whatever day of the week works best for you"). Taking Christmas week off entirely is fine.
- **Cannibalization reassurance / topical-authority logic** (hot sauce demo): 4 blog posts that each mention "hot sauce," plus a hot sauce money page, don't cannibalize each other because each full question is its own distinct keyword ("what is hot sauce made of" ≠ "what is the healthiest hot sauce"). Analogy: a dentist can't talk about teeth on only one page and still become a topical expert.
- **Audit-tool workflow**: open an issue category (e.g. "broken link") → see every page on the site with that issue → open a page, scroll to the specific flagged links → manually load each one; if it actually resolves (false positive — e.g. a live canva.com link the crawler couldn't reach), click **"manually validate"** to clear it from the count → click **"New crawl"** when done for the day (site recrawls every 24 hours). Missing H1 tags are flagged as serious — fix on real content/money pages using earlier Rank Week prompts (Matt shrugs off a missing H1 only on a throwaway page with zero SEO purpose — a ChatGPT-built/coded mini-game he embedded just for fun).
- **Competitor-audit tactic**: run the audit tool against competitor URLs too, not just your own site, to directly compare serious-issue counts, H1 usage, and page speed side by side (Eric Peltier ran it on his own Key West charter site plus his top 2 ranking competitors).
- **Prioritization when a site has many problem areas at once** (Dave Jackson's ~17 pages targeting one keyword; Manny's services+money+city+blog mix): start with whatever the audit tool flags most severe (duplicate title tags/meta descriptions/heading tags across pages first); fix money pages first overall, but keep publishing at least 1 blog post/week in parallel rather than pausing content.
- **Local backlink tactics** for a service business starting from zero (Ash, cleaning company): join the local chamber of commerce, join local business associations, list on local directories, cross-link with other local businesses (e.g. clean a business's office in exchange for a link), run a giveaway (free service), or support a local charity (e.g. ~$50) in exchange for a logo/link/shout-out on their site or social.
- **Expanding a local service into a new city** (Alejandro, cleaning co.): build a separate, unique-content location page per target city — no duplicated copy — and expect **3-6 months minimum** before ranking movement even if the page itself is solid.
- **Seasonal / multi-country service** (unnamed performer/artist, Scotland-based with an Ibiza summer-only offering): keep doing SEO year-round despite seasonality; set Google Business Profile to a **service area** (so a home address doesn't have to show publicly); if the service area is in a different country, ideally have someone local to that country complete/verify the Business Profile; run 2 separate money pages if the service is genuinely delivered in 2 distinct places/markets.
- **Splitting an all-in-one service page** (Scott, professional organizing — garages/closets/kitchens/bathrooms/holiday decorating crammed onto one "residential organizing" page): give every individual service its own page; structure top-level overview pages (commercial / residential / moving) that each link down into individual-service sub-pages (closets, garages, bathrooms, kitchen, laundry room, etc.); links from an overview page to sub-pages can be H2, H3, or plain paragraph text — format doesn't matter as long as the link exists.
- **Hyper-niche product, assumed low search volume** (Whitney, vintage/Danish designer furniture rental across LA/SF/Vegas/Phoenix): don't assume nobody searches your specific term from guessing — run it through a real keyword tool (SEMrush/Ubersuggest/Ahrefs); build a location page per serviced city even with one HQ city; for a known future event/spike in a specific city (e.g. Super Bowl landing in San Jose), stand up a location/event-specific page **6 months ahead** of the event.
- **Competing as an affiliate in an oversaturated category** (Joyce, K-beauty affiliate targeting midlife women): build "[Brand X] vs [Brand Y]" / alternatives / dupe-style comparison pages to capture branded search traffic even without your own checkout — these can sit in the footer, not the main nav; pair with direct influencer outreach (roughly 5K-20K+ follower range — "most of them will accept stuff for free").
- **Fixing a fragmented site structure** (Aaron Michelin, Brazil — hidden/unlinked landing and product pages, blog on a separate subdomain from the money pages): consolidate everything under one main domain, make every page reachable from the homepage/nav (an unlinked page is effectively invisible). Aaron committed on-call to migrate that same day and email Matt when done.
- **Batch-write, paced-publish**: CJ (limo company) writes/schedules posts in batches weeks in advance rather than writing one post right before it's due — decouples the writing effort from the publish cadence.
- **Getting reviews** (Michelle Fuentes): just ask — trigger an automated email or text after a purchase/service completes; an incentive can help but isn't required.
- **Diagnosing "why does a technically-worse page outrank me"** (Eric Peltier): once your own on-page signals check out, look at backlink count, anchor-text profile, how other sites describe/reference the page, internal link count, and blog-content volume/topical-authority ("are they seen as the expert?").

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- Weekly task load is intentionally capped low (roughly 1-2 hrs) specifically to prevent burnout — "I don't want this to feel like a burden on you."
- Blog cadence: **1 post/week is the target, 2/week is the max** recommended to most attendees; HeyTony's own agency publishes **4-8 pieces of content every 28 days** for clients. Joyce publishing 5 posts in one week was told "that's too many... probably won't hurt, but most of us can't sustain that."
- Backlinks: HeyTony builds **4 backlinks per month** for its own clients — explicitly not 4-10/week ("that's too many, you don't need that many").
- Never use BNI (Business Network International) for backlinks/networking — "BNI is a no, no" (Matt says he's been pitched to join repeatedly and refuses).
- A third-party booking page is **not** your money page — money pages are your own on-site service/product pages, and they're allowed to link out to a booking tool or another platform entirely.
- Every page should target one keyword; never combine multiple distinct services onto a single page.
- New-city location pages realistically take **3-6 months minimum** to rank (can be faster, rarely faster than that) — don't expect quick movement just because the page is well-built.
- Broadening a Google Business Profile's service area to reach a new city risks diluting or losing the map-pack ranking you already hold in your original area — treat as a real tradeoff, not a free expansion.
- Exact-match keyword in a URL/domain is not rated as a strong ranking factor ("some people have theories that exact match keywords matter, but I don't think that it plays that big of an impact") — don't migrate a working, recognizable domain just to force a keyword in.
- Audit-tool discount code: valid only for people live on this call, must be redeemed same day ("I'm gonna make the discount code go away tomorrow"). Signing up after the call still works, just without the discount.
- Audit-tool freemium mechanics: 1 month free via the code, but a credit card must be entered at signup; cancel before renewal if you don't want to continue. Standard pricing: **$10/month or $99.99/year**.
- Insiders price: **$57/month**, cancel anytime.
- Rank Week call replays are only available until **December 17** — gone after that unless you're in Insiders (Insiders retains every past call, including the entire Rank Week series, permanently).
- Not every "broken link" the audit tool flags is actually broken — verify manually before treating it as fixed (crawler false positives happen on domains it can't reach, e.g. canva.com).
- Fix money pages first when a site has multiple problem areas, but don't stop weekly blog publishing to do it — run both in parallel, since blog posts are what supply the internal links money pages need.
- Missing H1 tags matter on real content/money pages; they don't matter on throwaway pages with no SEO purpose (Matt's own example: an internal ChatGPT-built mini-game page).

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- More publishing isn't automatically better: Joyce's 5-posts-in-a-week was explicitly flagged as excessive against the stated 1-2/week ceiling — the real failure mode being warned against is burnout-driven quitting, not a modest pace.
- Fix-money-pages-first vs. keep-blogging is sequencing, not either/or (Manny) — blog posts are the internal-link supply money pages depend on, so fully pausing content to fix pages first would starve the money pages of the links they need.
- Expanding a Google Business Profile's service area into a new city is a real bet, not a free expansion — it can dilute or cost the map-pack position already held in the home city.
- The audit tool's free-month offer is framed candidly as still a business, not pure charity — Matt notes it costs him server/developer money to give away, and it requires a credit card plus self-initiated cancellation.
- Automated crawlers produce false positives (a live, working canva.com link flagged "broken" because the crawler couldn't access it) — manual verification is required before trusting the report.
- On-page technical optimization has a ceiling: a page can score ~90/100 on the audit tool and still lose to a technically messy competitor page, because ranking also depends on off-page authority (backlinks, anchor text, third-party mentions, topical/expert signals) that on-page fixes don't touch.
- Exact-match keywords in a domain/URL are treated as a weak or unproven signal ("some people have theories... I don't think it plays that big of an impact") — not worth migrating a working, recognizable domain to chase.
- Speed of results is highly conditional: the session's headline win is Day-1 blog posts already ranking #1 within days (built on an existing site's authority), while brand-new location pages into an unranked city get an explicit 3-6-month floor — same underlying system, very different timelines depending on whether you're extending existing authority or starting from zero in a new market.
- Seasonal/geographically-limited businesses are still told to run SEO continuously year-round, even in off-months with nothing immediate to sell — the payoff is deferred to the next season, not immediate.
- CJ's own reported cadence (batch-writing daily-scheduled posts, released "weeks in advance") sits well above the 1-2/week ceiling given to newer attendees — Matt doesn't flag this as a contradiction on the call, suggesting the ceiling is aimed at sustainable onboarding pace rather than a hard permanent cap for experienced, consistent operators.
