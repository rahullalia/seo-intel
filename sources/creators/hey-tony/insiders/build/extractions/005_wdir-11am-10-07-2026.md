## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Google Maps top-3 map pack strategy (Victor's method, confirmed by Matt as the exact HeyTony approach):**
  - Post to GBP at least twice a week.
  - Geotag every picture posted; put the target keyword in that picture (file/metadata).
  - Include a CTA/click-to-call link and a link to the website on posts.
  - Chain posts together: grab the share link from your *previous* GBP post and paste it into the description of the *new* post.
  - Build service-area pages on the website (one per neighborhood/service category) and link each one to the matching GBP service category (e.g., "local moves in Washington DC" → dedicated service page → linked to GBP as its own service).
  - Ask for reviews continuously; Victor gets 3-4 new reviews/week.
  - Done consistently for ~1 year across two locations (home city + DC).

- **Building a local/city service page (Cigar's web-design-in-Vancouver example):**
  1. Search both keyword variants people actually use for the service ("web design" and "website design") plus the city/neighborhood modifier (North Van, Burnaby, Surrey, Vancouver downtown, etc.) to see which phrase locals search.
  2. H1 = the exact keyword phrase, e.g. "Website Design in Burnaby."
  3. H2 = a variant like "Premier Website Design in Burnaby."
  4. Body copy: describe the local business landscape (e.g., "Burnaby has over [X] small businesses and only [X]% have a website; those with a proper mobile-optimized site are getting more business and growing") then a CTA.
  5. Optionally name-check specific local businesses you've built sites for, or businesses whose design you admire, as a hook ("if you're looking for something similar, contact us").

- **Blog cadence rule:** 2 quality posts/week is the recommended baseline. Build a bank of posts (write more than you need) and schedule them out so illness/vacation doesn't break the cadence — don't ramp volume up then cut back down, since that pattern looks like gaming the algorithm.

- **Positioning a "borrowed" lead magnet (someone else's course content, e.g., a therapist's PDF on an e-commerce site):**
  1. Never repurpose someone else's PDF/content as your own — make your own version.
  2. Give it an explicit, justifiable dollar value ("$100 value — free") and be ready to defend the number (either you actually sell it at that price, or bonuses stack up to it) — don't invent an unrealistic figure.
  3. Collect email address in exchange for the download — that's the actual point.
  4. Follow up via email automation/tags once they've opted in; don't forget to actually email them.
  5. Promote the underlying course/offer across socials and other channels to drive signups into that funnel.

- **Setting up tech you don't know how to build:** ask Claude/ChatGPT directly — state what you're trying to set up and your current tech stack (GHL, Kit, ActiveCampaign, etc.) and have it walk you step by step. If a step is unclear, screenshot your current screen, upload it to Chat/Claude, and ask "where do I click, what do I do, treat me like an idiot."

- **Diagnosing keyword cannibalization in Search Console:** Search Console → Query → click a query → Pages tab to see every URL ranking for it and each one's average position. Minor overlap (many pages showing at position 15-70+) is normal noise, not real cannibalization. Only worry when a *second* page is seriously competing for the position of your actual target page for a real (non-branded) keyword — then consider consolidating or 301-redirecting the weaker page into the stronger one.

- **Refresh vs. new post:** Refreshing an already-indexed/ranking page can be faster to get re-evaluated by Google (it's already indexed, and if it already has substantial content that's less work) — plus an old URL may already carry backlinks and internal links pointing to it, which a brand-new URL won't have. Still fine to create a new page/URL when the old one clearly isn't the right home for the topic.

- **Standing out on an IDX/MLS-feed real estate site (Linda):** since listings themselves can't be differentiated, win via blog content and homepage personality — local restaurant/coffee-shop/bike-shop reviews with your own photos and opinions, blog posts about relocating to specific areas, neighborhood breakdown pages. On social: post yourself talking inside houses (not scripted "tours") to show personality, not just square footage.

- **Geotagging photos (how-to):** use a tool like "Geo Imager" (search "imgr") or search "how to geotag photos" / "geotag photos tool." Upload the image, input the geo coordinates (some tools give a clickable map to set location).

- **Turning a live Q&A/interview into content without double the work:** Answer the blog-generation interview questions via voice mode or chat (don't record it) to get the blog post out. If you *also* want a video/YouTube asset, make it a separate, more engaging piece (e.g., walking around the actual neighborhood on camera) rather than trying to force one Zoom Q&A recording to serve both jobs — a flat Q&A read-off doesn't give people a reason to watch on YouTube.

- **Connecting a social profile to Google Search Console (major new feature, walkthrough):**
  1. In GSC, click the property switcher (top left) → "Add property."
  2. Choose to connect a social media profile; sign in and grant permission.
  3. Feature is rolling out gradually — if you don't see it yet, check back roughly weekly (mentioned on Monday or Friday calls when others get access).
  4. Once connected, the report shows Total Clicks and Total Impressions for your social posts/videos (separate from your website's search performance), plus a "top performing content" breakdown by individual post/reel, and a queries report showing which searches your content shows up for.

- **Client self-serve portal on a subdomain (Emmanuel/AMR Labs):** subdomain choices like `app.amrlabs.com`, `receiving.amrlabs.com`, `order.amrlabs.com`, `status.amrlabs.com` all work; expect little direct ranking benefit since it's a walled login page, but some upside from people running branded searches ("[brand] login," "[brand] status") and from added time-on-site contributing to overall site authority signals.

- **Review-building for professions that can't ask clients directly (therapists/counselors):** ask supervisors/colleagues for character-testimony reviews instead. Pace at roughly 1 new review/week rather than dumping a bank of them at once — steady pace signals legitimacy and compounds; six months of 1/week outpaces a competitor who has volume but has gone stagnant.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Post to GBP **at least twice a week** (Victor's cadence; matches HeyTony's general map-pack strategy).
- Blog cadence: **2 quality posts/week** recommended baseline; never ramp up then cut back down — looks like gaming the system to Google.
- **Don't** take someone else's content/PDF/course material and present it as your own — always make your own version.
- Lead-magnet value claims must be **justifiable**: don't invent a number ("$5,000 value for $500") unless you actually sell at that price or can point to real bonuses that add up to it.
- **Don't disturb a page that's already ranking #1** even if it looks "unoptimized" (no backlinks, low content) — changing it risks losing the ranking; leave what's already working alone.
- Geotagging is a gray area on actual ranking impact but costs ~5 extra minutes per image — "worth investing a day in doing that," do it anyway.
- Custom/branded email address (vs. generic) on your GBP is something Matt checks as a legitimacy signal (asked Victor if his was a custom GoDaddy address — confirmed yes, ruled out as the issue).
- GBP "profile strength" can drop (Victor: 100% → 80%) even without you removing anything; Google nudges you toward "complete to 100%" which routes to buying Google's paid business bundle (~**$27/month**) — clicking "complete" doesn't actually get you back to 100% without paying.
- Review pace for therapists/counselors (who can't solicit client reviews): **~1 review/week** is enough — competitor context cited was 23 reviews (a competitor using the same colleague-testimony strategy) and ~100 reviews (biggest competitor) — steady weekly pace still wins over 6 months.
- Ranking #2 or #3 (not #1) can still generate more business than you can handle — don't over-index on being #1.
- No best day of week to post blog content (implied, not a stated rule against any day) and explicitly **no best day for social media — "any day, all day, every day," post whenever.**
- Fix broken/incomplete site pages **before** adding more content channels or tactics — "your website's never going to be done... fix the big stuff" first.
- Deadline (community-specific, time-bound): backlink article submissions (from the Monday call's backlink initiative) were due **by end of day the same day as this call** — after that Matt's team stops checking the list/posting new ones.
- Branded keyword traffic (your business name variants) is a **positive** signal to Google, not a weakness — don't worry about "most of my keywords are just my brand name," keep optimizing for them too.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Keyword cannibalization isn't inherently bad — dozens of pages ranking somewhere for the same query is normal noise; it only becomes a real problem when a second page is genuinely competing with your actual target page for a real (non-branded) term, in which case consolidate/redirect rather than leaving both to fight it out.
- Refreshing an old page vs. publishing new isn't a strict rule — refreshing is often faster/higher-leverage (existing index status, existing backlinks/internal links), but creating a fresh page dedicated to a topic is still valid when that's genuinely the better home for it.
- A missing/blank keyword-difficulty score doesn't mean a keyword is easy to win — it may just mean the ranking pages aren't optimized for that specific query even though the site does other SEO well elsewhere; don't assume a gap is free real estate.
- Geotagging: Matt is explicit it's "kind of a gray area" — he doesn't claim certainty it drives authority/rankings, only that the cost (minutes) is low enough that it's worth doing regardless of uncertain payoff.
- Video content strategy is not one-size-fits-all: YouTube requires a genuine reason to watch (engagement/curiosity/personality), which is a different bar than what's needed for a blog-post transcript source — trying to make one asset satisfy both can mean more effort for a less effective outcome on both fronts.
- Ranking position isn't the same as business results — a lower-ranked or "invisible-to-tools" business can be fully booked (Hamilton therapist case) while a heavily-reviewed #1 competitor can lose the actual sale over UX friction (Elliot's own experience calling a therapist who required a phone intake instead of online booking).
- Being an 8-figure business with almost no backlinks and no formal SEO for years (Emmanuel #1) shows there's no single required path to revenue — brand recognition and paid lead-gen (Thomas Net) can substitute for organic SEO for a long time, though the upside of finally doing SEO/website-ownership was still immediate (visible lead lift within the first week of real content work).
- Timing/deadline pressure (3-4 week site rebuild for Alex) is framed as low-stakes relative to the whole business lifetime — better to keep contributing where possible (submit backlink to homepage now since that URL persists, keep writing blog posts to bank for future launch, use Monday working sessions) than to freeze until the rebuild is finished.
