# 087: Work Session Replay (09.02.2026)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Blog promotion checklist, post-publish**: share on Reddit (find a relevant subreddit, including local/city-specific ones), post a summary + link on LinkedIn, post on X, post on Facebook, send to your email newsletter (only if it's genuinely helpful content, not a sales pitch), and if you have contacts in an adjacent (not competing) business, ask them to link to it. If you're doing this for a client, get the client to post the article on their own LinkedIn too, since that adds validity.
- Point of all that promotion: prove to Google the content is getting traffic/links from outside your own site, i.e. "we're not just trying to game the algorithm."
- **Keyword/topic discovery workflow (Answer the Public)**: go to Search Engines, then Questions. Once results load, scroll to page 3 or 4 of the list, since fewer people go that deep and it's easier to rank there. Pick a question you can genuinely answer from experience.
- If your product/niche term returns zero results (example given: "motivational therapy tattoos"), broaden it first ("motivational tattoos," then just "tattoos") to get a batch of real questions, then niche back down from there.
- Set region to "United States" when checking keyword volume in Answer the Public.
- Even when a question shows near-zero/no scored volume, it can still be worth writing if there's other proof of demand, e.g. Reddit or another site already ranking for that exact question.
- **Content-drafting workflow**: pick your question/topic, open ChatGPT or Claude, paste a prompt asking it to interview you (see exact prompt below), answer its questions one at a time (Matt uses voice mode to dictate answers), then have it draft the post from your answers.
- **Topic-ideation fallback when you're out of ideas**: tell ChatGPT what you've already written, your expertise, your life experience, and what you sell, then ask it for a set number of new topic ideas optimized for SEO (Jorg's method: asked for 5).
- **Featured-image workflow**: generate an image with the exact prompt below (using existing images as style reference), convert the output to WebP, upload as the post's featured image.
- **Facebook-group promotion tactic**: when someone in a relevant local/niche Facebook group asks a question you can answer, don't just reply inline or drop your homepage link; write (or reuse) a blog post that answers it and share that link as your answer instead.
- **Turning a recurring support question into a reusable asset**: write the how-to once as a blog post, then send that link to every new client/customer instead of repeating the same walkthrough call each time. Can pick up organic traffic later on top of the time savings.
- **Blog-to-paid-product pipeline**: publish the free/shorter version as a blog post, then build a longer, more detailed version and sell that as a standalone guide.
- **Niche ecommerce keyword strategy when no single head term has enough volume**: build a portfolio of many small/medium transactional keyword variants (per zodiac sign, per birth month, per Chinese-zodiac year, etc.) instead of chasing one big keyword.
- **Seasonal/anticipatory publishing**: write a post for a keyword tied to a future calendar trigger (example: "gifts for a May birthday") months before that period's actual demand spike, so Google has time to rank it before the seasonal search volume arrives.
- **Where transactional keywords should live**: put high-intent/transactional terms (e.g. "Scorpio candle") on collection pages (a Shopify collection page = a category/landing page) or specific product pages, not necessarily blog posts. You can't rank for a keyword without a page built around that exact keyword.
- **Blog angle for niche ecommerce**: write "best gifts for [segment]" posts that link to (a) a relevant product and (b) other complementary products for that segment, plus one hub/master post ("find your horoscope," a full breakdown of all signs/months) that links out to each individual segment post.
- **Internal linking pattern, per blog post**: 1 internal link to the "money page" (the page you actually want to rank/convert), then roughly 2 to 5 internal links to other blog posts on the same page.
- **Verifying real ranking keywords**: use Google Search Console rather than manually searching yourself; it shows the actual queries you're appearing for, and a lot of it is driven by local relevance.
- **Checking national/competitive rank position**: use Semrush or Ubersuggest and refresh the stats to see where you rank across the country as a whole (tool coverage is incomplete though, see Tradeoffs).
- **End-of-session accountability ritual**: after each work session, post the link to the blog post you wrote that session into the Insiders community, for accountability (the host posts his own link too).

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- If a blog post and a service page are already ranking for the same keyword and it's working, leave it alone. Don't "fix" a technically-cannibalizing setup that's actually delivering results.
- Hyperlink UX rule: internal links should open in the same window/tab, not a new one.
- Internal linking ratio: 1 link to the money page + roughly 2 to 3 links to other blog posts (restated moments later in the same answer as a slightly wider 2 to 5 range). Do not send 3 links to the money page from one post (this was the mistake in Aaron's proposed technique, which Matt corrected).
- Signal to keep in mind: pages on a site that receive the most internal links pointing at them are treated as more important by Google.
- Don't replace regular blogging with transactional-keyword-only pages; do transactional targeting additionally, never instead of blogging.
- Content cadence referenced: 2 blog posts/week per client works out to roughly 50 to 60 posts/year, which is why teams run out of topic ideas and need multiple discovery tools.
- Candle case-study cadence: pick one keyword per week, write one blog post about it.
- Newsletter rule: only send a post to your list if it's a genuinely helpful piece of content, not something that reads like a pitch ("hire us to do this").
- Backlink outreach is optional, not mandatory; Matt personally skips it and prefers a lighter-touch ask to an adjacent, non-competing contact instead.

## Tools, prompts & resources (exact prompt wording if quoted; tool names; settings)

- **Ubersuggest** (paid subscription): enter competitor URLs, then use the competitor-tracking graph to see relative organic-traffic position over time. It crawls the actual SERPs for a keyword to determine who ranks.
- **Google Search Console**: the recommended source of truth for actual ranking keywords/traffic changes, over manual self-searching.
- **Semrush**: alternative to Ubersuggest for checking country-wide keyword ranking position.
- **Answer the Public**: question-based keyword research tool; navigate Search Engines, then Questions; check volume with region set to United States.
- **Hey Tony Insiders in-platform tools**: a "Reddit keywords" tool and a "People Also Ask" tool, used alongside/instead of Answer the Public for topic and keyword discovery.
- **ChatGPT / Claude**: used for both topic ideation and the 10-question interview drafting method; voice mode used to dictate spoken answers to the AI's questions.
- **Reddit**: promotion channel (post in relevant/local subreddits) and also a keyword-validity signal (if Reddit already ranks for a query, real demand exists even if a tool shows no volume).
- **LinkedIn, X, Facebook**: post-publish distribution channels.
- **Pinterest + Tailwind** (scheduling tool): used via Jenna Kutcher's Pinterest program, a paid course teaching how to treat Pinterest as a search engine and apply keyword research to pins; mentioned as closing enrollment on the day of this call.
- **Shopify**: collection pages (= category/landing pages) versus product pages, relevant to structuring ecommerce keyword targeting.
- **Google Ads search-term report**: used to check impression volume for niche search terms.
- **WebP**: target format for the AI-generated featured image before upload.
- Exact content-interview prompt, typed live in the session (filler removed): "Blog post about [topic]. Can you ask me 10 questions about my experience, expertise, case studies, opinions, etc.? Keep it limited to 10 questions and I'm going to use voice mode to answer these. Ask me one question [at a time]." Matt calls this "a very basic prompt" meant to be adjusted as needed; one participant ("V") runs a longer version that also tells the AI to keep the site's goals/audience in mind and not to write the full post until told to afterward.
- Exact featured-image prompt, as quoted by Matt: "Make me a featured image using this post. Using the attached images as style examples. The new image should not include elements from the examples, but create new ones. Do not include the title on the image."
- Jorg's topic-generation approach (paraphrased): tell ChatGPT what you've already written, your expertise, your life experience, and what you sell, then ask it for a set number of SEO-optimized topic ideas.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Blog and service pages ranking for the same keyword is technically a cannibalization risk, but the practical rule given is: don't touch a setup that's already working.
- Backlink outreach is a real lever Matt chooses to skip personally, judging the time cost not worth the uncertain payoff; he substitutes a lighter, informal ask to an adjacent (non-competing) contact instead.
- ChatGPT/Claude are useful for topic ideation but rated less reliable than Answer the Public or the Reddit tool for confirming real search demand, since the AI "can sometimes make things up" about what people actually search.
- Rank-tracking tools (Semrush, Ubersuggest) give a useful competitive/national view but cannot surface 100% of the keywords a site actually ranks for; Search Console is treated as the more complete source of truth.
- Zero or near-zero scored search volume isn't automatically disqualifying; a topic can be worth writing if other evidence of real demand exists (e.g. a subreddit already ranking for or discussing it).
- Pinterest ROI is explicitly industry-dependent: a 6-month, ~10-posts/day test produced strong impressions but weak clicks for a marketing/SEO-adjacent account, while Matt notes it's reportedly much stronger for the hair industry, so the same playbook doesn't generalize across niches.
- The 1-link-to-money-page-plus-several-to-other-posts internal linking pattern is presented as Matt's practiced heuristic ("that's a good signal"), not asserted as a documented, confirmed Google ranking factor.
- Todd's strategy trades one large, saturated head-term keyword for a larger number of small, easy-to-rank, long-tail terms that only add up to viable volume in aggregate, and depends on writing seasonal content well ahead of the demand curve rather than reactively.
- Transactional-keyword-only content is being tested by Matt himself as a parallel, unproven strategy, explicitly framed as additive to normal blogging rather than a validated replacement for it.
- A Safari-specific rendering bug on the Insiders keyword-tool page (content cut off/blank below the top) was reported by one participant and confirmed by another; it works fine in Chrome and was left as an open, unresolved issue at call time.
