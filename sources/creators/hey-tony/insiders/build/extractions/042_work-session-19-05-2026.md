## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Content workflow (question → voice answer → Claude polish):**
  1. Source a real customer question via AnswerThePublic, findquestions.com, or the Insiders toolkit's "blog idea finder."
  2. Answer the questions using ChatGPT in **voice mode** — voice/transcription is ChatGPT's strength (Whisper), not Claude's. (Same idea as the "Talk SEO" tool shown the prior week that texts you one question a day and transcribes your spoken answer.)
  3. Copy the entire ChatGPT conversation (all Q&A) and paste it into **Claude** — Claude is preferred for the actual writing/polishing into a blog post.
- Target **5–8 questions per post**, not the full default set: one member front-loads a "head start" of experience in the first prompt, then trims to 7–8 questions; another uses 5 to keep the post from running long. Optionally instruct the model not to exceed **~1,000 words**.
- Every new post should: include images, include internal links, be linked to from **3 existing older posts**, and always include **one link to a money page**.
- **Diagnosing a broken site:** if a page won't load, try the `www.` version of the domain; take a screenshot (Mac: **Cmd+Shift+3**) and upload it into ChatGPT, asking it directly what's wrong.
- **Restructuring nav for a multi-service local business** (Regina, wedding/event musician): don't default to a generic "Services" label — pick a category name that matches how the business actually breaks down (landed on **"Occasions"** with a dropdown for Weddings, Corporate events, Bar mitzvahs, etc.), plus a second nav branch for product/format variants (**"Ensembles"**: Quartet, Trio, Duo, Solo). A separate low-value "media" page should instead be folded into relevant service pages (e.g., wedding videos live on the wedding page, not a standalone media page).
- **Footer for local SEO (small scale):** contact button, phone number, email address, and a plain list of the cities/areas served (Regina travels Santa Barbara to San Diego) — no links needed yet at this stage. Also add links to social profiles (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok) in the footer so Google can associate those profiles with the business entity.
- **Video embedding strategy:** don't send visitors away to YouTube — embed a handful of your best videos directly on the relevant service page (e.g., wedding-performance videos on the wedding page) so visitors can evaluate you without leaving the site. Note in the text that "hundreds/thousands more videos" exist and link out to the channel for the long tail. Always put a contact-us link in every YouTube video description.
- When embedding YouTube videos, use the embed settings to strip out the "watch next"/recommended-video clutter and control autoplay — ask ChatGPT how to configure this if unsure.
- **Handling one product sold under many different names in a niche** (CJ, real estate continuing-ed course): write a paragraph on the page listing all the alternate names the program goes by ("you may have seen this listed under different names..."); consider a **separate blog post** purely comparing/explaining that the differently-named programs are the same thing; optionally build separate money/product pages per name variant. Validate first with keyword research that people actually search each name variant. Start with whichever name is easiest to rank for, test it, then expand to the others.
- **Community-over-competition posture:** actively promote other SEO practitioners rather than treating them as rivals — there's more demand than any one person can service.
- **Bug reporting:** email screenshots of tool errors to **matt@heytony.ca**; same address for submitting member-made tutorial files/videos to be centralized.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Use ChatGPT (voice mode + Whisper) to capture and transcribe question answers; use Claude to write/polish the final post — don't reverse the two.
- 5–8 questions per post is the working range (members use 5, or 7–8); going under the tool's default of 10 questions is fine and common.
- Cap posts around ~1,000 words if you tell the model to.
- Every post needs: images + internal links + links from 3 older posts to the new one + one money-page link.
- Don't force yourself onto a channel that doesn't fit your business: Jackie's industry (hair/beauty) is Instagram-heavy, so she skipped it and won via local SEO instead; conversely, Matt notes SEO practitioners are now crowding onto Instagram/TikTok, which is why he avoids that channel for his own business.
- Don't build a page for every possible name-variant of a keyword blind — confirm with keyword research that a variant is actually searched before investing in a dedicated page for it.
- Don't dump your whole YouTube library onto a single page — cap the embeds, link out for "the rest."
- Vimeo embeds still get indexed and "technically count" for SEO — choosing Vimeo over YouTube purely for a cleaner viewer aesthetic is not an SEO penalty, but it forgoes YouTube's reach (Matt frames YouTube as the world's second-largest search engine, ~3 billion users).
- Infrequent YouTube posting does not hurt SEO — "you're not trying to become a YouTuber."
- If a full program/page name is too long for a nav bar, shorten the nav label but keep the full name(s) and an explanation of naming variants in the on-page content.
- Do keyword research before assuming a niche's various naming conventions are all worth separate pages.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **Channel choice is business-specific, not universal:** Jackie's industry (hair/beauty) lives on Instagram, so she deliberately skipped it and won via local SEO instead; Matt's own industry (SEO) is comparatively uncrowded on Instagram/TikTok, so he leans there instead — "if it works for your business, it works for your business," no single right channel for everyone.
- **Fragmented niche naming is a real constraint, not a fixable keyword problem:** when 7 competitors each independently branded the same offering differently, there's no single dominant term to "capture." The pragmatic response is content that maps all the names to one thing (or separate pages per variant), gated by keyword research so you don't build pages for names nobody actually searches, prioritized by whichever variant is easiest to rank first.
- **Video hosting is an aesthetics-vs-reach tradeoff:** Vimeo gives a cleaner, distraction-free embed and still indexes/counts for SEO, but forgoes exposure to YouTube's much larger built-in audience and the chance of profile follow-through (subscribe, browse more). Neither choice is an SEO penalty by itself — it's a UX-vs-discovery call.
- **Local footer/location strategy scales in steps:** plain-text city mentions in the footer are enough at small scale; dedicated per-city landing pages (e.g., a "Quartet San Diego" page) are a legitimate next step but explicitly framed as "a bigger project," not a default first move.
- **Organic vs. paid (Bertha's frame):** paid ads are the familiar, faster lever; organic SEO takes longer to compound but "stacking the layers" reduces reliance on ongoing ad spend over time — a time-to-result vs. long-term durability tradeoff.
- **Question count vs. answer depth:** trimming to 5 questions keeps posts from running long, but Damien's session shows that longer, more expansive voice answers can still be excellent raw material even if it means not finishing all 10 questions in one sitting.
