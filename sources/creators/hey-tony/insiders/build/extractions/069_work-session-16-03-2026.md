## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Blog Writer usage cap: **10 article generations per account** — set deliberately to prevent abuse/runaway API cost ("cost me like $50,000" if uncapped). A failed generation still counts against the 10 (confirmed live, flagged as a bug to fix — currently no way to resume/retry without losing the used slot).
- Password reset requests are rate-limited (Matt: "I think it's like 10 or something" per 24 hours) as an anti-abuse measure — repeated resets in one session can trip it, requiring a manual admin reset.
- Internal links vs. backlinks — do not conflate them: **internal links** = links between your own pages/posts; **backlinks** = links pointing to your site from someone else's site.
- **Do not link to competitors** — direct instruction when asked whether to follow ChatGPT's suggestion to link to a competitor's published post.
- Local business cross-linking: fine to link to genuinely relevant nearby businesses, but don't link all of them and don't do it a lot — avoid making it look like a coordinated link network; link only where it "makes sense" for a human reader.
- FAQs: stacking ~10 FAQs at the bottom of every blog post (after the CTA) does **not** itself help SEO/AI-visibility — the bar is "is this actually helpful to a human being? Probably not" for a bolted-on generic block. An FAQ-*style* piece of content overall can genuinely help.
- Heading tags: no rule requiring a fixed count of H3 tags — treat similarly to H2 usage (roughly match/consider competitor counts) but it's not a hard requirement.
- **Once you optimize/rank a page, stop touching it.** Continued edits reset Google's evaluation — "Google's like, well, I need to restart from zero again."
- **If a page is already ranking, don't touch its text** — even if an SEO tool's suggested copy would read better or more "on brand." Applies explicitly to Shopify product pages.
- Give new location pages **3+ months** for Google to sort out rankings before touching/tweaking keywords again.
- Keyword-to-page mapping: near-duplicate/variant keywords with no location difference can share one page; adding a location term makes it "a whole new keyword" and needs its own page — otherwise the two pages cannibalize each other in rankings.
- No Google Search Console resubmission needed after fixing on-page SEO issues from an audit — Google's own crawl picks up changes automatically.
- Cloudflare (and similar) commonly blocks AI/crawler bots by default — must be temporarily bypassed/allowed for the toolkit's site-audit/blog-writer crawler to work.
- Orphan/non-SEO utility pages (e.g., a photographer's QR-code event promo pages) don't need to be deleted just to chase a perfect audit score — "it's never going to be perfect and that's okay."

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Cost vs. access: capping the Blog Writer at 10 generations/account is an explicit tradeoff to prevent a handful of power users from blowing up API costs, at the expense of a hard ceiling for everyone (and a bug where failed runs still burn a slot).
- Perfection vs. usefulness: a site's audit/health score is treated as directional, not a target to max out — intentionally-kept orphan/utility pages (QR codes, promo links) are an acceptable permanent ding.
- Ranking pages are frozen assets: any post-ranking "improvement" — whether from an SEO tool's copy suggestions or a brand-voice mismatch — is framed as a net negative move, prioritizing Google's existing trust in the page over stylistic/brand polish.
- Ancaster vs. Hamilton: a direct volume-vs-competition tradeoff — lower search volume but winnable on the flagship site vs. higher volume but much harder to rank, solved by splitting the keyword targets across home site + dedicated location page rather than picking one.
- Multi-language publishing: framed as a cost most small/solo operators shouldn't pay — one language plus browser-level auto-translate captures most of the value versus manually publishing in 2–3 languages, though transactional/money pages still need the customer's actual language.
- FAQ blocks: potential AI-answer/SEO value from FAQ-style content is acknowledged as real in the right format, but a generic FAQ list bolted onto every post is called out as not worth it if it isn't genuinely useful to a human reader.
- New-tool rollout: running a synchronous, everyone-onboards-at-once live demo of a brand-new, unhardened tool created a real queuing/performance problem on the call itself — acknowledged in the moment that staggering people through it would have gone smoother than doing it all live and simultaneous.
