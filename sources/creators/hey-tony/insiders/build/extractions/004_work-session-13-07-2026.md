## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Weekly blog-writing workflow (the core exercise):**
  1. Find a real customer question via a question-finder tool: findquestions.com, the HT Insiders app's "Blog Idea Finder," AnswerThePublic, Ask.com, or Answer/Ask Socrates. Type in what you sell to surface questions.
  2. Paste the question into the first (interview) prompt — AI asks up to 10 clarifying questions one at a time, answered via voice mode.
  3. Run the second (writing) prompt to generate a clean, WordPress-ready post — H2/H3 only, no dividers/tables/callout boxes.
  4. Publish to your own website first — one post per week, minimum.
  5. Link it: 1 internal link to a money page + 3 internal links to other blog posts (4 total internal links per post).
- **Pre-loading context to shorten the interview (Jorg's variant):** feed the AI relevant background/expertise up front in the same message as the question, so it only needs to ask ~5 questions instead of the full 10 — works because you're pre-answering what it would otherwise ask.
- **Multi-location content strategy (Elliot, infidelity counseling, multiple Canadian cities):** one blog post per city, not multiple cities crammed into one post; cycle through cities post-by-post. Use content-relevant hooks to justify the location mention (e.g., "infidelity stats are higher in cities like Edmonton, Calgary, Toronto") and link those city mentions to the location/service pages, which count as money pages. For markets with existing physical offices (Toronto, Calgary) plus nationwide online service, deprioritize the flagship big city (already has more demand than capacity, plus incumbent local competitors and local-publication backlinks) and focus content on smaller, lower-competition markets instead.
- **Multi-location build-out (Mariah, therapy practice, 6 locations):** main domain represents the flagship/best-performing location; build a dedicated location page per other location (e.g., "Therapists in Vienna, Virginia"), listing services offered there (PTSD, CBT, etc.); consider sub-pages per service per location only if search volume justifies it — don't build everything at once. For adjacent services (medication management, psychological testing), just mention them on the existing location page for now rather than creating new pages. Do keyword research per exact city to find the term locals actually use (e.g., "therapist" vs. "counselor" varies by region).
- **Non-local/online-only business localization (Kathy, small-business services):** don't force geo-targeting unless you have a real office/physical presence or explicitly restrict service to that area. If not location-bound, go after broader, lower-competition ("low-hanging fruit") general keywords instead of local ones.
- **Blog geo-targeting (Neri's question, generalized rule):** do not insert your target city into general educational/how-to blog posts — those should cast a wide topical net; outside-area traffic that lands on the post and stays builds site authority, which in turn helps local rankings.
- **FAQ implementation (Yaniv's question):** every service page can (not must) carry its own FAQ block, formatted as plain question-then-answer text directly on the page — never behind an accordion/toggle/dropdown, since that adds friction for Google/AI to parse.
- **Sequencing content publication across channels (Yaniv):** get the content live on your own website first; only after it's published there should you post/link to it elsewhere (Google Business, LinkedIn, etc.) — don't post the same content on LinkedIn/Medium/GBP before it exists on your own site.
- **Real-estate objection-handling content angle (Tommy):** turn client-facing objections that stem from industry misinformation (e.g., prospects fixating on "sale-to-list ratio" and "days on market" as the main quality signals for choosing an agent) into a blog post like "what to look for when hiring a real estate agent in [market]" that explains why those metrics are easily manipulated (e.g., agents deliberately underpricing to engineer a fast/"1-day" sale that leaves money on the table) and what actually matters instead.
- **Repurposing one weekly post into multiple local pieces (Tommy's "four-a-week" system):** write the main blog post for your own site as usual, then create three additional short/"mini" posts targeting specific sub-areas, published directly to Google Business Profile (not the website).
- **Directory/membership backlink engineering (Natalie, wellness-professional directory):** prompt each new member, once their profile goes live, to add a link back to your platform from their own site (e.g., in the footer, framed as "read more about my [directory] profile"). To avoid a pure reciprocal link (weaker signal), only link out to the member's social channels from their directory profile — drop the outbound link to their own website even though you still want to reward/promote them.
- **Contest/award opportunistic PR play (Stephanie's local "best of" nomination):** if nominated or if you win a local contest/award, write a press release ("[Business] voted #1 [category] in [region]") and publish it plus push it out on social — used explicitly as a way to influence AI-answer citations, not just vanity PR.
- **Local-keyword reversal test protocol (David):** if you inserted a town/city name into blog posts that were previously ranking broadly and traffic drops, it's fine to revert all of them back to the broad version at once (not gradually) — but give any such change (forward or reverse) roughly 1–2 months before judging the result.
- **Single-channel risk mitigation (Alex's GBP suspension):** don't depend on one platform (e.g., Google Business Profile) for all traffic/reviews/leads; build redundancy via social media presence and an email list so a platform-level account suspension doesn't sink the business.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Every blog post: **1 internal link to a money page + 3 internal links to other blog posts = 4 total internal links.** Restated twice in the call as the standing rule.
- Publish **one blog post per week**, minimum — the stated cadence goal for the whole group's results.
- Interview prompt default is capped at **5 questions**; can be raised to **10 questions** for a better result.
- FAQs: **plain Q&A text on the page only — never accordions/toggles/dropdowns.**
- **One city/location per blog post** — don't combine multiple cities in a single piece.
- **Do not** publish the same content on LinkedIn/Medium/other platforms before it's live on your own website — whichever platform publishes first becomes the "source of truth" in Google's eyes, and later copies elsewhere won't rank.
- **Do not** add your business contact/info block to the bottom of blog posts on your own site (repeating the same boilerplate on every page is discouraged); a link back to a money page is fine instead. (Note: adding business info **is** fine on a guest-post/backlink piece published on someone else's site — different context.)
- **Do not** insert your target city/location name into general how-to blog posts meant to capture broad topical search traffic.
- Only build dedicated location pages for places you **actually** service or are physically based in — otherwise ranking there is very difficult.
- Location-page buildout priority for multi-location, multi-service businesses: **location pages first**; per-service sub-pages only later, gated on search volume — don't overbuild upfront.
- Blog length: no fixed word count; **1,000+ words is fine "as long as the content is good"** — trim after the fact if needed, don't force brevity upfront.
- If reversing a geo-keyword experiment across multiple posts, it's fine to **switch them all back at once** rather than staggering the change.
- Directory/backlink hygiene: avoid pure reciprocal linking — if a partner already links back to you, don't also link to their own website from your side (keep only their social links) to avoid a mutual "link to each other" pattern that dilutes signal.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Broad, non-localized content can drive far more total traffic (David: <1,000 → 40,000) and builds topical/domain authority that indirectly helps local rankings — but it also pulls in visitors who will never convert locally. Matt treats that "wasted" traffic as a feature, not a bug, because time-on-site and volume signal trustworthiness to Google, which in turn unlocks harder/more competitive keywords.
- Localizing that same content narrows to more qualified visitors but can shrink total traffic and the authority signal that comes with it (David's drop after adding his town name) — there's no fixed right answer; Matt's resolution is to test and wait 1–2 months before judging.
- Chasing the biggest city in a multi-location rollout means fighting entrenched local competitors with their own local-publication backlinks; smaller markets have less search volume but are easier to actually win — a volume-vs.-winnability tradeoff (Elliot's case).
- Rewarding directory members with a full backlink profile (including their own website) is good for their engagement/retention, but a fully reciprocal link relationship dilutes the SEO value of the incoming link — Natalie's fix (drop the outbound website link, keep socials) trades a bit of member goodwill for cleaner link equity.
- Contest/vote campaigns read as gimmicky on their face (Matt: used to "hate these contests... just websites trying to get traffic") but are reframed as legitimate, low-cost PR/backlink/AI-citation opportunities once a community can be mobilized to vote and a win can be turned into a press release.
- Heavy reliance on a single high-performing channel (Google Business Profile) is efficient but fragile — an unexplained suspension with an uncertain appeal outcome can erase 15 years of reviews and traction overnight, arguing for deliberate diversification even when the dominant channel is working well.
- Pre-loading context into the interview prompt (Jorg's approach) trades a bit more upfront writing effort for fewer follow-up questions and a faster path to the final post — a legitimate variant of the standard flow, not a deviation from it.
