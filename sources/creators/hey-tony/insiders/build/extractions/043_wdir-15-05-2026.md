## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Humanizing AI-drafted blogs (Lori's workflow)**: draft in ChatGPT first with fairly detailed responses (so it "doesn't sound super AI-ish"), then export/create the file where you asked ChatGPT "everything it's learned about you," feed that plus the blog drafts into Claude, and let Claude refine/rewrite them so they read even better.
- **Tracking SEO progress on a brand-new site**: set up Google Search Console first, then Google Analytics; use the Insiders' **Looker Studio "Setting up your reports"** video to turn that GSC/GA data into a dashboard once it starts populating.
- **Fixing two pages that won't get indexed**: check whether they target the same/very similar keyword or near-duplicate content (e.g., "Interior Design Services" vs. "Full Service Interior Design" vs. "Interior Design Studio") — if so, **consolidate them into one page**.
- **Links that open in a new tab**: change them to open in the same tab — framed purely as UX, not an SEO rule ("why are we opening up so many tabs... most of us have like 50 tabs open at all times").
- **CTA placement**: don't stop at one CTA at the top and one at the bottom — add 2-3 more spaced through the body content, so a reader who gets convinced mid-page can act immediately instead of having to scroll to find contact info.
- **Page layout for a services/approach page**: move "who we work with" and "our design approach" sections up near the top (with an accompanying image) instead of burying them at the bottom.
- **Blog listing/category pages**: never post the full blog content on the listing page — show only the thumbnail image and an excerpt, with a click-through to the full post. Posting full content on both the listing page and the post page gives Google two pages with identical content and it will deprioritize/not index one.
- **Using a page-audit/keyword tool on a money page**: ignore the overall composite "score." Only look at (a) how many times the target keyword is used on the page, (b) total word count vs. competitors, and (c) keyword density — then be patient for rankings to catch up.
- **Heading tag structure check**: use a Chrome extension to pull up your page's heading outline (H1/H2s) and compare it against the heading structure of the pages currently ranking above you. Rule of thumb: use **one more H2 than your top competitor has** — you don't need to reverse-engineer a "correct" number, just slightly exceed what's already ranking. Multiple H2s under one H1 is fine; "paragraph" is not a heading tag.
- **Replacing iframe'd legal pages**: copy the actual text out of the third-party (e.g., lawyer-generated) privacy policy/terms/cookie policy tool and paste it directly onto your own page as real text — don't leave it embedded as an iframe. Can use Claude/ChatGPT to help rewrite the copy, but verify legal compliance separately ("I'm not a lawyer").
- **SEO for gated/login-walled content** (Josh's Wikipedia-style venue database app): look up how to allow search-engine crawlers/bots to access and index gated content even though human users hit a login wall — cited other sites that do this ("click this button and it'll load the rest of the page"). Also make sure all user-submitted content stays unique.
- **FAQ answers linking to money pages**: it's fine (encouraged) for a short FAQ answer to link through to the fuller answer — including linking straight into a specific answer/section on a money page via an anchor/hashtag URL, not just to the top of the page.
- **Recovering from a competitor-driven ranking loss**: check what competitors changed (new backlinks, updated content) when they overtake you, and respond in kind; meanwhile keep publishing the low-competition/low-difficulty blog content (the "Monday" question-based posts) since almost nobody is competing for that volume — ranking on many secondary keywords builds trust signal for the site as a whole, on top of chasing the money-page keyword itself.
- **When a head term is too competitive to win** (e.g., "bookkeeping St. Petersburg" for a small local bookkeeper): stop trying to optimize the homepage for it. Redirect effort into blog posts and easier long-tail variants (e.g., "bookkeeping small business," "bookkeeping near me"), and make sure the Google Business Profile is fully filled out.
- **Location-neutral or relocating businesses**: don't put a location on the homepage. Build separate location-specific landing pages instead (Pavitra's case: one page for "Interior designer in Wellington, Florida," a separate one for "Interior designer in Dubai"), each carrying the location + service keyword in the heading tags.
- **Diagnosing "why isn't my SEO tool/AI crawler able to read my site"**: check the site's `.htaccess` file for rate-limiting/bot-blocking rules; ask the host (or Claude/ChatGPT) how to add the needed **user agents** to the allowlist in `.htaccess`.
- **Diagnosing duplicated meta descriptions across many blog posts (Yoast)**: check the Yoast meta description field on the individual posts/category template — the likely cause is a template auto-inserting the same site title/boilerplate into every post's description field. Fix inside Yoast; don't touch or change the URLs (they're already fine).

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Google publicly states meta description is not a ranking factor — Matt: "They 100% do [affect rankings], in my opinion." Regardless of ranking impact, a unique meta description is the visible snippet text under the blue title link that drives click-through, so duplicates across dozens/hundreds of pages are worth fixing.
- Backlinks: most will point to your homepage, but people are far more likely to link to a **blog post** than to a money page — blog posts still benefit the site as a whole even when the link doesn't land on the homepage.
- iframe-embedded legal pages (privacy policy, terms, cookie policy) showing as "low word count" isn't itself an SEO penalty, but Google wants to see the actual policy text live on your own site — it "legitimizes the business." Missing/hidden legal pages can hurt SEO.
- Running paid ads (Meta, TikTok, etc.): platforms want to see a real terms-of-use/privacy-policy page on your site, and its absence "can actually impact how well your ads do."
- Gated content: crawlers must still be able to reach it even though humans hit a login wall, or it gets zero search visibility.
- Insiders program pricing: monthly price for **new** members raised from **57 to 97** (per the call's own summary, €57 → €97/month); price for existing members is **not** increasing. Explicit rationale: slow the rate of new-member influx so Matt can keep giving current members enough 1:1 time and attention.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Google publicly says meta description isn't a ranking factor; Matt explicitly disagrees ("They 100% do, in my opinion") — flagged here as his stated opinion, not a proven mechanism.
- LLM visibility and LLM-driven conversion are not the same thing: Jason gets ChatGPT-referred customers with **zero Google organic traffic**, while Jennifer's dating-profile client shows up in ChatGPT screenshots but still isn't converting to business — "something still has to tip back over somehow."
- Whole content categories can lose organic search demand at once when users adopt LLMs to self-serve the underlying task (e.g., people asking ChatGPT to "write me a dating profile" instead of searching for a copywriter) — Matt's proposed fix is a service-offering pivot (sell a "Claude skill" that writes the profile, or coach "still not getting dates using Claude — here's why") rather than more traditional SEO.
- Competitive keyword rankings can be lost quickly once competitors notice and respond (CJ's case) — recoverable, but requires matching their moves (backlinks/content updates) and patience.
- For very competitive local head terms, the better call can be to **stop** optimizing the homepage for that term rather than keep fighting for it — redirect the effort into content and easier long-tail instead (Nuri's case).
- Rate-limiting/bot-blocking via `.htaccess` is double-edged: it can protect against bad traffic but also silently block legitimate SEO tools and (implicitly) AI crawlers from indexing the site at all (Lindsay's case).
- Gating content behind a login can coexist with ranking goals only if crawler access is deliberately preserved separately from the human-facing gate — otherwise gated content is invisible to search entirely (Josh's case, unresolved in detail — Matt was not fully sure of the exact method).
- Scaling the Insiders community is an explicit tradeoff: opening up to a large new cohort at Rank Week (potentially hundreds of new joiners in June) grows the business but dilutes the depth/personalization of the existing group's Monday calls (repeat basic questions crowd out advanced discussion). Options discussed but undecided: a separate first-month/onboarding cohort call for new joiners; a "graduation gate" tied to completing foundational material (not just tenure) before merging into the main group (Rob's suggestion, based on another group he'd been in); and/or a separate "legacy" call for long-tenured members (Heather's suggestion, partly as a retention/trust signal). Raising the new-member price to 97 is explicitly one lever meant to reduce signup volume and self-select more committed new members, not a way to extract more money from existing members ("that is not the objective here").
- Recurring business-owner trap named by Matt and echoed by Jennifer/Jason: defaulting to 100% billable client time and letting your own marketing/SEO lapse until leads dry up — "working in the business" vs. "working on the business."
