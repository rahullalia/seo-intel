## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- Fixing a low-contrast/hard-to-see CTA: switch it to a **solid color** rather than an outline or low-contrast fill — directly tied to conversion rate.
- Video heroes slow site speed, worst on mobile. Fix: **turn the video off on mobile, keep it on desktop** rather than removing it everywhere.
- Serve images in a **next-gen format like WebP** to speed up the site; on WordPress, use a plugin that bulk-converts all images to WebP.
- Manual WebP conversion: use a free image-converter website — upload a JPEG, hit convert, get WebP back. Free tier caps around **10 pictures/day**; when you hit the cap on one converter site, just move to another free one.
- Checking if an image was uploaded oversized: right-click → **"open image in new tab"** to see its actual rendered pixel dimensions, then compare that to how much screen space it visually occupies.
- Image-sizing rule of thumb: design to scale first so you always know the exported pixel size is correct, instead of uploading an oversized image and letting the site squish it down (still costs the same server/render weight).
- Use a screenshot/measuring tool to check an image's on-screen pixel width before deciding what size to export/resize it to.
- Fixing competing CTAs on one page: pick **one primary CTA** and make it the only solid/bold-colored button; demote every other CTA to a subtler style (outline, white/neutral fill) so there's one obvious next action.
- Making a CTA "pop": give it a **solid background** instead of a transparent one (e.g., a solid blue block behind a white-text button).
- Fixing unbalanced whitespace: standardize the spacing above and below a hero headline/title so it isn't crammed against the hero image on one side and overly spaced on the other — reads as more "professional."
- Sourcing a color palette: search **"[color] color palette" on Pinterest** (e.g., "blue color palette") to find pairings that already work with an existing brand color.
- Maximizing CTA contrast: use the **complementary color** (opposite side of the color wheel) from the brand's main color — e.g., a blue brand pairs with yellow/orange for the highest-contrast CTA.
- Softening harsh contrast: swap pure black accents for a **dark navy** against a light/pastel brand color to avoid a stark black-on-pastel clash.
- Using gray as a background: keep it **almost white**, not a dark/muddy gray (a slightly-blue-tinted near-white was demoed live as an example).
- Fixing a dated ("early 2000s") look: remove gradient/bubble button and nav effects, remove glow effects and text-shadows especially over a busy background image, **left-align body text** instead of center-aligning it (center-align only for very short blurbs), and bump font size up (at least ~16px was implied as the floor).
- Picking the hero's one CTA destination: identify the business's **highest-value ("money") page** first, then send the hero CTA there instead of somewhere generic.
- CTA copywriting: swap flat/descriptive labels for more intriguing verbs, e.g., "**Discover** [what's weakening your body]" instead of a plain statement, to prompt the next click.
- Keep background color and header/nav styling **consistent across every page** of the site — switching backgrounds page-to-page reads as if the visitor left the site (bounce risk).
- Placeholder dropdown nav item with no page yet built (WordPress): Menus → **Custom Link** → URL = `#` (hash), Label = the section name (e.g., "Services") — creates a working dropdown parent without linking anywhere yet; add real child pages under it later.
- Product pages/sections need actual **product photography**, not just decorative/mascot branding imagery — balance mood imagery with clear "here's what you're buying" shots (referenced a rubberized pool-decking site's before/after style: mood shot + literal product shot).
- Incorporate **playful brand voice** directly into headline copy when the brand personality supports it (example floated: "it'll blow your flip-flops off").
- Redesign-scope decision: if a page is **already ranking**, keep its existing verbiage/keywords/images and just reorganize the layout; if it's **not ranking strongly**, feel free to rewrite/redesign from scratch.
- Fixing "nobody reads my wall of text" (despite good SEO copy volume): break the copy into **headline-led sections** — visitors read the headline first, decide whether to read the paragraph beneath it; an unbroken text block gets skipped even by people who go on to buy.
- Long copy is fine for conversion as long as it's chunked under scannable subheadings — length itself isn't the problem.
- Reading **lazy-loading** as a good sign during a live PageSpeed/scroll check: images visibly popping in as you scroll down means the page is only loading what's near the viewport, which speeds up initial load.
- Iterating on an already-converting site: don't guess-and-change. First establish current lead/quote volume, then test **one variable at a time** (e.g., CTA color) and measure over roughly **a month** before deciding a change actually helped.
- Mobile hero structure: stack strictly **Headline → Copy → CTA → Photo**, top to bottom — never place a photo beside the text on mobile.
- General visual-aid principle: a photo/image per section makes content faster to read/understand, but it's not mandatory everywhere — FAQ sections can use icons instead of photos, testimonial sections can rely on stars/logos instead of photos, as long as the section isn't a dense, ungraphic wall of text.
- Pick section imagery/icons based on **matching the section's literal content/headline** (e.g., a house icon for "residential moves"), not just on what looks visually nice — the goal is helping people scan and read faster.
- For a services/class business with no homepage intro: add a hero section that states **who the business is** before the visitor hits the service/class list.
- Multi-product ecommerce homepage strategy: stop trying to rank the **homepage** for one keyword; instead give each featured product its own clearly organized homepage section (with descriptive text) and push actual SEO/ranking effort onto the **individual product and collection pages**.
- Keyword-reinforcement tactic for a product collection: repeat the exact target keyword phrase in **every product title** in that collection (e.g., every piece titled with "canvas art print" to reinforce ranking for "canvas art prints").
- Brainstorming keywords for a hard-to-classify/quirky product line: reason from a specific **customer persona and their literal search language** ("what would your quirky aunt search for?") rather than guessing generic terms; treat this as a starting point, not a substitute for real keyword research.
- Replace a hero background video that's just promotional/campaign footage (not showing the literal product) with a **static image + text overlay** — keep video only when it actually demonstrates the real product.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- One primary CTA per page/section — solid, bold color; every other CTA must visually recede (outline or muted/white).
- Gray backgrounds: near-white, never dark gray (dark gray backgrounds read as "dingy").
- Left-align body/paragraph text; centered text is only acceptable for short blurbs/titles.
- Bump undersized body text up — flagged around **16px** as a floor when diagnosing a dated-looking design.
- Design/export desktop images at roughly the **size they'll actually display** rather than oversized — for a full-width desktop hero context, the designer's own working canvas width was **1400px**; don't go bigger unless designing specifically for a TV/large screen.
- Example half-screen-width image measured and resized to about **540–600px wide** (not any bigger) since it only occupies half the page width.
- Free WebP-converter tools cap around **10 images per day** on the free tier.
- Keep background color and header/nav design **identical across every page** — no per-page background swaps.
- Redesign rule: **ranking already → preserve existing text/verbiage/images**, just reorganize visually. **Not ranking / ranking weakly → free to fully rewrite.**
- Multi-product ecommerce sites: don't force a single homepage keyword — put keyword-targeting effort on product/collection pages instead.
- Mobile hero layout: always **stack vertically** in the order headline → copy → CTA → photo; never side-by-side on mobile.
- Turn off hero background video on **mobile** if it's hurting speed; desktop can keep it.
- Don't keep a hero video that's just commercial/campaign footage and doesn't show the actual product — swap it for a static image.
- Test only **one variable at a time** on an already-converting site (e.g., CTA color), and give it about **a month** before reading the result.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Video heroes aren't banned outright: they help when they show the actual product (aids comprehension/trust) but always cost site speed, especially on mobile — the fix is selective (disable on mobile only, or drop entirely if it's just campaign/commercial footage) rather than a blanket rule against video.
- WebP conversion isn't automatically "the fix" for a slow site — Leanne's images were already correctly saved as WebP and still small, yet the site was still slow because of an unrelated cause (a 7-slide testimonial carousel). Diagnose via PageSpeed's actual flags before prescribing an image fix.
- Complementary (opposite-wheel) colors maximize CTA contrast/pop, but harmonious Pinterest-sourced palettes are the better call when the goal is overall cohesion rather than maximum stand-out — framed as a choice based on the section's goal, not a universal rule to always max out contrast.
- Not every section needs a literal photo — FAQ and testimonial sections can rely on icons, stars, or logos instead. The real requirement is "not a dense wall of text"; photos are one way to satisfy that, not the only way.
- Long copy doesn't hurt conversion on its own — it only fails when it isn't chunked under scannable headlines. Length is not the enemy; undifferentiated density is.
- Redesign scope is entirely conditioned on current ranking status: strong existing rankings mean preserve verbiage/keywords and just restructure visually; weak/no rankings mean full rewrite is safe. This came up twice in the same call (Garden Fairy Botanicals explicitly, plus stated as a general rule), so it's a repeatable decision framework, not a one-off judgment call.
- For multi-product ecommerce stores, chasing one homepage keyword is treated as a losing strategy relative to just ranking individual product/collection pages — an explicit "don't fight the medium" call rather than an attempt to optimize the homepage anyway.
- A site that's already converting well (the electrician example) shouldn't be redesigned on gut feel — the explicit process is: measure current quote/call volume first, then test one variable at a time over roughly a month, rather than making design changes proactively just because something "could" be improved.
