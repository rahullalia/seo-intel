## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Reverse-engineering AI citations:** when you prompt an AI tool (ChatGPT etc.) about a client and ask it to search the web, it shows you which sources it's pulling from ("bubbles" of what/why it searched). Track this over time per client/query to learn what sources a given AI trusts (e.g. Reddit) and to start figuring out why it cites different sites on different days.
- **Rank-Week method reapplied to AI citations:** take the sources the AI cites (treat them like your page-2/page-3 competitors), then compare their word count and approach against your own content to find gaps.
- **Reddit-as-AnswerThePublic:** Matt used Claude to scrape Reddit threads as an AnswerThePublic-style tool — pulling real user questions from Reddit to generate blog topics that answer what people are actually asking (referenced from a recent Matt social post, not demoed live on this call).
- **Genuine Reddit participation (vs. astroturfing):** comment authentically in niche-relevant threads as someone who's actually used the product/service (what Tara does) rather than posing as a customer while secretly being the business ("infiltrating" threads — a tactic Kira says small businesses commonly use but she personally doesn't do it with her clients).
- **Human-vs-optimized decision framework:** for every on-page choice (title tag, keyword placement, tone), consciously place it on a spectrum from "fully human/natural voice" to "fully optimized/robotic but keyword-perfect." No universal right answer — it's a per-business, per-brand call you make deliberately.
- **Natural keyword-phrasing exercise:** ask ChatGPT ~10 genuine questions relevant to your industry/product, read back the natural phrasing it returns, then work a couple of keywords into your copy from that language instead of forcing exact-match phrases.
- **Yoast triage order:** don't chase orange→green first. Go through every page/post that's still **red** (or blank/no score) and fix those first — older sites (built before these plugins existed) usually have the most pages missing basics entirely.
- **Yoast meta-description fix:** if the keyword is present in the meta description but still scores yellow, move it into the **first five words** of the meta description — this alone can flip the score to green with no other change.
- **Framework-first learning approach:** when new to a strategy/program, execute every single step exactly as taught first to build muscle memory/repetition. Only start customizing once you know *why* you'd deviate — "learn the language before you decide to break it."
- **Handling a misspelled/"wrong" high-volume keyword** (used with a lawyer client and a plastic-surgery client): when the top-volume search variant for a topic is technically misspelled or backwards, still write the blog post targeting that exact term/spelling, and use the post itself to explain why it's actually wrong — don't avoid the keyword just because it feels unauthoritative.
- **Custom GPT / Claude Project context diet:** if a knowledge-base doc is producing vague, generic answers, cut it down significantly (don't add more) and re-test. Overloaded context dilutes the assistant the way overwhelming a new hire with everything on day one does.
- **Event/service keyword research workflow** (done live for a paint-and-sip business): 1) pick a keyword tool (Semrush or KeySearch); 2) search close variants of event name + city ("Paint Night Toronto" vs. "Paint and Sip Toronto"); 3) compare volume and difficulty across variants; 4) pick the best volume/difficulty tradeoff; 5) check what your current ranking page is targeting and consider swapping it in.
- **Recurring vs. one-off event architecture:** if an event type happens regularly (e.g., weekly paint nights), build it a dedicated **service page** — keyword worked in repeatedly, embedded signup calendar, description of the experience — rather than only listing individual event instances.
- **One-off events:** keep these on Eventbrite, Meetup, and Facebook (Events/Pages) instead of building a page per occurrence.
- **Internal-linking structure for recurring offerings:** keep/build a broader umbrella page or blog post (e.g., "fun things to do in [city]") that links to the narrower dedicated service page (e.g., "Paint and Sip Toronto"), and link back the other way too, for internal link equity. Repeat the dedicated-service-page treatment for each other recurring offering (pottery, team building, etc.).
- **Testing a "spammy-feeling" news cadence:** if worried that multiple news-style posts/day looks spammy, publish anyway as long as each is non-duplicate and offers real value, then watch traffic for a couple of months — only cut frequency if traffic plateaus, not preemptively.
- **Gut-check qualitative test:** read the finished post back and ask "am I proud of this?" — use this alongside (not instead of) the Yoast score, especially early on when you're still building traffic.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Never try to A/B test two pages/posts for the same keyword — that's **keyword cannibalization** (ranking for one keyword on more than one page/post on your own site undercuts both). Test variants sequentially over time instead, and know the result is confounded if the pages also differ by keyword.
- Illustrative Yoast checklist behavior: it may flag e.g. "keyword used 4 times, recommended is 5" — the plugin gives you the exact count and target to hit.
- Don't blindly trust Yoast Premium's new AI-generated title/meta suggestions — a suggestion like "photography services that elevate your brand" can score green while dropping the geo + service keyword (e.g., "Hamilton wedding photographer") that actually drives relevance/rankings. Check that AI suggestions preserve location + service terms.
- Agency-recommended client cadence: **2–3 blog posts per week** (what Hey Tony typically sets for clients). If your real capacity is 1/week, that's fine — don't hold yourself to more than you can sustain.
- Duplicate-content coordination: clients doing frequent posts (e.g., weekly new-product-release posts) just need to give the agency a heads-up in advance so duplicate content doesn't get written — confirmed as fine as long as it's coordinated.
- Typical client news-post cadence is closer to **once a week**, not multiple times a day — flagged only as a frequency comparison point, not a rule, when the theatre-ticket business asked about posting 2–4x/day.
- SEO plugin color scores (Yoast) are, per a participant (Alex) reading in chat, "not data driven" — metrics often come from title length and similar heuristics rather than actual ranking data. Treat them as a directional gut-check; favor real ranking/traffic data when it conflicts with the score.
- Give yourself grace on scope: a solo business owner covering SEO + social + everything else is effectively running "multiple full-time jobs" — don't hold yourself to an SEO-team-level cadence solo.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Human-vs-optimized is explicitly a spectrum with no correct point — a brand/personality decision, not an SEO rule. You can knowingly sacrifice a perfect Yoast score for voice, or vice versa, and both are valid.
- Reddit strategy has two distinct modes with different comfort levels: fully genuine participation/recommendation (Tara's approach, results-backed) vs. posing as a customer while secretly representing the business ("infiltrating," acknowledged as common but explicitly not something Kira does with clients).
- A/B testing works for Instagram Reels and YouTube titles/thumbnails but structurally doesn't work for blog posts/pages targeting the same keyword — attempting it triggers keyword cannibalization, so any sequential comparison (yellow page vs. green page) is confounded by other variables like differing keywords.
- Yoast/SEO plugin scores are a useful gut-check but contested as not fully data-driven (Alex's view) — real ranking/traffic data should outweigh the tool's internal heuristics when they disagree.
- Wrong/misspelled high-volume keywords create genuine tension for reputation-sensitive niches (law, plastic surgery): chasing the traffic can look "unauthoritative" to the client even though it's the statistically correct keyword play — resolved by writing content that both targets and corrects the term.
- High-frequency news posting (2–4/day) was treated as an open experiment, not a settled rule — guidance was explicitly conditional ("I can't imagine it would come off [spammy]... but test it") with a traffic plateau as the signal to change course, not a firm cap.
- Framework-first, customize-later is bounded by personal/brand values — if a recommended approach conflicts with what a business is comfortable representing (e.g., a "wrong" keyword that feels unauthoritative), that's a legitimate point to deviate, not just a later-stage optimization.
