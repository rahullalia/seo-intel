# WDIR — 13.03.2026 (Group Coaching: Mental Health Practice, Pest Control, Math Tutoring, Human Design, Photography)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Fixing a bloated header (Efra, mental health practice):** strip anything above the fold that isn't the core value prop. Move "serving [4 locations]" down to a simple "Serving New York City/Manhattan" line if 3 of 4 offices are basically inactive. Move the "available 7 days a week" line + phone/email to one side instead of centered/stacked. Target: the whole header/contact block should occupy roughly **1/6 of the page**, not half of it (her before-state had the nav+headline literally splitting the first screen in half).
- **Nav consolidation:** collapse every individual condition/niche link (anxiety, dating, fertility, pregnancy/postpartum) into a single "Services" or "Treatments" nav item that leads to one hub page listing all service categories. Never build a giant per-condition dropdown — especially on mobile, where Matt cites **~60% of internet traffic** and calls dropdowns "terrible" UX there.
- **De-prioritize non-conversion nav items:** a "Career/hiring" link shouldn't sit top-level next to the primary CTA — move it under "About." Decide the one action you want visitors to take (here: "Book an appointment") and design the whole page around not distracting from it.
- **Pillar → cluster architecture, correctly applied:** the pillar is the overarching *service* (e.g., "Psychotherapy" / "Services"), not the topic itself. Clusters are the individual conditions/offerings (anxiety, dating, fertility, pregnancy). Click Services → see all clusters listed → click into one → land on that specific service page.
- **Service page vs. blog post — the dividing test:** if content ends with a "conclusion," or is purely informational/educational, or answers a *question* someone has — it's a blog post, not a service page, regardless of what page type it's built as. A service page should read as if the visitor is already past the education stage (they know they have the problem) and just needs to know you offer the solution + how to book.
- **Service page anatomy example (Matt's own site):** headline states the transformation ("Website design that elevates your brand") → CTA button ("Get a free quote" / here: "Book a free consultation") going straight to a form → short "how we do it" section. No re-education on what the service *is*.
- **Rename generic condition pages into service-page titles:** e.g. "Anxiety" → "Anxiety Therapy in Manhattan." Ties location + service into the H1 instead of leaving it generic/educational.
- **Location-page consolidation (Annie, pest control):** if a business has one generic "Residential Pest Control" page plus separate city pages (Naples, Fort Myers, Orlando) all targeting overlapping keywords, pick ONE primary location and merge the generic page into it directly (H1 becomes "Residential Pest Control Orlando"), instead of running two competing pages after the same intent.
- **Fixing slow Google Maps embeds:** don't embed Google's live Maps iframe (Google's own PageSpeed test flags its own embed code as a page-speed drag). Replace with a static screenshot of the map, exported as **WebP**, that links out to the real Google Maps/GBP listing on click — visitors weren't interacting with the live embed anyway, they just want to click through.
- **Diagnosing duplicate/thin location pages:** open two city pages side by side and scroll them in parallel — if the top-of-page copy, "Why choose us in [city]," testimonials section, and "[City] Pest Control Services" blocks are worded identically except for the swapped city name, that's a content-differentiation failure, not just a heading issue.
- **Heading-tag fix for repeated sections:** blocks like "Why choose us," "kind words our customers say," "[city] services" should be **H3**, not H2 — they support the H1, they aren't co-equal to it.
- **Keyword-opportunity hunting method (used live, repeated across every caller):** open SEMrush (or Ubersuggest), search a candidate long-tail phrase, check monthly volume + keyword difficulty. If KD reads at/near 0% and volume is non-trivial (even 20–30/mo), it's worth building a page for — "zero backlinks needed to rank #1" for terms at this level.
- **Reverse-engineering a competitor's structure (Massey, national pest-control co.):** when a competitor outranks you, open their site and map their information architecture directly — service hub page → individual service pages (Pest control, Mosquito control) → dedicated city micro-sites (e.g., "Adamsville") that each list the services offered *in that city* → and a dedicated single-keyword page (Spider Control) built specifically for that city+service combo. Copy the structure, not the content.
- **Reframing for non-local/online businesses (Sharon's math tutoring, Karina's coaching):** stop targeting geography. Target the *feeling* or *problem* the buyer has instead. Don't mention city names, "East Coast/West Coast," etc. — the page should speak to the pain point and the fix.
- **Keyword-to-page weaving instruction:** once you've picked the target long-tail phrase, use it in the page's copy **at least once** — not repeatedly, just present — and structure the rest of the page naturally around the underlying problem/solution.
- **Word-count calibration:** for a very long, low-competition long-tail keyword (6-word phrase), check what the pages currently ranking for it actually contain — Matt's estimate for one such term: maybe only ~400 words needed, since it's competing against almost nothing.
- **Heading-tag hierarchy method ("chapters of a book"):** H1 = book title (main topic/keyword). H2s = chapter titles — keep them **short**, clearly reinforcing the main topic; most (not all) should relate back to the H1's keyword. Flowery/long marketing-speak sentences should be demoted to plain paragraph text, not formatted as a heading at all.
- **Cannibalization fix (Ariel, photography):** if multiple pages target what is really the same search intent with only word-order/pluralization differences (e.g. "Chicago luxury newborn photographer" vs. "Chicago photographer newborn" vs. "Chicago newborn photography"), these are ONE topic — consolidate to a single page rather than splitting traffic across near-duplicates.
- **Making local pages genuinely unique (photography + florist examples):** don't just template-swap the city name. Reference actual local landmarks, parks, monuments, wedding venues, neighborhoods you've shot in, and (for newborn photography specifically) local hospitals where clients likely gave birth. For a florist client: mention nearby funeral homes, hospitals, high schools/event spaces the business delivers to — these are the "local signals" that make a page defensible.
- **GBP posting + geotagging (Kenneth's technique, for Map Pack ranking):** post to Google Business Profile with an image attached; geotag the image's file metadata to different corners/areas of the target market's map grid before uploading. Even though Google strips EXIF metadata on download, Kenneth reports the ranking signal still moves the needle — "wherever we put it, the rankings start going up there."
- **Fast indexing via internal linking (Kenneth):** submit an indexing request on a new page; if internal linking is done properly, pages can rank within ~2 hours — contradicts the common "don't check for a month" advice.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- One keyword = one topic = one page. Never let two pages compete for the same search intent (Google will index only one and ignore the other — wasted work).
- Never format a blog-post URL/title as "[Post Title] – [Target Keyword]" (Ariel's example: "What to Pick in Your Hospital Bag - Chicago Newborn Photographer"). Matt: "if it feels spammy, it probably is spammy." The target keyword belongs to its own dedicated page's URL/H1 — mention it elsewhere in body copy if relevant, don't force it into unrelated post titles.
- Don't chase Yoast SEO's green "optimized" light blindly — its internal rule can push you to use the keyword a specific number of times (Matt's example: 5) when the actual #1-ranking competitor might use it only twice. Over-hitting Yoast's checklist = over-optimization/keyword stuffing.
- Phone numbers in the header must be tap-to-call/clickable, especially on mobile.
- Any Google Maps embed should be replaced with a static WebP screenshot linking out — never leave the live iframe embed in, since it fails Google's own PageSpeed test.
- Don't require the exact literal keyword in every single H2 — most headings should reinforce the main topic, not all of them.
- Broken/leftover schema markup visible on-page should just be removed or fixed, not left in.
- Location pages with templated/copy-pasted content per city can still work, but only if the business also has strong backlinks, meaningfully different (even if subtly so) content, or long domain history — otherwise, thin duplicate city pages are "not good."
- Listing multiple (e.g. ~12) locations at the bottom of a page is a legitimate tactic — but execution quality varies a lot; most businesses do it poorly (literal city-name swap only, no local specificity).
- For online/remote-service businesses: don't build location-based pages/keywords at all — the ranking lever is problem/feeling-based keywords, not geography.
- Specific keyword thresholds surfaced live as "good, low-competition, worth targeting" during the call: "spider control Orlando" — 20 searches/mo (US), KD ~0%/"not available" (later re-checked and shown as 2%); "home based elementary math school tutoring services" — volume 30/mo, KD 0; "homeschool tutor" — KD 22% (called "great"); "Human Design for Business" — KD ~0.
- New pages don't need to "sit and wait a month" to rank — properly internally-linked pages can index and rank within hours of an indexing request (Kenneth's stated experience).

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- **Geotagging metadata:** Matt acknowledges the common counter-claim that Google strips image EXIF metadata on download (he tested this himself ~2 years prior and confirmed the stripping) — yet Kenneth's clients still show ranking movement correlated with geotagged GBP post images. Presented as an anecdotal, works-in-practice tactic, not a proven/explained mechanism.
- **Backlinks are deliberately deprioritized** in this coaching style — the group targets near-zero-KD long-tail keywords specifically to avoid needing backlinks at all, since backlinks are "a hassle" and expensive to buy. Implication (not stated outright but consistent with Matt's framing): this approach caps out once the easy long-tail terms are exhausted and higher-competition terms are pursued.
- **Duplicate-looking city pages aren't automatically disqualifying** — Matt concedes competitors ranking with what look like copy-pasted, city-swapped pages can still work, IF they have real backlinks, subtly-real content differentiation invisible to a casual read, or simply long domain tenure ("doing it long enough"). The safer, controllable path is still genuine differentiation (landmarks, unique services, local signals) rather than betting on those three variables.
- **Template/theme limitations are reframed as solvable, not blocking** ("anything's possible" — re: a claimed "can't move the logo" template restriction) — but Matt allows the fix "might not be nice," i.e., acceptable to require a hacky workaround rather than a clean setting.
- **Local vs. problem/feeling targeting is niche-dependent, not universal doctrine** — Matt runs local-city-page strategy hard for pest control and photography, but explicitly reverses that advice for two online/remote-service businesses (math tutoring, Human Design coaching) in the same call, showing the "go local" playbook is conditional on whether the business is actually local-bound.
- **Content depth scales with keyword competition, not a fixed word count** — the guidance to potentially need only ~400 words for an extremely low-competition 6-word phrase is explicitly contingent on checking what current top-ranking pages contain first, not a general minimum/maximum rule.
