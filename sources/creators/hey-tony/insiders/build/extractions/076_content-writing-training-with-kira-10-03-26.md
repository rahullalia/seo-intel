# 076 — Content Writing Training with Kira (10.03.26)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Homepage keyword selection**: pick ONE primary commercial keyword for the homepage (example: "beard oil" for Oil Can Grooming) rather than brand-only H1/H2 language. Reserve category/product "money keywords" for their own product pages so they don't compete with the homepage term.
- **Blending a keyword into existing brand copy** rather than replacing brand voice outright: take the on-brand phrase (e.g., "Tools for the Trade") and prefix it with the keyword phrase, separated by a colon — e.g., "Best Hair Pomades for Guys: Tools for the Trade."
- **Using a competitor's brand name as a keyword**: never target it as your primary keyword (you won't outrank an established brand for its own name). Instead, drop it into body copy as a comparison angle — e.g., "Compared to Suavecito pomade, our pomade is made with 100% organic [ingredient]..." — this can also surface you to people searching the competitor's name directly.
- **Word count benchmarking**: don't chase a fixed word count. Google your primary keyword, look at the top 2–3 ranking competitors, and check their word count as a reference point. Use judgment — if competitors hit 2,500–3,000 words but it's mostly fluff, don't copy that just to match length.
- **Enriching thin collection pages**: if a collection page is just two sentences at the top, add real content — benefits of the specific product vs. a generic alternative, category/sport history and context, styling/pairing suggestions, and a Frequently Asked Questions section at the bottom. Can sneak the keyword into the FAQ heading itself (e.g., "Frequently Asked Questions: Paddle Shirts").
- **Get product names into H2/H3s** on collection pages, not just body copy — secondary product terms (e.g., "pomade," "clay," "paste") sitting in header tags carry more on-page weight ("link juice") even when they aren't the primary keyword.
- **Fixing Shopify H1/H2 tag mixing**: this is a theme code issue and cannot be fixed from the standard page editor — you have to go into "Edit code" on the theme. Can be self-fixed with ChatGPT's help, or with help from another community member who does Shopify dev work.
- **Ship the backlog**: if you have a stack of written-but-unpublished posts (mentioned range: 12–30), start publishing now rather than waiting for the site/images to be perfect. SEO already has a ~3-month lag before results show, so nothing is lost by publishing early and imperfect.
- **Blog posts are living documents**: unlike most other platforms (explicitly contrasted with YouTube), you can go back anytime — add images, edit copy, even swap which keyword the post targets.
- **Quantity-then-quality sequencing**: while you're still working through a backlog of unpublished posts, prioritize getting them all live (quantity). Once you're caught up and posting on a steady rhythm, shift focus to quality per post.
- **Topic ideation filter**: write about what you're personally curious about / already researching, cross-checked against what your customers actually want to know — use both filters together.
- **Cadence example**: one post per week, done on a fixed day (one participant's is Monday — his "blog day").
- **Refresh cadence**: revisit old posts "every few months or so." At minimum, update the published date to look more current; ideally also revise the content itself.
- **Don't let missing images block publishing**: publish without them and add them in later (one participant added images ~6 weeks after posting).
- **Internal-linking to not-yet-published/scheduled posts** (no perfectly clean solution — two workarounds):
  1. Link to the future post's URL slug at time of writing/scheduling and accept it'll be a broken link for a few days until that post goes live.
  2. Keep a tracking spreadsheet logging each post's scheduled live date; revisit it on a set day each week, confirm which posts have gone live, and go back to insert the internal links then. (Real example: a client's assistant checks this spreadsheet and adds the links every Friday.)
- **Anchor text rule for internal links**: ideally use the exact primary keyword you're trying to rank the target page for as anchor text. If that doesn't fit naturally, use something close/similar — it does NOT need to be the literal blog post title. Practical trick: build a "Related" mention into the post and also link the blog's own title elsewhere in the post — that title already contains its keyword, so it counts as a second internal link for free.
- **External linking workflow**: write the post first, then go back afterward and place 1–2 external links to sources clearly unrelated to your own target keyword (e.g., link out to a shoe brand for a footwear recommendation inside a shirt-focused post) — never to a page/post competing for the same keyword you're targeting.
- **Researching an unfamiliar niche for content** (example: padel/paddle shirts): brainstorm with ChatGPT — what the sport/category is, its origin, where it's popular, product-specific benefits (moisture-wicking, washability, durability), and styling/pairing questions (what shoes/shorts go with it) — turn those into collection-page copy and FAQ content.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Internal links per post: **3–5, usually ~4**. (Older guidance a few years back was "do a bunch, like 10" — walked back because too many internal links can look spammy.)
- External links per post: **1–2**, and they must be credible sources.
- Never externally link to a page that is targeting the same keyword you're trying to rank for — that hands your competitor extra "link juice" toward the exact ranking you want.
- No fixed word-count rule — benchmark against the top 2–3 ranking competitors for your exact primary keyword; don't pad with fluff just to match a bigger number.
- SEO takes about **3 months** to show results — publishing something imperfect today costs nothing because "no one's gonna see it" yet anyway.
- A Shopify theme's H1/H2 mixing issue does not necessarily resolve itself even on a newer theme — one participant confirmed her newer "Craft" theme still had the same bug, so it needed the manual code fix regardless of theme age.
- Refresh old posts "every few months or so"; at minimum touch the publish date.
- Ahrefs basic plan referenced at roughly **$23/month**.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- SEO optimization vs. brand voice is a constant tension ("walking this line") — some site owners will fully sacrifice brand voice to chase rankings/affiliate income; others (Kira included) deliberately give up some SEO purity to keep the copy feeling human. Neither is presented as wrong — it's a per-business choice.
- Long-tail, highly specific phrasing (e.g., "best hair pomade for guys") is easier to rank for but can read unnaturally when repeated enough times on a page to register as the target phrase — the writer has to balance natural copy against the repetition needed to rank.
- Competitor word count is a reference point, not a target — matching a competitor's higher count with filler content is explicitly discouraged; only match it if the extra length would be genuinely useful content.
- Publishing imperfect content now vs. waiting for perfection: the 3-month SEO lag means early, imperfect publishing costs nothing strategically — the real blocker discussed is psychological (fear of being seen/judged), not technical or SEO-driven.
- Linking to not-yet-live posts has no fully clean solution: a temporary broken link is simpler but leaves a real 404 for a few days; a tracking spreadsheet is cleaner but adds a recurring manual step (potentially requiring delegation to an assistant).
- Anchor text should ideally be the exact target keyword, but close/similar phrasing is an acceptable fallback when the literal keyword doesn't fit the sentence naturally.
- Keyword-tool disagreement is real and expected: the same or near-identical phrase can show meaningfully different volume/difficulty numbers across tools (Ubersuggest vs. whatever tool Jorg was checking) — treat the numbers as directional rather than absolute, and don't assume "correcting" a spelling to the dictionary-correct form is automatically better than keeping whatever spelling is already ranking.
- A competitor's brand name is only useful as a secondary/comparison keyword woven into body copy — never worth pursuing as a primary target, since outranking an established brand for its own name is unrealistic.
