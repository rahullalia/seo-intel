# 077 — Rank Week 2026: Day 1 (live kickoff, ~March 2026)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Full live workflow demonstrated end-to-end (20-minute exercise), in order:**
  1. Go to Matt's **Blog Topic Finder** tool (built specifically for Rank Week). Type in **one thing only** — what you sell / what you fix / your industry (e.g., "wedding photographer," "plumber," "roof repair"). Typing more than one query dumps you into a shared processing queue with everyone else on the call and slows/breaks it for others.
  2. If it returns results, click one of the generated **blog post titles** — these are phrased as real customer questions (e.g., "How to find the right wedding photographer," "What's the difference between posed and candid wedding photos?").
  3. If the Blog Topic Finder is stuck in queue / not loading, fall back to **answerthepublic.com**: type in what you sell, click "questions," get a free list of real questions people search.
  4. Save the entire list you're given — it's a bank of future blog post topics, not just the one you write today.
  5. Take one question and open **ChatGPT** (or any LLM — Claude, Gemini, whatever). Use **Prompt 1** (below). Voice mode is Matt's preferred way to answer (faster than typing).
  6. Answer the 5 follow-up questions the LLM asks, one at a time, injecting real expertise/experience/case studies/opinions/stories.
  7. In the **same conversation**, run **Prompt 2** (below) to generate the full blog post.
  8. Paste the draft into a Google Doc / Word doc / Microsoft doc and **clean it up**: strip horizontal rule bars, emojis, em dashes ("the longer dash"), fix any spacing/formatting weirdness, and re-read for sense.
  9. Go to your own website (blog section, or services pages if no blog exists) and find **2–3 other pages** to link to from the new post. Anchor text should be 2–3 words that *describe* the destination page's topic (doesn't need to be an exact match, just needs to signal to Google what the other page is about).
  10. In the doc: highlight the anchor words → Insert Link → paste the URL → Apply.
  11. Rule for the link mix: **2–3 links to other blog posts, plus 1 link to a service/product page** (money page) — not the other way around.
  12. Repeat until you have your internal linking done, then the post is ready to publish.
- **Image placement rule**: add at least one featured image plus one in-body image; insert an image roughly **every 200–300 words** to break up long blocks of text ("people are lazy... people don't like just reading giant blocks of text").
- **Image SEO basics** (from Dara's Q&A): rename the actual image file to describe what it is before uploading (e.g., "west-african-wedding-ceremony-toronto" instead of the camera's default filename), then set the **alt text** to the same descriptive phrase.
- **Local-traffic-from-anywhere tactic**: even for a hyper-local business, it's fine (and free) to write broader content that attracts visitors from anywhere — those visits still send engagement signals to Google. Close the loop back to local conversion by embedding a local CTA + anchor inside the content itself (e.g., "looking for a wedding photographer in Hamilton? Give us a call" with "wedding photographer Hamilton" linked to the booking/contact page).
- **Local SEO for a business that can't control its own website** (franchisee case): redirect SEO effort toward earning placement on third-party **"best of" lists** (e.g., "best Italian restaurant in [city]") and pitching local media/radio stations who publish those lists — they effectively do the SEO/authority-building for you.
- **Reviews as a local ranking + AI-visibility lever**: ask every single customer for a review, consistently, without stopping. This is also what LLMs surface on — "if I type in best Italian restaurant near me, it automatically looks for the ones that have the most reviews, the highest average star rating."
- **Google Business Profile completion tactic**: fill out the profile as fully as possible — add the menu, complete every field — in addition to collecting reviews.
- **Client/agency onboarding tactic (for people doing SEO for others)**: run a 30–60 minute interview call with the client asking them to "tell me about this, tell me about this," capture the transcript, and feed that transcript into a larger reference document that an LLM can pull from when writing blog posts in the client's voice. Alternatively, build a standing "brand book / story guide" Google Doc during onboarding and pull specific answers from it per post.
- **B2B / high-end niche visibility tactic** (from Daniel's fashion/product photography case): don't write only for the end decision-maker (creative director) — write for the **junior staff who search on their behalf** (assistants, coordinators) with content like "how to find a product photographer" / "what to look for in a product photographer." Those juniors get promoted over time and remember you.
- **Content-type-by-search-intent split** (from Phil's snow removal Q): if someone would Google the topic looking for *information*, write it as a blog post ("how to do snow removal in a parking lot"). If they'd Google it to *hire someone*, write it as a service page. Same topic, different content depending on intent — don't blend the two.
- **Announcements/news content rule** (from Zach's chorus/performing-arts Q): don't force company news/announcements into the blog stream unless people are actively *searching* for that information — put quick announcements on social media instead. Only turn an announcement into a blog post if it can be reframed as genuinely useful/searchable content (his example: "brand new patio opens in [area]" rather than just "we built a patio").
- **Publishing cadence / backlog management** (from Henry, who had 20–30 posts pre-written): don't launch them all at once. Launch the site with **10**, then schedule the rest to drip out at **2 per week**. This (a) signals to Google that you're actively maintained/consistent, and (b) gives you a buffer of 2–3 months so a missed day/sick week/vacation doesn't break the cadence.
- **Agency-internal QA process** ("Why doesn't it rank?"): Matt's team runs a weekly session taking one client page and having the whole team critique it against a checklist — too long / keyword under- or over-used / no internal links pointing to it / page is buried in site structure / title tag doesn't reflect the right topic. This process now runs as the Friday session inside Hey Tony Insiders.
- **Reviews as social proof images vs. indexable content**: a review-screenshot image is fine as a trust signal but Google cannot read the text inside an image — if you want the review's actual words to carry SEO weight, embed or copy-paste the review text as real text rather than (or in addition to) a screenshot.
- **AI for review replies**: fine to use ChatGPT to draft responses to reviews for SEO purposes — just review/edit before publishing, don't auto-post.
- **Cross-posting for legitimacy signal**: share the blog post link on LinkedIn/Facebook/social — this doesn't directly change rankings but signals to Google that you're a "legitimate business" people engage with across platforms.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Type **only one** query into the Blog Topic Finder — extra entries queue up and disrupt other users' results.
- Post cadence ceiling: **1–2 blog posts per week, max** (Matt's own agency standard for clients: "one blog post a week or two blog posts a week. That's the max we're going to do"). Posting **daily (365/year) is "too much"** — risk of Google reading the site as AI-spam churn rather than genuine, useful content.
- Internal linking ratio per new post: **2–3 links to other blog posts + 1 link to a service/product (money) page.**
- Image cadence: roughly **1 image per 200–300 words**.
- AI-draft cleanup checklist: remove horizontal-rule bars, emojis, em dashes/double-dashes, fix spacing issues, sanity-check that it reads correctly.
- Reviews: **ask every customer, consistently — never buy reviews or do anything "scammy."**
- If an agency/freelancer is doing your SEO and has **not set you up with Google Search Console access**, Matt's flat statement: "they are not doing good SEO."
- No keyword research / keyword density targeting is needed for this exercise — the target questions are long-tail, roughly **5–10 searches/month**, and Google will not suddenly trust a brand-new "fully optimized" site with a flood of traffic; trust has to be earned over time.
- Don't over-plan/batch-publish a large pre-written backlog at once — launch with 10, drip the rest at 2/week (see Henry above).
- Content must pass a genuine-usefulness test before publishing (see Tradeoffs quote below) — don't create a page purely because you think it'll rank; it won't rank if it doesn't actually help a real person.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **Keyword stuffing debate**: a member (Ariel) observed competing photographers ranking despite obvious keyword stuffing/location-tag repetition. Matt's position: it might genuinely be working for them, but you can't isolate stuffing as the cause — those sites may be ranking *despite* stuffing, driven by other unseen factors (backlinks, press mentions). His practical rule is outcome-based ("if you're ranking, keep doing it") rather than dogmatic, but he doesn't teach stuffing as a default strategy at Rank Week scale.
- **Local vs. broad-reach content**: writing broader, non-hyper-local content doesn't cost anything extra and can still send positive engagement signals to Google — but Google does appear to favor a closer/local business's content over a farther-away competitor's when local intent is detected, so broad content should still be anchored back to a local conversion point.
- **Content-type-by-intent**: the same underlying topic (e.g., snow removal) needs fundamentally different framing depending on whether the searcher intent is informational (blog post) or transactional/hire-intent (service page) — conflating the two dilutes both.
- **Announcements vs. searchable content**: not everything worth telling customers is worth a blog post — if nobody is actively searching for it, it belongs on social media instead, even though it still might matter to your audience.
- **Reviews-as-image vs. reviews-as-text**: a screenshot builds visual trust with a human visitor but is invisible to Google's crawler; there's a real tradeoff between authentic visual proof and indexable SEO value, resolved by using both (screenshot for humans, embedded/copied text for Google).
- **Backlog publishing speed**: publishing everything you've already written at once seems efficient, but it forfeits the "consistency" signal to Google and burns your buffer against future gaps — better to hold most of it back and drip-feed.
- **Franchise/no-site-control situations**: sometimes the right SEO move is to stop trying to fix a site you don't control and redirect effort entirely to earned, third-party placements (best-of lists, local media) instead.
- **Volume-vs-spam ceiling**: more content generally helps (snowball effect building "trust, reputation, authority" over time), but there's an explicit upper bound — daily publishing tips into looking like low-quality AI-generated spam rather than genuine expertise, even if each individual post is well-written.
