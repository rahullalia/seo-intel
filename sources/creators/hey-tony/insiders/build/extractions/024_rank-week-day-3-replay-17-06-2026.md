# Rank Week Day 3 Replay - 17.06.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Competitor gap-analysis workflow** (core Day 3 exercise, builds on Day 1's audit spreadsheet):
  1. Open your Day 1 audit spreadsheet.
  2. Open a fresh ChatGPT (or Claude, or any LLM) conversation.
  3. Grab the exact prompt from **heytony.ca/prompts** (too long to hand-copy or retype) and paste it in first, then press Enter with no other input yet.
  4. Copy your **entire audit spreadsheet** (all competitor columns, e.g. through column/row E if you audited 3 competitors) and paste it into the same conversation as the next message.
  5. Optional bonus: try uploading the file directly, or ask the LLM to crawl the competitor URLs live (works only if the LLM can actually reach those sites) - adds depth but isn't required.
  6. If a competitor site is blocked from AI crawling, that's good news for you: if AI can't crawl it, AI can't recommend or cite it either.
  7. Read the output: it explains why you aren't ranking and gives page-specific, actionable optimization steps.
  8. **Challenge vague or self-serving AI claims.** If it says "your page is the strongest commercial page," push back: "then why aren't I ranking #1?"
  9. Cross-check every suggestion against the real SERP (see word-count rule below) rather than applying it mechanically.
  10. Reusable going forward on any page on your site, not just the one page done live today.
- **Intent-matching fix**: if the SERP for your keyword is dominated by a different page type than yours (e.g. collection/category pages ranking while you only have a product page), build/optimize the page type Google is already rewarding instead of forcing your existing page type to compete.
- **Word-count/depth calibration**: benchmark the word count of the **lowest-ranking** competitor in the top 3, not the highest, and beat it by a small margin (e.g. if #3 ranks with 500 words, aim for ~600, not 3,000 just because #1 has 3,000). Add only information you're genuinely missing versus competitors, not everything they list.
- **Don't fabricate location claims**: if an LLM suggests "list multiple locations" but you don't have multiple offices, mention specific places/neighborhoods you've served instead of inventing business locations.
- **CTA/FAQ layout is CRO, but has an indirect SEO effect**: test button copy and FAQ placement; if visitors land and don't click or convert, that non-engagement is a negative signal that can help a competing (converting) page outrank you.
- **No-competitor / new-niche keyword-finding process**: open a keyword tool (Semrush or heykeywords.com mentioned), search a broad seed keyword, review the suggested variations, and narrow progressively until you find a term close to your actual offer, but not so narrow that nobody searches it yet. Part of the work is training your audience to search your term.
- **Location-based long-tail targeting for commodity questions**: instead of competing for a giant head term ("how much does it cost to start a cleaning business") against Stripe/Wix/Slack, target it plus location ("...in Ontario," "...in Ohio") where real regulatory/insurance/tax differences exist. Keyword-research which specific states/provinces people actually add to the query, then answer with real screenshots, real numbers, and local expertise rather than covering every state.
- **Repurpose a blog post into a Google Business Profile post**: feed the blog post to ChatGPT, ask for a shortened version, add an image plus a "Learn more" button, link back to the original blog URL on your site. Confirmed as good practice.
- **GBP posting cadence** (Matt's agency, for clients): **2-3 Google Business Profile updates per week**. Posts should carry useful information, not just "call now"/"buy now" CTAs.
- **E-commerce platform decision rule**: if your current site is already ranking/outranking competitors, do not move the main site/domain. Bolt any new shop onto the **same domain/URL**, not a separate one, to protect existing SEO equity. If the current shop/site isn't ranking at all, you're free to rebuild wholesale on a stronger platform.
- **Multi-location Google Business Profile strategy**: you can run the same business name in two GBP locations; add a location page on the website for each area. Google now requires **video verification** (recording yourself entering the building) per location. Recommended sequencing: prove out one location first (rank speed, workload) before committing to a second.
- **Service-area business flexibility**: start a GBP as a service-area business (no public address), then add/upgrade to a physical address later; no need to restart from scratch.
- **Sitemap troubleshooting**: if blog posts aren't indexing, check Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool per URL; check whether the sitemap stopped updating (this can happen after renaming a blog section/slug in a site builder). Multiple sitemaps are fine, either one index sitemap listing all sub-sitemaps, or each sitemap submitted individually.
- **Don't dilute a money/sales page** with 101-level "basics" content just because an LLM prompt suggests padding it for depth. Write a separate blog post covering the basics/foundational info, and link to it from the sales page. Keep the sales page focused on converting.
- **Homepage/page consolidation check**: audit whether information an LLM says your page is "missing" already exists elsewhere on your site. If so, evaluate whether consolidating it onto the one landing page (so visitors don't have to click around) improves competitiveness against top-3 competitors' single-page experience.
- **Differentiation vs. large/corporate competitors**: don't try to out-scale a competitor many times your size; reposition around what your smaller scale enables (e.g. "intimate," "quiet," "peace and tranquility") and write benefit-driven copy around that instead of matching their quantity/feature claims.
- **Reviews across platforms**: doesn't matter whether reviews concentrate on Google or TripAdvisor (or any local directory). Ask each customer which platform they personally use and direct them there; a mix is fine.
- **Blogging cadence**: publish **1-2 blog posts per week**. Don't overthink the "right" topic before publishing: "it's better to get on and do it."
- **Blog visibility**: the blog must be linked from a visible nav tab, not hidden/unlinked on the site.
- **Non-ranking blog posts still have value**: an operational how-to post sent directly to clients/leads saves time even if it never ranks, write it anyway.
- **Marketplace/multi-vendor sites**: never let vendors publish their own posts under your domain, risk of them free-riding on your domain authority and of spam/backlink-selling abuse.
- **Writing standard**: target roughly an **8th-grade reading level** (12-13-year-old comprehension) for all SEO content (blogs, sales pages, landing pages). The easier it is to understand, the more people convert.
- **Off-page/expertise building beyond backlinks**: build an "expertise profile" across the web, guest posts (also a backlink), podcast features/appearances, being mentioned elsewhere. LLMs aggregate these signals into how they judge your authority.
- **Community engagement recognition**: Matt opens each session by naming an "engagement winner" (the member who helped others most meaningfully in chat, not just posted most) plus honorable mentions, as a retention/engagement mechanic for the community.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- The sites showing up on Google for your focus keyword **are** your competitors, full stop, even if your real-world competitor (e.g. the shop next door) doesn't show up in that SERP; they're a competitor in real life but not online.
- Never treat AI/LLM audit suggestions as gospel; validate against the actual top-3 SERP competitors, especially the lowest-ranked one, before implementing.
- Don't fabricate business locations to satisfy "add more locations" style suggestions.
- Don't copy a well-funded/VC-backed competitor's CRO tests outright; take inspiration, test your own version instead.
- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is not separate from SEO**: "everything we're doing is SEO, which AEO is part of."
- **Never use AI-detection tools** on your own content, and don't chase "humanizing" your writing. Write from real human experience/expertise first; let AI only format/structure it coherently.
- A landing-page-only website is not enough to rank; you need more than one page.
- After publishing a blog post, you don't need to manually request indexing in GSC if internal linking is set up properly (you can request it manually if unsure).
- Multiple sitemaps are fine, one index sitemap listing sub-sitemaps, or each submitted individually, both acceptable.
- Don't move/rebuild a main website that's already ranking/outranking competitors just to add a shop; keep new shop functionality on the same domain.
- Don't let marketplace vendors post their own blog content on your domain.
- Reading-level target: ~8th grade / 12-13 years old, for all content.
- GBP: **2-3 updates/week** (agency's own client cadence); posts should give useful info, not just "buy now"/"call now" CTAs.
- Multi-location GBP now requires **video verification** (record yourself walking into the building).

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- **Word count/depth**: more isn't automatically better. Benchmark the lowest-ranking top-3 competitor, not the highest, to avoid wasted over-optimization.
- **CTA/FAQ placement** sits in CRO territory, not straight SEO, but matters to SEO indirectly through engagement/conversion signals; worth testing, not worth copying a competitor's exact wording.
- **Cornerstone/pillar content vs. many smaller posts**: Matt personally leans toward smaller, direct-answer posts over giant cornerstone pieces (readers want the answer, not a 5,000-word scroll), but frames this as a question back to the room ("are you reading all of that?") rather than a hard rule.
- **Multi-location GBP expansion**: amplifies presence/authority in theory (map-pack eligibility in each area) but dilutes/splits review volume across profiles and doubles the maintenance workload; sequencing (prove one location first) is the safer path.
- **Migrating e-commerce platforms**: freedom to rebuild wholesale only applies when the current asset isn't ranking; if the primary site/domain already ranks well, protect that equity by keeping new builds on the same domain rather than risking a fresh URL.
- **Old/dated-looking websites**: some longtime/older customers explicitly say they like the simplicity and readability, but visitors calibrated to slick modern sites may trust an outdated design less; no universal right answer, weigh audience against category norms.
- **Off-page SEO importance**: still real, but "not in the same way that it used to be"; pure backlink volume is being supplemented by broader expertise/authority signals LLMs pull from guest posts, podcasts, and mentions.
- **Local multi-location authenticity**: expanding a GBP footprint to a market you don't physically live in (Tom's NYC case) is workable but needs genuine local proof (case studies referencing local clients, physical verification); a thin/PO-box-style presence is exactly what Google has cracked down on.
- **Insiders positioning**: pitched as valuable for total beginners (fastest questions, most progress, per Matt) and for advanced marketers (via the technical Friday Q&A), but the group's average skill level is not advanced; advanced users should calibrate toward the 1:1 Q&A value rather than peer-group depth.
