## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Rank Week = 4 concrete deliverables**: write a blog post, fix a money page, do internal linking, make an SEO plan. That's the entire scope.
- **Debugging AI-built code/integrations**: take a screenshot of the error (code or console), drop it into Claude Code or Codex with "I'm getting this error, I don't know what to do, look into this and tell me the solution, then go fix it." Repeat as new errors surface.
- **Setting up a CLAUDE.md file**: tell Claude Code "I need you to make me a file that has this stuff in it — all these rules" for the project. Claude will offer to create a CLAUDE.md file. Have it include: setup steps for any new project, security measures to apply by default, and instruction to look at other existing projects for patterns before building something similar. Result: a new lead-magnet site went from ~3 hours to ~1 hour to build because there's less troubleshooting.
- **Extracting relevant info from a long transcript**: downloaded a paid program's kickoff-call replay, pulled the transcript, uploaded to Claude with a specific prompt (paraphrased): "Knowing that I already post on social media and generally have a sense of what will/won't do well, tell me all the information in this that would be helpful and relevant to me, and give me a breakdown of how it's specifically relevant to me." Claude returned both a full breakdown and a personalized relevance breakdown.
- **Segmenting a spreadsheet with Claude**: upload a spreadsheet (e.g., Rank Week attendee list), prompt: "Tell me who — how many people bought upgrades, how many people did this — then make me two separate spreadsheets with those lists, make them CSV files." Claude produces actual downloadable CSV files.
- **Reining in an AI that digresses**: give pointed, narrow prompts — state exactly the problem and the exact output format wanted (e.g., "give it to me in 10 points," not "give me options"). If it drifts, explicitly say "no, we're working on this, tell me this specific thing, stay on track."
- **Checking who backlinks to you in GSC**: Search Console → Links (left sidebar) → three sections show: External links / Internal links / Top linking sites. Click "more" under Top linking sites to see the full list and which pages they link to.
- **Checking/using llms.txt**: visit yourdomain.com/llms.txt to see if it exists. If the site runs Yoast SEO, it's generated automatically — no manual setup or hosting needed.
- **Finding what social content works (the method)**: post consistently for a long stretch to gather data, then go back and "overanalyze" the videos that performed best — identify the specific format/hook/setup that worked, then deliberately replicate that pattern in future posts.
- **Cross-posting workflow**: post the identical piece of content to every platform rather than tailoring per-platform. Use Instagram's built-in cross-post toggle to auto-publish simultaneously to Facebook and Threads.
- **Turning one blog post into many social posts**: treat the blog post as "macro" content covering many points; break each individual point out into its own "micro" post/video (one tip per video, not the full explanation). Test which specific points resonate, then produce more content around those specific points.
- **Photographer-specific content ideas given (for Ariel)**:
  - Set a phone on a tripod, record a time-lapse of an entire shoot, caption "pictures incoming in 3, 2, 1," then cut to the finished photos appearing.
  - Collect client testimonials and pair them with the actual delivered photos; post both to social and to Google Business Profile.
  - Use Pinterest as a channel — people go there specifically looking at photos.
  - Film a "shock" one-shot: set up a surprise (e.g., bucket of water) on a child, capture the genuine shocked reaction with a fast shutter/time-lapse, post the raw reaction clip followed by the finished styled photos and a final "happy kid" shot to offset any "that's cruel" reaction.
  - Film a mundane interaction (e.g., a drive-through) while being "overly polite," caption it "this is what a clean comedian looks like going through a drive-through" (adapted example, same technique applies to any niche persona).
- **Diagnosing a ranking drop after a lapse**: don't panic-edit the page. Instead, keep publishing new blog posts weekly and keep doing internal linking consistently. Only after ~3-4 months of consistency, go back and update the title tag/meta description to give people more reason to click.
- **Pricing a Rank Week-style engagement for a client**: scope it as the 4 deliverables above, estimate realistic hours (see Rules section for numbers), and price against that — not as a flat premium package.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- Rank Week execution time: **~4 hours solo**, or **~2 hours with a team**, to do all 4 deliverables (blog post, money-page fix, internal linking, SEO plan) — assuming client already has some assets in place.
- Rank Week pricing guidance: realistic range **$1,000–$5,000** depending on what the client already has; **$5,000 flagged as "very, very, very expensive"** for that scope. Price by hourly rate × realistic hours, not a flat number.
- Matt's own social posting cadence: **1 post/day for the first 3 months**, then **3 posts/day for the next 3 months** — needed to generate enough data to see what works.
- Ideal short-form video length (his own derived heuristic, not guaranteed): **as close to exactly 31 seconds as possible**, and must **hold attention through at least the first 15 seconds** — that combination has historically correlated with going viral for him, though it doesn't work every time.
- Volume framing for social: think ~20 posts a week (not 20/day); it's a volume/practice game — "sometimes they knock 1 out of 20 out of the park."
- **Don't tailor content per platform** — post the same piece everywhere. Rationale: reaching even 200 (minimum) views on each of 10 platforms = 2,000 people/day for free; making platform-specific versions isn't worth the time.
- **Competitor backlinks are good — never disavow/remove them.** A competitor linking to your page is treated as a trust signal, not a threat.
- **LLMs.txt is not mandatory** and no confirmed ranking impact yet — but expect Google/AI agents to weight it more as agentic crawling grows. Low-effort, worth having.
- **A ranking drop after a publishing gap is not necessarily "Google punishing you for not posting."** It's more about whether users click through and stay (pogo-sticking back to search results signals the content/position isn't right), so Google re-sorts. Still — don't create gaps; be consistent.
- **Consistency beats perfection**: ship an imperfect page/post now rather than delaying for a "masterpiece" — "I'd rather you put out [it] than just wait around and strategize."
- **Don't try to do everything** (SEO + social + door-knocking + associations, etc. all at once) — pick what fits your actual time/energy capacity; some tactics work for some people and not others.
- **Minimum viable social cadence: once a month is legitimate** for people who find camera work draining/unnatural — "some is better than nothing." Contrast: people for whom camera work is second nature should lean into that channel more.
- Claude token/cost note: a team on the **Claude Max plan** can **max out in a single day** if using it heavily for writing/content work.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- **Claude vs. ChatGPT is not a flat winner**: Claude is more concise, handles large files/spreadsheets better, and produces real output files — but burns tokens/costs more (a Max-plan team can max out in a single day of heavy writing use). ChatGPT is friendlier and offers more follow-up suggestions, which some people (Heather) actually prefer because it helps her rule things out. Try both against your own budget and workflow.
- **LLMs.txt**: no proven ranking benefit today and not mandatory, but framed as a cheap hedge against a future where AI agents crawl and use sites more directly.
- **Rank Week as a resellable service**: Matt does not sell Rank Week alone as a client service because his agency's real work includes more technical SEO beyond the 4 Rank Week tasks; anyone charging for it should scope pricing to the real (short) time investment, not treat it as an automatically premium package.
- **Ranking drops aren't simple cause-and-effect with publishing gaps**: the mechanism is more about user click behavior reshuffling SERP position than Google directly penalizing a pause — but the practical advice is still to stay consistent to avoid triggering that reshuffle in the first place.
- **Social media's value is real but conditional on personal bandwidth/energy** — explicitly framed as a long game like SEO, "do what you can manage," not a mandatory daily grind. Once-a-month posting is legitimate for people who find camera work unnatural; daily/multiple-times-daily makes sense for people who find it "second nature."
- **Cross-posting vs. platform-tailored content**: Matt firmly favors posting identical content everywhere over customizing per platform, accepting that performance will vary by platform — the aggregate free reach outweighs the lost per-platform optimization.
- **Growth ambition tradeoff**: chasing scale (per Hormozi's $100M Offers framing) vs. lifestyle-first framing (per "Big Enough") — more revenue/team doesn't automatically mean more freedom or even more net income; deliberately choose a growth pace that matches the lifestyle you actually want.
- **Audience purity vs. reach**: Marisa's dilemma (serve fans vs. bookers vs. planners with different content) — Matt's stance is to keep making authentic content for the real engaged audience and trust that other audiences (bookers, employers, etc.) will discover and self-select in anyway, rather than diluting content trying to speak to everyone.
- **Video length has no fixed universal answer**: the ~31-second/15-second-hook target is Matt's own derived heuristic from trial and error, explicitly caveated as inconsistent ("I can't keep the interest every single time") — the real instruction underneath is to trial-and-error your own numbers.
