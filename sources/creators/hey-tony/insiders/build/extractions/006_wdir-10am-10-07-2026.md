## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Finding a guest-post/backlink topic on a host site with nothing that matches your niche** (Ariel's case — newborn photographer, host site has no photography category):
  - Don't trust the site's own on-site search box — it can hide relevant content (Ariel's search showed "safe search blur" and returned nothing, even though matching content existed and was indexed).
  - Instead Google-search the domain directly with `site:[domain].com [rough topic]` — Matt used `site:plaidzebra.com` + "photographer" live on the call.
  - The topic doesn't need to be closely aligned to your niche — "it doesn't matter if it's 100% aligned with what you're talking about." A loosely-related "photographer" feature was judged usable for a newborn photographer.
  - Click into candidate results and sanity-check that the topic is generic/relatable enough before committing.
- **Structuring the guest post / backlink content:**
  - First person is fine if that's your natural voice.
  - CTA at the end should name you and state ONE specific, narrow offering plus one location (e.g., "Chicago newborn photographer," not a vague "Chicago photographer, I do a bunch of genres").
  - Only one link back to your own site; place it in the **top half** of the article.
  - Anchor text should be specific and descriptive (e.g., "clean comedian, Boca Raton"), not a vague brand/book name — unless you deliberately want traffic routed to a specific product.
  - Bonus move that signals quality to the host site: also link to one of the host's own existing internal articles, not just your own site — "these guys did proper SEO, they're referencing our own content" is the impression this creates.
- **Location-page architecture for a saturated metro market** (Prito — Toronto/GTA wedding photographer):
  - One keyword per page, always — never combine two keyword targets on one page/title (she had "Toronto wedding photographer / GTA wedding photography" stacked together).
  - Flagship/home page: pick the single biggest city term only (told to just use "Toronto wedding photographer," drop "GTA").
  - Build separate location pages for individual towns/suburbs (Hamilton, Stouffville, Mississauga, Oakville, King City, etc.) instead of one generic regional "GTA" page — go after small towns specifically because almost nobody else is targeting them (Stouffville called out by name as a near-zero-competition example).
  - Reasoning: searchers type their specific city/town, not a regional abbreviation.
- **Reading Google Search Console "average position" when it doesn't match manual search results:**
  - GSC → Pages tab → click the specific page → it auto-filters "Queries" to just that page's ranking keywords.
  - The page-level average position is an average across every query variant that page ranks for — a keyword you type by hand can sit on page 30 while the page's overall average looks much better because other variants rank #1–2.
- **Content strategy for the coming "personalized search" era:**
  - Premise (from a Gary Vaynerchuk / Edward Sturm podcast conversation Matt watched): Gemini/Google is predicted to win the AI race because Google already has calendar, Gmail, and search-history data — search is trending toward heavy personalization.
  - Tactic: write blog posts answering very specific, narrow customer questions with real expertise, not generic service descriptions. Example given: not "we fix tires, we change tires" but "how to check tread depth on Michelin X-Ice tires using a coin/dime test." Generic content is predicted to lose out as personalization increases.
- **Generating reel content ideas / building on-camera comfort:**
  - Ask your own audience what questions they want answered, then film reels answering those (Ariel's approach, praised by Matt).
  - If nobody responds, message friends/family in a group chat to like/comment and seed engagement — but not on every video; constant outside-traffic funneling stops the platform's algorithm from distributing organically ("prime the pump," don't lean on it every time).
  - Expect many takes as normal (Ariel did ~20 for one reel) — record freely, cut the bad/dead parts in editing rather than trying to nail it in one take.
  - Start at a sustainable cadence (once/week is a fine starting goal); reps build comfort and editing/ideation skill over time, then increase frequency.
- **Using Claude Code for non-developer technical/API work** (David's win):
  - When stuck, take a screenshot of the current screen and ask "what do I do next?"
  - Explicitly instruct it "talk to me like I'm an idiot" / "slow it down" to force granular, literal step-by-step guidance.
  - Applied twice: (1) pulling API credentials out of his shop-management platform to build custom reports, (2) vibe-coding and submitting an app to the Apple App Store screenshot-by-screenshot.
- **Funnel/offer teardown method for researching a competitor's or a guru's course** (Matt's approach, demonstrated via the "Yap Challenge"):
  - Judge by what they actually DO in their funnel, not just what they claim in marketing.
  - Check whether they use an order bump (Matt's own example: his "Rank Week" is $10 to enter, +$27 upsell for the replays — that extra is the order bump).
  - Check whether they have a back-end/upsell offer.
  - Sign up **twice** with two different email addresses: one where you actually buy/opt in (to see the real follow-up email sequence), and one where you reach checkout, enter an email, then abandon (to see retargeting/abandoned-cart emails and ads). Compare messaging, length, and quality between the two paths.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- One keyword per page. Always.
- Never combine two keyword phrases on one page/title — you win neither instead of owning one ("you're not even getting a slice").
- Build separate pages per specific town/suburb rather than one page for a whole metro region.
- Guest-post backlinks: **one** link to your own site only; place it in the **top half** of the article.
- Anchor text should be specific and geo-qualified, not a vague brand/product name — unless you deliberately want traffic sent to that specific offer.
- Guest-post topic needs no topical alignment to your niche, and no geographic alignment to your local service area either — the backlink is the point, not rankings on the host site.
- Blog-post submission deadline for the shared spreadsheet: due **by end of day** the day it's due; nothing accepted after the cutoff (this batch's cutoff referenced as the coming Saturday/Monday).
- Posts are being scheduled at **3/day**, rolled out into August, deliberately to look natural since the host site doesn't normally post that often; also cross-posted to the host's Facebook page.
- Request **read-only** access to the shared submission doc/spreadsheet unless you specifically need to correct something.
- Submission email doesn't need to match your Hey Tony account email — any email you actually check works. It's used only so the team can reach you, not to filter out fakes, and is never published on the blog.
- Friends/family engagement-seeding: fine occasionally, **not every post** — overuse blocks organic algorithmic distribution.
- Posting cadence: start at once/week if new. Matt's own current target is **3–5x/day** after his own reach and followers dropped when his cadence fell to roughly once/week.
- Google's inclusion-in-AI setting is **on by default**; most accounts don't yet have the option to control it. Turning it off is how you opt OUT of appearing in AI results.
- Social content getting picked up in search/AI results appears to be specifically **Reels/video**, not static posts (per live discussion — "I'm pretty sure it's reels" — flagged as not fully confirmed, more testing planned).
- New GSC features (Generative AI report, "Add property" for social) are rolling out **unevenly per account** — not having it yet isn't user error; check back periodically (Matt suggested checking again next week).

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Friends/family engagement-seeding works to "prime the pump" but breaks organic algorithmic distribution if used on every post — treat it as an occasional bootstrap, not routine practice.
- Quantity vs. quality of posting is openly debated live: Heather argues quality/once-a-week is enough once you're skilled; Matt holds that quantity/reps is what builds the skill (editing speed, ideation, on-camera comfort) in the first place, and that his own reach/followers dropped when his cadence fell to ~once/week — implying the calculus can shift once competence is high, but reps come first.
- Traditional Search Results performance and Generative AI (AI Overviews) citation don't correlate: a page can look "bad" by click metrics (3 clicks) yet be heavily cited in AI Overviews (14,000 impressions) — don't judge a page's value by classic click data alone anymore.
- Enterprise SEO clients (RBC) pay far more than small businesses but bring execution paralysis/red tape and, in Matt's framing, deliver less real personal/economic impact than small-business work — an explicit reason he prioritizes small-business clients emotionally, not just financially.
- Guest-post/backlink placements don't need topical or geographic alignment with your business — the value is in the link itself, so over-engineering a low-stakes guest post (agonizing over category fit, forcing a local anchor onto a nationally-focused host site) is wasted effort.
- New GSC features (Generative AI report, social "Add property") roll out unevenly and unpredictably per account — Matt states this explicitly ("Google really doesn't play favorites... I'm always the last one") so members don't mistake a missing feature for their own error.
- Viral/hype funnels (Yap Challenge) can have real revenue proof and genuinely simple core mechanics, but Matt frames them as a "flash in the pan" versus sustainable, fundamentals-based SEO/content — while still recommending members study the funnel mechanics (order bumps, back-end offers, dual-email signup testing) for tactical ideas, separate from buying into the hype itself.
- The personalized-search content strategy is explicitly forward-looking: Matt concedes today's specific-expertise blog posts "might not do much for us" right now, framing them as an investment that pays off more as search personalization increases rather than a guaranteed immediate ranking win.
