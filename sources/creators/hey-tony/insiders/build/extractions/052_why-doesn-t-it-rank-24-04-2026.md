# Why Doesn't It Rank — 24.04.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **One page = one question/problem rule**: every page on a website should answer one question or solve one problem, and no other page should compete for that same intent. A single page can still legitimately rank for many keyword *variations* of that one topic (people phrase the same intent differently) — you don't need a separate page per variation.
- **Keyword-variation example (Keith's SEO business)**: one service page targeting "SEO in Hamilton" can also rank for "SEO Agency," "SEO Agency Hamilton," "SEO company in Hamilton," "SEO Services" — all as natural variations of the same page, not separate pages/H1s.
- **Service-page architecture (Nary's bookkeeping site)**: homepage = the broad money/service page (e.g. "Bookkeeping Services in St. Petersburg, Florida") — this is the page meant to rank for the core service term; don't create a duplicate services page targeting the same keyword. The `/services` page becomes a minimal "gateway" page (not meant to rank itself) that briefly lists offerings and links out to dedicated sub-service pages (e.g. Monthly Bookkeeping, Cleanup, Catch-Up) — those specific pages are the ones meant to rank.
- **Pricing content structure (Caroline's cat-behavior-consultant site)**: "How much does it cost to hire a [X]?" is a blog post, not a static page. Add a "Pricing" nav link that points to that blog post. Also fine to list price directly on every individual sales/service page. Include a Calendly (or booking) link on sales pages — it won't get parsed by AI, but it's better real-user UX ("book an appointment" with no way to actually book is a bad experience).
- **Local Chamber of Commerce backlink tactic**: join your local chamber, then ask them to build you a member landing page with your logo/text and 2-3 links pointing to specific service pages on your site (not just one generic "visit website" link) — most chambers don't know SEO and will let you structure it however you want. You can join multiple neighboring-town chambers if they'll let you (they don't cross-check each other). Bonus: chambers run local networking events.
- **Charity backlink tactic**: contact local charities and ask what a donation costs to get your logo listed as a donor/contributor — typically $20-50 — make sure the logo itself links to your site (not just alt text).
- **Vendor/partner backlink tactic**: if you buy from or supply a local business, ask to be listed on their site as a vendor/partner with a link ("it'll help me sell your products more").
- **Backlink-gap tactic**: use the HeyTony Insiders Link Gap Analyzer — enter your site vs. a competitor's site, it surfaces links the competitor has that you don't (often free directories, news sites, local listings) — then reach out to those sites for a guest post, interview, or directory submission.
- **Orphan/event landing pages**: for a short-lived event or promo page, treat it as a true landing page — don't add links to anything else on it, only the single desired conversion action. It's fine to temporarily link to it from the homepage for a few days (so visitors who met you at the event can verify it's really you), but you don't need it to rank or be indexed since it's short-term.
- **Domain authority/backlink target-setting**: look at your top competitors' domain authority and backlink count — that's your floor; aim to pass them "a little bit." Don't just count raw links — check whether competitor links are low-authority, citation-only, or no-follow, since those add much less value than they appear to.
- **GMB troubleshooting when a ranking drops**: (1) check if you changed anything recently; (2) compare review count AND review velocity against top competitors (velocity can be better than a competitor's even if their total review count is higher); (3) confirm citations are built (Bright Local); (4) confirm NAP (name/address/phone) is in the site footer and it links to the Google Business Profile via the CID link; (5) check whether the services listed on the GMB profile match/exceed what's actually on the website (mismatch may look like profile "overkill"); (6) factor in that Google periodically updates its map-pack algorithm independent of anything you did.
- **Rank-checking discipline**: don't check daily — give a change 30 days before judging it. Once a page is optimized, stop touching it ("stop fucking around with it") and let Google settle where it wants to rank it.
- **Cross-device image bug troubleshooting**: if images show fine on your own machine/browser but disappear in incognito or on other devices, paste the site into Claude (or similar) and describe the exact symptom ("images aren't showing up on other devices, what's happening here") and troubleshoot from there. Also: never batch-convert a whole site's images at once — change one page, verify it works, then move to the next; test on a low-stakes page first, not the homepage.
- **"Should I under-optimize to match sloppy competitors?"** — no. Still follow the basics regardless of what competitors do: one H1 tag, use the target keyword a natural handful of times, and write modestly more content than the current top results (e.g., if they have ~200 words, aim for ~300) — you don't need a 5,000-word page to beat weak competition, just don't dumb your own page down either.
- **AI traffic tracking**: you can track AI-referred traffic (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity) as a source inside Google Analytics — that's different from "AI visibility" (showing up as a cited/recommended answer), which currently cannot be tracked by any tool.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- **You do NOT need hundreds of backlinks to rank.** Many members are already ranking from blog posts alone (long-tail, low-competition keywords) without meaningful link building.
- For HeyTony's own clients: **4 backlinks per month** (~48-52/year) is the standard cadence — "that's all we do" and it gets "crazy results." Don't chase 10, 20, or 50 links/month.
- Ignore cold outreach emails promising "100 links" or similar bulk-backlink pitches — Matt's direct instruction: "ignore that shit."
- **Backlink Management IO** (~$40/month bulk backlink subscription) — explicitly flagged as bad value: "that can't [be right]... don't do that."
- A prior vendor charged **Lori $1,500** for 75-78 identical directory listings plus a **$199/month** "refresh" upsell, promising #1 rankings in 6-12 months, claiming to be a "Google partner" with algorithm access. Matt: **the only real "Google partner" status is a Google Ads partner** — nobody has special algorithm access. Verdict: way overpriced (equivalent citation work costs ~$2/citation one-time via Bright Local) — cancel it, no ongoing management is needed.
- **AI visibility cannot currently be tracked by any tool**, including Semrush's "AI visibility" feature — those tools make up their own test prompts/searches and check if you show up, which isn't the same as tracking real user prompts. Anyone selling "AI visibility" services is working from invented data.
- **No hashtags on Google Business Profile posts.**
- If you don't have fixed studio/office hours (appointment-only), use GMB's **"appointment only"** hours option rather than posting fake fixed hours.
- **Don't set your GMB hours to 24 hours** — bad user experience (someone calling at 3am can't reach you anyway) — use your actual regular hours.
- A fully **online-only business with no real local service area will get its GMB listing rejected** — verification for local-service businesses now may require video + signage proof (not just the old mail-in postcard code).
- Service-area-only GMB listings (no fixed physical address, e.g. traveling photographer) are allowed and still helpful, but **less effective than a listed physical address**.
- **Site-wide/aggregate click-through rate is not a useful metric** — it blends every keyword and every ranking position together. A page ranking #10 might convert clicks near 0%; a page ranking #1 might get ~10%+. Sita's overall CTR of **2.3%** is not something to worry about in isolation — look at CTR per keyword/position instead.
- **About and Contact pages don't need to rank** and shouldn't be over-optimized to compete with the homepage or service pages for the main keyword — they only rank (if at all) for branded/contact-intent searches (business name + "contact").
- **Keyword cannibalization**: if multiple pages on the same site could rank for the same query, Google will only reliably surface one — because serving several results from one domain for one query is a bad experience for searchers who want to compare across sources. Fix: keep each page targeting one distinct topic/intent.
- **Third-party rank trackers (SEMrush, SE Rankings, etc.) may not have 100% accurate/up-to-date position data** — don't panic over a data point from these tools; wait for refresh or just give it time.
- **Quizzes**: if you can't confirm they're driving traffic or converting, they're "probably not doing anything for you" — fine to remove for a cleaner site, unless data says otherwise.
- General principle repeated multiple times: **"Always think of the user. If it's going to be beneficial for somebody, great. If not, leave it."**
- Domain Authority is volatile and non-linear — Al's DA rose 24→28 (+4) after ~6 months of steady decline, attributed to a Guardian (UK newspaper) press mention/backlink, though he has other press links that didn't move it — more press/mentions is still the stated path to keep it climbing.
- HeyTony's own backlink profile as a benchmark: **1,229 total referring domains**, but only **598 are active + follow** links, accumulated over ~5 years — reinforces that raw link counts overstate real link equity.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Domain Authority / backlink counts are directional, not precise — raw link totals overstate value because many links are low-authority, citation-only, or no-follow; HeyTony's own site has 1,229 referring domains but only 598 are active+follow, illustrating the gap between headline link count and real equity.
- Rank volatility after a legitimate optimization (unpublishing a competing page, fixing a keyword conflict) can look alarming day-to-day — a page jumping to #7, then #9, then disappearing is framed as expected noise, not a sign the fix failed; judgment should wait a full month, and third-party trackers may add their own data-freshness noise on top of Google's real volatility.
- Pricing transparency is not a one-size-fits-all rule: a cat-behavior consultant is pointed toward putting prices on every sales page plus a dedicated pricing blog post; a luxury newborn photographer is validated in deliberately withholding prices because her buyer's decision journey depends on an in-person/call experience rather than comparison shopping — Matt frames the real test as "what does your actual buyer's decision process look like," not a universal disclosure rule, and tells her to track conversion over the next month or two to see if it's working.
- Citations vs. backlinks: both matter and both can be inexpensive, but they're not interchangeable — citations are cheap/free directory-style NAP listings (Bright Local, ~$2 each) while backlinks require more relationship-driven work (chambers, charities, guest posts, vendor partners) and carry more direct authority weight.
- GMB profile "services" richness can cut both ways: filling out more services than your website actually documents may look like profile padding/overkill to Google rather than being purely beneficial — worth auditing for a mismatch instead of assuming "more is better."
- Third-party AI-visibility scoring (e.g. Semrush) isn't fraudulent so much as structurally unable to reflect reality yet — it's a best-effort proxy built on invented test prompts, not actual user query data, since neither Google nor the major AI chat products expose that data today.
- Local Chamber of Commerce value varies member-to-member: several members had great outcomes ($75-$500 cost for meaningful backlinks/landing pages plus networking), but at least one participant left theirs, calling the org overly focused on new-member acquisition rather than serving existing members — worth vetting the specific local chapter, not assuming uniform quality.
