# Rank Week Day 1 Replay — 15.06.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Step 1 — Find a real customer question.** Go to one of the question-finder sites (see Tools) and type in what you sell (one product/service, e.g. "nail salon," "emergency plumber," "pedicure"). Pick one question your actual customers ask. If you already collected questions from a live sales call / tech-check homework, skip the tool and use those.
- **Step 2 — Interview yourself with Prompt 1** in ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini (any LLM): paste the question in, let the AI ask you 5 questions one at a time, answer with **voice mode** (faster/more natural than typing) or by typing — either works.
- **Step 3 — Generate the post with Prompt 2** (the "clean WordPress ready" version — see Tools for exact wording). Output = only H2/H3 headings, paragraphs, and bullet points; no dividers/tables/callouts.
- **Step 4 — Paste the output** into a Word doc or directly onto a blog post on your site (do NOT publish yet).
- **Step 5 — Open 3 target pages** in tabs: (1) a product or service page ("money page"), (2) your homepage, (3) another existing blog post related to this new post's topic. If you have no other blog post, just use the first two.
- **Step 6 — Internal-link the new post to those 3 pages.** In the doc/CMS, highlight words in the new post that describe each target page (business name, service name, product name, or the general business type) and turn each into a hyperlink to that page — one link per target page for this exercise.
- **Title the post with the question itself** (the question you answered = the blog title), not a longer keyword-stuffed variant.
- To learn the mechanical how-to for adding a link from highlighted text on your specific platform, ask ChatGPT/Claude: *"How do I add a link to a blog post or to another page on my site from highlighted words?"* — then tell it your platform (WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, etc.).
- You can try asking the AI to insert the 3 links itself (paste the 3 URLs, ask it to add them) — Matt says this "doesn't always work," which is why the live exercise has people do it manually.
- For podcast-based blog content: don't just link to the podcast episode as one of your 3 internal links (low search volume for podcast-episode lookups unless you're Alex Hormozi/Gary V-tier). Instead, grab the podcast transcript, paste it into ChatGPT/Claude with an instruction like "remember this for the future when I'm writing a blog post — this is from a podcast episode I did" — this trains the AI to pull your real expertise/stories/voice into future posts.
- For local/location-based service businesses: don't gate SEO efforts to only local searchers. Keep content up (even info not locally relevant to your service area) because engagement signals from non-local traffic (time on site, page visits, form fills) still build domain authority Google uses when ranking you for local/transactional terms.
- Applies to every site builder/CMS: WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, custom-coded, Shopify, "show it" websites (heard as "Showit") — the "WordPress ready" instruction in the prompt is just a format signal to the AI, not a platform requirement. Doesn't work well for Substack per Matt.
- Timed session structure Matt actually ran live (useful as a template for self-running this exercise): 30 min for steps 1–3 (find question → prompt 1 → prompt 2) → 2 min to paste into doc/site → 2 min to open the 3 target links → 4 min to highlight + insert the 3 internal links.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- **Internal links per post:** do 2–3 as a normal rule of thumb, max around 5; today's exercise standardizes on exactly **3** (product/service page, homepage, one related blog post).
- **Don't over-link a repeated word/phrase** — if a term appears multiple times in the post, link it only **once**, not every occurrence.
- **Adding too many links is a bad thing** — stated as a flat warning, not just a soft guideline.
- **Title length:** keep it near **60 characters**, ideally under **~55**, or it gets cut off in Google (especially on mobile).
- **Images:** add a photo roughly every **200–300 words** — don't leave a "giant wall of text."
- **Alt text:** keep it simple — just describe what's in the image (e.g. "cat photography pose"); precision here is a "very small thing," not high-stakes.
- **Always add a featured image** to the post, wherever your platform surfaces one.
- **No duplicate boilerplate link blocks** (e.g. the same 4–5 "resource links" pasted at the bottom of every single page) — Google reads that as duplicate content. If you want a repeated resources section, vary it or link to one clearly-labeled resource, not a static block.
- **Landing/squeeze pages (money pages) should stay single-purpose:** don't add the blog to the site navigation, and don't link out from the landing page to the homepage or blog. The one-job conversion page should not be diluted. Blog can still exist on the site elsewhere.
- **Word count doesn't matter for this specific exercise** (the informational/expertise-driven blog post) — word count (800–1500 was raised as a question) applies more to Day 2's topic (transactional/money-page content), not to today's format.
- **Google/AI do NOT penalize AI-written posts.** What gets penalized is regurgitated, generic, same-as-everyone-else information. Adding your real expertise/experience/opinions/stories (via the 5-question interview prompt) is specifically what avoids that penalty.
- **Don't rely on SEMrush-style dashboards as your KPI** — Matt calls it "vanity metrics" / "made up." The only KPI that actually counts: bookings/form-fills/purchases. Secondary, directional metrics worth tracking: total traffic and AI-mention counts (via the still-rolling-out GSC AI Overview mentions feature).
- **Keyword categories** (from Matt's book "Get Found," page 47) — user intent buckets:
  - **Informational** — what today's exercise produces (someone has a problem, wants information).
  - **Navigational** — brand+destination searches, e.g. "Netflix login," "Amazon login."
  - **Commercial** — comparison content for people close to buying but still deciding ("this camera or that camera").
  - **Transactional** — ready-to-buy searches ("emergency plumber," a specific product with size/attribute, e.g. "9oz mug with Santa Claus on it") — this is Day 2's topic.
  - Also flagged (not covered in depth this session): branded keywords, evergreen keywords, time-sensitive/trending keywords, geotargeted keywords.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- AI-written content isn't inherently penalized — the differentiator is whether the AI is used to package *your* genuine expertise/experience/stories (via the interview-style prompt) versus producing generic, context-free "write me a blog post" output that reads the same as every competitor's.
- Traffic from outside your service area isn't wasted, even though it will never convert to a local customer directly — it's reframed as an authority-building signal to Google that indirectly helps your local/transactional rankings. Matt explicitly warns against deleting content just because "nobody local is searching this."
- Websites still matter despite AI Overviews/zero-click anxiety, but the reasoning is nuanced: AI systems still need fresh, accurate source information to answer from, and a business without a website is invisible to that pipeline — the risk isn't "SEO is dead," it's "your specific content isn't feeding the machine."
- KPI measurement is currently incomplete: real conversions are the only fully trustworthy metric; traffic and AI-mention counts are useful directionally but the tooling to track AI mentions (new GSC feature) is not yet broadly available (1 of Matt's clients has it), so most site owners can't yet build a complete "AI visibility" dashboard — a live tradeoff between wanting better data and the data not existing yet.
- Squeeze/landing pages and content-marketing/blog strategy aren't in conflict, but they also shouldn't be merged: keep the high-converting single-purpose page pure (no nav links out) while still building out a separate blog section for organic/AI discovery — the two serve different jobs on the same site.
- Podcast content is valuable as raw material for training the AI on your voice and stories, but Matt advises against treating a podcast episode as a primary internal-link destination for most businesses, since almost nobody searches for a specific person's specific episode by name — reserve that linking treatment for creators with outsized personal brand recognition.
