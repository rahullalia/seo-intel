## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **"AI Markdown" post structure (Matt's current experiment, prompted by a member suggestion to add key takeaways):**
  1. **TL;DR / Summary at the very top** (label it "TL;DR," "Summary," or similar) — answer the target question in 1-2 sentences immediately. Example given: Q: "How much does SEO cost in 2026?" → "Quality SEO typically costs between [X] and [Y] per month. For most small businesses, it takes six to 12 months to see real results. [Pricing] usually includes content creation, backlinks, technical fixes, strategy. Cheap SEO often costs more in the long run." Written so an AI Overview/ChatGPT/Claude could clip it verbatim.
  2. **Restate the answer again in the opening paragraphs** with a bit more context (e.g., a "SEO pricing summary" covering cost, scope, goals) before going into full detail in the body — gives AI another extraction point in case it misses the TL;DR.
  3. **Key Takeaways section at the end**, bulleted, reinforcing the same core facts a third time (cost/duration, cheap-SEO-compromises-quality, what pricing includes) — a safety net in case a reader or AI crawler misses the top of the post.
  4. Style model: write like decent social content — cut the fluff, cut filler ("ums," irrelevant asides), get to the point inside the first couple of paragraphs.
  - **Test protocol:** only run this on 2-3 pieces of existing content at a time (over the weekend or Monday), not 10-20 — too many simultaneous changes makes it impossible to track which change caused what. Test TL;DR-at-top + in-line answer first.
  - Schema note: Matt uses **no schema** on his own site. He researched (via a YouTube video) who ranks #1 for a term and what AI recommends — the #1-ranked page had heavy schema (product + review schema) *and* better content, but AI overviews weren't recommending it first regardless. His read: AI is optimizing for "pull the answer out and hand it back to the user" — extractability of the answer seems to matter more than the presence of schema itself.

- **Self-audit checklist for "why doesn't this page rank" (used live on Alex's, Georg's, and Jill's sites):**
  - Count H1 tags on the page — should be exactly one, and it must contain the keyword you're targeting.
  - Check whether a repeated site-wide header/section (e.g., a "what's going on in the [region]" block) is being rendered as an H1 on *every* blog post — this tells Google every page is the same page; remove it from all posts.
  - Answer the target question directly in plain language right away — cut hedge phrasing like "the short answer is no"; just say "No, [X] cannot [Y]."
  - Add an FAQ block, formatted as plain question-then-answer (no need to literally prefix "Q:"/"A:").
  - Add a Key Takeaways block, which can sit separately from the FAQ block.
  - Count internal links — 1-2 is too few.
  - Count external links — cite an outside authority (government/official body) for factual/regulatory claims and link to it.
  - Check for a tags/keywords field (common in Go High Level's blog editor) creating one archive page per tag with only a single post on it — this is thin content; strip the tags/keywords field off every post.
  - Compare heading count/structure and word count against whoever is actually ranking — don't assume you need more content; verify competitor word counts by hand (tool-reported counts can be wrong).
  - Watch for keyword cannibalization from your own copy: using 3+ near-duplicate phrase variants ("indigenous art fly net hat," "mosquito net hat," "indigenous art protection print fly net hat") instead of committing to one primary keyword confuses the target signal — pick one main phrase.
  - Strip low-value filler copy that doesn't help conversion or answer the query (e.g., a "we're taking preorders" block sitting above the fold on a product page) — shrink or remove it so buyers aren't forced to scroll past it.
  - When in doubt about removing a content block, move it to **draft** rather than delete outright; re-test over the next month or two and adjust from there.
  - Turn each FAQ question into its own standalone blog post — free additional content + internal linking opportunity.
  - Ranking-target strategy: don't fixate on beating an entrenched #1 that has a mountain of backlinks — aim to take position 2 or 3 instead.

- **Diagnosing a Google Search Console dashboard that looks "overwhelming" or stagnant (Nick's site):**
  - Don't default to the last-3-months view for a newer site — pull the **last 12-16 months** to see the actual trendline.
  - Read Impressions first (trending up = early traction, even before clicks show up).
  - In the Queries report, ignore any query with a tiny impression count (e.g., "average position 1" on a query with only 8 impressions over 16 months) — statistically meaningless, don't chase it.
  - Expect junk/irrelevant queries (e.g., a stray address string, a Spanish-language phrase, "Kia Sorrento," "Tesla Model X," "Ford recalls video vehicles") to start appearing in Queries as a site ranks for more terms — this is normal at scale ("Google just does its weird thing"), not a sign of a problem. Query-report row counts (going from ~8-10 rows to 67) climbing is itself a positive signal of expanding reach.

- **Verifying whether a competitor is actually winning via Google before copying their page (Marla's vacation rental case):**
  1. Ask: are they even ranking #1 (or at all) for the keyword in question? If not, stop comparing your on-page/schema to theirs for that keyword.
  2. Ask whether they might be using a longer-tail/more specific keyword instead.
  3. Paste the competitor's **exact URL** directly into Google search — if nothing comes up, they aren't ranking for anything there.
  4. Check the **Ads Transparency Library** to rule out that their traffic is paid.
  5. Interrogate the "they convert better" claim: how do you actually know they get more bookings, and how do you know those bookings come from Google (vs. direct marketing, other listing partners, referrals)? Don't let ChatGPT's competitive analysis (which can assert a "primary keyword" the competitor doesn't even rank for) stand in for this verification.
  - Separate concept: getting people to the page (ranking/keyword-intent match) is a different problem from converting them once they land — matching intent matters more for ranking; conversion copy is a distinct, secondary lever.

- **Adding a site to Bing Webmaster Tools (walkthrough given to Marla):**
  1. Go to Bing Webmaster Tools → "Add a site."
  2. Choose to import from Google Search Console — since the domain is already verified there, it pulls straight in with no separate verification step ("takes two seconds").
  3. Sign-in option: use your Google account (keeps it tied to your GSC connection) rather than Microsoft — this is Matt's own preference, not a hard requirement.
  4. Agencies/managers of multiple sites can do a **mass import** of all their Google Search Console profiles at once.

- **Reading a client's existing GA/Search-Console report (Michelle's Pro Weave-built Looker Studio dashboard):**
  - Ignore engagement rate, channel groupings, page title breakdowns, and geographic (city/region) breakdowns for this purpose — not actionable.
  - Go straight to Google Search Console → Performance → look at raw **Clicks**, not "Views" (views can double-count one visitor hitting 5-6 pages).
  - Google Business Profile activity is a separate data source and will not show up inside Search Console.

- **Splitting one business into two Google Business Profiles for two audiences (Sandy's roofing company):** if a business genuinely serves two distinct audiences (commercial vs. residential), set up a second, separately-optimized GBP rather than funneling both segments to one un-optimized shared/portfolio landing page. General validation method for any strategy question: check analytics for actual clicks first — if you're not getting clicks, it's the wrong strategy, regardless of how confident you feel about it.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Test new format experiments (like the AI Markdown/TL;DR structure) on **2-3 posts max at a time** — never 10-20 — so results stay trackable.
- Every blog post should have **exactly one H1**, and it must contain the target keyword.
- A repeated header/module rendered as H1 across every post is a hard no — remove it site-wide.
- Don't leave a tags/keywords field populated in the blog editor (especially in Go High Level) — it silently spins up thin-content tag-archive pages.
- Minimum internal links per post: more than 1-2 (Alex's 2 internal links was flagged as too few).
- Cite and link to at least one external authoritative source for factual/regulatory claims.
- Don't just auto-convert an existing YouTube video or podcast into a blog post without keyword research backing the topic choice.
- When unsure about cutting content, move it to **draft**, don't delete — re-test over the next month or two before finalizing.
- Pick **one primary keyword**, not 3+ near-duplicate variants, to avoid self-cannibalizing your own on-page signal.
- Don't chase beating an entrenched #1 result that has heavy backlinks — target position 2-3 instead.
- For a newer site's Search Console, check the **12-16 month window**, not the default last-3-months.
- Ignore any query row with single-digit impressions over many months (e.g., 8 impressions/16 months) — not worth acting on.
- Junk/off-topic queries appearing in Search Console as impressions grow (67 rows, up from ~8-10) is expected, not a red flag.
- Google's own Search Console query-report table caps out around **~1,000 rows** shown (Matt's own site example).
- Before copying a competitor's page, confirm they actually rank for the keyword in question (search their exact URL in Google) and confirm their traffic source (check Ads Transparency Library) before assuming their approach is worth copying.
- **Minimum viable SEO retainer: at least $1,500/month.** Below that, per Matt, an agency/freelancer "is not able to do enough work for you."
- **Don't hire SEO help based in the Philippines or India**, per Matt's direct experience — "the SEO that I've seen has been far subpar... I've never seen anything good come of it."
- Optimize for real qualified traffic/business results, not raw click volume — a sudden, unexplained spike in clicks (e.g., 1,000/month → 45,000/month within ~2 months) is a red flag for bought/fake traffic, not a win.
- Buy the SEO reference book in **both formats**: physical (to write/underline in) and Kindle (for when you don't have the physical copy on hand). Title not stated in the transcript — referred to only as "this book," described as covering start-to-finish SEO.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Schema markup is not the deciding factor for AI-overview recommendation — Matt observed a heavily schema'd, better-written #1-ranking page still get passed over by AI overviews, leading him to conclude extractable-answer formatting (TL;DR/key-takeaways) may matter more than schema for that specific outcome; this is presented as an inference from one observed case, not a proven rule.
- Ranking (getting the click) and converting (getting the booking/sale) are separate problems — keyword/intent match drives the former; on-page persuasion copy drives the latter. A page can under-rank while over-converting, or vice versa, so don't treat conversion-focused rewrites as an automatic SEO fix.
- Competitor benchmarking is only valid if the competitor's success is actually coming from the same channel (Google organic) you're trying to win — Marla's competitor appeared to out-convert her, but had no verifiable Google ranking and no confirmed traffic source, so copying their page/schema would have been copying the wrong thing.
- Chasing the #1 spot outright can be the wrong target when the incumbent has a large backlink moat — realistically competing for position 2-3 can be the higher-leverage move, and still delivers meaningful traffic.
- A Search Console dashboard full of odd/irrelevant queries (product names, foreign-language phrases, addresses) isn't a sign something is broken — it's a normal side effect of a site gaining broader visibility as it ranks for more terms; the temptation to "fix" or worry about this noise should be resisted.
- Tool-reported word counts can be wrong (a competitor page reported at "1300 words" was manually verified at only 174) — always sanity-check a competitor's actual on-page content by eye rather than trusting a single tool's number when making a content-length decision.
- Cheap/offshore SEO retainers aren't just "lower quality" — they can actively harm a business by inflating vanity metrics (bought/junk clicks) that look like progress on a dashboard while producing zero real leads, making it harder for the client to realize something's wrong until an outside audit (like this call) surfaces the real click numbers.
