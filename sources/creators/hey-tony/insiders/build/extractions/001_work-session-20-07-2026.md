## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Weekly blog-post exercise** (core recurring drill, "we should all be doing this every single week"):
  1. Find a real customer question — go to findquestions.com (free, public) or, for Insiders, Insiders Toolkit → Blog Idea Finder (same mechanism, type in what you sell).
  2. Alt/better route for a narrow niche: skip the tool, ask actual customers what questions they had before buying, or survey past customers/contractors on why they chose you (chat tip from member "Michael," used to unstick Debbie).
  3. Take the question to ChatGPT/Claude using "prompt 1" (posted live in chat and also stored in Community → Prompts → "blog prompt") — it interviews you with questions about the topic. Default is limited to 5 questions; Matt's version uses 10 "because we want to be a little bit more specific."
  4. Answer via voice mode (Matt's preference — "I'll digress and might reveal something else I wouldn't think of if I was typing") or by typing.
  5. Feed the same thread "prompt 2" to turn the Q&A into the actual blog post.
  6. Add 3 internal links to 3 other blog posts + 1 link to a money page = 4 links total on the new page.
  7. Add images, then edit so it "sounds exactly like you" — the underlying answers are your own expertise/case studies/opinions, so the draft should already be close.
  8. Fine to publish without images and add them later — "get something up rather than not get something up."
- **Reviews micro-site tactic** (to influence Google AI Overviews / ChatGPT answers about your business):
  1. Register a domain like "[business name] reviews" or "[product] reviews" (examples: rankweekreviews.com, heytonyreviews.com).
  2. Fill it with real reviews pulled from anywhere they exist — Google, Reddit, email, verbal client feedback — not limited to reviews already public elsewhere ("some of mine are, like, pulled from Reddit... I just put the first name").
  3. Build fast/cheap: Matt's own build = dumped review screenshots into Claude Code, described what Rank Week is, said "here's what I like, just build this out" — no further design brief.
  4. Host it on its own separate root domain, not a subdomain or hyphenated path off your main site — a separate site reads as a more independent third-party source to Google/Bing/ChatGPT.
  5. Link FROM your main website TO the reviews site (the direction matters — Matt corrected a member who had it backwards).
  6. Keep your on-site reviews/testimonials page too — it's additive, not either/or ("keep that there as well," told to AJ).
  7. If you register more than one of these, don't redirect them into each other — each stands alone with its own reviews content.
  8. Don't connect/verify these reviews domains in Google Search Console.
- **Reddit/social for fast AI-result influence**: post something conversational in a relevant subreddit (Matt's example: "How was Rank Week? Did you enjoy it?"), get your own community to upvote/comment. Stack other controllable signals around the same brand-name query — your own Facebook posts, Instagram, a press release (Matt uses AB Newswire), your BBB listing — these all get pulled together into AI Overview/ChatGPT summaries about your business name. Posting fresh "latest" social content regularly signals to Google you're a real, active business.
- **Getting reviews, by business type**:
  - Hyper-local (e.g., new real-estate/homebuilding business): focus entirely on Google Business Profile; ask every client and vendor; circle back to past clients too — sample script: "we just launched our Google business profile — it'd be great if you could leave a review... we'd love for you to share that message with anybody else looking."
  - No GBP (e.g., stand-up comedian): gather reviews live from clients/audience after each show/appointment — ask directly, "can I get a review from you?"
  - Trustpilot / bark.com specifically for AI visibility: send a direct link to the review page and ask people to leave a review there (works around Trustpilot's clunkier flow); or watch over someone's shoulder as they go through the process to learn it.
- **Hyper-local/hyper-specific service page** (Victor's win): write a page about one specific neighborhood/sub-area (not just your city) backed by genuine unique research/value about that micro-area, then manually submit the URL for indexing in Google Search Console right after publishing instead of waiting.
- **Fixing low CTR despite decent impressions** (Miranda/Mike Rob): find pages ranking on page 2, or bottom of page 1, in Search Console; rewrite the title tag and meta description on those specific pages — "that's like the billboard saying click me instead of your competitors and you want to get more clicks."
- **Benchmarking content depth for pages with no obvious word-count target**: look at whoever ranks #1–#3 for the target term, note their word count and categories of info covered, match/exceed that baseline — but layer in your own expertise, direct experience, and real examples/case studies competitors can't replicate. Don't start from scratch; don't just copy structure either.
- **HVAC-style service-page structure with no fixed pricing** (Gabrielle's brother): top nav → "Services" button → services page listing each distinct service as its own category (furnace install / furnace swap / furnace repair / AC install / AC swap / AC repair); each gets its own page; end every service page with "contact us for a free quote" — that CTA is the money page even without listed prices.
- **Backlinks via journalist-request platforms** (Bill): pitch as an expert source through "help out a reporter" (as transcribed — almost certainly HARO / Help A Reporter Out) to earn links back to medically-themed blog posts. Described as a lot of back-and-forth and pitching, but legitimate.
- **Crowdsourcing content ideas live from your own community/customers** when a niche is narrow/B2B (Debbie's off-site plasterboard business): ask the room/customers directly what questions they had pre-purchase. Real questions surfaced live in chat: "what's the cheapest way to install drywall," "best way to clean up after plasterboard installation," "how do I avoid dust in my house when installing drywall." Matt's refinement: go more specific and connect the angles rather than list generic separate benefits (e.g., not just "low dust" and "quick cleanup" as separate claims — ask does paint stick better because of low dust?).
- **Community feedback loop**: post your URL in the Insiders community/chat and ask for constructive feedback. Reciprocity is the norm — Ariel reviewed another member's site in detail and it led to real changes to that site.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Publish a blog post every single week — repeated as the baseline regardless of niche.
- Reviews micro-site strategy is explicitly **not** white-hat: "this is not like a white hat SEO strategy, like above board, all that kind of stuff... it's not something that's like that. I would write in my book, for example." It manipulates search results (something Google doesn't want) — Matt does it, isn't sure of concrete risk, but advises not verifying these domains in Google Search Console.
- Don't buy up competitor-style review domains as an offensive tactic — Matt says people do this but doesn't recommend it ("I'm not saying to go buy your competitors... I'm not suggesting you do that, but that's something that people do").
- Do **not** use keyword-density optimizer tools (e.g., Drop In Blog telling you "you've only used the SEO word three times, you need to try and fit it in there again") — that's old-school keyword-stuffing SEO, not this method.
- Core philosophy: "our objective is not to game the algorithm... it's to write a piece of content that is so unique and uses our experience that it talks to somebody with that specific problem" — even Matt's own agency-built AI still needs human review before publishing.
- WordPress blog **category pages**: don't optimize them — thin content, will never rank. Example cited: 851 words spread across 31 H2 tags on one category page.
- Shopify **collection pages**: opposite rule — these can and should be optimized/ranked. Don't conflate "category page" (WordPress/blog) with "collection page" (Shopify/product) — different platforms, different treatment.
- Same content, different CSS = fine. Same content repeated across pages = the actual problem. Don't blindly trust a platform's built-in AI assistant on SEO (Shopify's flagged CSS repetition as a risk, which conflates styling with content) — bring real technical questions to the Thursday technical-SEO call with Denroy instead.
- Reviews-microsite naming: company-name-based or product-name-based both work, no fixed rule ("you can do both").
- BBB listing: worth having even without provable ROI — Matt paid for his, later stopped paying, and the listing stayed up anyway; framed as a plausible trust/ranking signal ("it is like a ranking factor in something, like legitimate businesses do").
- Fine to publish a blog post with no images yet — add them later, don't let missing assets block publishing.
- Don't chase broad head-terms against much bigger incumbents (Bill vs. hospitals on general injury terms) when your real differentiation is a specific long-tail question — this method is built to win exact-question searches, not broad competitive ones.
- For a national/DIY-funnel product (e.g., self-treatment ebooks), use qualifier keywords like "free guide," "free resource," "DIY," "do it yourself" instead of competing on local "near me" terms that pit you against hospitals/major providers.
- GBP at a shared address for a second business line: add a suite/unit number (e.g., "Suite B") if possible; Matt believes ("fairly certain") Google now tolerates multiple listings at one address (shared-office scenarios) but wasn't fully sure.
- Facebook impersonation: Matt never sends friend requests or DMs (about Bitcoin, investing, etc.) — any such message claiming to be him is fake; report the impersonator profile; verify before assuming legitimacy even on a "verified" account.
- geoimager.com is for Google Business Profile photo geotagging, **not** blog post images — don't bother using it on blog images.
- Watch hosting/domain cost creep for solo operators ($10–20/yr domain + monthly hosting adds up) — look for a host that lets you add a second/third site to an existing plan at no extra cost rather than paying full separate hosting per micro-site.
- A slow-loading anti-bot/"checking your browser" interstitial (observed on one member's shared ~40-site hosting account, roughly a 5-second delay) is called out explicitly as a bad speed signal to Google.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- The reviews-microsite tactic is presented candidly as gray/black-hat-adjacent ("it is to manipulate the search results, which they don't want you to do") — Matt does it and hasn't seen concrete downside, but deliberately keeps distance from it: won't write about it in his book, advises against verifying those domains in Google Search Console.
- Fast, opportunistic wins (a Reddit thread cited in an AI Overview within 24 hours; Victor's neighborhood page hitting #1 in AI Overview within 2 days) appear tied to genuine specificity/uniqueness of the underlying content, not something guaranteed by speed or volume alone.
- BBB listing value is fuzzy — Matt kept the listing even after he stopped paying for it and frames it as a plausible trust/ranking signal, but offers no hard evidence of ROI ("I don't know what to tell you about that").
- Bill's broad-vs-narrow keyword tension: competing head-on with hospitals on generic injury terms is a losing game, but his business straddles two audiences — local health-coaching clients (needs location mentioned) vs. a national DIY-ebook funnel (needs "free"/DIY-style keywords instead) — the right keyword approach differs by which side of the business a given post serves.
- ChatGPT-vs-Claude and the instant/medium/high reasoning-effort setting materially affect output quality/consistency over a long thread — a real practical tradeoff members hit (Robert's case), not just a stylistic preference.
- GSC's "Generative AI" impressions report only tells part of the story (impressions, no clicks, not available on every property) — reassuring a client that "visibility is still good" during an apparent traffic dip is partly an act of faith pending Google exposing more data, not something fully provable today.
- Category pages (WordPress) vs. collection pages (Shopify) look similar but require opposite treatment — a conflation risk Ariel specifically flagged and had clarified live.
- Platform-native AI assistants (Shopify's built-in AI, in Coco's case) can give plausible-sounding but incorrect SEO advice — worth a sanity check against a human technical lead (Denroy's Thursday call) rather than trusting it directly.
- Multiple business lines at one physical address (Dimitri) and shared-hosting-account bloat (David's ~40 sites causing slow load) both show the same pattern: operational choices made for convenience can quietly create SEO-relevant side effects (duplicate-address flags, slow page speed) that need active management.
