## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Client-expertise-extraction alternative to hiring an interviewer (origin of the Talk SEO app):** Matt considered hiring someone whose whole job was monthly Zoom calls with clients to extract expertise, then rejected it (too expensive, too slow, clients no-show). Instead he built a low-friction daily tool: one push notification, one voice-recorded answer, feeds straight into content generation — "two-minute SEO." Directly reusable for any agency wanting client voice/expertise in blog content without a manual interview cycle.
- **CJ's proven backlink-timing sequence** that got AI to answer with his business by name within 6 hours of launch:
  1. ~2 months before launch, publish a blog post about the upcoming program and build its landing page as a placeholder (don't link it yet).
  2. On launch day, publish a new post specifically about the launch.
  3. Immediately go back and add a backlink from the original (placeholder-era) post to the correct new program page — double-check it resolves to the right URL, not a wrong/old one.
  4. Result: 6 hours later, an incognito search for the program name returned CJ's business as the sole direct answer, plus one adjacent party and the regulatory body (Division of Real Estate).
- **Testing whether a brand/program gets picked up by AI (CJ's assignment for a program with two names):** post in a Facebook group only — not X, LinkedIn, or anywhere else at the same time, so you can actually isolate what caused any effect. First 7-10 words of the post: "[Program name], AKA [other program name], is officially launched for [target]," then a short description. Engagement/reactions don't matter — the only failure mode to avoid is the post getting removed/unapproved by group moderators. Report back on Monday or next Friday, or sooner in the community "wins" channel if results show up faster.
- **Press releases without real news (Matt's approach via AB Newswire.com):** frame it as "[client] is a top-rated business in [industry]," then talk about their reviews and why they're highly rated. No actual newsworthy event required.
- **Migrating a site to a new CMS while preserving SEO (Christopher's case):** keep the exact same domain; duplicate all content, URLs, and heading tags identically to the old site; launch the *entire* duplicated site at once rather than a partial version — don't leave a subset of old URLs 404ing while the rest gets finished. On-page optimization (internal linking, meta descriptions, title tags) happens *after* launch, not before.
- **Cleaning up broken/404 links:** run the "hey Tony" audit tool to surface every broken link, then work through them in small batches (~10/week) rather than trying to bulk-fix or bulk-delete (the links all point to different destinations, so there's no single bulk action).
- **Handling a product/program with two different names:** don't default to building two full separate money pages — test which name variant actually gets AI/search pickup first (see Facebook-group test above) before committing to duplicate pages.
- **DIY CSS fix without a developer (Ritza's approach):** find a YouTube tutorial for the specific fix, take screenshots of the current state first so you can undo it if it breaks something, then make the edit yourself.
- **Requesting a tutorial from the team:** post the specific ask in the Insiders community tab and tag the person directly (Varuna tagged Denroy for an advanced Google Analytics event-tracking tutorial, after ~2 weeks of asking).
- **Live site-teardown checklist Matt ran on a "cat behavior expert" site (Caroline, Montreal, fully online):**
  1. Replace blurry/low-quality photos — trust signal ("who are you going to trust, somebody with a nice clear image or somebody with a blurry image").
  2. Make sure H1/H2 keyword phrasing actually matches your real target keyword (her H2 said "cat education services," which didn't line up with her actual target, "cat behavior expert").
  3. If you translated a keyword into a second language, validate search intent/phrasing in that language separately — don't blindly machine-translate and hope.
  4. Compare word count/structure against whoever *actually ranks* for your target keyword — not local competitors, and not by piling in more content. Her page ran ~2,000 words with many dropdowns; the actual ranking competitor's page ran 896 words and was simpler.
  5. Move certifications/credentials higher on the page (hers were buried at the bottom).
  6. Link out to legitimate press/features (boards, publications) as external credibility signals — only if genuinely earned.
  7. Rewrite generic service copy into specific-problem language: instead of "cat education services," ask direct questions like "does your cat hide when someone comes over?", "is your cat fighting?", "does your cat scratch you when you cut its nails?"
  8. Cut the number of options/CTAs on the page.
  9. Because the business is fully online (not local), benchmark against whoever ranks nationally for the exact keyword, not city-specific competitors.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- **Never block AI crawlers, even when they're overwhelming your server.** Matt: "I firmly believe we should not be blocking AI from crawling our sites because how else are they going to recommend it?" Cloudflare has a one-click "block AI bots" toggle — don't use it.
- **If AI-crawl volume is crashing your hosting, fix it with better hosting + a WAF (Web Application Firewall), not by blocking bots.** Carolina had 100,000+ bots (legitimate crawlers — Apple, Meta, etc., not attackers) hit one site simultaneously after adding FAQ schema; it crashed shared hosting for all 4 of her sites at once (~$300/year total hosting cost). Cloudflare's AI-bot-block setting was already on and didn't prevent the crash.
- **Hosting brand matters less than plan tier** — "depends on your hosting plan" (re: Siteground); the actual fix is moving from a shared plan to cloud/VPS-tier hosting. Matt pays ~$30/month via Crockweb.com (cloud hosting) for his own hey Tony site.
- **If bots are already flooding/indexing your site, feed them more, don't throttle them** — publish 2x/week rather than trying to slow the crawl down.
- **Google's AI crawl-depth limit:** Google used to let AI crawl the top 100 search results; now AI can only see the top 10 — called a "massive change."
- **Google's share of search (Jennifer's own estimate, not verified/Matt-confirmed):** ~92-95% of all searches still happen on Google.
- **Don't fully automate content creation or posting, even with agentic AI.** Even members manually writing Monday-session blog posts still edit their AI drafts before publishing. Platforms can detect automated content: YouTube has announced ("a memo") that AI-made videos get labeled as AI-generated and can detect this without any disclosure from the creator; Google and Facebook can also "tell" when things are automated.
- **Instead of automating, spend ~1 hour/day being intentional about content** (e.g., Facebook posts) — use AI to brainstorm, rewrite, or generate images, but not to generate final video content wholesale. Every platform is pushing video over image/carousel content, and real video can't be fully AI-automated.
- **Engagement doesn't determine whether an AI-visibility test post is "worth it"** — per a podcast Jennifer watched (two unnamed SEO people), it doesn't matter whether people engage with a public post, and comments reportedly don't get pulled into AI systems at all, so negative comments on a public test post don't hurt.
- **Only public-facing content counts for this kind of AI indexing** — public Facebook groups and public posts qualify; personal/private profiles reportedly don't.
- **Brand visibility beyond your own site is increasingly weighted:** press releases and posting specifically on X, Facebook, and LinkedIn were called out (per the podcast) as "massive in terms of helping the AI understand what is going on on your site."
- **Toggles/accordions/hidden text:** Matt's current belief is that hidden-behind-a-toggle content is seen but probably weighted less effectively by AI/search. He's actively moving his own site away from heavy toggle/menu use, though he says it's "fine in some cases, but not in every case."
- **Insiders' free blog-writing tool intentionally runs at roughly 1/4 the processing power of the agency's client-facing Content Engine** — a deliberate resourcing decision, since agency clients fund the extra compute. Matt says he'll release more capability to Insiders "as things get better."
- **Don't launch a rebuilt site with only some pages live if the old URLs matched** — Google reads missing pages as deleted content, worse than shipping a full (even unoptimized) duplicate.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **AI-bot traffic is simultaneously a hosting liability and an AI-visibility asset** — Carolina's site crashed from bot load, but Matt's advice was to absorb the cost (better hosting/WAF) and even publish more, rather than block bots, because blocking directly trades away AI recommendation potential.
- **Page length/comprehensiveness isn't automatically an advantage** — Caroline's 2,000-word page underperformed a simpler 896-word competitor page for the same keyword; more content and more options can reduce both rankings and conversion.
- **Full AI automation of content/posting is technically possible now but explicitly discouraged** — the tradeoff is detection risk (platforms increasingly label/flag AI-made content) and authenticity loss, weighed against time savings; Matt's own team still manually edits AI-assisted drafts rather than publishing them raw.
- **Whether Google or the AI platforms "win" long-term is unresolved and framed as personal opinion, not settled fact** — Jennifer argues Google wins because of its indexing head start and crawl-access gatekeeping (top-10 only, down from top-100); Matt doesn't confirm or dispute this outright on the call.
- **The Insiders' free content tool is intentionally capped below the agency-grade version** — an explicit business tradeoff (client revenue funds the extra compute) rather than a technical limitation, with Matt committing to raise the ceiling "as things get better."
- **Toggled/hidden content is a genuine open question even on Matt's own site** — he suspects it under-performs for AI visibility but stops short of a hard rule ("fine in some cases, not every case"), while actively moving his own site away from heavy toggle usage.
- **CJ's brand-mention test is being run as a single-variable, uncontrolled experiment, not validated methodology** — Matt deliberately restricts it to one channel (Facebook group only) specifically because posting across X/LinkedIn/Facebook simultaneously would make it impossible to isolate which channel, if any, caused a result.
