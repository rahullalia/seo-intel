# Web Design for SEO — 24.06.2026 (Leanne Jones, Leanne Digital)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- Design before development: wireframe first (Figma, Photoshop, Canva, or even grid paper) — gets tweaks out of the way before you're fighting the page builder, produces a happier end result and less constant re-tweaking live.
- Workflow order: **copy first, then layout** — copy makes the page structure obvious once it exists.
- **Google Ads landing page structure** (built for high-intent, already-searching traffic):
  1. Hero: one clear CTA + contact form as close to the top as possible — zero scrolling required to convert.
  2. Trust indicators row (pick ~3): years of experience, number of happy clients, licensed/insured, 5-star reviews.
  3. "Why choose us" section + its own CTA.
  4. Reduce-anxiety callout section (reassurance blurb, also breaks up the visual layout).
  5. Services/offerings grid — overview of everything you cover.
  6. Testimonials — Google reviews widget embed, pasted-in favorite reviews, video testimonials, or screenshots.
  7. Process section — plain numbered steps of what happens next (removes uncertainty) + CTA.
  8. FAQ section — handle objections directly.
  9. Final CTA at the very bottom — repeat the hero's trust indicators again here as a second reassurance pass.
  10. Small "we do respond" trust line near the bottom, e.g. "we respond within 24 hours," "a real person will review this," or personalize it ("Amanda will review this and get back to you").
- **Funnel / high-ticket landing page structure** (built for cold/emotional-decision traffic):
  1. Hero: hook the specific problem immediately (frustrated / stuck / overwhelmed) + video or product image. Video is common (real-estate-course example: hook = "make money without quitting your job").
  2. Empathize section — "I've been there too" personal framing.
  3. Name the specific challenges/obstacles the audience faces.
  4. Explain your method/process for solving it (numbered steps).
  5. Outline exactly what's included in the offer.
  6. CTA at every section; use jump-links so mid-page CTAs scroll straight down to the form/checkout (or route straight to checkout if the purchase doesn't need a live conversation).
  7. Success stories — video + testimonials, heavy volume ("as many testimonials as you can" — deliberately excessive vs. a standard LP).
  8. "Who is this for" section — filters good leads in, bad leads out; lets the reader self-identify ("yeah, I'm ready for change").
  9. "Meet the founder" — personality/trust section; needed for service/course offers, skippable for pure product offers.
  10. FAQ section.
  11. Final CTA appealing to emotion ("you don't have to figure this out alone") + contact form.
- Every page on a site — not just landing/funnel pages — should end with a final CTA; think about what a visitor who scrolls to the bottom should do next and make it obvious.
- Service-page IA split: build an **overview service page** (lists all services, exists mainly for navigation into the individual pages) plus **individual service pages** (this is where you go hard on SEO for that specific service).
- Landing pages built for paid traffic: strip the navigation down to just a logo linking home — no full nav, no secondary CTA competing in the nav bar.
- Never put a paid landing page in the site's navigation; treat it as intentionally hard to browse into.
- Don't chase on-page SEO structure (H1/H2 keyword density) on paid landing pages — traffic is bought, not organic, so it isn't needed; save that structure for core/organic pages.
- URL/slug rule: put the page's focus keyword in the URL — applies in any language (confirmed for a Thai-language site) — it's a positive single ranking signal.
- H1 rule: focus keyword must be in the H1; pad it with extra natural words so it doesn't read as pure keyword-stuffing (can ask ChatGPT: give me a headline using this focus keyword).
- Color-pop tactic for a CTA button: pick the CTA color and the background color from **opposite sides of the color wheel** (complementary), but mute the background rather than using full saturation on both — full-bright-on-full-bright is jarring; muted-background + saturated-CTA lets the CTA pop and clarifies "what to do next."
- Bounce-rate triage checklist (used live on a call): (1) is the hero too visually busy — blurry background photo competing with foreground images/text; (2) is the page loading slow; (3) does the branding/vibe mismatch what the visitor expected from the ad/referral; (4) does the CTA button visually blend into the background hue. Fix path tested: strip to a simpler/plainer background, apply the color-wheel-opposite trick, and rewrite the headline to hit an emotional/social angle instead of a generic action label.
- Multi-location + multi-service local business (12 locations x 2 services): don't build the full cross-product of location-service landing pages — structure as **location-first pages**, and cover all services within each location page; keep separate general/overview service pages at the site level too.
- E-commerce without paid traffic yet and only a simple catalog: a dedicated landing/funnel page isn't necessary until there's a reason for focused traffic — a high-ticket item, a themed sale/blowout, or a single specific product being advertised.
- When the actual purchase step lives off-platform (e.g., Gumroad checkout), still build a full trust-building landing page on your own site (bio/testimonial on why you made the product) and route every on-site CTA out to the external checkout — keep the external platform to literally just the payment step, don't rebuild the pitch there.
- Multi-pricing-model service business (hourly / project / retainer): pick ONE model to sell per landing page rather than splitting into three; frame the value prop as "the expertise of a full-time hire without the full-time-hire cost," then lay out retainer benefits, then close with the same call-or-form CTA pattern used elsewhere.
- Testimonials placement: no single dedicated page required — sprinkle them contextually on the homepage AND on the specific service pages they're relevant to; a standalone reviews page can exist in addition, not instead.
- Squarespace H1 constraint: heading style/size is locked globally by the template and can't be resized per-instance. Practical fix: accept the large H1 as correct (H1s should be big/impactful anyway), just verify it isn't visually larger than the page's separate "title" style element; keep the focus keyword inside it regardless of size.
- Venue/personal-story site: place the origin story directly on the homepage near/after the hero photo, written emotionally ("it all started when…"), and rename a generic "services" section to "key features" for a venue-type offering.
- Homepage vs. landing page framing: homepage should make a strong first impression and introduce the brand/vibe for casual, colder-intent visitors; a Google Ads landing page should be built to sell hard because that visitor already has buying intent.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- Exactly one CTA color per page, chosen to contrast against the rest of the site's palette — don't offer multiple equal-weight CTAs, or visitors freeze and pick none.
- Show 3 trust indicators in the hero, drawn from: years of experience / number of happy clients / licensed-insured / star rating.
- Every section of a landing page gets its own CTA.
- Prefer screenshots and slightly unpolished proof over over-polished imagery — screenshots read as "more real," especially now that so much content is visibly AI/"vibe coded."
- Response-time copy should be a specific number ("24 hours"), and ideally personalized with a name so it feels human rather than automated.
- Paid landing pages: no nav, no listing in site navigation, no chasing organic on-page SEO, and it is fine — expected, even — if an SEO tool flags the page as "orphaned"; consider noindexing these pages.
- Focus keyword goes in both the URL slug and the H1, in whatever language the audience uses.
- Avoid literal keyword-stuffing in the H1 — wrap the keyword in natural surrounding words.
- Color pairing for CTA visibility: complementary (opposite-wheel) hues, background muted, CTA saturated — never both fully saturated.
- Every page on the site (not only landing pages) needs a final CTA at the bottom.
- Rough high-ticket threshold for "needs a funnel page": priced like a significant course, or something people won't buy on a whim (a "luxury car" or "a course that's like a grand" was the comparison used) — a £450 bundle was judged as probably below that bar.
- If checkout is off-platform, don't rebuild your full sales page a second time on the external platform — keep that side to just the transaction.

- **David** — sells PR/marketing/advertising/communications services under three possible models (hourly, project-based, retainer). Advised to pick one model per landing page, position around "expert without the full-time-hire cost," never list the page in nav, and expect/accept an "orphan page" SEO warning.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- The theoretically "ideal" location x service landing-page matrix (12 locations x 2 services) is explicitly acknowledged as "a lot of work" — Leanne recommends the simpler location-first structure as the practical compromise, not the gold-standard version.
- Whether any business needs a funnel/landing page at all is price- and traffic-dependent, not a blanket rule: a small, low-price, no-ad-spend Shopify store may not need one yet, while a course or high-ticket item almost always benefits from one.
- Homepage vs. landing page is a matched-to-intent decision, not just a different template: hard-selling on a homepage to cold/casual traffic risks giving visitors "the ick," while a soft-sell on a paid landing page (traffic that's already high-intent) leaves conversions on the table.
- Squarespace's global H1 lock is treated as a real platform limitation to work around (accept the size, watch the relative hierarchy), not a reason to switch platforms.
- Landing pages deliberately break normal SEO/IA hygiene (no nav placement, no internal inlinks, "orphan page" warnings, possible noindex) — this is a conscious tradeoff of SEO citizenship in favor of paid-conversion focus, not an oversight to fix.
- Full on-site checkout is preferable for conversion ("probably would help with your conversions") over an off-platform checkout like Gumroad, but when the platform constraint exists, the fallback is to keep the trust-building on-site and accept one extra click to the external payment page.
- Testimonial placement has no single right answer — the nuance is contextual sprinkling (homepage + relevant service pages) rather than committing to one location.
- Diagnosing a high-bounce landing page required weighing several plausible causes at once (branding mismatch, load speed, visual clutter/low contrast) rather than one fixed diagnosis, and Sam's own prior A/B test (moving "featured on" to the top) had already produced a counter-intuitive result — a reminder that testing intuition doesn't always predict outcomes.
