## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Press-release-to-AI-results tactic (AB Newswire)**:
  1. Buy a press release through AB Newswire (Matt bought the ~$80 single-release tier, then a bulk package around $901 to test multiple releases, bringing the effective cost down to roughly $8/release).
  2. Link the press release to your best/target existing page (Matt linked his to his ranking blog post and to his Amazon book listing).
  3. You don't need real "news" to justify one — a review or routine business update works: e.g., "[Company] receives raving review," then talk about the review and what you do.
  4. Outcome: even one press release can get you referenced inside Google AI Overviews/AI results for a broad, generic term, not just your brand name.
- **Answer the Public keyword workflow (reiterated)**:
  1. Type in what you sell/do, click "search engines" (left side), then "questions."
  2. Skip ahead to later results pages (Matt went to page 4) — competitors are less likely to have already targeted those questions, so they're easier to rank for.
  3. Pick a question you can actually answer from your own expertise.
  4. If a narrow/niche long-tail term returns nothing useful, go broader instead (e.g., plain "SEO" rather than "marketing for service businesses") to surface a wider pool of usable keywords — only get specific if the specific version still returns real results.
  5. Scroll further down the results page — there's a second, less-obvious set of questions sourced from Bing (most people miss this and only look at the Google-sourced set).
- **ChatGPT voice-mode interview-to-blog-post workflow**:
  1. Open a blank doc/chat (Matt stopped screen-sharing first to avoid exposing client data).
  2. Paste the interview prompt (see Tools section) with your chosen question/topic inserted.
  3. ChatGPT returns up to 10 clarifying questions.
  4. Switch to ChatGPT's voice mode (click the voice icon, not "dictate") and answer the 10 questions out loud, one at a time — it remembers the whole conversation.
  5. Once done, prompt it to write the full blog post from your answers.
  6. Prompt it separately to generate a featured image using previously attached example images as the style reference (exact prompt in Tools section) — title text not required on the image.
  7. Go back and rewrite/re-prompt for structure: force the direct-answer to appear in the first paragraph, and repeat the keyword/question near the top more than once.
  8. Prompt for a "key takeaways" section at the end — 4-5 bullet points covering everything.
  9. Never skip internal links before publishing.
- **Direct-answer-first structure (for AEO/LLM citation)**: restate the question as a subheading/lead-in, then answer it immediately and plainly right after. Matt's live-drafted example: question "Can ChatGPT do SEO?" answered immediately with "Yes, ChatGPT can help with SEO, but it can't do it on its own." Reasoning: LLMs/AI Overviews are scanning for direct Q→A pairs, so put the pairing at the very top and repeat the keyword.
- **TL;DR placement rule of thumb**: only add a top-of-post TL;DR for longer pieces of content; if the direct answer is already in paragraph one, a separate TL;DR isn't necessary. Key takeaways at the end are worth doing regardless of length.
- Long-tail keywords don't have to be phrased as a question — a plain long-tail statement (e.g., "how long can I keep my LED lights on for" or a "top 10 X" list format) works just as well as a question-format post.
- **Fixing a split-blog-domain setup**: if your blog currently lives on a different domain/subdomain than your main site (e.g., Wix blog + Shopify store), consolidate it onto the same domain as the main site — don't keep it separate for formatting convenience.
- **Friday "Why Doesn't It Rank" hot-seat method**: pick an underperforming page, then directly compare it to whoever currently ranks for that query — check their backlink count, how often/where they use the target keyword, whether their content is simply better, whether they use a top summary, whether they use key takeaways at the bottom, and how many internal links point to their page.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Backlinks: you only need **ONE link from one domain** — additional links from that same domain lose value/diminishing returns. This directly overturns advice Samantha had previously received (that a separate blog domain would generate valuable backlinks to the main site).
- Blog content should live on the **same domain** as the main site, even when the native blog editor is worse to work with (Shopify's "Blogs"/"Posts" section has limited image/formatting control) than an external tool (Wix) — migrate anyway.
- FAQs: cap at **3-4 questions maximum** if used at all.
- Zero or negative search-volume numbers on a keyword tool are not a reason to skip a question — negative/zero just means "as low as the tool goes," not "don't write this."
- **~15% of all searches every day have never been searched before** (brand new queries) — zero historical volume doesn't mean zero future volume.
- **Bing carries ~4% of global search volume** — not negligible. ChatGPT pulls from **both Google and Bing**, so Bing-sourced questions (visible further down Answer the Public's results) are worth targeting too.
- Press releases officially aren't supposed to use superlative language like "best" — Matt broke this rule and it still worked.
- AB Newswire pricing: **~$80** for a single release; a bulk package (**~$901**) brings the effective cost down to **~$8/release**.
- PR Newswire (alternative, pricier service) requires requesting a demo rather than self-serve checkout — flagged as "not ideal."
- Always add internal links to a new post before publishing — "never forget to do your internal links."
- Use your own judgment on the press-release AI-manipulation tactic: Matt doesn't believe it will harm a site long-term the way buying spammy backlinks would, but he isn't depending on it to rank and doesn't know if it will keep working.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- The press-release AI-results tactic works right now and produced real pickup (USA Today, briefly Globe and Mail, AI Overviews for a generic term), but Matt explicitly frames it as unproven long-term ("is it going to work forever? Maybe, maybe not") and tells members to use their "own will" before trying it — while also saying he doesn't think it risks harming a site the way spammy paid backlinks would.
- Publicizing a growth-hack can backfire on itself: Edward Sturm's Instagram exposure of the tactic drew enough scrutiny that Globe and Mail pulled the referenced article — the exposure was itself what neutralized part of the win.
- Broad vs. specific seed terms on Answer the Public: default to broad for more usable keyword variety; only narrow the search if the narrower version still returns real results.
- TL;DR vs. key-takeaways vs. neither is still an open experiment for Matt personally (he's testing combinations) — his interim stance: key takeaways at the end are worth doing regardless of post length; a top-of-post TL;DR is only worth adding for longer content where paragraph one's direct answer doesn't already cover it.
- FAQs vs. TL;DR/key-takeaways: Matt personally avoids FAQs in blog posts (dislikes the toggle/accordion UX), reserving them, if used at all, for landing pages and capped at 3-4 questions — flagged explicitly as personal preference, not a hard rule ("your mileage may vary").
- Blog domain consolidation (Samantha's case): the SEO-correct move (merge onto the main domain) trades against real practical friction — the native platform editor (Shopify) offers less formatting/image control than the external tool (Wix) that prompted the original split; Matt acknowledges the downside but still recommends migrating, deferring the "how" to later in the call.
