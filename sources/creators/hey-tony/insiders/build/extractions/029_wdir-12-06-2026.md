## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Posting a press release win on social:** frame it as "why we started this business" personal narrative (not a bare announcement), post to Facebook, ask friends/family to actually share it (not just like) — Jason got ~10x his normal views this way (2,000 vs. his usual ~50).
- **Tracking your own press-release pickup:** don't wait for AB Newswire's report email (may not arrive or lands in spam) — just Google your own name/business directly to see which sites picked it up.
- **Use a never-before-used photo in the press release** so that whenever you spot that exact image surfacing elsewhere (Google Images, other sites), you know it's a press-release pickup, not organic mention (Jason's tactic).
- **Check exact keyword phrasing variants**: Google is sensitive to "stand-up" vs. "standup" vs. "stand up" as separate strings — audit your site copy for the exact phrasing searchers use and add missing variants (Maritza's case).
- **Deliberately target low-volume/low-competition long-tail keywords to "own" a niche term** rather than chasing high-volume broad terms — Laura's approach with "brand storyteller staffing" (a low-search-volume term she now ranks #1–3 for).
- **After a press release goes out, proactively pitch local media directly** — don't expect them to find it on their own:
  1. Go to the local publication's website.
  2. Find their contact form / contact page, and look specifically for a press or media contact.
  3. Reach out directly: "I was featured in [USA Today / publication] — I'm local to you, would love to chat, do an interview, or have you repost this."
  4. Use Claude or ChatGPT to draft the outreach messaging if unsure what to say.
- **Wait ~1 month before re-editing a page after publishing or updating it** — don't touch it again right after a launch/update even if it's not ranking yet or dips slightly (see Rules below for the mechanism).
- **If you're getting sales from a third-party platform (e.g., Etsy) but not from your own site's SEO:** run a press release aimed specifically at that platform/niche instead of just optimizing your own website — e.g., title it something like "Funny tennis T-shirts on Etsy" (suggested to Yorick/Jorg).
- **When leads/traffic spike hard, double down on marketing** rather than pulling back to handle fulfillment — hire/delegate admin instead of cutting content output (Tatiana's move: hired help to keep writing/shooting instead of slowing down).
- **Fixing a spam-damaged main domain that's dragging down a subdomain** (Nicole's case — old web designer had added a community/forum feature that got flooded with bot-posted spam backlinks):
  - Option A: leave the main site as-is, wait a couple of months, see if rankings/authority recover once the spam signals age out.
  - Option B: register a brand-new domain, migrate over the good content (not the spam), and abandon/dissolve the old domain.
  - Either way: keep blogging 1–2x/week, keep working on backlinks, do a press release ("we're top-rated" / reviews angle) to try to "light a fire," and show up to Monday calls for accountability.
  - Matt's rule of thumb: "unless you're in love with your domain, buy a different domain and switch things over."
- **Building a simple one-page reviews microsite** (Marisa's `marisabrusoloreviews.com`):
  - One page only, plain/simple design (white or cream background, black text) — doesn't need to match brand or look polished.
  - Reviews must be **written out as text**, not posted as screenshot/JPEG images.
  - Workflow: drop the review screenshots into Claude/Claude Code and instruct it to build the page with the review content transcribed as real text, not images.
- **Pitching local magazines after a press release** (Natalie's question): send them the press release itself and say "I was mentioned in [USA Today/publication], I'm local to you, would love to have a conversation" — use Claude/ChatGPT to help write or fully draft the message.
- **Canonical-link troubleshooting on Squarespace** (Mark's homepage canonical/trailing-slash issue, unresolved for 2-3 months): routed to Denroy (the community's technical SEO specialist) rather than solved live on this call.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- **Schema markup**: Matt does not believe custom schema markup has any ranking/AI impact beyond the default schema that Wix/WordPress/Shopify auto-generate. He does not implement custom schema for any client, at all.
- **Google's "freshness gaming" patent/algorithm behavior**: when a page is newly published or updated, Google will sometimes purposely suppress ("tank") its rankings temporarily, then wait a few days and recheck whether the publisher panics and makes a flurry of changes — that panic-editing pattern is itself treated as a spam/gaming signal. Rule: once a page is live, give it **about a month** before you go back and re-update it.
- **Don't knee-jerk react to ranking dips within days or a week of an edit** — that's expected noise, not a verdict on the change.
- **Never cut marketing** — not when business is bad (obvious reason not to), and not when business is good either ("we don't need to spend more time/money on marketing" is exactly backwards). When things are working, double down.
- **Google/bot crawl depth**: Google used to let bots crawl up to the first 100 results; now bots (including tools like Semrush) can only see/crawl the **top 10** results.
- **How AI actually finds content to recommend**: AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, etc.) don't independently crawl the whole internet — they run searches against Google/Bing and pull from those existing results, then synthesize an answer from ~5-10 of those searches. Matt's standing claim, reinforced by a podcast debate he watched (Edward Sturm's show, guests David Quaid vs. "Zach"): "If you rank on Google, you will get recommended by AI."
- **AI-visibility scoring tools (Semrush etc.) are not measuring what AI actually looks at**: these tools use AI to generate hypothetical search prompts, then use AI again to crawl and check if you show up in the answers — a self-referential, "backward" system, not a real signal of anything AI itself weighs.
- **Google/AI care about niche authority, not SEO polish**: don't optimize for "properly optimized, perfect" technical sites — Google is trying to surface genuine experts/authorities in a niche, not the most technically-optimized page.
- **Low-volume long-tail keywords are worth deliberately targeting** even at just a few clicks/month, because searcher intent (ready to hire/buy) is much higher than on broad terms — most competitors ignore these because they chase volume instead.
- **A main domain's authority governs every subdomain attached to it** — a spam-damaged or low-authority root domain will drag down an otherwise clean subdomain built on it.
- **Recovering from embedded spam (bot-posted backlinks via a forum/community feature)** can take **6–12 months** to fully clear from Google's index/algorithm even after the spam source is removed.
- **Google Search Console will soon show AI/generative-search performance data directly** (not yet live) — until then there's no reliable first-party way to see your AI-answer visibility.
- **AB Newswire distributes only into its own signed-up network of sites** — small local/regional newspapers are typically NOT in that network, so pickup by them requires direct manual outreach, not passive distribution.
- **A press release's value doesn't depend on it looking polished** — "it looks a little cheesy" is fine; "AI doesn't care what it looks like," and neither should you if it's driving results.
- **Backlink asks inside the community must stay topically relevant** — e.g., a comedian's site should not link to a nanny-vetting platform; only link where it genuinely makes sense for both parties' niches.
- Press releases serve **three distinct purposes**: (1) leverage for direct local-media outreach you couldn't otherwise get, (2) AI Overview/answer-engine visibility (gets treated as "in the news," reinforcing relevance), (3) shareable social-proof content that lets you talk yourself up without sounding self-promotional (a publication calling you "the best" lands differently than you saying it).

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **"Incognito" search testing isn't a neutral baseline** — Maritza and a friend 45 minutes away saw meaningfully different rankings for the identical query, both in incognito; location still skews results even when personalization/history is supposedly excluded.
- **Aesthetic quality of a press release or social post doesn't predict performance** — a "cheesy"-looking press release or a "shitty looking" social post can still drive outsized results; judge by outcome, not appearance.
- **Custom schema markup correlated with AI Overview appearances in Laura's case, but Matt is explicit he doesn't credit schema itself** — he attributes it to her content specificity and consistency instead, leaving the actual causal driver ambiguous even in a case being used as a positive example.
- **Patience vs. instinct conflict on freshly published/updated pages**: the natural instinct when something isn't ranking is to keep tweaking it, but doing so within the first month can look like algorithm-gaming to Google and suppress rankings further — restraint is required even though it feels like inaction.
- **Subdomain risk isn't visible from the subdomain's own content** — Nicole's new nanny subdomain is topically clean, but the main domain's spam history can still drag it down purely through domain-level authority inheritance.
- **Local press pickup from a distributed press release is unlikely to happen passively** — "they probably won't see it because there's so much noise" — the real payoff requires manual, direct follow-up outreach after distribution, not the distribution alone.
- **Long-tail/conversational keyword targeting is framed as a forward-looking bet tied to voice search growth** (people talking into phones/AI instead of typing) — supported only by anecdote (a mastermind contact's voice-driven "vibe coding," a woman in her 60s wanting instant voice-based answers for her business) rather than hard data, but used to justify doubling down on long-tail/conversational content now.
- **Technical/site-health fixes (meta description length, etc.) produced a real, measurable score jump (73→93) but are positioned as secondary** to niche-authority and content-consensus signals — necessary hygiene, not sufficient alone for AI/ranking visibility.
- **Whether to fix a damaged domain or abandon it for a new one is treated as genuinely case-by-case**, not a hard rule — "wait a couple months and see" vs. "start fresh" both offered as valid, with attachment to the existing domain name as a legitimate (if non-technical) factor in the decision.
