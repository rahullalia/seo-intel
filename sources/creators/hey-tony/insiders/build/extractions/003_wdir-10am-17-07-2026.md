## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Geotagging GBP images (step-by-step)**: upload a photo to geoimgr.com ("Geo Imager") → choose the exact spot on the map you want the image tied to (doesn't have to be the literal shoot location, just plausible for your service area) → download the geotagged file → upload that file to your Google Business Profile (posts or photos).
- **Ranking a wider area with geotagging**: instead of tagging every photo to the same point, pick a "grid" of locations across your target zone and vary the geotag per photo — e.g., if the business sits on the edge of a city but wants to rank more toward the center, geotag some images to downtown, others elsewhere in the grid.
- Real photos taken on a phone with location services on are typically already geotagged in EXIF — no manual tagging needed if you can shoot real local photos.
- For stock photos: strip the existing embedded location metadata (re-save through Photoshop or Canva, even with no edits, then re-download) rather than manually faking a geotag for a location that isn't plausible for the business.
- Geotagging also works the same way natively in Instagram (tag city + business name on upload); whether that geotag carries over to help Google Business Profile ranking is unconfirmed/anecdotal.
- **Diagnosing GSC-vs-third-party-platform traffic mismatches** (Jorg's Shopify question): treat mismatches as expected — Google Search Console pulls directly from Google, Shopify counts/attributes independently. Go to GSC for the "what Google actually says" number; go to Shopify for "what Shopify says," and don't expect them to reconcile.
- **Using Pomelli** (Google's free AI marketing tool, referred to in-call as "Pomelli"/"Pumeli"/"Pimlé"): sign in with a Google account → enter your website URL → connect your Google Business Profile → tool auto-pulls images from your GBP and auto-generates ad/social campaign creative.
  - Edit any generated on-image text/copy directly, then generate a call-to-action.
  - Use the built-in photo/image generator to create new product shots by prompt.
  - Choose output format per use case: Feature, Feed, Square, or Story.
  - Pull the auto-generated **brand book** (colors/hex codes, logo variants, fonts, values, tone-of-voice descriptors) to hand to any agency/freelancer as instant brand guidelines.
  - Tool can also draft an entirely new website using your existing site's text/layout, and appears to offer publishing it (untested by Matt).
  - Ignore the post-signup confirmation-looking email from taboola.com ("Welcome to Realize") — it's unrelated spam, not part of the flow; being signed in with your Google account is sufficient, no separate email confirmation step exists.
- **Advanced ad-automation stack (Josh)**: Pomelli generates the ad creative → a Claude-prompted browser-automation extension pushes it straight into Google Ads → a second tool pulls a 4:5 crop of the same asset and auto-posts it to Meta.
- **Niching down saturated keywords via platform-specific content** (Karen's email-deliverability case): instead of competing on broad terms like "email marketing," write technical how-tos tied to specific platforms people actually search for (e.g., "how to set up DMARC/DKIM in ActiveCampaign"), then a natural follow-up post per platform (e.g., "now that your emails are delivering, here's how to raise your open rate").
- **Countering a defamatory competitor "vs" page that outranks you for your own brand name** (Josh's situation):
  1. Send a cease-and-desist.
  2. Consider trademarking your business name to restrict the competitor's use of it.
  3. Outproduce them: if they have 1 comparison article, publish roughly 10 pieces of content across different sites.
  4. There is currently no clean mechanism to get an LLM to retroactively discount or correct a false claim it's already citing as fact.
  5. Escalation option (explicitly not endorsed by Matt): publicly expose the manipulation — post content stating the competitor is "hacking Google," show his real Google Business Profile review count against his public claim, and show a business-registry/ASIC lookup proving true ownership behind a false "female-owned" framing; make a video about it since the competitor isn't making videos.
  6. Cleaner alternative (from Jason, relayed in chat): standard reputation management — keep any rebuttal professional, then bury the issue under a wave of positive content.
- **Reframing being attacked/hated on online**: don't engage publicly — check whether the criticism is actually true to who you are; if not, dismiss it as a hater. Matt's own applied example: when someone made a 30-minute video bashing one of his 30-second clips, he DM'd the critic privately, thanked him for the attention, invited him to share what's actually working for him, and proposed a collab instead of a public fight.
- **Getting a page unstuck from indexing purgatory** (CJ's fix, resolved a 3-week "can't find robots file" GSC error): wrote 2 new articles with direct backlinks into the stuck page, added 2 internal links from older articles, made ~7 total page-level changes, then requested indexing again every 2-4 days until GSC finally returned "indexing requested" instead of the robots error.
- **Justifying two similar-looking course pages on one site** (CJ's catalog vs. continuing-education pages): if two pages look duplicative on the surface, check whether they're actually targeting distinct keywords/intents (his case: "real estate continuing education" vs. "online real estate programs") — if so, keep both; the apparent overlap isn't a problem.
- **Book-launch press release structure**:
  - Announcement article title = topic keyword + "book" + author framing, e.g. "How to Build a Bookkeeping Business: Book Launched by Brand New Author [Name]" — rationale: people searching the topic keyword are also plausible searchers for a book on that topic.
  - Article body: what the book is, who it serves, where to buy it.
  - Reviews follow-up article title = topic keyword + "book" + accolade, e.g. "How to Build a Bookkeeping Business Book Gets Five-Star Reviews."
- **Amazon #1-bestseller PR play for a book launch**: check your Amazon category/micro-category ranking daily after launch (it can take only a handful of sales to hit #1 in a narrow micro-category); the moment you hit #1, screenshot it immediately since the position is often fleeting; then issue a press release announcing the #1 bestseller status and keep reinforcing that claim in follow-up content so it also feeds AI answer engines.
- **Turning YouTube/social into a second search channel**:
  1. Post the identical video across every platform (Instagram, YouTube Shorts, etc.) rather than tailoring per platform.
  2. Connect all social profiles to Google Search Console (via Settings → Properties, once the feature is live on your account).
  3. Sort GSC results by impressions to see which of your videos are surfacing in search and for what queries.
  4. Feed those winning queries back into future video topics (e.g., seeing "Google Search Console" perform as a query prompted Matt to make more content specifically about GSC).

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- Geotagging matters mainly for **local businesses** trying to rank on Google Maps/the local pack; on-site website images get little to no confirmed benefit from it ("I don't know that has like a significant impact").
- **Never falsely geotag** a stock photo to a real location it doesn't plausibly belong to (Heather's employment-lawyer example — can't geotag Toronto onto a stock photo when there's no real local shoot).
- ChatGPT's own verdict, quoted live by Heather and not disputed by Matt: geotagging is **"one of the most overrated SEO tactics"** — treat it as a minor lever, not a priority, unless local competition is genuinely tight.
- Fundamentals before/instead of geotagging: get more reviews, complete every section of the Google Business Profile, regularly add photos, keep a good website connected to the GBP.
- Time cost estimate for geotagging: **roughly 1 minute per photo**.
- Test geotagging on **1-2 clients** before rolling it out broadly; keep it if it works for that client's competitive situation.
- **Never review-gate**: don't run an NPS or 1-5 rating survey to filter sentiment before deciding whether to send someone a review link — this is explicitly review gating and against Google's policy. If caught, Google can **ban the entire Business Profile**.
- Always send review requests as a **direct, unfiltered link straight to the Google Business Profile** — no "how was your experience" pre-screen step.
- If review requests run through an automation (e.g., a GHL workflow) that pre-screens sentiment before sending the link, that also counts as review gating.
- **Avoid in-person QR-code review requests** (e.g., a table tent at a restaurant) — Google doesn't want reviews solicited while the customer is still on-site/being watched, since that biases toward artificially positive reviews; texting/emailing the link later (when the customer is home) is the Google-approved pattern and yields more honest reviews.
- Real-world risk: a customer or competitor can report an in-person QR-code review ask and get an entire **franchise account/profile shut down** — described as "very dangerous."
- Possible (unconfirmed) detection signal for review gating: Google may look at referral-source patterns — if all reviews funnel in from the exact same automated path, that's a potential tell.
- **Security**: every high-value account (domain registrar, hosting, primary email, banking) needs **2+ administrators** — never let a single person be the sole holder of credentials.
- Use a **hardware security key (YubiKey)** on important accounts; must be physically touched to complete login.
- Minimum YubiKeys to own: **2** (losing your only key locks you out of everything); Matt's own setup: **3** — one on his person at all times, one hidden in his office, one in a safe.
- Don't reuse the same email address across banking and lower-security consumer accounts (e.g., Netflix) that are more likely to be breached — that shared email becomes a single point of failure for account takeover. Framed as a risk affecting the vast majority of people ("that's like 99 of people aren't secure").
- Only create SEO/blog content targeting platforms you'd actually be willing to service — don't rank for a tool you personally dislike, since ranking there converts into leads asking specifically for that tool.
- **AI avatars/AI-cloned presenters kill trust** for personal-brand content — audience reaction is explicitly negative ("this isn't legit... this isn't what I'm following this guy for"). One participant's data point: AI-avatar-only videos cap around **12 views** before going dead; being physically in the video is required for real traction; that participant's own typical range is **1,000-2,000 views per video**, with one outlier hitting **200,000**.
- Matt's own ad spend rule: runs **paid ads only ~3 weeks**, leading up to his own Rank Week launches — everything else is organic social.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- **Geotagging is genuinely disputed even among practitioners** — Matt's agency reports "great results," ChatGPT calls it overrated, and metadata may or may not even survive Google's ingestion ("there's a lot of speculation online that Google just strips those"). Matt's real position is outcome-based, not mechanism-based: test it on a client or two rather than assume either extreme, and weight it by how tight local competition is.
- **Platform analytics will never fully reconcile** (Shopify vs. GSC) — different attribution and counting logic on each side; treat this as a permanent condition to work around, not a bug to fix, and default to GSC for the Google-specific truth.
- **Fighting a defamatory competitor is legally weak ground in some jurisdictions** — Australia's regulator won't act on something this small, and a comparison page alone isn't automatically illegal. The practical options trade aggression against professionalism: public callouts can work but Matt explicitly declines to endorse them ("I'm not condoning this... that's not, you know, above board"), while the clean reputation-management path is slower and does nothing to fix already-cited false claims in LLM answers, for which there is currently no correction mechanism at all.
- **Organic-social-to-search strategy cuts against prior advice some coaches were giving clients** — Heather flags the tension of having told clients "don't worry too much about social, focus on your website," now reversed by Matt's GSC/YouTube impressions data showing real incremental search traffic from video content.
- **AI cost/usage trajectory**: most AI is currently free or cheap because providers are subsidizing usage at a loss (Google being the exception with deep enough reserves to sustain it); expect consolidation toward whichever platforms can keep it free long-term, which will reshape which AI tools remain viable content/traffic channels.
- **Book bestseller-rank PR tactic is real but time-sensitive** — a genuine #1 in a narrow Amazon micro-category is legitimate and can take only a handful of sales, but the position is often fleeting, so the tactic's actual power comes from disciplined daily checking and immediate documentation (screenshot) rather than the ranking itself.
