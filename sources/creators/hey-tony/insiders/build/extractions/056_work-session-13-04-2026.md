## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Monday routine:** repeat Rank Week "Day 1" tasks every single Monday — the call is protected time to actually write a blog post, not just learn about it.
- **Core weekly blog workflow:**
  1. Find a real customer question via answerthepublic.com, findquestions.com, or the "Blog Idea Finder" inside app.heytonyinsiders.com — type in what you sell (e.g., "SEO") to surface questions.
  2. Feed the question into an AI (ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini) using the standard interview prompt (see Tools) to get up to 10 clarifying questions.
  3. Answer the questions via voice mode, one at a time.
  4. Take the full transcript (AI's questions + your voice answers) and hand it to a second AI to actually write the post from that material plus any needed web research.
  5. Edit the draft ~10-15%: strip em dashes, add photos/screenshots, format tables, fix internal links.
  6. Publish and track which version/site ranks over time.
- **Cross-AI relay (what several members are now doing):** use ChatGPT for the interview/voice-transcription step (it handles dictation better), then paste that entire ChatGPT conversation into Claude and have Claude do the actual writing — Claude edits more conservatively on rewrites.
- **Seasoning an AI with your business knowledge:** ask ChatGPT to "make a document that will teach any AI everything you know about my business," then paste that document into Claude as standing context before blog-writing sessions.
- **A/B testing AI writers against each other (Matt's own test):** write the same target post with two different AIs, publish one version on your main site and the other on a secondary/unused site, then compare which one actually ranks.
- **Local page structure:** build one page per city/location you want to rank in; put the service + location in the first line of that page's description. Peter reports ranking #1 for a city page within about two weeks doing this.
- **GBP posting:** it's fine to post the same content you post on social media directly to your Google Business Profile; consider adding your city name into each post's caption (Peter's approach — deferred to the community post for full detail).
- **Backlink acquisition, in order of effort:**
  1. Run a backlink gap analysis (Hey Keywords, SEMrush, or Ahrefs) to see which links competitors have that you don't — many turn out to be free directories or guest-post-friendly sites.
  2. Pitch guest posts on those identified sites (often paid, sometimes free).
  3. Once organic traffic grows, links start arriving naturally, and link-outreach specialists start emailing you — reply and negotiate a reciprocal link ("I'll link to you, but I need a backlink in return").
- **Video repurposing order of operations:** don't start from an existing product video and try to turn it into a blog post. Start with the keyword/question, write the blog post first, then attach existing relevant videos as supplemental content underneath (works well on brand/product pages).
- **Matt's own multi-platform posting flow:** post video to Instagram first (auto-cascades to Threads and Facebook), then manually post the same video to LinkedIn, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat, and Blue Sky; carousels/photos stay Instagram-specific; the same caption is copy-pasted everywhere. Not currently posting to X ("probably should").
- **Snapchat-specific tactic:** post video content to Snapchat "Spotlight" (the discovery feed), not just your story/followers.
- **Per-platform rewriting:** instead of posting identical written copy everywhere, run the blog content back through the blog tool to regenerate it "in a different voice" for each platform (LinkedIn post vs. Facebook post vs. Google Business post).

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Blog **writing** itself should take ~30-45 minutes; images, formatting, and tables are what actually eat the time.
- No real "don'ts" for blog structure beyond **keyword stuffing** — bullet points, tables, videos, and images are all fine to use.
- Each page should target **one specific keyword**; keyword variations can still be logged in the Rank Week audit spreadsheet for that page.
- Keyword difficulty **20-30** is "definitely achievable," just may take longer to rank.
- A Blog Idea Finder question does **not** need to be location-specific unless the query itself is inherently about doing something in a city.
- **Domain rule:** if a domain already has backlinks, is ranking, or is getting traffic, don't change it. Google doesn't weight the domain/business name itself much — it reads the content and what's on the site.
- You **can** safely rename a Google Business Profile (e.g., add "Bookkeeping" to a bookkeeping business's name) without starting a new profile — Google may resist the change request, but it should go through eventually. Do this especially when every local competitor has the service word in their GBP name and you don't.
- Don't worry about "bad"/spammy backlinks flagged by checker tools — most accounts won't actually have harmful ones, and even confirmed ones rarely need action unless truly egregious (Matt's example: a porn site linking to you).
- **Never buy backlinks from Fiverr** or similar cheap sellers; ignore unsolicited emails selling backlinks.
- Matt has **never disavowed a backlink** — disavowing "can do more harm than good."
- Guest post pricing benchmark: roughly **$20-50** typically charged by a site to allow a guest post; sometimes free.
- HARO ("Help A Reporter Out") still works for backlinks but is now heavily saturated with SEO practitioners — unclear ROI.
- Blog cadence: keep writing **1-2 posts/week indefinitely** ("forever ideally") until revenue/traffic justifies hiring in-house or an agency to take it over — there's no natural end point.
- Topics never run out: a broad seed term in the questions tool returns **40+ questions**, and a different term yields 40 more; Matt's agency writes **8 blog posts/month per client** without running dry.
- Always cut/edit **em dashes** out of AI drafts — treated as a visible "I used AI" tell.
- The blog-writer tool is supposed to auto-include links to your own site in every post; if it stops doing that, it's a bug (Jessica hit this: 3 posts generated with zero links).
- Don't post the same video 1:1 from Instagram straight to YouTube — it needs to be reformatted differently for that platform.
- If a video isn't tied to a keyword/question target, turning it into a blog post "won't do much" for SEO.
- If a location+keyword search shows no map pack and one competitor visually dominates it, you can't compete via GBP/map pack there — you have to out-rank them organically on your website instead.

## Tools, prompts & resources

- **answerthepublic.com** — type what you sell (e.g. "SEO") to surface customer questions; increasingly paywalled (~$20/month cited by a member), possibly from heavy free-tier use by this community.
- **findquestions.com** — free alternative; gives 3 instant questions, then gates further results behind an email capture (lead magnet).
- **app.heytonyinsiders.com → "Blog Idea Finder"** — included with Insiders membership; log in with the same email used for Insiders, but must create a new password to activate the account.
- **Rank Week replays** — on the Insiders homepage, scroll down past login to find them among the other call replays.
- **Core interview prompt** (as given, paste into ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini after finding a question): "I'm writing a blog post answering this question: [question]. Ask me anything you need to know the answer to in order to write a blog post that is truly helpful and unique. It should include my experience, expertise, case studies, examples, opinions, etc. Keep it limited to 10 questions, and ask me them one at a time. I'll be using voice mode to answer them. Give me time to breathe as well before going to the next question."
- **Relay-into-second-AI prompt** (Carol's, substance verbatim): "Here's my chat with ChatGPT about writing a blog post. There are questions it asked me and my transcript I did in voice mode to answer. Please write me a blog post using this information plus whatever searches you need to do online to get the most accurate information."
- **Refinement instruction that worked despite imprecise wording:** "Can the first sentence be an answer to the question in the title tag?" (she meant H1 heading) — Claude inferred the correct meaning anyway and produced a notably better result.
- **Business-knowledge seeding prompt for ChatGPT:** "Make me a document that will teach any AI everything you know about my business" — then paste that doc into Claude for context.
- **heykeywords.com** — Matt's own tool; used for backlink gap analysis and as the live test site for his "cheapest keyword research tool" blog post (his own tool is the stated #1 answer, disclosed transparently in the post).
- **SEMrush** and **Ahrefs** — alternative backlink gap analysis tools.
- **HOTH.com** (H-O-T-H) — paid backlink service Matt has "used... for years"; "their backlinks are good, but they're not cheap."
- **HARO** — for backlink acquisition; oversaturated by SEO people currently.
- **GMB Everywhere** — Chrome extension for local SEO/GBP auditing, recommended by a community member (Muhammad); Matt hasn't personally used it but says "probably safe," suggests checking reviews first.
- **Picoto/Peakato** (spelled aloud "P-E-A-K-A-T-O") — AI image/video cataloging tool: upload external hard drives, it indexes photos and videos into a searchable database (e.g., search "purple shirts"), stores small thumbnails rather than full files, and indexes video clips too. Not a sponsor/affiliate mention. Too slow to be useful at ~500,000-600,000 images (Todd's test) — better suited to smaller libraries.
- **Voice mode** dictation is central to the whole workflow — used to answer the AI's clarifying questions naturally instead of typing.
- Matt's own posting stack: Instagram, Threads, Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube (Shorts + long-form), Snapchat (incl. Spotlight), Blue Sky; not currently on X.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **ChatGPT vs. Claude:** ChatGPT currently handles voice/dictation transcription better; Claude makes far more minimal edits when revising a draft, whereas ChatGPT tends to over-rewrite ("making a lot of changes" per Ariel). Several members (Carol, Ariel) now split the workflow — ChatGPT for the Q&A/transcription, Claude for the actual writing.
- **Em-dash suppression:** ChatGPT eventually "remembered" to permanently stop using em dashes once corrected; Claude keeps forgetting between sessions unless the rule is reinforced or set as a standing profile/custom instruction. (A member, Nick, separately commented in chat that ChatGPT was "worse than Claude" — exact dimension he meant wasn't specified.)
- **Backlink-checker false positives:** tools may label backlinks as toxic/damaging when they aren't — treat those warnings skeptically rather than acting on them; disavowing is framed as the riskier move.
- **Guest post cost varies site to site** — sometimes free, sometimes ~$20-50 — no fixed rule; multiple members' questions on this signal real uncertainty in the community.
- **HARO:** legitimate backlink channel in principle, but so many SEOs use it now that the effort-to-result ratio is unclear.
- **Map pack vs. organic:** when a single competitor visually dominates a location+keyword query with no visible map pack, that's a signal you're now competing purely organically/on-page, not via GBP — different tactics apply. Reporting an inaccurate competitor GBP business name to Google reportedly didn't produce any visible action (Peter's experience).
- **Picoto/Peakato** is valuable for photo/video cataloging but not a universal fit — impractical at very large (500k+) library sizes; better suited to smaller asset collections.
- **Video repurposing** only pays off SEO-wise when tied to an actual keyword target; otherwise it's a nice-to-have for brand/product pages, not a ranking driver.
- **Cross-posting** is largely "same everywhere" (captions, video files) except Instagram-to-YouTube, which needs reformatting, and except platform-native written posts (LinkedIn/Facebook/GBP), which Matt deliberately rewrites in a different voice per platform rather than reusing verbatim.
- **GBP renaming:** Google may resist a business-name change request even when justified, so expect friction/lag even though the change is ultimately allowed.
