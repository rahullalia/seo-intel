# WDIR 11AM 26.06.2026 — Knowledge Digest

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Checking keyword rankings in GSC**: Search Console → Search Results → by default shows only clicks/impressions → click "Position" on the graph to add average position → scroll down to the query table. Click a specific query row to filter, then click a specific page to see that page's position for that exact query (Matt's own example: "hey Tony" ranked across 61 different pages at wildly different positions — 11.9, 21.2, 45.4 — but his homepage alone sat at 1.4 for that query).
- **GSC "Insights" report** (new this year per Matt): shows clicks trending up, impressions, top pages by clicks, and pages trending up/down — use it to catch pages losing traffic and decide whether to update them.
- **Auditing your own top pages for problems**: sort GSC by top pages/clicks with no filters — Matt found his own unfinished "author page" (just said "Matt D.", not formatted) was pulling 167 clicks and needs fixing. Do this pass on your own site.
- **H1 audit workflow**: go to the page → open the Page Audit extension → check the "title tag" field → check "headings" for the H1 → compare to the keyword you actually want to rank for → rewrite so the H1 contains the target keyword in natural, readable English (not "gobbledygook").
- **Fixing a GHL orphaned blog page** (duplicate blog setups): clone the "Create Blog Site" version of the page, change its URL to the intended new URL, then delete the old orphaned page.
- **Fixing individual attachment/media pages getting indexed (WordPress + Yoast)**: Dashboard → Yoast SEO → Advanced → Media Pages → toggle off the "redirect attachment URLs to the attachment itself" setting.
- **Fixing a GSC sitemap error that says "HTML" instead of XML**: ask ChatGPT/Claude "how do I find my Yoast sitemap, here's the link to my website" — or just try swapping "html" for "xml" / trying `sitemap_index.xml` in the URL — then submit that XML link to Search Console.
- **Generating a privacy policy fast**: go to ChatGPT or Claude and say "I need a privacy policy, ask me questions" / "tell me what I should include and then ask me questions to get that info from me" — conversational, interview-style generation. Add both a privacy policy and terms & conditions to the site footer.
- **Weekly content/keyword workflow (avoid overwhelm)**: don't research keywords far in advance (trends/phrasing shift and the research gets wasted). Instead, each week: go to the Find Questions tool, type in your category/what you sell, pick 1-2 keywords or questions to target that week, write the post, move on.
- **Blog category strategy**: don't start by inventing categories. Start from real client/customer questions, write content answering them, and only then decide which category each piece falls under. Categories should be 1-2 words (e.g., "Relationships," not "Confusing Relationships"), and only spin up a dedicated tag/category (e.g., "Narcissism") once there's enough volume of content to justify it.
- **Organizing keyword/content ideas**: track in a Google Sheet or Notion — "no wrong way to track it," just pick one and use it consistently.
- **E-commerce keyword strategy for a genuinely unique product (no direct competitor)**: name the product/page around the generic, descriptive term a buyer would actually search (e.g., "heart shaped vibrator," "white beaver fur felt hat") rather than an invented brand/proper name — then use the real product/brand name secondarily. Build a supporting blog post or collection page around the broader concept (e.g., "inconspicuous/discreet vibrators") that can mention your products and even others'. If a similar product from someone else is already popular, build a "[competitor product] alternative(s)" page listing your product.
- **Writing informational content in a space dominated by big authorities** (e.g., a small e-commerce brand writing about perimenopause and sexual health against Mayo Clinic): still write it — if the reader finds the answer on your site, they stay in your ecosystem regardless of whether you can out-rank Mayo Clinic.
- **Preserving SEO equity during a full site redesign**: for any URL currently getting organic traffic, keep the exact URL and keep the on-page content the same (visual design/branding/colors can change freely). Same rule for any URL with existing backlinks — keep URL and content. Don't change everything site-wide at once (e.g., adding H1s across every legacy page in one pass) — it causes Google to visibly "adjust" across the board; make changes incrementally instead.
- **Finding quick-win keywords during/after a redesign**: in GSC, find queries where a page ranks positions 11-20, then make small optimizations (add a paragraph, add/fix the H1) to try to push onto page 1 — this is the fastest lever for a traffic increase.
- **Cross-posting a blog post to social/other platforms**: don't repeat the exact target keyword/content verbatim elsewhere. Instead post a summary and link back to the full post (Medium, LinkedIn, etc.).
- **Turning a blog post into a YouTube video without duplicate-content risk**: fine to do, as long as you're not just reading the blog text verbatim (add more/different info) and it's not an AI-generated video (YouTube is actively cracking down on AI video).
- **Nationwide business wanting local presence**: still build a location-specific page (e.g., a dedicated St. Louis page) tied to a local Google Business Profile, even if the company serves the whole country.
- **Getting a web designer to set up a blog structure you don't control**: ask them to build one blog page + one category-page template, send them a sample post to use as the model, and separately ask for a quick Loom/video walkthrough of how to update it yourself going forward.
- **Heading structure rule for AI-readiness/SEO**: H1 (biggest, top, states the page topic) → H2s (subtopics) → H3s nested under an H2 → can drop back to another H2, but never skip a level (H2 straight to H4, or H2 to H6). Don't use H4/H6 at all really. Every blog post is its own page with its own single H1 and its own H2s/H3s.
- **Where to put the answer on a page for AI/LLM crawlers**: put the direct answer to the page's core topic right at the top, since there's no way to know how deep any given AI crawler actually reads into a page.
- **Fixing "competing H1 tags"** (e.g., a hero/lead-magnet box plus the real page heading): don't demote the extra one to another heading level (H2) — that breaks heading sequence. Instead convert it to plain paragraph text, just styled/sized larger.
- **Quote/inquiry forms on e-commerce product pages**: keep one full dedicated "Get a Quote" page, but also add a small inline quote form (name, phone, email, short message) directly on each individual product page — mirrors what competitors already do and gives Google more on-page conversion signals.
- **Checking internal links per page (no dedicated free tool)**: use GSC to see which pages have few or no incoming internal links, and prioritize linking to those. Not needed if you're already doing weekly blog posts each linking to 3 other pages + 1 money page (per the prior Thursday's internal-linking lesson).
- **Localizing SEO research for a French/Quebec market**: use a private/incognito browser window set to French to check what SERPs look like for that market.
- **Reporting to clients on technical SEO fixes**: don't itemize every single fix (especially sitewide fixes that touch many pages) — take a screenshot of before/after and summarize ("we fixed 30/50 of these"). Full itemized reporting on technical SEO takes longer than doing the fixes themselves.
- **Client-facing monthly SEO report structure** (per Matt's own agency, described to Eve/Dr. Serkante's clinic): pages updated, what was changed on each, backlinks created, links to new blog posts — a transparency-first, Looker-Studio-style report.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- Don't chase a perfect Page Audit score — **80%+ is fine**; 100% doesn't guarantee a #1 ranking.
- Don't link excessively between your own multiple sites — "a couple of times is fine," but prioritize backlinks from sites you don't control.
- Don't run active SEO on multiple sites simultaneously — leads to burnout, and if the sites are in the same/similar niche you risk cannibalizing your own rankings. Treat separate sites as separate SEO projects.
- Local listings: submit to **Bing Places** (import directly from your Google Business Profile — "takes like two minutes") and **Apple Business Connect/Maps** (more involved — requires adding a TXT record to your DNS; the record must include "www" if your site uses www, or verification fails).
- "Near me" keywords: you cannot make a page rank for "near me" by literally putting those words on the page — Google infers proximity from the searcher's location automatically. If "near me" content is already a top referring keyword, leave it as-is.
- Meta description length target: **120-150 characters**.
- Heading structure must be strictly sequential: never skip levels (no H2 → H4, no H2 → H6); avoid H4/H6 entirely.
- Every page's H1, meta description, and product name/description must be unique across the site — don't reuse or duplicate them page to page.
- Don't keyword-stuff or over-optimize: e.g., don't use the same keyword 30 times when competitors who outrank you use it 10 times, or write 2,000 words when competitors use 500 and still rank. Use semantic variations, not repetition.
- SEO plugin scores (Yoast, All in One SEO, Rank Math, "AI SEO") are built for long-tail, natural-language questions the wrong way — a real long question from Find Questions will always get flagged ("title too long," "meta too long") — ignore the score for blog content. **Only apply plugin score guidance to money pages.**
- Yoast usage rule: use the **focus keyword field only on money pages** (skip it for blog posts); title tag and meta description fields are fine to use on every page including blog posts.
- Don't allow blog comments — "the only thing you'll get on blogs is spam."
- Category/menu depth: if a product/page needs **more than 2 clicks** to reach from anywhere on the site, it's effectively hidden from both users and Google.
- Category maintenance cadence: you should be able to add at least one new piece of content to a given category roughly **every 2 months** — only commit to "pillar" categories you're confident you'll keep filling.
- Ranking timeline expectations: 2 weeks with no movement is completely normal ("if somebody's ranking within two weeks, that is like magic"); realistic client timeline is **3-6 months**.
- Don't require a terms-and-conditions/privacy policy? — Do require it: no privacy policy hurts legitimacy signals and can reduce ad delivery (e.g., Meta ads shown to fewer people without one on the page).
- Two brands/sites in the same location offering overlapping services: pick **one** site to own the shared keywords — whichever one is already ranking — rather than competing with yourself.
- During a redesign, if a page/content type is already getting traffic or has backlinks, keep its URL and content exactly the same even if it doesn't fit the new site's design — don't delete/restructure it away.
- Core Web Vitals on GoHighLevel (e.g., Cumulative Layout Shift flagged orange, Total Blocking Time at 310ms in one member's audit): not fixable — GHL doesn't allow code or server-level changes, and results vary by the device/network Google uses to crawl.
- White label services: Matt does not offer them — "I don't believe in other people taking cuts and charging a small business more money."

## Tools, prompts & resources (exact prompt wording if quoted; tool names; settings)
- **Google Search Console** — Search Results report (clicks/impressions/position toggle), and the newer **Insights** report (trending pages, top pages, clicks/impressions over time).
- **Page Audit tool / extension** ("Hey Tony" tool) — checks title tag, H1/heading structure, and other on-page factors; used live on calls to check pages in real time rather than guessing.
- **AI Readiness audit** — separate audit surfacing issues like meta description length and heading-structure sequence errors.
- **Find Questions** — tool used weekly to pull real user questions/keywords by typing in a category or "what you sell."
- **"Hey Keywords" tool** — recommended specifically for finding keywords to use on money pages (not for broad exploratory research).

- **WordPress** — cited as roughly 50% of the internet, high documentation/tutorial availability; recommended for people wanting more control than Wix/Squarespace.
- **Wix / Squarespace** — recommended if you don't want to spend much time building/maintaining the site yourself.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- **Perfection vs. progress on page audits**: 80%+ is enough; chasing 100% wastes time that could go toward publishing or backlinks, and doesn't guarantee rank #1.
- **SEO plugin scores are context-dependent**: useful guardrails for money pages, actively counterproductive for blog posts targeting real long-tail questions — apply the tool's judgment selectively, not universally.
- **Reporting depth vs. time cost**: full itemized technical-SEO reporting can take longer than doing the actual fixes — screenshot summaries are the pragmatic middle ground, at some cost to granular client visibility.
- **Backlinking between your own sites**: some benefit early on, but leans low-value/self-referential past a couple of links — external, uncontrolled backlinks carry more weight.
- **Running SEO across multiple owned sites**: splitting attention risks burnout and, in similar niches, self-cannibalization — better to sequence focus one site at a time.
- **Unique-product naming**: an invented/branded name feels distinctive but carries zero existing search volume; a generic descriptive keyword wins on findability, at the cost of "cleverness" — keep the brand name as a secondary identifier, not the primary SEO hook.
- **Two brands, one location, overlapping services**: you can't rank both for the same terms — you must sacrifice one site's ambitions for those shared keywords in favor of whichever already has traction.
- **Site redesigns**: the instinct to change everything (a fresh look "deserves" fresh URLs/content) directly conflicts with preserving ranking equity — the resolution is to decouple visual redesign from URL/content structure entirely, and phase any structural SEO changes gradually rather than all at once.
- **Accessibility work for a low-vision user (David)**: standard video tutorials assume sighted use and don't transfer to his actual screen/setup — even a custom-made tutorial video wouldn't solve this, so text-based AI walkthroughs are the more genuinely accessible tool for him specifically.
- **GoHighLevel tradeoff**: fast to spin up sites/funnels/email in one platform, but you give up the ability to fix Core Web Vitals issues (CLS, TBT) since there's no code/server access.
- **Keyword research timing**: researching too far ahead of publishing feels efficient but is often wasted work once trends/phrasing shift — small, weekly, just-in-time research beats large upfront keyword-mapping projects.
- **"Near me" keywords**: counterintuitive that you can't directly optimize for the literal phrase — the entire mechanism is Google's own geo-inference, so the "keyword" isn't really a keyword to write for at all.
