## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- Weekly blog workflow: find a real customer question (via HeyTony Insiders' in-app "blog topic finder," FindAQuestion.com, AnswerThePublic, or just a question you've personally heard from customers) → record a voice answer → transcribe/interview via ChatGPT → take that transcript to Claude to rewrite/polish into the finished post, cross-checking any AI claims against competitor data → target one finished post per week.
- Why split ChatGPT/Claude: use ChatGPT for the voice back-and-forth Q&A and transcription (it's "really good with the voice back and forth and the transcriptions"); then hand that to Claude for the actual writing pass ("Claude is really good at writing good content").
- Nick's high-performing technique: prompt ChatGPT with "Ask me anything. I'm writing this blog post about this question. Ask me anything. Up to 10 questions," then answer honestly/personally. Result reads as ~99% personal voice with AI only smoothing flow — doesn't read as AI-written.
- On-page keyword optimization sequence: pick your target page, spend the week optimizing it (see density rule below), then leave it alone and move on to writing blog posts — don't keep re-touching the optimized page.
- Keyword targeting: when a keyword-research tool surfaces two distinct target phrases (e.g., a "small business bookkeeping" term and a related "outsourced accounting" term), put each on its own separate page — do not combine two distinct keyword targets onto one page.
- Cannibalization check: run your own domain through the Keyword Conflict Detector (inside HeyTony Insiders) to surface pages competing for the same keywords; also try keywordconflict.com (a second tool Matt built) for a second opinion.
- Ranking without using the exact-match phrase on-page: send the topical signal instead via (a) internal links from blog posts using the target phrase/variations as anchor text pointing to that page, and (b) backlinks using variations of that phrase as anchor text.
- Distinguishing comparison content from cannibalization: a dedicated page per product/brand (e.g., a Botox page) is informational; a separate "Brand A vs. Brand B vs. Brand C" page is a different content type entirely (consideration-stage, further down the funnel) — build it as its own piece, not a merge into the informational pages.
- For a ranking blog post that isn't sending traffic onward to a brand/product page: add a pop-up, or place a link higher up in the post with a descriptive call-to-action (e.g., "if you want to learn more about [X], click here") rather than "click here" alone.
- Menu/navigation UX test: hand your site to someone non-technical (Matt's phrasing: "a mom or a grandparent") and watch, unassisted, whether they can find a specific page; simplify based on where they get stuck. Don't force every topic into the nav menu.
- Image placement: roughly one image every 200–300 words of blog copy.
- Image sizing: ~750px wide by ~400–500px tall.
- Image sourcing priority: real/own photos or your own screenshots (fully unique, can't be replicated by competitors) first; AI-generated images are fine only if relevant to the specific content (never a random filler image); match images to brand colors.
- Novel/category-defining products with no existing search demand (nobody is searching for them yet): don't force a "question" workflow — instead write an "old thing vs. new thing" comparison post showing the differences.
- Social cross-posting: turn on Instagram's built-in toggle to auto-post videos to Facebook; afterward, manually go re-tag the people/organizations on the Facebook copy since tags don't transfer automatically.
- Video/content "bucket" format Matt is planning: scope videos to ~60-minute, single-task sessions (e.g., "60-minute keyword research," "60-minute backlinks") because business owners realistically have about an hour between meetings for SEO, not 20 hours a week.
- Related content idea Matt floated: actually go try a viral SEO tactic seen online on camera and report the real before/after results and pros/cons (e.g., buying backlinks from Fiverr, or buying the most expensive backlinks available).
- Unreleased concept Matt is designing: a daily text/SMS containing a link to a question; you tap it and record a voice answer; answers accumulate into a personal database; at week's end it auto-compiles into a blog post — over time the database means most new topics only need one or two additional questions answered, since much of the answer already exists.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Cadence target for members: **one finished blog post per week** ("just one, one a week").
- Cadence used on the case-study client: **2 blog posts/week + 4 backlinks/month** — "that's literally all we're doing. Not anything more, not anything less."
- Don't worry about "outdoing" competitor articles on the same question — Google rewards genuinely new/unique content, and personal answers are inherently unique.
- Don't check whether you're ranking for the exact target question right after publishing — not worth tracking yet.
- Don't fully trust ChatGPT's SEO advice (e.g., keyword density counts, "combine these on one page," "is this a good idea") — it will often agree with you or give generic/inaccurate guidance. Matt: "you are the expert, you're doing the research."
- Keyword density/usage count: no fixed number — match however many times your competitors use the exact target phrase on their ranking pages (could be once, four times, five times). If a competitor isn't using it at all, use it once yourself.
- Ignore other tools' "keyword density too high" warnings — "whatever they're doing, ranking number one, Google likes, you're gonna do that too."
- Ranking #1 with zero clicks in week one: don't panic, don't assume Google will penalize you for it — wait it out. CTR optimization is a later step, not a week-one task.
- A keyword with a good score but no reported search volume is still worth targeting.
- Meta keywords field (WordPress SEO plugins flagging it "missing"): ignore it completely — dead since the 90s/early 2000s, does nothing for Google now.
- Don't use WordPress "tags" on blog posts — they create a pile of thin, low-value pages. Instead pick **5 to 10 categories total** and put each post into exactly one category.
- Comparing your own brand's product pages (e.g., Botox vs. Dysport vs. Xeomin) is **not** cannibalization — it's a distinct, valid comparison/consideration-stage content type.
- Editing an already-ranking page carries real risk of hurting its ranking — only worth the risk if the page is currently converting at zero.
- Title length: aim for **60 characters or less** going forward. For older posts already published longer, don't retroactively edit them — revisit in **3–4 months** and change only if they're producing nothing.
- Heading structure: only one H1 (the post title). Headings must go strictly sequential — H1 → H2 → H3, never H1 straight to H3 ("you can, but you shouldn't"). If you prefer the visual size of H3, restyle H2's font to match it instead of skipping levels.
- Revenue growth is capped by fulfillment capacity, not just lead volume — more SEO traffic doesn't move the bottom line if the business/team can't handle the additional work.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- AI-assisted content risk is really about input ratio: content that's 99% your own personal information/stories with AI just smoothing the prose reads as authentic; content compiled mostly by the AI itself tends to resemble everything else out there.
- Ranking #1 with zero clicks in week one is normal, not a red flag — the sequence is rank first, then optimize title/CTR later, not simultaneously.
- Comparison pages between your own brand's products are a legitimate separate content type (consideration-stage, different search intent) — but this is distinct from Neri's case, where two genuinely different keyword targets should NOT share one page. The line is intent/keyword-target difference, not simply "multiple related pages exist."
- Editing an already-ranking-but-low-converting page is a real gamble: you might improve conversions, but you also risk knocking the existing ranking down — safest to attempt only when the page is converting at zero anyway.
- Squarespace's own title-length guidance (allowing 81 characters) conflicts with Matt's tighter ≤60-character standard; rather than mass-editing old posts on this basis, only revisit posts that are producing zero results after 3–4 months.
- Facebook cross-posting from Instagram captures outsized reach cheaply, but costs you tag/mention data — every cross-posted video needs manual re-tagging on the Facebook side.
- More leads/traffic doesn't equal more revenue once a business (or agency) is past its fulfillment capacity — at that point conversion-rate work and pricing become the actual growth levers, not additional SEO volume.
- Category-creating/novel products don't fit the standard "find and answer an existing question" workflow, since no one is searching for them yet — comparison-to-known-alternatives content fills that gap instead.
- Keith's listicle question (comparing his product to competitors) got no direct yes/no from Matt — it was used instead to make the broader point about not trusting ChatGPT's validation of an idea; Matt redirected him toward a concrete, safer blog topic ("why I built [X]") rather than ruling on listicles directly.
