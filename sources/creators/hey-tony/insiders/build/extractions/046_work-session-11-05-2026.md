## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Weekly cadence is the whole system**: show up every week for an hour and work on something — that alone produces progress. Publish at least one blog per week.
- **Standard blog-writing pipeline**:
  1. Go to the **Blog Idea Finder** (in the Insiders toolkit) — or Answer The Public, or findquestions.com if Answer The Public isn't free for you — and type in what you sell (e.g., "nail salon").
  2. Pick one of the generated questions.
  3. Paste the AI interview prompt (see Tools section) into ChatGPT or Claude to have it interview you about that question.
  4. Answer the questions — voice mode or typed, whichever you prefer (see Tradeoffs).
  5. Have Claude (or ChatGPT) write/rewrite the post from your answers.
  6. **Paste the AI output into a plain text document first**, then copy from the text document into WordPress — this strips hidden AI-tag formatting before it hits the CMS.
  7. Write the title tag + meta description by directly asking the AI: confirm the target keyword appears in both.
- **Keyword-variant decision rule**: when a term has multiple spellings/forms (e.g., "stand-up," "standup," "stand up"), don't guess — check which variation gets the most search traffic and is easiest to rank for, then target that specific variation.
- **Link building via Link Seeker**: type in what you sell (e.g., "automotive"), it generates a query you paste into Google, and it returns a list of high-authority backlink opportunities (e.g., guest-post targets). Same underlying method as Matt's older manual spreadsheet, just automated.
- **Order of writing blog topics**: the topic/keyword list from the finder tool is not ranked — write whichever topic is most topically appropriate next, not necessarily #1 then #2 then #3 in list order.
- **PressMaster client workflow (Bill's setup)**:
  1. Use Find Questions to source a topic/question.
  2. Feed it into PressMaster's interview app; client answers by voice (e.g., while driving).
  3. App pulls the answers and assembles a draft blog post.
  4. Review the draft before publishing.
  5. In PressMaster, go to **Optimize → Humanize** — it scans the whole article, strips AI flags, runs multiple passes, then presents each change for accept/reject (safe to accept nearly all of them).
  6. Auto-publishes to the client's (or your own) site once done.
  7. From that same post as source material, generate up to **20 days** of social content, with separate agents per platform (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, etc.).
  8. Separate "pin" feature: capture any idea or outside content (e.g., a YouTube video) via the phone app; it syncs to the desktop app as future blog material.
- **Sourcing content ideas from outside your niche**: deliberately watch what other course/membership creators do, or look at completely unrelated fields (Matt's example: a band's live-show structure) and adapt the underlying principle to your own content/business.
- **Handling tangents during the AI interview**: if you ramble, you can tell the AI "ignore the last couple things I said" — but recognize the tangent itself may be usable material, since talking it out (even long-winded) trains the AI to sound more like you.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Do **not** check/track rankings daily — stated as prior guidance (David admits he still does it "because of OCD").
- **Use the interview prompt every single time** you start a new blog post — never assume the AI remembers context. "AI is dumb. You need to make sure that it's staying on track."
- **Never paste AI output directly into WordPress.** Paste into a plain text document first, then copy from there into WordPress — direct paste leaves a hidden tag/div in the backend that flags the content as Claude/AI-generated.
- If ChatGPT is being unhelpful or repetitive, don't keep fighting it — switch to Claude. Matt: Claude is better for "like 99% of things," and all of his own agency output runs through Claude.
- Running both ChatGPT and Claude subscriptions is fine (ChatGPT for voice-mode capture, Claude for writing) — but watch subscription creep; Matt estimates he personally spends "a couple hundred bucks a month" across all his AI/LLM subscriptions.
- Do **not** actually deploy the full 20 days of PressMaster-generated social posts per blog if you already post daily elsewhere — avoid over-repetition/saturation from one source post.
- There is **no way to fully automate blog writing** — you still have to personally answer the interview questions and manually review the draft; skipping that is what produces generic "oatmeal"/"AI slop" content.
- **SEMrush free trial**: signing up for the 7-day trial and being even a couple hours late to cancel results in an immediate charge. Stated refund policy: monthly subscriptions are **not** refundable, only yearly plans are. Cancel before the trial ends — and even then you might still get charged. Widely reported as a scam complaint on Reddit. SEMrush was acquired by Adobe; Adobe cancellations are described as "impossible... no refunds, no anything."
- Benchmark expectation: typical agency clients take **2–3 months** to show real ranking movement — the Insiders members in this session are seeing meaningful movement in about **a month and a half**, faster than that norm.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **Voice vs. typed answers** for the AI interview: voice is faster for some but risks rambling/misspeaking (Jason found typing lets him edit and go back, so he prefers it despite feeling "lazy"). Either mode is fine — and even a long-winded tangent can be valuable raw material rather than something to trim, since it trains the AI toward your voice.
- **Automation has a ceiling**: even the most advanced pipeline (PressMaster's humanize + auto-social + auto-publish) still depends on a human answering the original interview questions and reviewing the draft before it goes out — full automation is explicitly rejected as inferior ("I just wouldn't do that").
- **Volume vs. saturation**: PressMaster can generate up to 20 days of social posts from a single blog post, but Matt cautions against actually shipping that much repetitive content from one source if you already post daily elsewhere — the concern is content quality/repetition, not an algorithmic penalty.
- **Keyword-variant targeting is judgment, not guesswork**: Google often matches intent across spelling variants, but when a niche has real variant confusion (stand-up/standup/stand up), check actual traffic and difficulty per variant rather than assuming.
- **Multiple LLM subscriptions can be worth it** (ChatGPT for voice capture, Claude for writing) but the cost adds up fast — Matt flags "a couple hundred bucks a month" across his own stack as a caution, not a recommendation to over-subscribe.
- **Topic ordering is flexible**: there's no required sequence for working through a keyword/topic list — pick by topical relevance, not list position.
- **Course content density varies**: even a well-structured program (Matt's own praise for how Hey Tony Insiders is built) can still contain individual lessons that are padded relative to their actual information content — a caution about time-budgeting around any course, including your own.
