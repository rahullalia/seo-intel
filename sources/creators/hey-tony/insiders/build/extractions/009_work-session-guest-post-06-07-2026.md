# 009 — Work Session: Guest Post (06.07.2026)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Guest post exercise on The Plaid Zebra (Matt's site)** — full process:
  1. Go to findquestions.com (or the Hey Tony "blog idea finder" inside Insiders) and grab a real user question by typing in what you sell.
  2. Go to ChatGPT and use the Community prompt (Insiders → Community → Prompts) — the same interview-style prompt used every Monday where ChatGPT interviews you to answer the question.
  3. Unlike a normal post on your own site (3 internal links), this piece gets only **one link**: a money keyword anchor pointing to your own money page/homepage.
  4. Also include **one link to an existing article already on The Plaid Zebra** — search the site's search bar (top right) for a relevant existing piece (e.g., a notary business searched "lawyers" to find adjacent content to link).
  5. Write the post as a Google Doc. Title the doc/article — this becomes the H1 tag and URL slug.
  6. Set the Google Doc sharing to "Anyone with the link can view."
  7. Get one featured image (and possibly one in-body image) as a **separate image file** — not embedded in the doc. Resize to 750px wide, keep file size small.
  8. Submit via the form: name, email, Google Doc link, one featured image. Link posted in the Insiders community tab.
  9. Deadline to submit: **Friday, July 10**. Team will publish ~3 posts/day, rolling out over ~30 days; may start publishing same week depending on submission volume.
  10. Posts also get shared on The Plaid Zebra's Facebook page (~98k followers) after publishing — partly to "influence AI."
- **Repositioning tactic (Robert Starling's win)**: stop competing for global/generic head-term keywords (e.g., "voiceover," "voice actor") against 100,000+ competitors chasing the same big clients. Reposition around a differentiator (his: "48 years of business experience") and target the much larger pool of small/local businesses instead of a few big global ones. Referenced the "Acres of Diamonds" concept — the opportunity is often local/overlooked, not far away.
- **Trademark leverage tactic (for David's new trademark win)**:
  - Look up your registration number on the US Patent and Trade Office site — a page exists there for it.
  - Write a press release framed as "here's what I do" with a keyword as the press release title, linking to the USPTO page and to your homepage — helps you rank in AI.
  - Also use that USPTO trademark page intermittently as an **external link** on money pages, collection pages, or blog posts to signal legitimacy ("here's an external piece of content... it is still talking about you").
- **Fixing keyword cannibalization (Sarah's approach)**: when collection pages, product pages, and blog pages are all targeting the same keyword, fix them in sequence — collection pages first, then product pages, then blogs. One keyword per page.
- **Choosing a guest-post topic that could overlap your own content**: if a candidate question feels too similar to something already on your site, either (a) broaden or narrow the angle/niche so it's differentiated (e.g., "photographer" instead of "newborn photographer"), or (b) skip that question for now and come back to it later reframed differently once you've used up the clearly-distinct topics.
- **Internal/external linking anchor text rule**: link anchor text should be descriptive of the destination page's actual topic (e.g., anchor "cosmetic tattoo" → the page that is about cosmetic tattoo).

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Guest post = **one money-keyword link to your own site** + **one link to an existing Plaid Zebra article**. Not three internal links like the usual weekly exercise.
- Google Doc must be shared as "Anyone with the link can view."
- Images must be separate files (not embedded in the doc), **750px wide**, smallest file size possible.
- Submission deadline: **Friday, July 10**.
- Do **not** write the guest post in a self-promotional tone (packages, pricing, "hire me"). Keep it generic/topical with a CTA-style link at the end, not framed around "me."
- Do **not** publish the same/similar piece on your own site as the one submitted to The Plaid Zebra — it competes with yourself and is near-duplicate content since the guest post already links back to your site.
- Cannibalization test: it's about the blog post's **topic**, not the anchor link destination. Fine to link many different blog posts to the same money page, as long as each blog post's own topic differs from the money page's topic.
- Don't put your core service keyword in a blog post title if you already have a dedicated money page for that exact term — **unless** the blog post's topic is literally about choosing/comparing that service (e.g., "How do you choose a website management service" is fine).
- Search Console manual indexing after publishing: **not required**. Google will find it on its own. If it hasn't been found after a couple of weeks to a month, then request manual indexing. No downside either way, and you can do it every time if you want — it's optional.
- New content can take **3 to 6 months** (varies by site) before it gets any views/traction — zero views early is not a red flag.
- Core content litmus test: "Would my customers actually need this? And read this. If the answer is yes, then publish it."
- Trademark usage: must include the **TM** symbol every time the brand name is used, or it can become public property even after being trademarked. Also register a **service mark (SM)** — not available in Canada, but recommended where it is.
- Fiverr site-speed recommendation is **Shopify-only** — does not apply to Squarespace.
- Hey Keywords tool: if you're confused about how to use it, **pause using it** rather than burning through your (50-word) keyword allowance — tutorial coming.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- A guest post that overlaps or even mildly "competes" with your own business topic is still worth doing, because it links back to your site regardless — the backlink value outweighs the topical overlap concern.
- But you should NOT mirror/duplicate the guest-post content on your own site afterward — that would create competing near-duplicate content against a page that's already sending you a backlink.
- Cannibalization is a page-topic issue, not a linking issue: many different blog posts can all link to the same money page without being "cannibalization," as long as each post covers a genuinely different topic/angle than the money page and than each other.
- SiteGround problems are only partially fixable from the Hey Tony side (DNS tweak fixed sitemap recognition) — full crawling reliability remains a known limitation ("there's only so much we can do"). Migrating to CrockWeb is offered as a workaround, and Matt states it should not affect existing rankings/work, but this is reassurance rather than a guarantee grounded in a specific case shown on the call.
- Indexing patience cuts both ways: manual GSC indexing requests are optional and low-risk, but the real lesson is that lack of traction (zero views after a few posts) is expected for months and isn't itself evidence of a mistake — conflating "not indexed/no traffic yet" with "doing it wrong" is the trap Matt is steering members away from.
- The Fiverr recommendation is deliberately narrow in scope (Shopify speed fixes only) — Matt frames his own general skepticism of Fiverr explicitly before making the one exception.
