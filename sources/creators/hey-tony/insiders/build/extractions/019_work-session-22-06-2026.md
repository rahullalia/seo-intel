## Rules / dos & don'ts

- One keyword ("Focus keyword" in Yoast terms) per page — must be unique per page, never reused across pages.
- Categories: keep the total list to 5–10 for the whole site; every post goes in 1–2 categories max, never more.
- Tags: never use tags — "never, ever, ever." If tags already exist on the site, delete them.
- Internal links: 3–4 per page/post.
- Images: at least one every 200–300 words.
- Word count: no fixed max — "there's not really a too long word count" — just don't let it read as a wall of text; today's exercise (10 questions answered) lands around 1,000–1,500 words.
- Refresh cadence: leave a published page/post alone for 6 months. After that: if it's ranking, don't touch it; if it's not ranking, re-optimize it.
- Duplicate-topic products: don't create two separate pages/posts targeting near-identical keywords for the same product — consolidate to one page.
- Local pages at scale: each city/location page must be completely unique — no copy-paste-with-city-swapped content (risk of being demoted/penalized).
- Directory/provider pages (many independent contractors on one site): don't spend SEO effort optimizing these individually for competitive terms.
- Can't manipulate ChatGPT/Claude into recommending your business via prompt instructions in your own private sessions — has zero effect on what the model surfaces to other users.
- Publishing cadence: one question/one blog post per week — don't try to plan or gather material for an entire year up front (Natalie had over-prepared 50+ questions and was told to slow down).
- Don't make your blogging cadence dependent on third parties (interview subjects/clients) answering questionnaires — write what you can yourself now; treat outside input as supplemental, not load-bearing.
- Page vs. post: service-in-a-location = page; comparison or informational content = post (rule repeated, applies broadly across niches).

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Podcast/video-derived content is valuable as "supplemental" AI-training material even though nobody searches for the podcast/video itself directly — the payoff is indirect (better-informed future drafts), not a standalone keyword play.
- Crowdsourcing content from clients/experts (Natalie's case) adds credibility and breadth but creates a dependency bottleneck when they're slow to respond — guidance favors writing from your own expertise first and treating outside input as supplemental, not load-bearing.
- Terminology choice is a real tension: the technically-correct or brand-preferred term (rug, Ayavastra, "biomass boiler" as the more formal name) can lose to the commonly-searched market term (mat, woodchip boiler) — SEO rewards matching audience language over correcting or educating it, even when the founder feels the popular term is inaccurate.
- Scaling local pages with AI drafting is efficient but risks thin/duplicate content if not manually differentiated per location — the fix (naming real local partners, real local color) costs more manual effort per page than a templated approach.
- Directory/provider sites face a temptation to SEO-optimize every individual profile; guidance is that's wasted effort — better return on optimizing shared informational pages while individual providers rank via brand-name search or Google Business Profile on their own.
- Comprehensive existing "article pages" (Francoise) create duplicate-content risk against new blog content covering similar ground — the resolution isn't to stop blogging, it's to angle new posts narrower/more experiential (first-person "tour guide" framing) rather than re-covering the same informational territory.
- AI chat privacy settings: keeping ChatGPT/Claude conversations private has no real cost to discoverability, since AI recommendations are generated via live search rather than session history — a perceived tradeoff that isn't actually one.
- The Page Audit competitor-comparison tool's "N/A" failure mode was left ambiguous/unresolved on the call (could be the user's site or the competitor's crawler-blocking) — flagged as open troubleshooting, not a settled answer.
- Booking-friction removal (Ariel's scheduler-to-button change): felt "emotionally hard" trading a lower-friction CRM-linked scheduler for page speed, since standard marketing advice pushes toward frictionless booking — outcome unverified at call time ("we'll see how that goes").
