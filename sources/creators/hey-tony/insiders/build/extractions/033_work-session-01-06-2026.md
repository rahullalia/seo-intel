## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Build a sizing/fitting calculator with Claude**: feed Claude a handful of reference examples from other sites you like ("I like this, I like that, what can you do?") and let it generate the tool — Catherine's worked on the first attempt. Claude Code itself isn't required; she "looked at Claude code and freaked out" and just used normal Claude chat instead.
- **Move interactive tools above the fold** — get the calculator/tool higher on the page so users don't have to scroll to reach it. Cite the "investment calculator / tax calculator" pattern: if a visitor has to scroll past a bunch of content to find the tool, they bounce back to Google and pick a different result that loads it right at the top.
- **Add visual cues next to instructions inside a tool** — e.g., an image showing which clasp/hook the item should fasten on, rather than describing it in text only.
- **Add a same-size product filter** near/under the calculator so users can filter the whole catalog down to just their result size (analogy given: a shoe site letting you filter to "only shoes in size 45").
- **Have Claude build a "remembers your size" layer** — once a user gets a calculator result, surface "we have this in your size" prompts as they browse the rest of the site instead of making them re-check fit on every product.
- **Add a post-quiz qualifying question** that routes to the right collection page (e.g., "what type of bra are you looking for — comfort, going out, strapless, workout...?") and link the answer straight to that collection.
- **Add an email capture tied to the tool** — e.g., "sign up to be notified when your size is back in stock" or "enter your email for 10% off your first order."
- **Geo-gate popups/offers** to only the regions you actually serve — don't show a UK-only discount popup to visitors you can't ship to (Catherine can't sell into North America from Jersey).
- **Use searchers' literal phrasing as on-page copy** — do keyword research to confirm exact terms (e.g., "bra size calculator" vs. "bra size estimator") and use that verbiage on the tool/page itself.
- **Also accept manual measurement entry** alongside a guided quiz, since fit calculators are inherently approximate ("bra fitting is more of an art than a science") — let people who already know their numbers just input them.
- **Write a blog post benchmarking your tool against other calculators/guides** in the space, specifically calling out where competitors' tools get it wrong. Builds topical authority and can help you rank for "[X] calculator" broadly, not just your own tool.
- **Segment blog content by audience/life-stage angle** rather than one generic post — e.g., one post for people new to the category with no clue what they're doing, another about how cup size changes with life events (weight gain, aging) and how people may still be wearing a size from years earlier.
- **Weekly blog workflow**: go to the Blog Idea Finder tool → type in what you sell → get a real user question back → take that question (or one you've heard elsewhere) to ChatGPT or Claude using the supplied prompt (posted in the community chat) → it answers the question as your blog post.
- **Press-release workflow** (occasional alternative to the weekly blog exercise): write and distribute a press release for the site/client, then check whether it gets referenced/cited in AI Mode or AI Overviews for a relevant search.
- **Fixing a keyword-density gap vs. competitors around a core update**: pick exactly ONE page to edit — ideally one that's already dropped low or off page 1 (little ranking left to protect) — rewrite/expand it, and check the impact before touching any other page.
- **Match competitors' derivative keyword variants**: if competitors split a term into two close variants (e.g., "auto repair" and "automotive repair"), add both variants onto your page rather than only the one you already use.
- **Google Business Profile service descriptions**: work the city/keyword into the natural copy once per listing, describe what the service actually is, optionally mention the neighborhood — don't repeat the city keyword in every single service entry, and don't put a full street address inside the description text.
- **New/newly-indexed site basics**: submit the site to Google Search Console and install Google Analytics before troubleshooting why rankings haven't shown up yet.
- **Target a keyword even if a competitor outranks you without using it** — if the current top result doesn't contain your exact target phrase anywhere on the page, that phrase can still be an easy opportunity; their ranking is more likely coming from Google Business Profile presence, citations, authority, or tenure than from on-page keyword usage.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Google Business Profile service description limit: **300 characters**.
- Put the location keyword in a GBP service description **once** per listing — not repeated across all ~30–40 service entries.
- Do **not** put a street address inside a GBP service description.
- When editing during/around a core update: touch **one page at a time**, never batch 10 pages at once — check results before moving to the next page.
- If a page has already dropped to the bottom of page 1 or off page 1 entirely, it's safe to **fully rewrite/redo** it since there's little ranking left to lose.
- If a page is still ranking reasonably (e.g., dropped from #1 to #4 but no further), make **conservative edits only** — don't do a major rewrite.
- For blog/Q&A content, the target keyword **is** the literal question being answered — you don't separately insert keywords; they appear naturally through writing the answer.
- If a keyword-density gap vs. competitors predates the current core update (i.e., wasn't caused by it), it's fine to proceed with fixing it.
- Press release / paid distribution cost referenced: roughly **$80–$90** ("80 bucks or the 90 bucks").
- New sites need real time to rank even when everything is done right — a slow start on a new site isn't evidence of doing something wrong.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Fit calculators are inherently imprecise — even Catherine's main supplier, known for in-store fitting expertise, gave her a different size than her actual in-person fitting. That cuts both ways: it undercuts blind trust in any one calculator, but it's also a usable content angle (compare/critique competing calculators) rather than a weakness to hide.
- Outranking a competitor who doesn't even use your exact target keyword isn't automatically a pure content/keyword gap — their position may rest on Google Business Profile presence, citations, accumulated authority, or tenure, so a new site should expect to need real time regardless of the apparent keyword gap.
- Editing during a live core update is judged by current position, not a blanket rule: pages with little left to lose (bottom of page 1 or worse) can be freely rewritten, while pages still holding a reasonable rank should get conservative edits only.
- Matt deliberately keeps the weekly exercises free (ChatGPT/Claude prompts cost nothing) while flagging that paid tactics like press release distribution cost money and, in his own past experience, sometimes only produced a temporary (~1 week) ranking bump before fading — framed as optional ("if it works, it works"), not something members are pushed into.
