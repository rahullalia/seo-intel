# Rank Week Day 2 Replay — 16.06.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Define your money page first**: any product/service/booking page someone Googles because they intend to buy — Invisalign page (dentist), residential shingle/roofing page (roofer), sell/buy-a-home page (realtor), booking/reservation page (Airbnb-style), each individual product (ecommerce). Pick the one that **isn't ranking but you wish it could**.
- **Money-page audit workflow** (pageaudit.com):
  1. Go to **pageaudit.com**, paste in your money page URL, and enter your **target keyword** (what you think people type into Google to find that page — e.g. "Hamilton Electrician," "Movers Queen West," not just "Movers Toronto").
  2. Click Analyze. **Ignore the overall score/percentage entirely** — not used in this exercise.
  3. Click the **Content** tab, select the entire page (Cmd+A / Ctrl+A), copy it.
  4. Paste into ChatGPT or Claude — **do not press Enter yet**.
  5. Grab the ready-made prompt from **heytony.ca/prompts** and paste it underneath the page text in the same message, then press Enter.
  6. Paste the AI's output into **column B** of the audit spreadsheet (from the same prompts page).
  7. Go back to pageaudit.com's **Metadata** tab, copy the **meta title (title tag)** and **meta description**, paste them into the spreadsheet in place of the "NA" placeholders.
- **Repeat the identical process for your top 3 organic competitors**: Google your exact target keyword (incognito), skip all ads/sponsored results, skip Reddit, skip Yelp/IG results, take the next three genuine organic results, run each through pageaudit.com → Content → paste to ChatGPT/Claude with the same prompt → paste output into "Competitor 1 / 2 / 3" rows in the spreadsheet, then repeat metadata copy for each.
- **If a competitor (or your own site) returns all "NA"**: the tool couldn't crawl it (usually bot-blocking). Fallback = install the **pageaudit.com browser extension** ("Add to Chrome" from the pageaudit.com site), open the actual page, and it surfaces title tag, meta description, H1/H2 heading tags, word count, and — if you type in the keyword — keyword occurrence count and keyword density directly in the browser.
- **Check if yesterday's blog post is ranking**: literally Google the target question/keyword in incognito and see if your site shows up — that's the whole test.
- **Fixing keyword overlap between a landing page and a blog post** (Tom's Virginia Beach case): keep the exact money keyword ("homes for sale in Virginia Beach") only on the landing/IDX page. Write the blog post around a related **long-tail, evergreen angle** instead (e.g. "best areas to live in Virginia Beach"), and link from that blog post back into the specific home-listings/landing page.
- **Location-page strategy** (Steph G's Ohio case): don't try to rank for an entire state — pick the largest metro you actually serve (Columbus) and stack it with specific service-modifier long-tails (anxiety therapy, couples therapy/marriage counseling, PTSD therapy, CBT) rather than the generic head term; find whichever modifier is easiest to rank for and let it generate calls first. Support the highest-impression/least-ranked term (anxiety therapy, in her case) with blog posts first.
- **Two-sided marketplace / two-audience sites** (Daniel's book-marketplace case, Aaron's serviced-accommodation case): pick which side of the marketplace to court first (the side you understand best / the side that's cheaper or easier to acquire and will pull the other side in — analogous to how Airbnb targeted hosts first). Write content that answers that side's actual Google-able questions, not content built around your brand's invented terminology.
- **Keyword strategy for novel/branded concepts** (Daniel's "ghosts" AI-persona feature): don't try to rank for your invented product term — nobody Googles that. Instead identify the underlying problem your target user is Googling (e.g. an author would Google "nobody buys my book, what do I do") and target that.
- **Fixing "keyword overstuffing" flags on a niche whose brand name = a government program name** (Anil's disability-tax-credit case): make the homepage the general category ("tax specialist"), position the specific scheme as a specialty underneath it, and build dedicated pages around the actual task users need help with (e.g. how to fill out the specific disability tax credit forms — guides, step-by-step videos, larger-text/accessible versions for visual disabilities, or a tool that fills the form and emails it to them).
- **"Secret pages" fix** (Aaron's geo-page case): if location/geo pages aren't linked from the homepage or main navigation, Google cannot discover them even though they're live and even rank when checked directly — they must be linked from somewhere crawlable.
- **Adding a blog post to an AI-prompted site** (built via ChatGPT/Claude + Netlify, no manual coding): just prompt the same AI tool — "I wrote this blog post, put it on the site as a blog post."
- Content-publishing philosophy: think of every page as **"a door into your business"** — build one door at a time and make sure each is solid before adding the next, rather than mass-publishing many thin pages at once.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- **One keyword per page, always.** If the same keyword sits on two pages, Google can't decide which to rank and may rank neither.
- **Publish at most 1–2 blog posts per week** ("twice a week...two blog posts per week at the most") — going faster/auto-publishing reads to Google as spam or AI-generated content.
- At that cadence: **~104 pages/year**, **~208 over two years** — long-term compounding game, not instant.
- **Turn off Cloudflare's bot-blocking setting** if you have it enabled — it prevents both the audit tool and real AI/search crawlers from reading your site.
- When picking "top 3 organic competitors": **skip ads/sponsored, skip Reddit, skip Yelp/IG** — organic results only.
- Don't chase the page-audit tool's overall score or percentage — irrelevant to this exercise.
- Don't hit Enter in ChatGPT/Claude until the prompt has been pasted underneath the full copied page text.
- Fill out the audit spreadsheet (your page + 3 competitors) **before tomorrow's session** — day 3 builds directly on this data to diagnose fixes.
- Typical ranking timeline is **3–6 months**; some content ranks faster (a few Day-1 posts were reportedly ranking #1 in SERP and even in AI Overview within ~24 hours, but that's not the norm) — "give SEO time, it's not a switch."
- Community/chat conduct: don't spam, don't repeat the same comment, don't promote your own Discord/program/community inside this chat — moderators will kick/ban repeat offenders (one participant, Shahzad, was directly warned on-call).

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Keyword-research tool numbers (SEMrush, Ubersuggest, Google Keyword Planner, heykeywords.com) actively disagree with each other on volume/difficulty — treat any of them as directional only, not authoritative; Matt personally weighs "what are people actually searching" and difficulty-to-rank over tool output, and thinks keyword research is getting less important generally as AI search grows.
- Blog cadence is a deliberate ceiling, not a floor: 1–2/week is the target specifically to avoid Google's spam/AI-content detection — faster publishing (or fully automated AI publishing) was flagged as the likely cause of another trainee's over-frequent-publishing penalty.
- Location targeting is a granularity tradeoff: broader (whole state) inflates the difficulty of ranking with little practical benefit if your service area is really one metro; narrower (a specific neighborhood or modality) is easier to rank and still converts — advice consistently pushed toward narrower.
- SEO is explicitly framed as one growth channel among several, not mandatory for every business model — for Daniel's marketplace, Matt was open to partnerships/influencers/community distribution being the better lever than SEO.
- Landing-page builders (Flodesk) are fine for social/ad-driven funnels but cannot rank in Google search — the tradeoff is real website + SEO investment vs. staying purely social/referral-driven.
- Reddit is a viable content-distribution channel for community-style questions, but carries real moderator risk if posts/comments read as repeated self-promotion rather than genuine help.
- Ranking timelines are probabilistic, not fixed: most participants should expect 3–6 months, though a handful of Day-1 posts reportedly already ranked (even in AI Overview) within 24 hours — framed as the exception, not something to expect broadly.
- Bot-blocking settings (e.g. Cloudflare) protect against unwanted scraping but simultaneously block the legitimate AI/search crawlers a site depends on for both SEO and being audited — a direct security-vs-visibility tradeoff site owners need to actively manage.
