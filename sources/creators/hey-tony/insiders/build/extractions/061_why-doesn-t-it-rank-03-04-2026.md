# Why Doesn't It Rank — 03.04.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Keyword research from scratch** (Neri, bookkeeping, St. Petersburg FL): open Semrush → type seed keyword → set location to United States (not left on Canada/global) → check volume → widen/narrow the term ("bookkeeping" → "bookkeeper" → "St. Petersburg FL bookkeeping" → "bookkeeping service in St. Petersburg" → "bookkeeping service, St. Petersburg") → treat it as guesswork, start broad then get specific.
- **Deciding how many times to use a keyword on a page**: Google the target term, open the #1-ranking competitor, and count keyword occurrences (via a page-audit tool) to calibrate — competitor's page used "bookkeeping" 27 times; Matt's take for a weaker-optimized competitor set: "use it three or four times and see what happens."
- **Vertical/niche page strategy for a service business**: build one dedicated page per client-type served, e.g. for a bookkeeper — "bookkeeping for HVAC companies," "bookkeeping for landscaping and lawn care," "bookkeeping for pressure washing businesses," "bookkeeping for pest control," "pool service," "junk removal." Roll out over time, not all at once.
- On each niche vertical page, write to that industry's actual specifics (e.g. HVAC companies buy from known vendors — name the vendor, note where they're based for local relevancy, and link to them).
- **Fixing keyword cannibalization on one page** (Jorgie/York, tennis apparel): a collection page was simultaneously chasing "tennis T-shirts," "tennis polo shirts," and "tennis hoodies" at once. Fix: make the parent/hub page broader ("tennis apparel" / "explore our tennis apparel collections"), and push each specific product keyword down to its own dedicated collection page.
- **Repeatable on-page fix sequence** used across nearly every audit: (1) target keyword at the very start of the title tag, (2) target keyword at the very start of the H1 tag, (3) rewrite the meta description as an independent sales pitch (not a copy of the title tag) naming specific services/differentiators, (4) make sure the full location name is stated explicitly and repeatedly (not an abbreviation like "D.C." if "Washington D.C." is the real search term).
- **Local-business title tag reorder** (Victor, DC movers): move keyword/city to the front and brand name to the end — e.g. "Movers in Washington D.C. — Hire Movers, Free Quotes | Your Moving Men" instead of leading with the company name.
- Add hyperlocal relevancy: mention/link specific neighborhoods (Capitol Hill, Penn Quarter, Adams Morgan) and, where available, link out to neighborhood-specific local news/community sites (e.g. "Admo News" for Adams Morgan).
- Add trust/citation signals to local pages where applicable: Trustpilot, Yelp, Chamber of Commerce / BBB links.
- Grab a Google Business Profile "CID link" and use it in local content/posts; geotag images and add alt text (Victor already doing both).
- On the GBP itself: list every service area served, list every service with its own written description, and keep posting regular GBP updates.
- **Fixing orphaned pages** (David, vehicle repair — 62 orphaned pages: vehicle models + repair types): add a dropdown/sub-navigation under the top-level "Vehicles" nav item so every page is reachable in roughly 3 clicks from the homepage; group logically by category/make rather than one giant flat list — it still has to be usable for an actual person, not just a link dump.
- **Diagnosing a stagnant directory site** (Alicia, healing-arts directory): check whether target pages are real location pages with actual search demand (tourist-town pages) vs. thin/archive-style directory listings; check what people actually type (e.g. "yoga," "massage") rather than the site's internal vocabulary ("healing artist").
- Individual practitioner/listing pages need a clear CTA and a direct path to action (link out to book/their site) — a bio plus a logo image with no link or CTA is "not giving anybody any useful information."
- **Fixing UGC/member blog quality**: either (a) enforce guidelines before publishing — required meta description, internal links, H2 tags, minimum word count — or (b) move member-submitted posts out of the blog entirely into a discussion forum, since forum content still counts as indexable UGC without diluting the SEO-structured blog.
- **Check if a URL is indexed**: Google `site:` + the exact URL; use `site:rootdomain.com` to see every indexed page on that domain.
- **One-page sites can't rank** (Lori B, Durango Holiday Home, built in Canva): must be split into multiple pages, each targeting a distinct keyword/service/season, before real ranking is possible.
- **Building a seasonal/service business's page architecture**: write down every distinct service and every season/holiday offered, then create one dedicated page per service/season (Christmas page now; Halloween/Easter/fall pages later); give ancillary services like "seasonal storage" their own page too.
- **Fixing an overlong homepage** (Ellie Boudoir): break topic-specific sections (e.g. "maternity boudoir") out into their own dedicated pages targeting their own keyword rather than stacking every offering on one page; convert images to WebP; remove duplicated heading tags caused by separate mobile/desktop blocks each rendering their own H-tags.
- Request indexing in Google Search Console every time a page is updated (doesn't force an immediate crawl, but is the standard nudge).

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- One page = one main keyword/topic, always. Never target the same primary keyword on two pages, and never let a single page try to rank for 2-3 keyword variants simultaneously.
- Target keyword must sit at the very beginning of both the title tag and the H1 tag.
- Never copy the title tag verbatim into the meta description — the meta description's job is to earn the click (name specific differentiators/services), not restate the title.
- Fine to use superlatives ("best") even if a competitor already claims the same word — "you're not copying... you're saying, well, actually, I'm the best."
- Cut vague catch-all phrases from headings/meta ("and more," "beautiful holidays, effortlessly handled") — Matt's test: if a totally unrelated business (e.g. a travel company) could reuse the exact same line, it's too generic.
- No dedicated page for a keyword/location = no ranking for it, period. A map graphic or an "areas we serve" text blurb is not a substitute for individual location pages.
- Don't use blog tags — platforms like Wix auto-generate a page per tag and throw SEO errors site-wide. Use 5-10 categories instead and place all content into those categories.
- Never allow open/unmoderated member or guest posting on a site's main blog without SEO guidelines (meta description, internal links, H2 tags, minimum length) — it wastes crawl budget site-wide.
- Minimum content-length benchmark stated: **5-6-700-800 words minimum** for a blog post (contrast: a flagged member post was only ~250-300 words).
- Explicit page-length ceiling: **~2,000 words max per page** (Ellie Boudoir's homepage measured ~10,000 words total, with an estimated ~2,000 words inside heading tags alone).
- Remove emojis from title tags — "Google doesn't necessarily like that."
- Orphaned pages (zero internal links pointing to them) will not rank, however good the page itself is — Google can't find them.
- Company name in the title tag (e.g. appended after a dash): optional. "Not going to hurt it, but it also might not help" — no fixed rule either way.
- Realistic client ranking timeline: no fixed number, but typically **3-6 months** before a "big win" (real traffic increase) shows up; can happen sooner but don't promise it.
- Zero/near-zero measured search volume on a long-tail or niche keyword can still be worth targeting if it signals high buyer intent (a hyper-specific B2B vertical page for a bookkeeper; "string duo" at ~0 volume).
- National keyword-difficulty/volume numbers can badly mislead for local intent — a term can show KD 88 nationally with no realistic chance a city alone accounts for that volume; check the local pack directly instead.

## Tools, prompts & resources (exact prompt wording if quoted; tool names; settings)
- **Semrush** — Matt's stated primary/preferred keyword tool: "there's not really anything free that does this kind of keyword research." Always set location to the correct country/city before reading volume/difficulty. Free trial available (about a week) for members to test before paying.
- **Semrush walkthrough video** — a ~45-minute YouTube tutorial Matt made covering Semrush end-to-end, posted publicly and shared into the Insiders community; caveat given live — UI has changed slightly since recording, but the strategies still apply.
- **ChatGPT for keyword brainstorming**: paste your page into ChatGPT and ask something like "what are some keywords and keyword variations that I should be going after for this page?" — then cross-check results in Semrush. Caveat: when Matt used ChatGPT to actually write keyword-targeted content, "it did not do a good job whatsoever."
- **Google Search Console** — set up for every new site first; use the Performance/Queries report; use "request indexing" after every page update. Insiders resource named directly: video training "Level 1 — Does Google know about your website? Do you have a sitemap? Registering your site with Google."
- **Page-audit browser extension** — used live in two audits (Neri, Jorgie) to check on-page keyword density/occurrence counts; can misreport count if the wrong URL/page variant is checked.
- **"Money Page Doctor"** — a ChatGPT-prompt/spreadsheet-driven tool that analyzes a "money page" against currently-ranking competitors and flags issues (e.g. flagged including "San Francisco Bay Area" in an H1). Caveat direct from Matt: it's "AI analyzing the page based on some rules, but it's again, not always going to be 100% accurate" — it can't know that a compared competitor omits the location simply because they physically operate there.
- **`site:` search operator** — type `site:` + a URL, or `site:rootdomain.com`, directly into Google to check indexing status of a page or an entire site.
- **CID link** — a Google Business Profile citation-link type Matt's team adds to local content/posts.
- **Wix → WordPress migration path** — flagged as the fix for Wix's page-bloat/duplicate-content problem; confirmed Wix has an official export tool via RSS-feed import; Matt's suggested next step: "have a conversation with chat to figure this out."
- **WebP image format** — recommended conversion target for image-heavy pages to improve load time.
- Insiders "video training" library, Level 1 section — canonical resource pointed to for GSC/sitemap setup basics.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Average position is a misleading north-star KPI once a site ranks for many keywords — it can rise even as SEO improves, simply because new (harder, lower-ranking) keywords start earning impressions; look at clicks instead when this number confuses a client.
- Long-tail/near-zero-volume keywords are frequently still worth targeting because low volume often correlates with high buyer intent (niche B2B verticals for a bookkeeper; "string duo" for a musician).
- Tool-reported national keyword difficulty/volume can badly mislead for a local business — always cross-check against the actual local pack/local competitors rather than trusting a blended national number.
- AI grading tools like "Money Page Doctor" are useful directional signal, not gospel: they infer rules from current top-ranking competitors and can generate false "fixes" (e.g. telling a business to remove its own location from the H1) because they can't know a competitor's omission is simply because that competitor is physically headquartered there.
- Ranking timing is unpredictable and multi-causal — a totally unoptimized page can already rank on page 2 from age/tenure, unrelated backlinks, or anchor text, while a freshly optimized page can still take 3-6 months for a "big win." Requesting indexing in GSC helps nudge it but doesn't force or guarantee a fast crawl.
- User-generated/member content creates real tension between community value and SEO health: it drives goodwill, local relationships, and off-site social sharing, but left unmoderated it typically has no internal linking, no structure, and quietly burns crawl budget. The resolution isn't banning it — it's either enforcing lightweight SEO guidelines or moving it to a forum format instead of the blog. Alicia's own framing of the tension: SEO practices can feel like they have "less integrity than what we would actually say to each other."
- Whether to invest in SEO for a very low-population, low-search-volume rural market depends on seasonality — a resident population of ~2,000 can still justify the effort if a large seasonal/tourist inflow (2 million annual visitors) drives real, if lower-volume, monthly search demand.
- Including your own company/brand name in the title tag is genuinely neutral for rankings — a stylistic/branding choice, not an SEO requirement.
- Long, image-heavy, highly designed pages (cursive logo fonts, hidden hamburger nav, big hero sections) can look good from a pure design perspective while actively hurting usability and keyword clarity — repeatedly weighed as "looks nice" against "a search engine/user can't tell what this page is for."
