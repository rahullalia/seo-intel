# Rank Week Day 1 Replay (Matt Diamante)

*Opening live session of a multi-day Rank Week event (chronologically Day 1 of this cohort, ~early Dec 2025 based on adjacent transcript dates); 77m49s, ~800–900 live attendees — the biggest Zoom session Matt says he's run. Zoom's webinar Q&A panel had to substitute for chat all session (technical hiccup, no bearing on SEO content, mostly omitted below). Core exercise: AnswerThePublic → ChatGPT two-prompt chain → manual cleanup → internal linking → meta description/cover image, done live end-to-end on Matt's own agency site as the worked example.*

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

1. Go to **answerthepublic.com**, type in whatever you sell — product or service both work (live examples: hot sauce, SEO, wedding photography, mental health/therapy, website design, iPhone/phone repair, voiceover, B2B marketplace).
2. On the results wheel, go to **"Search engines" → "Questions"** (left panel) and scroll to find a question you can actually answer.
3. Deliberately go to **page 4 or 5** of the results, not page 1 — everyone else is fighting over page-1 keywords.
4. If AnswerThePublic is down/slow: use **alsoasked.com** as a backup, or skip straight to a generic template question like **"How much does [blank] cost?"** (per Marcus Sheridan's "Big Five" — see Tools section).
5. Open a **brand-new chat** in ChatGPT (or Claude/Gemini/any LLM) and paste **Prompt 1** (verbatim below) with your question substituted in.
6. ChatGPT returns up to 10 clarifying questions — answer each one directly in the same thread, in your own words. 2–3 sentences per answer is enough as long as it reflects genuine unique experience/opinion, not exhaustive detail.
7. Once all 10 are answered, paste **Prompt 2** (verbatim below) in the **same thread** to generate the full post.
8. Copy the entire draft into a blank Google Doc / Word doc — don't build the page directly in the CMS yet.
9. Clean-up pass, line by line — remove: the AI's horizontal/vertical bar-style line breaks; the stray leading space before the first word on some lines (an AI "tell"); numbered-list structure (unless the post is genuinely meant to be a listicle); emojis; checkmarks. Em dashes are fine to leave in.
10. Tighten the title if the AI's version is generic — live example: "SEO when building a website" rewritten to **"SEO when building a website, getting it right from day one."**
11. Add exactly **3 internal links** inside the new post: highlight a relevant keyword/phrase, click the link/chain icon in the editor, paste the URL. One of the 3 must go to a product/service page; the other two to existing blog posts. If the exact keyword you want to link doesn't appear anywhere in the draft yet, write a short bridging sentence that contains it (live example: added "We have a full breakdown on how much does SEO cost at Hey Tony" specifically so "how much does SEO cost" could become anchor text).
12. Publish — works identically on WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, or plain HTML.
13. In the **same ChatGPT thread**, ask **Prompt 3** ("Write me a meta description for this page") and drop that into the CMS/SEO plugin's meta description field.
14. Optional — generate a cover image in the same thread: ask it to make a cover photo for the post **"in the style of"** an attached reference image (can be a photo already on your own site, or literally a photo/style you like from someone else's website); optionally attach brand guidelines too.
15. After the new post is live, go back to **3 existing/already-indexed pages** (ideally blog posts) and, from within each, add a link out to the brand-new post using the same highlight → link-icon → paste-URL method. This is the inbound half of the loop — the new page ends up with 3 outbound links and 3 inbound links.
16. Time-box the whole thing: **max ~1 hour/week on SEO**, publishing **1–2 posts/week** — this is explicitly designed to not become a 5–20 hour/week task.
- **"Avalanche technique" / "snowball effect"** (full version taught inside Insiders): keep stacking these small, zero-competition, near-zero-volume question posts, all interlinked. Individually they barely move traffic, but the accumulated topical authority ("big massive ball of traffic") is what eventually lets the site rank for the harder, higher-volume head keywords it couldn't touch directly at the start.
- No blog section yet? Create one first — every major builder (Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, WordPress, even hand-coded HTML) supports a native blog.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Cap ChatGPT's clarifying questions at **10** in Prompt 1 — Matt ran it uncapped once and got ~30 questions back, "way too much" since you won't use all of it.
- Answers to the 10 questions: 2–3 sentences each is enough — don't over-detail, just make each one a genuine unique thought/experience.
- Never paste raw AI output straight onto the live site — always move it to a separate doc and edit first.
- Must remove before publishing: AI-style bar line-breaks, the stray leading space before a line's first word, numbered-list structure (unless truly a listicle), emojis, checkmarks.
- OK to leave: em dashes.
- Don't run the finished post through an AI-detection tool before publishing — called explicitly pointless.
- Cap internal links inside the new post at **3**; at least 1 of the 3 must be a product/service page, not just other blog posts.
- After publishing, add inbound links from **3** older/already-indexed pages back to the new post — don't skip this half.
- Keep the whole workflow for one post (Q&A, draft, meta description, image) inside **one continuous ChatGPT thread** so later prompts self-reference earlier context — don't start fresh chats mid-process.
- Article length doesn't matter for this method, especially against an uncontested keyword.
- Keyword usage/repetition count doesn't matter for this method.
- **Zero measured search volume / "zero impressions"** on the target keyword is fine and expected — that's exactly what makes it easy to rank.
- Don't need to know, for this exercise: heading tags, title tags, meta description mechanics, Core Web Vitals — none required to get a result (helps "a little bit" but isn't necessary).
- Time budget: **max ~1 hour/week** on SEO total, **1–2 blog posts/week** — don't over-invest; a full-time job matters more.
- No paid tools required for Rank Week — everything free or free-trial, aside from optionally upgrading ChatGPT to the **$20/month** plan if you hit free-tier limits (Matt says it roughly doubled his own output).
- Works in any language — the process/prompts aren't English-only; Matt just personally works in English.
- Any LLM is acceptable: ChatGPT (free or paid), Claude, Gemini, Copilot (an attendee noted it's free/unlimited), Perplexity.

- Matt ran this exact exercise himself roughly **2.5 weeks** before this call and reported **ranking within 4 hours**, already pulling traffic.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Matt frames this explicitly as a simplified version of his real process: "ours is a little bit more in depth than this" — Hey Tony's paid client work goes further than what's taught live.
- Not claiming maximum optimization: the resulting post is "not the best SEO optimized piece of content you could write," just good enough to beat "99% of the garbage that's out there."
- Manual cleanup is a deliberate choice, not a shortcut to remove: when asked why not just prompt ChatGPT to skip the bars/formatting issues, Matt said it might save time but "I'm not trying to get chat GPT to do literally everything for me" — some human time on the piece is intentional so it's actually good/helpful, not just faster.
- Technical SEO (Core Web Vitals, title/meta mechanics, heading-tag knowledge) is deliberately deprioritized here — helpful "a little bit" but not required, and reserved for the separate biweekly technical-SEO-with-Denroy track instead.
- AI-detection avoidance is a stated philosophical position, not universal consensus: checking AI-written content with an AI detector is inherently contradictory in Matt's view ("using AI to look at AI... doesn't make any sense").
- Zero-search-volume targeting is a deliberate tradeoff: individual posts aren't meant to drive meaningful traffic alone — the payoff is compounding topical authority (the avalanche/snowball effect) that unlocks bigger keywords later, not immediate volume.
- Google may not display the exact meta description you write — it can generate/substitute its own if it judges that more relevant; set one anyway, but treat it as a strong suggestion rather than a guarantee.
- The "Big Five" framework from "Endless Customers" is referenced but only one of the five categories ("how much does it cost") is actually used in this session — the other four aren't covered here.
- Course-vs-Insiders is a deliberate positioning tradeoff: the comprehensive course suits people who want full SEO literacy; Insiders suits people who want weekly forward progress without becoming SEO experts themselves.
