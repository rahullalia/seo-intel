## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Accessibility self-test**: on Windows, open the search bar, type "narrator," turn Narrator on, and let it read your site out loud. If it doesn't read what's actually on the screen, that's a concrete bug to fix. Do this yourself in addition to (not instead of) running an SEO checker / accessibility checker.
- **Heading hierarchy**: structure content like a book — H1 (title), then H2 (chapters), then H3 (subheadings within a chapter); you can drop back down to H2 after an H3 if needed. Always flow in sequential order, never skip levels.
- **Alt text convention**: treat every image like a captioned figure in a nonfiction book ("Figure 5.8") — the alt text should describe what the image actually is.
- **Write at roughly an 8th-grade reading level** with no industry jargon, so the widest possible audience — including people relying on screen readers — understands the content. Wider comprehension = wider ranking net.
- **Add social media handles to Google Search Console** (feature still rolling out, not on every account yet): click "Add Property" → if a social media profiles option appears, add it there and just sign in — no verification step required. If you only see two boxes (for adding a website), you don't have the feature yet; wait for rollout.
- **Mining GSC for content gaps**: in the Performance report, sort by *impressions* (not clicks) to surface topics/queries you're showing up for but not getting clicked on — that's your next content brief. Also check which individual pieces of content are carrying disproportionate traffic and lean into that angle.
- **Fixing dead external links**: if a post cites an external site and that site later restructures/breaks the linked page, go back and either remove the link or replace it with a working one (same site or a different one) — don't leave users hitting a dead page.
- **SEO vs. paid ads decision**: no fixed ratio. If ads are profitable, keep running them. As organic rankings climb for the same terms/keywords, you can scale back ad spend.
- **Localizing service-area pages for non-delivery businesses** (e.g., telehealth, where you can't say "we deliver to this town" the way a florist could): anchor each city page in genuinely local hooks instead of repeating the core pitch — nearest hospitals/urgent care, local grocery stores/farmers markets carrying diet-relevant foods, local gyms (potential partners), local hiking trails or 5K routes.
- **Avoiding duplicate content across local pages**: check your top 3 competitors' local/city pages for the same keyword, note their word count, and write the same length or **~100–200 words more**.
- **Money-page linking rule**: money pages (location, product, service, collection pages) should link out to your own blog content and to authoritative external sources, and should include a link/CTA to book or convert — even if the page itself isn't the final conversion step.
- **Multilingual/bilingual local pages**: don't just translate — do fresh keyword research *in that language* (the top keyword is often different), then write using the natural keyword in context. Applies to Spanish for bilingual-physician practices, and was demonstrated with French/German for a Swiss business.
- **New post not indexing**: don't fixate on one post. Keep publishing on your normal cadence (Matt's team runs 2 posts/week for clients). To jump-start it, send the link to a few people directly, or share to email/social, to generate some initial traffic/engagement signal.
- **Forcing indexing**: Google Search Console → URL Inspection → paste the URL → if it comes back "not found," request indexing. If it doesn't take right away, repeat the indexing request until it's done (also useful for forcing a metadata refresh on a time-sensitive page, e.g. a contest page with an outdated date).
- **Backlink outreach phrasing**: never say the word "backlink" to a non-SEO person or org (e.g., a chamber of commerce) — they won't know what it means. Ask instead: "Can I include a link to my business [on your site/directory/profile]?"
- **Chamber of commerce / association membership** = an automatic backlink once you're listed; you can also add their logo to your own site noting the membership.
- **Event listings** (e.g., posting an event on Eventbrite) automatically create a backlink — no extra step needed to "claim" it.
- **Embedding social content on your site**: embed Instagram posts and YouTube Shorts on blog/website pages — increases time-on-page (a positive signal to Google) and lets visitors consume content in their preferred format. Skip embedding TikTok (may require the viewer to be signed in to view). YouTube embeds carry extra weight since Google owns YouTube.
- **LLMs.txt on Shopify**: install the Yoast SEO plugin — it auto-generates the LLMs.txt file for you.
- **Reading the new PageSpeed "agentic browsing" score**: go to pagespeed.web.dev, paste in your URL, and look at the new section scored out of 3 — covers LLMs.txt presence, Web MCP (agent tool-use support — not relevant for ~99% of businesses), cumulative layout shift, and accessibility tree well-formed (sequential heading order). A low score here does not currently affect ranking in AI Overviews or ChatGPT — it only reflects whether an AI *agent* can crawl/operate your site.
- **Structural page audits**: install the "Page Audit" Chrome extension to catch on-page issues like a missing H2 while reviewing your own blog posts.
- **Getting unstuck on content ideas**: talk it out conversationally with ChatGPT or Claude — e.g., "Hey, I'm really stuck here, what can I do, do you have any ideas?" — described as reliably effective.
- **Pumeli workflow** (free tool): paste in your website URL (optionally connect your Google Business Profile) → it auto-generates a "Business DNA" profile (fonts, colors, tagline, brand values, aesthetic, tone of voice, business details/hours) → it then auto-generates ready-to-use on-brand social posts and ad creative in multiple formats/aspect ratios, pulling in your GBP photos plus AI-generated imagery → it can also generate an AI "photo shoot" (multiple image concepts of your product/book) → and a full brand book (colors with hex/RGB/CMYK/HSL values, logo + variants, typography, values/aesthetic/tone) — useful to hand to any designer, developer, or vendor. Took about 4 minutes to populate after entering the URL.

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- ADA lawsuit exposure: the Americans with Disabilities Act mainly obligates a "place of public accommodation" with **15 employees or more**. Solopreneurs and small shops (3–4 people, ~$100K/year or less in revenue) generally are not held to the same obligation. Threat letters are frequently sent by opportunistic lawyers running what was described as "a shakedown" — don't panic and don't just pay; get advice first.
- Accessibility conformance has tiers — **Single A, Double A, Triple A** — and your legal obligation depends on which level applies to your business/situation.
- Canada accessibility note (raised in-call, unverified against statute): obligation to be "fully accessible" was described as kicking in around **50 employees**. Matt's own team (~20–22 people) added an accessibility widget to their site when they had only 5 people, purely as a precaution.
- ADA-related lawsuit claims cited in the community as running **$100,000–$150,000**; a third-party accessibility widget was described as costing roughly **$300/year** — "way less than one hour of a lawyer."
- **accessiBe** (what Matt uses on his own site) runs roughly **$300–400/year**; cheaper or one-time-payment WordPress alternatives exist.
- Don't obsess over backlink counts — see it, note it, move on. Google Search Console does **not** show your backlinks at all; you need a separate keyword/SEO tool (e.g., Semrush) to see them, and even then, don't fixate.
- Not every blog post will get indexed, rank, or drive traffic — that's expected and not a failure signal. Matt's team publishes 2 posts/week for clients and typically **6 of every 8 posts per month "aren't doing much."**
- "Money page" applies broadly: **location pages, product pages, service pages, and collection pages are all money pages.**
- There is no fixed SEO-vs-ads spend ratio ("there's no golden rule... you need 50% of your traffic from ads and 50% from search") — the only rule is: if you're profitable, keep doing it.
- Search "going viral" isn't really a thing the way social virality is — search success is intent-driven (someone actively looking), not algorithmic feed-push like social platforms. Don't describe an e-commerce ranking spike as "going viral."
- A low (e.g., 0/3) score on PageSpeed's new "agentic browsing" section is not something to panic about — it does not currently affect AI Overview/ChatGPT visibility.
- You don't need 100% / perfect scores on every SEO or accessibility checker to rank — Matt maintains near-perfect scores himself because clients and prospects scrutinize his own site for credibility as a paid service provider, not because it's a ranking requirement for a typical business.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Google Ads value is highly niche-dependent: urgent/low-consideration purchases (garage door repair) can convert well even at $2/day; considered/referral-driven services (psychotherapy) may not convert from cheap ads at all, though the client may still justify the spend for competitive visibility ("ego") rather than lead volume.
- SEO vs. paid ads is not a fixed-ratio decision — "if you're making money and profitable, keep doing it" — and ad spend should only be scaled back in response to real organic ranking gains for the same terms, not on a fixed schedule.
- E-commerce can sell out from a strong keyword ranking, but this is distinct from "going viral" — search-driven spikes come from active intent-based demand, while social virality comes from algorithmic feed-push to people who weren't looking. Don't conflate the two.
- Localizing service-area pages is much harder for non-delivery/service businesses (telehealth) than for delivery-style businesses (florist, funeral homes) — it requires creative, genuinely-local hooks rather than literal repetition of the service pitch, to avoid duplicate content across city pages.
- The new PageSpeed "agentic browsing" score (0–3) sounds like an AI-search readiness signal but is currently unrelated to ranking in AI Overviews/ChatGPT — it only reflects whether an AI *agent* can crawl/operate the site (e.g., via Web MCP), which isn't relevant for ~99% of businesses today.
- Traditional keyword research ("go after the number one keyword") is described as "slowly going to start going to the wayside" as AI search grows. The suggested replacement approach: publish content that answers real customer questions, then let performance data (sorted by impressions, not clicks) reveal what's actually being searched — rather than pre-committing to a keyword list.
- Backlink tracking is inherently incomplete: Google Search Console shows none of your backlinks at all (a separate tool like Semrush is needed), and even then the guidance is not to fixate on the count — treat backlinks as a nice-to-have signal, not a KPI.
- Perfect accessibility/SEO scores (100%, 3/3, all-green) aren't required for rankings for most businesses — Matt maintains them on his own site specifically because his own credibility as a paid SEO expert is scrutinized by prospects, which is a different bar than what a typical client needs to hit.
