## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- Two-stage blog workflow taught live on the call:
  1. Find a real customer question (from Rank Week research already done, or fresh via the Blog Idea Finder tool / findquestions.com / answerthepublic.com / AlsoAsked).
  2. Paste the question into ChatGPT (or Claude) using Prompt 1 (the "ask me up to 10 questions" prompt below) and answer the questions using voice mode, one at a time, with a pause between each.
  3. Once all questions are answered, feed the same thread Prompt 2 (the "write clean WordPress-ready blog post" prompt).
  4. Format check: H2/H3 headings only, no horizontal rules, paragraphs + bullet points where appropriate.
  5. Add an image every 200–300 words (own images best > ChatGPT-generated > stock).
  6. Add 3 internal links inside the new post: 2 to older blog posts + 1 to a money page, using descriptive anchor text (not "click here").
  7. After publishing, go back into 3 different older blog posts and add a link from each of them pointing to the brand-new post — so linking runs both directions.
  8. Model/intelligence selector tip: use "Instant" mode for the back-and-forth Q&A stage (faster, decent depth), then switch to "High" intelligence mode for the actual writing stage.
- Repurposing an interview/podcast/transcript into a blog post: modify Prompt 1 to add "Here's a transcript from a conversation I had. Use that information when you can and where you can to pull my expertise, experience, all that kind of stuff" — do not just hand over a raw transcript/YouTube video and ask for "a blog post," it won't do well.
- Diagnosing a site that dropped out of rankings (live diagnostic run on Jason's site): check Google indexing via a `site:domain` search, count total pages, pull the site in SEMrush to check ranked keywords and referring-domain count/quality, check whether authority is the bottleneck.
- When the client's platform/host blocks the Hey Tony crawler tool from auditing the site (used-car dealer on a "Cars For Sale" platform, contacted them, got a flat no): use Screaming Frog as the audit workaround.
- Domain acquisition tactic (Kim's case): if someone else owns the exact-match/full-name domain you want, DM them directly (she used Instagram) to ask about buying or trading for it. If you get it cheap, take the deal, migrate the actual site to the new domain, and 301-redirect the old domain rather than running both.
- Bootstrap backlink tactic for new web designers: add a "website design by / powered by [You]" credit link in the footer of client sites you build, linking back to your own site — legitimate at the start, but pair it with press releases and local-association links (chamber of commerce, etc.) for the long term.
- Weekly minimum viable action for anyone unsure what to prioritize: publish one blog post per week. Nothing else is required at minimum.
- For local SEO on top of the weekly blog: add 1–2 local/suburb-targeted pages per week.
- New sites with no back-catalog yet: just publish without internal links; once you have ~10 posts, start doing internal linking where it makes sense.
- Session structure used on the call itself: share both prompts in chat, then host goes camera-off/mic-off for a ~30-minute quiet writing block before returning for questions.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Clarifying-question prompt: cap at 10 questions, ask one at a time, give the user time to breathe before the next one.
- Body headings: H2 and H3 only. No horizontal rules. No other "AI-formatting" clutter — just headings, paragraphs, bullet points where appropriate.
- Images: one at least every 200–300 words.
- Internal links per new post: exactly 3 — 2 to older posts + 1 to a money page — with descriptive anchor text.
- Reciprocal step required: after publishing, must go add a link to the new post from 3 different older posts.
- Cadence: minimum 1 blog post/week.
- Local pages (if applicable): 1–2 per week, layered on top of the weekly blog post, not instead of it.
- Word count: no fixed target — "as few words as you can possibly get away with." Sweet spot is 750–1,500 words; can run longer only if the topic truly needs the depth. Don't overdo length just because you can (flagged even for the host's own money-page rewrite).
- Never feed a raw podcast/YouTube/interview transcript straight into a "write me a blog post" ask — always route it through the structured Q&A prompt first.
- Press/media mentions: okay to write "featured in [publication]" and display the publication's logo — do NOT hyperlink to them.
- When asking community members to swap backlinks to help someone (Jason's case): explicitly "don't link back and forth" — one-directional links only, avoid obvious reciprocal exchanges.
- Never post a domain name you're considering buying into a public/group chat — treat it as compromise-able information ("I hope everybody here doesn't fuck around").
- Agency pricing model (Matt's own agency, given as the benchmark answer to a packaging question): don't bill hourly. Flat monthly retainer instead.
- Agency package specifics: $3,000 USD/month or more; 8 pieces of content per month (8 blog posts, OR 8 page updates, OR 8 new pages such as location pages); 4 backlinks/month; ongoing technical fixes; monthly reporting; dedicated point-of-contact per client.
- H-tag rule reinforced by a real bug: product/page titles must be H1, not H3 (Kate's Gem Pages/Shopify vendor had miscoded them).
- Google Business Profile must be public — check for "not public due to missing information" errors, which can silently block visibility for months.
- Cannibalization rule: a blog post cannibalizes a collection/money page only if it targets the same keyword. Same broad topic with a distinctly different (typically longer-tail) keyword is fine — e.g., collection page "natural granola" vs. blog post "benefits of natural granola" = not cannibalization.
- Backlinks are page-specific, not domain-wide: if a competing page only has 5 backlinks, you're only really competing against those 5 — check follow vs. no-follow status too, don't just compare raw domain backlink totals.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- "Instant" vs "High" model intelligence is a genuine tradeoff, not a fixed rule: Instant is faster but shallower ("surface medium"); High is slower but deeper. Suggested split (Instant for Q&A, High for the final draft) is a workflow tip, not dogma.
- Word count is deliberately left to judgment — the host resists giving a hard cap, framing 750–1,500 as a sweet spot rather than a rule, because usefulness/depth should outweigh a target number.
- Raw backlink counts can mislead: a competitor's "430 backlinks" sounds intimidating, but backlinks are page-specific and follow/no-follow status matters — the real competitive set may be much smaller than the headline number suggests.
- "Powered by" credit-link backlinks are explicitly framed as legitimate only as a bootstrap/early-stage tactic for new web designers — not a durable long-term backlink strategy; must graduate to press releases and local-association links.
- Facilitating community backlinks came with a built-in caution against reciprocal ("back and forth") linking even while encouraging one-directional help — implying obvious link exchanges carry more risk than one-way links.
- Manual index requests post-publish are presented as low-stakes and contested even among practitioners ("some swear by it, some say it does nothing") — not worth treating as a rule either way.
- Cannibalization is keyword-specific, not topic-specific: overlapping subject matter between a collection page and a blog post is not itself a problem if the target keywords differ enough (e.g., differing specificity/length).
- Platform choice (Lovable) and rankability: host gave a hedged, honesty-flagged answer ("as far as I know," "I haven't used it myself") rather than asserting confidence — reinforcing that ranking success is generally driven by time and consistency rather than the site-builder tool.
- Full site redos carry real ranking risk: Jason's March rebuild is shown live causing a ranking collapse (page 7 → page 3 → off all 10 pages) despite a subsequent press release — recovery requires patience, continued content, and real backlinks, not a quick fix.
- Domain-switch decision (Kim) weighs brand-name consistency across other platforms (YouTube, podcast) against sunk effort on the current domain (9 months, but "barely anything on it") — host's advice favors long-term brand/domain match over preserving the newer, low-investment domain.
- Local-page cadence deliberately subordinated to blog cadence for a newer member (Michael) — explicit prioritization decision ("don't want to overwhelm you") rather than doing everything at once.
