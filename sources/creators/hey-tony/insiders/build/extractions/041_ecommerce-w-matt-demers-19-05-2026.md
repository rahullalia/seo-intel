## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Collection vs. product page keyword split:** go broad on the collection page (e.g. "Paddle T-Shirts"); go specific/attribute-based on product pages (material, style — e.g. "polyester paddle shirt," "silk paddle shirt").
- **Core on-page checklist (from Matt's shared Canva deck):** keyword in product name/H1 (= SEO title), in meta title, in meta description, in the first 100 words of the description, naturally throughout the copy, in at least one subheading, in an FAQ section, in image alt text, and in the image file name — plus a clean URL slug.
- **FAQs:** build them as collapsible rows/collapsible content blocks in the Shopify theme editor. Roll out to collection pages first (broadest, most weight, and far less labor) before attempting per-product FAQs, which become a huge project at scale. FAQs matter for two reasons: they preempt purchase-blocking questions (conversion) and they feed AI/long-tail search, since people now type full-sentence queries, not 3-4 word ones.
- **Fixing a page with too many H2s already (9-10+):** don't cram the keyword into yet one more H2 — instead reword an existing generic H2 into a keyword-carrying one (e.g. turn "Why buy from us" into "Why is our 5-HTP Plus formula different?").
- **Demoting non-structural headings:** "Related products" carousels and "Items added to your cart" modules that a theme auto-marks as H2 should be pushed down (H3, or H4 at the highest) — flag for a developer if it requires editing the theme code.
- **Internal linking:** point links at product pages using the target keyword in the anchor text, but only when it reads naturally — never force it.
- **"Best of" roundup blog posts** are the recommended internal-linking vehicle: e.g. "Best Paddle Shirts for Rain," "Best Paddle Apparel for Cold Weather," "Best Tennis Shirts" — list your own products and link to them with keyword-rich anchors, with an image for each because it supports the internal link.
- **Mentioning competitor products** inside these roundups is fine and often a best practice — especially for items outside your own catalog (e.g. paddles, if you only sell paddle apparel) — because Google reads it as an honest, non-self-serving recommendation. Skip this for clients who explicitly don't want competitors named.
- **File-name optimization for images:** rename the actual image file (not just the alt text) to include the target keyword before uploading — e.g. not "1234.jpg" but "black-flame-paddle-shirt.jpg." Google reads the literal file name. Framed as low priority/quick-win, not something that "ranks you to the top of Google" on its own.
- **Live keyword-research process demonstrated end-to-end in Semrush** (starting point: a guessed keyword with very low volume):
  1. Type the current/guessed keyword into Google (or straight into Semrush) and find who ranks.
  2. In Semrush, pull up **Keyword Ideas** for that seed term to see variations, volume, and keyword difficulty (KD).
  3. Click into the highest-volume variant shown to see the top-ranking page for it.
  4. Scroll to **SERP analysis** (called "the most underutilized part of Semrush") — shows page authority score, referring domains, backlinks, traffic, and total keyword count for each ranking URL.
  5. Open a top competitor's full **keyword report** (everything that URL ranks for) and manually scan it for themed sub-niches (e.g. spotted "deep conditioning cap," "microwavable" as a recurring modifier).
  6. Build a dedicated **keyword list** in Semrush ("Create new empty list") named around the emerging theme (e.g. "heat cap" + "microwavable"); add every relevant variant/synonym you find, including near-duplicates.
  7. Use Semrush's search/filter bar to catch synonyms you might otherwise miss.
  8. Open the **Keyword Strategy Builder** on that saved list to see the aggregated total volume and difficulty across every keyword in it — this is where a single 90-volume term becomes part of a much larger addressable pool.
  9. Repeat the "open competitor's full keyword list" step against a second/third competitor to make sure nothing is missed, adding any new relevant terms to the same list.
  10. Use the resulting theme(s) to build content outlines: target the highest-volume terms directly, and treat the rest as supporting concepts — worked into alt text variety, subheadings, and on-page "feature" callouts/symbols — rather than stuffed word-for-word onto one page.
  11. For long exported keyword lists (Matt's example: ~100 terms), upload the list to ChatGPT and ask it to extract the underlying themes — faster than doing it visually term-by-term.
- **Diagnosing a duplicate/wrong meta description shown by two different tools:** treat the CMS back end as source of truth. Use Cmd+F (Mac) / Ctrl+F (Windows) on the page's source/content to search for the description text and find exactly where it's actually being pulled from, then correct it there.
- **Fixing Shopify's auto-appended store name in SEO titles** (title shows product name + dash + company name automatically): there's a setting to turn this off in Shopify — ask ChatGPT to locate it. Caution: flipping it retroactively affects every product page's title at once, so confirm every page is already formatted correctly for the new behavior before changing the setting.
- **Fixing a Shopify theme's empty/injected H1** (common cross-theme Shopify bug, not limited to the Dawn theme): requires manual theme-code editing; Diffuse Digital planned to film a walkthrough for Insiders next time they fix it for a client.
- **Image/alt-text audit workflow:** use an alt-text viewer tool to see every image on a page plus its current alt text and file name in one place. Check for (a) generic/duplicate alt text repeated across visually distinct variants (e.g. every diamond-shape photo just saying "diamond"), (b) any empty alt text fields, (c) file names that don't match the optimized alt text/keyword — download, rename, and re-upload the file to fix.
- **Making a plain-text CTA clickable** (e.g. "Book your free consultation" written as static text) — not an SEO fix, but flagged as an easy conversion win to make it a real link/button.
- **Before touching a URL slug that already ranks/has traffic** (even to fix an obvious typo): first check how much organic traffic and how many sales that URL currently drives, and confirm someone can properly implement a 301 redirect. If either is unclear or a fix isn't guaranteed, leave the slug alone rather than risk it.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- SEO/meta title: keep **under 60 characters**.
- Meta description: keep **under ~155-160 characters** — old rule of thumb was 140, but Yoast has since started cutting descriptions off even shorter, so exact precision matters less than it used to.
- Keyword must appear in the **first 100 words** of the product description.
- Use the keyword naturally in **at least one subheading** — but don't add a redundant H2 just to do it if the page already has 9-10+ H2s; rewrite an existing one instead.
- **One H1 per page** — multiple H1s (one page had 3, including an empty one) is a defect to flag and fix.
- Reserve H2 for real structural sections — "related products," "items added to cart," and similar auto-injected modules should **not** be H2; demote to H3/H4 or remove the heading tag entirely.
- Every image needs alt text — **none should be empty**, and alt text must be specific per variant (not the same generic word repeated across every image of a product line).
- Rename image files to include the keyword **before** uploading — file name is a distinct, separate signal from alt text.
- Don't jam every researched keyword onto a single page — use the highest-volume ones directly, treat the rest as supporting concepts/alt-text variety.
- It's fine, even good practice, to mention or link competitor products in honest "best of" content — unless the client has explicitly opted out of naming competitors.
- Don't change a URL slug that already has organic traffic/sales unless a proper 301 redirect can be implemented — "update this with extreme caution."
- **Broken links are a bigger priority than an over-length title tag** — fix broken links first.
- Rough keyword-volume floor for a low-authority/new site (dependent on site authority and backlink strength, not a hard rule): **product pages ≥ ~30 monthly volume, collection pages ≥ ~50 monthly volume**; can go lower than that for a brand-new site.
- Character-count and heading-count "errors" flagged by a tool like Semrush are usually low-severity — fix them because "any variable we can control" is worth controlling, but don't treat them as urgent the way broken links or missing alt text are.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- FAQs pay off for both conversion and AI/long-tail SEO, but are labor-intensive at full-catalog scale — the recommended sequencing is collection pages first, individual products only selectively.
- Running a multi-niche store (tennis + padel + horseback riding apparel in one shop) creates real keyword-research friction — each niche needs its own research and doesn't share an audience — but Matt validated it can still work under a unifying frame ("it's all sports"), calling it explicitly "a good thing and a bad thing."
- Title-tag/meta-description length overages are low priority fixes ("probably just going to ignore this for the most part") relative to higher-impact technical issues like broken links, empty headings, or missing alt text — worth fixing because every controllable variable helps, but not urgent.
- URL slug changes are high-risk even when unambiguously "correct" (fixing a typo) — the right call depends on weighing existing organic traffic/sales against 301-redirect execution risk; sometimes leaving a flawed-but-ranking URL alone is the safer choice, and even a textbook-correct redirect doesn't always fully preserve traffic.
- Naming competitors in content is generally a trust signal to Google, but it's client-specific — some clients explicitly don't want competitor products mentioned, and that preference is honored rather than overridden.
- Meta-description length guidance is a moving target: the old 140-character rule of thumb has shifted as Yoast changed its own cutoff, and Google increasingly rewrites meta descriptions on its own regardless of what's set on-page — reducing how much exact character-count precision actually matters.
- Two SEO tools (a Hey-Tony-linked "On Page SEO AI" instance and Semrush) reported different character counts for the identical live meta description on Marianna's site, with no resolution found on the call — underscoring that the CMS back end, not any third-party checker, is the real source of truth.
- Deciding what to do with a broad keyword theme uncovered through research (e.g. "microwavable heat cap," born from a 90-volume seed term) depends on how much of the actual catalog fits it — apply it site-wide, to one collection, or to a single product, based on whether most/all relevant products genuinely have that attribute.
