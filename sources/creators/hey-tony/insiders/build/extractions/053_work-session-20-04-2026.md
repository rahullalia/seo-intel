## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Weekly blog-content workflow (the exercise run live on this call)**:
  1. Go to **findquestions.com**.
  2. Type in what you sell/do (examples used live: "SEO for local," "SEO backlinks").
  3. Pick a real question it surfaces.
  4. Paste the exact interview prompt (given in the call chat) into **ChatGPT**: *"I'm writing a blog post answering this question. [paste question]. Ask me anything you need to know the answer to in order to write a blog post that is truly helpful and unique. It should include my expertise, experience, case studies, examples, opinions, etc. Keep it limited to 10 questions. Asking them one at a time. I'll be using voice mode to answer them. Give me time to breathe as well before going to the next question."*
  5. Answer ChatGPT's questions using **voice mode**.
  6. Take your answers into **Claude** to have the actual blog post written.
  7. Separately: source images, then format the post in your CMS (e.g., WordPress) with images and links added.
- **Mining client-call transcripts for content (Bryn's method, endorsed by Matt for anyone running client Zoom calls)**: take the transcript of a client conversation, paste into Claude/ChatGPT, and ask "what are all the questions and answers that happened in these calls?" Use the real questions/answers as blog content — also captures your natural brand voice and how you actually handle situations.
- **Mining Zoom chat for audience sentiment (Matt's Rank Week method)**: alongside the session transcript, also feed the full Zoom chat log into Claude and ask "how are people feeling today?" Use the read (e.g., "people are frustrated, today was very hard") to adjust the next day's content difficulty or to circle back and re-explain a concept more simply.
  - To capture the chat: if you're the one running/recording the Zoom meeting, Zoom emails you the chat log afterward automatically; there's also a manual "save" option inside the chat panel.
- **Transferring a "knowledge base" from ChatGPT to Claude**: in ChatGPT, ask it to produce a `.md` (markdown) file containing "everything you know about me and everything that another AI would need to know in order to operate the same way that you do with me." Copy that file's contents into Claude to carry context over. (A `.md` file is just markdown — meant for the AI to read, not something you need to visually review yourself.)
- **Fixing an internal-linking mistake without causing overwhelm**: don't go back and re-fix everything at once. Fix "one or two a week or a day" and otherwise keep moving forward with new posts.
- **Choosing between competing product terms (bra example — "balcony bra" vs. "demi bra")**: run the candidate terms through a keyword research tool to see which one people actually search, then either standardize on one term site-wide, or build a single page comparing "type 1 vs. type 2 vs. type 3" and explain any real differences (or state plainly that they're just different names for the same thing) rather than building a separate page per synonym.
- **CTA rotation**: don't end every blog post with the identical CTA. Rotate through multiple CTA types/destinations (e.g., every third or fourth post repeats a given CTA, then switches) so you're not always pushing the same link.
- **Domain forwarding (not transferring) from GoDaddy**: use the **change name servers** method rather than the other GoDaddy-offered forwarding option. Set up the domain's email afterward, once the new host is in place.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Keyword research tools (SEMrush, etc.) only reflect the ~85% of searches that have already happened; roughly **15% of Google searches are brand new every single day** and won't show up in any keyword tool — this is the segment the "find questions and answer them" method is built to capture.
- Readability scores (Yoast-style, Flesch-based): don't chase "all green." Rule of thumb — **if an eighth grader could understand what you wrote, you're good.** The underlying algorithm "is not 100% accurate."
- Ignore Yoast-type complaints about transition-word counts ("only 25% contain transition words") — treated as noise, not worth acting on.
- Ignore "key phrase in introduction" and "key phrase density" checks — "I don't care about the key phrase and introduction or the key phrase density. Not at this point."
- Blog/SEO titles: don't overload the title with extra flavor text on top of the core phrase (live example fixed: *"What to wear for a newborn photo shoot in pregnancies by smart photographer who's seen it all"* → cut down to just *"What to wear..."*). Put the actual target keyword toward the **front** of the title, not buried at the end.
- "The Ultimate Guide to X" title framing: fine to keep using **if it's still working for your industry/audience** — don't force the word "ultimate" in if it doesn't read naturally. Ask ChatGPT/Claude for alternate title phrasings if unsure.
- The SERP title snippet not filling both preview lines is not a problem — rendering differs by device (mobile vs. desktop), and Google can rewrite your title tag and meta description regardless of what you set.
- Meta descriptions: keep them plain and direct (e.g., *"Newborn photographer shares what to wear for your newborn session,"* or *"What to wear for any [photo] session, from colors and patterns to what kids should wear"*). Don't force in a secondary keyword (e.g., "newborn photographer") on every page just because the AI tool keeps suggesting it — "it'll come up naturally... don't try to include it a bunch of times."
- Internal links: always use the CMS's **"existing page"** link option, never paste a raw web address/URL, when linking to your own pages. Matt's theory: choosing an existing page means the link auto-updates everywhere if that page's URL ever changes; a hardcoded URL breaks silently if the target URL changes (no auto-update, requires manual redirects).
- Don't try to retroactively fix a batch of prior internal-linking mistakes all at once, even if you have spare time — go slow (one or two fixes at a time). Overwhelm/feeling behind is explicitly named as **"the number one thing that ruins your SEO"** — people quit because they feel they're doing too much or don't have time, not because the tactics stop working.
- Google Business reviews mentioning staff first names: **don't retroactively ask clients to edit or change existing reviews.** Leave past reviews as-is. Only start asking **future** reviewers to omit staff names if you actually notice reviews with names getting removed/taken down by Google. Company name appearing in a review is fine — not treated as a risk.
- Blog posts must have their Google Search Console indexing setting **enabled**, not left disabled.
- CTA repetition across a blog is a real weakness to fix, but there's no single mandated cadence beyond "don't repeat the same one every time" — rotate on roughly a 3–4 post cycle.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Keyword-tool blindness is a double-edged insight: the same invisibility that lets a niche site "sneak past" competitor research (zero apparent traffic/ranking in SEMrush) also means you can't use those tools to forecast or prove ROI on FAQ-driven content before you publish it — you're trading measurability for genuine white space.
- Readability/SEO-analyzer scores are directionally useful but explicitly "not 100% accurate" — optimizing to make every check green is treated as wasted effort versus just writing at an eighth-grade clarity level.
- Title/meta tags are only ever a suggestion to Google — it can and does rewrite both regardless of what you configure, so precise on-page control (line-wrap, exact wording shown) isn't fully in your hands.
- "Ultimate Guide" titles are an old, somewhat cliché pattern, but Matt doesn't recommend dropping them outright — whether to keep the framing depends on whether it's still converting clicks in your specific industry/audience, i.e., click psychology can outweigh strict SEO logic.
- Ads (GLSA/Local Services Ads) vs. SEO: ads buy immediate visibility "in front of the right people" but don't guarantee a hire and cost money continuously; SEO is free but slow and inconsistent — "some people are seeing results very quickly, but that's not the case for everybody." Many businesses run both simultaneously, or supplement with social, rather than picking one.
- Deciding when to scale back ad spend once SEO is working has no clean formula given on this call — Matt's suggestions (ask customers directly how they found you; watch for more inbound than you can handle) are heuristics, not a system. True answer would require conversion tracking, which Matt explicitly scopes as "a whole more technical side of things" outside what these calls cover.
- CTA/content strategy and blog-deletion experiments are framed as personal, ongoing experimentation on Matt's own site, not a template to copy wholesale — repeated disclaimers that his approach won't fit every business model and shouldn't be treated as gospel.
- Fixing David's suspected internal-link/indexing problem pits urgency (pages have stopped indexing now) against sustainability (redoing dozens of past posts risks burnout) — Matt explicitly chooses the slower path even when David has spare time available immediately, prioritizing long-term consistency over a fast fix.
