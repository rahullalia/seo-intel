## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- Live work-session blog-drafting workflow used on the call:
  1. Find a real customer question — via Hey Tony Insiders → Tools → **Reddit Keywords** / **People Also Ask** / **Blog Topic Finder** (type in what you sell, e.g. "hot sauce"), or **answerthepublic.com** (Search Engines → Questions), or your own team's actual recurring customer questions.
  2. Take that question to ChatGPT or Claude with the interview-style prompt (paste prompt, paste question, answer the 10 follow-up questions one at a time) — or skip straight to the Hey Tony **Blog Post Writer** tool, which automates the same interview end-to-end.
  3. Open the post with a sentence that answers the question directly (see template under Tools).
  4. Add a bullet-point "key takeaways" block at the end.
  5. Add 2–3 internal links to existing blog posts + 1 external authoritative link + at least 1 link to a product/service (money) page.
  6. After publishing, go back into 3 different older blog posts and add a link from each pointing to the new post — speeds up indexing.
  7. Alternative/additional indexing speedup: open the new URL in Google Search Console and click Request Indexing.
- Diagnosing "why is the wrong page ranking" cannibalization (William's case): run the target keyword through the Money Page Tool; if it reports a *different* page of yours (e.g. homepage) as the #1 ranker instead of the page you're optimizing, that's the cannibalization signal.
- De-cannibalizing two competing pages (Kenneth's tip, confirmed working by William): paste the full content of both pages into Claude and ask it to compare them and recommend what to change on each so they stop competing.
- Diagnosing a page the Audit Tool flags as "not accessible"/orphaned (Linda's case): start from the homepage and try to click through to the flagged page manually; if you can't reach it via on-site navigation, it's orphaned or buried too deep — needs a link from somewhere higher in the site structure.
- Repurposing podcast episodes for SEO (Mike's case): don't publish raw transcripts as indexed pages. Instead, write a blog post answering a question people actually search, and inside it reference the podcast/guest as supporting authority — e.g. "Jim and I talked about this on our recent episode — here's what Jim had to say about ___." For frequent guests, build a "Featured/Recent Guests" page with photo + short bio + link to their episode, to pick up "podcast with [Guest Name]"-type searches.
- Handling a blog-now/service-page-later plan (Leah's case): don't pre-plan a future 301 from a blog post to a not-yet-built geo-specific service page. First check what already ranks for the target keyword — if service pages dominate that SERP, a blog post targeting the same term won't rank well regardless. Wait until budget exists for the real service page; meanwhile write a different blog post (one you won't need to redirect later) and internally link it to the new service page once it's actually built.
- Fixing a BigCommerce + DropInBlog setup that won't rank (Nelly's case): connect Cloudflare to DropInBlog so blog URLs resolve as clean same-domain paths instead of exposing query strings/raw HTML; DNS needs to sit with Cloudflare rather than being split between GoDaddy (registrar) and BigCommerce.
- Migrating off a third-party blog add-on onto a platform's native blog (BigCommerce Storefront → Blog, replacing DropInBlog): don't bother 301-redirecting the old posts — just republish the same content natively, then cancel/delete the old service; those old URLs were barely indexed/ranking anyway.
- Realtor on a brokerage-controlled site with limited editing/no GSC access (Jess's case): take it to the separate Friday "why doesn't it rank" review call for a direct look rather than trying to diagnose it in the Monday work session.
- Pricing-page/blog hygiene: whenever prices change, update every page/post that states them, not just the main pricing page — clients will hold you to whatever number they found on any indexed page, including old blog posts.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Never touch/edit a page that has started ranking well — leave it alone (told to David; reinforced to Shanae re: her golf-course pages).
- If a page is already ranking for a keyword and someone wants it converted to a different format (page → blog or vice versa), don't — unless you're fine losing that traffic.
- Don't build in a redirect for later (blog post → future service page) — extra work now, confuses Google later; only redirect once the destination actually exists.
- Check what format currently ranks (service page vs. blog post) for a keyword before choosing your own page type — writing the "wrong" format for that SERP won't rank.
- Backlinks: never reciprocate — one-directional only. Even a single reciprocal swap with one or two people "destroys the link equity you're building." Getting 10–20 people to link to you while you link to none of them is fine.
- Never sign up for directories based in a country you don't operate in.
- Do sign up for local directories — chamber of commerce, local newspaper, local business directory — they send local relevance signals. National/global generic directories are lower-value ("lower level links") but not harmful.
- Domain forwarding (old domain → new domain, homepage → homepage) is fine, even after the old domain's already been printed on business cards/QR codes.
- Full domain changes are high-risk: expect to lose essentially all existing rankings and backlink equity even after notifying Google of the move — it's effectively starting over.
- Before any major site change (domain switch, redesign, restructure): keep any page that's already getting traffic as close to unchanged as possible — content and URL.
- Image alt text: use the exact target keyword phrase on roughly half (or fewer) of the images on a page — e.g. on a page with 12 photos, "maybe half, not even." Never repeat the same keyword phrase sequentially across every image (don't do "Newborn photographer Chicago 1/2/3/4").
- Keep each page focused on one topic/keyword — don't stuff the identical keyword into every image's alt text sitewide; differentiate per page (e.g. interior photography vs. exterior photography alt text on their respective pages).
- Word count: no fixed target for question-based/long-tail posts — competition is already lower given keyword specificity. If you want a number, check what's currently ranking for that exact keyword and match their length.
- SEMrush: trust **Keyword Difficulty** as the primary signal; don't weight "Competitive Density" heavily.
- Don't pay for both Yoast and SEMrush — do keyword research anywhere (e.g. Ubersuggest) and manually enter the numbers where needed.
- New blog post checklist before publishing: 2–3 internal links to existing posts + 1 external authoritative link + at least 1 link to a product/service page; after publishing, add links from 3 older posts pointing to the new one.
- GSC gives **10 free index-request submissions per day** — use them; pages typically index/rank within **about 2 hours** of requesting.
- Don't paste an already-finished, question-answering blog post into ChatGPT and ask it to "make this SEO friendly" — it can go off the rails and change the topic. Directly answering the question already makes it SEO-friendly; it isn't about keyword density.
- Pricing posts: state prices plainly ("these are our prices") rather than date-stamping them ("as of March 2026") — and still update every instance sitewide whenever pricing actually changes.
- Cornerstone content isn't something to deliberately build toward — treat it as something that emerges naturally, not a checklist item.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Claude vs. ChatGPT isn't absolute: Claude is the default for SEO blog writing (more natural prose, fewer edits, and it's what powers Hey Tony's own Blog Post Writer), but Matt still reaches for ChatGPT when Claude's output on a specific task (e.g. newsletter copy) comes out "long and convoluted."
- Domain forwarding is generally safe but not risk-free: fine for a clean-history domain, but a domain with a lot of bad backlinks or a prior Google penalty could carry risk into the destination — tempered by Matt's belief that Google is generally smart enough to discount deliberate negative-SEO link dumping from competitors.
- Podcast transcripts look like they should help SEO (dense, keyword-rich text) but don't, because raw transcripts don't answer one specific searched problem — the fix is extracting question-answering blog content and citing the episode as supporting authority, not publishing the transcript itself.
- Reciprocal links vs. one-directional: even limited/occasional reciprocal exchanges are treated as link-equity-destroying, while receiving unlimited one-directional links (without linking back) is fine — a deliberate asymmetry, not a rounding error.
- Chasing a low-value keyword you already rank for vs. redirecting effort to a higher-value one (William's case): reassigning a page to target a bigger keyword risks losing the smaller, already-secured ranking — a real tradeoff, not a free upgrade. Kenneth's Claude-comparison trick manages the transition rather than removing the risk.
- Image alt text/titles are valuable for topical relevance (Jason's case) but only where the underlying query has real search demand (e.g. "architectural photographer in Atlanta") — optimizing alt text for something nobody searches (a specific building's name) does nothing, and over-tagging every image sitewide with the same phrase dilutes each page's topical focus.
- AI answer-engine visibility panels (WIX, SEMrush-equivalent) are informative but not fully trustworthy — they generate their own guessed prompts about what a site is about rather than reflecting real user queries, and there's currently no way to directly edit what an AI says about a business.
- Manual index requests and reciprocal internal linking are both offered as indexing accelerants, not mutually exclusive — use either or both after publishing.
- Word count guidance is deliberately non-prescriptive for long-tail/question-based posts: competition is already low from the keyword's specificity, so length should follow the topic's actual needs rather than a target number, with competitor benchmarking offered only as a fallback if a number is truly wanted.
- Not every AI critique of a piece of content should be accepted wholesale (Morvi's case): a blanket "make this SEO friendly" pass can derail the topic entirely, but individual suggestions can still be legitimate improvements worth judging on their own merits.
