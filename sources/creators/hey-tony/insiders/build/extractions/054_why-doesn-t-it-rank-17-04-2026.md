# Why Doesn't It Rank — 17.04.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Lead-magnet landing pages**: build them on your own website (not just inside your CRM/GHL funnel builder) so they're indexable and findable via organic search, while still routing form submissions into your CRM. Add internal links plus external links out (e.g., to your LinkedIn company page). A "hub" page can collect case studies, lead magnets, and testimonials together.
- **External links don't have to leave your orbit**: link out to a press release about you, an award site, the Better Business Bureau, or your Chamber of Commerce — all valid, low-risk external links.
- **Add a pricing page** if you don't have one — buyers comparing options need the price to justify choosing you over competition; no price shown = harder sale.
- **Unlinked-mentions backlink play**: find every place that already mentions your name/business without a link, then email them with the exact paragraph already written, link inserted — so all they do is copy-paste it in. (Example: a weed-store client's rankings "went through the roof" after this.)
- **Set up Google Alerts** for your own name/business name so future mentions notify you automatically.
- **Finding who's already talking about you**: use ahrefs' unlinked-mentions tool (paid, expensive) or Google Alerts (free).
- **Paywalled mentions**: if a local newspaper mentions you behind a paywall without linking, ask for a direct link anyway — takes ~5 minutes, low cost, and Google likely still crawls behind the paywall.
- **Guest posting**: reach out to adjacent-audience sites (e.g., certification schools in your field) offering to write content for them in exchange for a backlink — frame it as "I want to help you create content," not "give me a link."
- **Local backlinks/mentions**: join your local Chamber of Commerce and local directories; look at where your dominant local competitors (e.g., roofers beating a gutter-cleaning business) get their backlinks and get listed in the same places.
- **Backlink gap analysis**: compare your backlink profile against competitors' to find links they have that you don't, then go get those same links.
- **Favorite backlink strategy (Matt's own)**: sponsor a local charity (e.g., become a "bronze sponsor") for a small fee just to get a logo/link on their site — backlink + tax write-off + goodwill in one move.
- **Leverage unsolicited on-air praise**: if people are praising you unprompted on a podcast/radio show, ask the host to post episodes to YouTube (auto-transcribed = crawlable text = SEO value) rather than leaving them Facebook-Live-only; then contact the specific people who praised you on-air and ask them to link back to your site from their own local website.
- **Borrowing someone else's third-party press for E-A-T**: don't link away from your own site to their old articles — instead go TO those sites and ask them to link back to your site (unlinked-mentions play applied to a person's professional bio).
- **E-A-T build-out for expertise/health niches**: create individual sub-pages per credentialed team member (doctor, therapist, etc.); add a relevant quote from that expert plus their credentials into existing articles; if no published study exists yet, link out to the expert's LinkedIn or other authoritative profile; conference talks and published papers are good credibility "juicers" (Kenneth's term).
- **Google Business Profile (GBP) post formula**: put the target keyword at the very top (optionally in quotes) → link to the specific service page you want that keyword to rank for → add your GBP CID link → link to a PREVIOUS GBP post at the bottom (internally linking your own GBP posts to each other). Post at minimum Monday/Wednesday/Friday.
- **Getting your CID link**: open Google Maps → search your business → click Share → copy the link → paste into Matt's "What is my CID" tool. Linking your website to your GBP via the CID link passes more authority than linking to a generic Maps place-page link.
- **GBP "Edit Services"**: click into each listed/suggested service and add a brief description for every relevant one — a quick, high-value fix for a dormant profile.
- **Competing against a category-flooding rival** (e.g., gutter cleaner vs. roofers dominating the map pack): lean into your narrower specialization in your content — you only do gutters/leaf guards, they do everything — rather than trying to out-broad them.
- **Relocating a business**: start publishing blog content about the NEW location now, before moving, while keeping the site's current copy pointed at the CURRENT location; try to register a GBP in the new location ahead of the move; once the old physical location closes, go through every page and swap location references old → new.
- **Money-page vs. homepage conflict**: if your homepage naturally outranks your intended "money page" for the target keyword, stop fighting it — treat the homepage AS the money page (add the keyword/talk about the topic more there), and point future blog links at the homepage instead.
- **City/location "orphan" pages**: don't blanket-write a blog post for every target city. Only write location-specific blogs where there's genuine local value ("best venues in Dallas," "best magicians in Dallas") and link those to the city page. For general topic posts, just name-check 2–3 service-area cities in the body and link back to those location pages — don't over-build content per market.
- **Diagnosing weak GBP call-conversion**: check the mobile vs. desktop split in GBP analytics; if mostly mobile, make sure the phone number is a clickable, text-labeled number (not just an icon) anchored at the BOTTOM of the screen — bottom beats top for thumb reach while scrolling.
- **Legal/ToS page indexing**: safe to noindex low-value legal/policy pages with high bounce and no sales value — EXCEPT pages people specifically search for by name (navigational intent, e.g. "security document request"); check keyword-level traffic before noindexing rather than applying one rule to the whole category.
- **Fixing brand confusion against a bigger, similarly-named competitor**: build consistent-name social profiles everywhere, get more backlinks, be active on Reddit and other forums/communities, run local press releases, join the local Chamber of Commerce — the goal is enough name-mentions/links that Google stops assuming you meant the other company.
- **Fix internal linking gaps**: check that existing articles link to each other (a gap Christopher hadn't noticed on his own site).
- **New or platform-migrated sites** (under ~1 month old): don't expect ranking movement yet — Google needs time to trust a new site; keep publishing and wait.
- **H1 tag audit**: confirm your H1 is the page's target keyword, not the site logo/name accidentally set as H1 (a common theme default) — every page's H1 should be unique and match that page's keyword.
- **URL vs. keyword mismatch decision framework**: before renaming a URL to match a "better" keyword, check whether the current page (a) ranks for anything, (b) gets traffic, (c) has backlinks. None of those → safe to change the URL. Any of those present and working → don't change the URL; consider adjusting the on-page keyword focus instead, or leave it alone.
- **Avoid industry jargon in targeting** — write for "the average Joe," not people already in your industry (e.g., "help desk" is internal/corporate; better public phrases: "how do I fix my computer," "computer help," "computer services near me"). For nationwide/remote service businesses, get specific with virtual modifiers, e.g. "virtual IT support," "virtual Outlook inbox IT support."
- **Zero-volume keyword workaround**: broaden or vary the phrasing ("lawyer headshots" → "professional lawyer headshots" → "attorney headshots" → "law firm headshots") until you find a version with real volume/difficulty data, then append the city name even if that exact combined phrase shows 0 volume.
- **Deciding if a 0-volume keyword is worth it**: check who ranks on page 1 for it and look at their backlink counts — if any page-1 result has zero backlinks, the keyword is winnable despite the 0-volume reading.
- **Competitor traffic check**: use Semrush's Domain Overview on a competitor's domain for estimated organic traffic, ranking keywords/positions, and backlink counts; the target country can be switched inside the tool.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- Never do reciprocal/mutual link exchanges with peers — links are a vote, and "if two people are voting for each other... that kind of cancels it out."
- Out-of-state backlinks carry different (lesser) relevance weight than same-state ones for a locally-focused business.
- Homepage must canonicalize to itself — this is correct, expected behavior, not an error, even if a platform (Squarespace/GoDaddy) won't give full manual control over that tag.
- A brand-new site (under ~1 month old) will not rank meaningfully yet, regardless of on-page fixes — it needs time before Google trusts it.
- Don't add random/irrelevant expert quotes or studies just to check an E-A-T box — any quote or external link must be topically relevant to that specific piece of content.
- **GBP posting cadence**: Monday/Wednesday/Friday at minimum; 1–2x/week is the absolute floor.
- Request reviews consistently/continuously — treat it as an always-on habit, not a one-time push.
- SEO tool metrics (search volume, keyword difficulty, Semrush's Map Rank Tracker position) are estimates/snapshots from the vendor's own database/servers — "not going to be 100% accurate." Cross-check against real incognito search results and actual conversions/reviews.
- **~15% of all searches every day are brand-new queries** never searched before — one reason a 0-volume-in-tool keyword can still be worth targeting.
- Keyword difficulty is calculated largely from the backlink profiles of pages currently ranking for that term — heavily backlinked top results make a keyword harder.
- GBP appears to prefer/require its built-in CTA buttons (Book Now/Call/Visit) over a raw hyperlink pasted into post text — one member's link-in-post submissions were rejected as "against terms and conditions" (Matt's own team doesn't see this on their clients' profiles — possibly account/site-specific, unconfirmed).
- New-location ranking timelines vary: **3–6 months, possibly up to a year**, depending on existing authority/backlinks, competition, and market.
- Gray-area tactic (seen working, not endorsed): some businesses put their target keyword directly into the GBP business-name field to dominate that exact-match search; Matt has seen clients do this and confirms it works, but explicitly does not recommend it.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Keyword-tool data (search volume, difficulty, Semrush's Map Rank Tracker position) is directionally useful but explicitly acknowledged as inaccurate/estimated/a "snapshot" — cross-check against real incognito search results and actual conversion/review data rather than trusting the dashboard alone.
- Reciprocal link exchanges are discouraged as mutually-cancelling, but the call surfaces a real gray area: a member proposed getting one-directional links from a large community while only pretending she'd reciprocate — Matt's response ("I wouldn't lie to people, but you do what you got to do") stops short of endorsing it without shutting it down either.
- URL-vs-keyword conflicts require a cost/benefit judgment call, not a blanket rule: changing a URL to match a "better" keyword is only safe if the existing URL has no traffic/rankings/backlinks; otherwise the disruption risk outweighs the keyword-match benefit.
- 0-search-volume keywords aren't automatically worthless — worth targeting when real buyer intent plausibly exists locally/nicheally AND current page-1 competitors show weak/zero backlink profiles; otherwise skip.
- Noindexing low-value legal/ToS pages is a good default, but navigational-intent traffic (someone searching for one specific document/form by name) is an exception worth preserving — requires checking keyword-level traffic, not a blanket page-category rule.
- Competing against a broader, entrenched competitor category (gutter-cleaner vs. roofers dominating the map pack) is framed as a differentiation play (niche specialization), not a rankings hack — realistic expectation-setting, not a quick fix.
- The exact-match-business-name-as-keyword GBP tactic is presented as something Matt has witnessed working for others while he explicitly declines to recommend it — a live gray/black-hat tactic acknowledged to exist without confirming it's safe or sanctioned.
- A backlink's value is treated as partly dependent on geographic relevance to the business (out-of-state links "definitely matter" differently) — cuts against a one-size-fits-all "just get any backlink" approach.
- Google's local map-pack UI may be shifting (reviews/star ratings reportedly missing for one member, unconfirmed by anyone else on the call) — a reminder that local SEO surfaces can change without notice and shouldn't be over-indexed on a single observation.
