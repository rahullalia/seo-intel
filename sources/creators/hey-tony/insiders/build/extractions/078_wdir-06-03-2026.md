## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Rank Week prep (logistics):** Sunday = team tech check on Zoom (waiting-room testimonial video, slides/screen-share verification). Monday = two work sessions back to back (regular insiders session + Rank Week session). Come to Monday with a question already pulled from **Answer the Public** for day one's work session.
- **Standing weekly cadence:** Monday is "blog day" for the insiders group.
- **Matt's own blog-writing workflow during Rank Week:** come in with either a question already picked or a post already started, then finish by finding images, doing internal linking, and sanity-checking that it's actually valuable to a real reader — not just a checkbox exercise.
- **Building an interactive quiz/questionnaire as a freebie (Jennifer's method):** (1) use ChatGPT to develop the concept and question set; (2) build/code the interactive flow in Claude Code; (3) connect it to GoHighLevel to capture the lead; (4) end with a branching result (e.g., "your problem is messaging/website" vs. "your problem is SEO/traffic") and a CTA to book an audit.
- **Client audit → CTA fix (AI Readiness Alliance case):** review the client's existing site, flag that there's no clear CTA at the top and no defined focus keyword/key phrase, and tell them directly the current approach isn't working — propose a clear top-of-page CTA plus a defined keyword focus.
- **Working an on-page/technical audit tool's flagged issues:** go down the tool's "down to top" issues list in order; for generic content pages (author bio, privacy policy, category/legal pages) flagged for missing meta descriptions, treat as non-issues — the tool can't tell which pages matter; only fix flags on real content-facing pages (e.g., a "pricing" category page missing a meta description).
- **What to do once a page starts ranking (even weakly):** stop editing/re-optimizing the page itself. Instead, reinforce it externally — publish topical blog posts and build internal links pointing to it — and let time do the rest.
- **LinkedIn-post-to-blog-post workflow for thought leadership:** take existing LinkedIn posts (written by SMEs/leadership), use ChatGPT to flesh each one out into a full blog post on your own site, then cross-link the two (blog post links to the specific LinkedIn post as a quote/authority signal; LinkedIn post can link back to the blog for full detail).
- **Social sharing rule:** post short-form takes with your own added thoughts/context on platforms that support clickable links (LinkedIn, Facebook, X/Twitter, Reddit, Tumblr, Pinterest), and use that post to link to the full blog post on your site — don't just paste a bare link.
- **Roofing contractor content pairing (portfolio + blog):** keep the portfolio/project page as the visual proof piece (photos of the completed job, e.g. a hospital roof) aimed at conversion; write a separate blog post about the *type* of roofing/material used and its benefits, and inside that post link to the specific portfolio project. This creates two potential ranking surfaces (the roof type and the named project) tied together.
- **Boosting a blog post's credibility with third-party quotes:** get a quote from the GC or architect on why a given roofing choice matters, and link their name to their LinkedIn profile — adds validity and shows "you're not just making this up."
- **Avalanche method (keyword sequencing):** target low-volume/low-difficulty keywords first to win them, which signals to Google that your site can be trusted with traffic (engagement, clicks, time on page, conversions); only after that foundation is built do higher-volume/higher-difficulty keywords become winnable. Described as building floor by floor (foundation → 1st floor → 2nd floor…).
- **Diagnosing a page that won't rank for its target keyword (Etsy/Shopify t-shirt case):** pull the live SERP for the exact keyword and look at what page *type* is actually ranking (collection/category page vs. product page). If the top results (including on Amazon) are dominantly collection pages, stop trying to rank a product page for that term — build/optimize the matching collection page instead.
- **Competitor SERP teardown technique:** check the top-ranking pages' heading structure (is H1 actually first, or buried under a run of H3s?), and how much/little they literally repeat the keyword in body copy — match or beat whatever the winner is actually doing, since sometimes the bar is lower than expected (e.g., the #1 result barely used the keyword at all).
- **Fixing keyword placement + heading order (same case):** move the target keyword phrase to the very top of the page (in the big/H1 text), and correct the tag order so H1 leads rather than following several H3s.
- **Avoid internal-link topic mismatches:** if a "ski t-shirts" collection page links to a blog post titled "what makes a good t-shirt" (not ski-specific), that mismatch confuses Google about what the collection page is actually about — keep the linked content's topic aligned with the linking page's own target keyword.
- **Naming products/collections for searchability:** don't name a product after an internal joke or cryptic code (e.g., "10:27") and expect it to rank — nobody will search that string. Build the primary title/keyword around what people actually type (e.g., "Apres-ski t-shirt," "funny apres-ski t-shirts") and save the joke/wordplay for the description copy.
- **Multi-location service business SEO, in order:** (1) build a dedicated page per location — do this before anything else; (2) write blog posts that link to those specific location pages; (3) set up citations/Google Business Profile for every location you can — skip citations for locations you can't cover rather than blocking on it.
- **Diagnosing a page missing from the XML sitemap (Rank Math):** (1) confirm the page is actually set to index; (2) confirm it isn't excluded from the sitemap; (3) right-click → View Source on the live page and check the canonical tag — verify it points to the page's own URL and not a different/duplicate URL; (4) check for redirects or a second competing URL for the same content; (5) if the root cause can't be found, delete and recreate the page to force a clean re-index.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Bring an **Answer the Public** question to Rank Week's Monday session — come prepared, not empty-handed.
- Ignore audit-tool flags on non-content pages (author, privacy policy, legal/accessibility, generic category pages) missing meta descriptions — "these are really just non-issues."
- Once a page shows ranking movement (even just page 3), **do not** touch/re-optimize it further — a member over-optimized after reaching page 3 and dropped out of the rankings entirely as a direct result.
- **Page 3 ≠ position 3** — explicitly called out as a critical distinction; don't panic-react to "page 3" as if it were rank #3.
- Never publish the same full content on both your own website and LinkedIn — LinkedIn has higher authority, and Google can flag the weaker copy as duplicate content, which can hurt your SEO.
- LinkedIn posts sharing a blog link should run roughly **100 words** of your own added thought/context, not a bare link.
- Don't post bare links with no framing/hook on any platform.
- **Never post links on Instagram or TikTok** — those platforms don't support clickable links in posts.
- Don't build a plugin/widget that auto-pulls your latest social (e.g., LinkedIn) posts onto your website — slows the site down and isn't useful to visitors; compared directly to old "latest tweets" / "latest Facebook posts" widgets. Use static social profile links instead.
- One target keyword per page (reaffirmed as the standard approach multiple times).
- For multi-location businesses: location pages come first, full stop, before other tactics.
- If full location-citation coverage isn't feasible, skip the gaps rather than stalling progress.
- Two-subfolders-deep URL structures before reaching the real content page are a red flag — one subfolder (`/services/`, `/blog/`, `/video/`, `/case-studies/`) is the norm.
- A page's canonical tag must match its own URL — a mismatched/duplicate canonical is a real, fixable defect (in this case, the actual cause of the sitemap-omission bug), not cosmetic.
- Site speed flagged as a likely bottleneck/priority ("if anything the thing that's slowing you down is the site speed") — fix ahead of chasing smaller audit line items.
- Watch for tool inconsistency: a title checked in the page-level view can read "correct" at a given character count while the tool's aggregate "overview" simultaneously flags it as "too long" — don't take either view as gospel in isolation.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Portfolio/project pages are built for conversion (visual proof), not necessarily to rank — pairing one with a topic-focused blog post is how you actually capture search intent while keeping the portfolio page doing its trust/conversion job.
- Realistic product photography (e.g., models actually skiing/in-context) matters more for user trust and conversion than it's confirmed to matter for ranking directly; Matt acknowledges Google's image-understanding AI has improved but is explicitly unsure how much algorithmic weight it carries — framed as a genuine open question, not a settled ranking factor.
- Auto-embedding your own social feed on your site vs. writing standalone content: skip the live-pull plugin (low value, adds load time) but still let the content exist once as a full blog post and link out to/from the social version rather than mirroring it verbatim.
- Big-ticket B2B sales ($100K/12-week program) can still benefit from SEO/content top-of-funnel discovery even when internal stakeholders assume "nobody buys $100k programs from a website visit" — the counter is that answering decision-makers' actual search questions drives them to the page, and the deal still closes through direct outreach/CTA rather than self-serve checkout.
- Not every flagged audit "error" needs action — generic audit tools can't distinguish an intentionally noindexed or low-stakes page (author bio, legal page) from a meaningful content gap, so judgment is required before working down the list.
- Citation coverage for multi-location businesses is a "do what you can" tactic, not all-or-nothing — partial coverage still helps; missing locations shouldn't block moving forward.
- Diana's own take on blog cadence: writing "smart," genuinely interesting posts that teach her something too (not just the reader) is a deliberate win-win choice, even though she acknowledges it takes meaningfully longer than a fast one-hour post — presented as a personal tradeoff she's making consciously, not a mistake to fix.
