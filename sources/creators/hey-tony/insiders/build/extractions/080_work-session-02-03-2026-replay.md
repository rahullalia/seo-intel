## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Free keyword/topic research process (AnswerThePublic)**:
  1. Go to answerthepublic.com.
  2. Type in a term related to your business/product (e.g., "meta ads"). If nothing useful comes up, get more generic (e.g., "social ads," "Facebook ads," "web hosting") or more specific depending on results — people search using different vocabulary than you expect.
  3. Click "Search engines" on the left nav, then scroll down to "questions." A wheel/visualization of questions loads.
  4. Don't just take page 1 — scroll to **page 2, 3, or 4**. These questions are more ignored by other content creators and likely easier to rank for.
  5. Volume data sometimes shows next to a question (not always). Even ~10 searches/month is fine to target — you may end up ranking for more keywords than just that one exact question.
  6. Pick a question you can actually answer from your own expertise.
  7. Same exact process works using Google's "People Also Ask" section instead of AnswerThePublic.
- **ChatGPT blog-drafting process (the core weekly exercise)**:
  1. Open ChatGPT, paste the interview prompt (see Tools section) with your chosen question dropped in.
  2. ChatGPT asks up to 10 clarifying questions to pull out your real expertise/experience.
  3. Reply with the voice-mode follow-up prompt (see Tools section) instead of typing answers.
  4. Click ChatGPT's voice mode and answer the 10 questions aloud, one at a time — it transcribes the whole conversation automatically.
  5. Once all questions are answered, prompt ChatGPT to write the blog post using those answers.
  6. Result: AI-written, but built entirely from your own words/ideas/experience ("although it is written by AI, it's all you").
- **Fixing a niche keyword that returns nothing on AnswerThePublic** (Jason's ColdFusion web hosting case): broaden the search term (typed "web hosting" instead of "ColdFusion web hosting") to get real questions, then write the post about the broader topic while pulling the answer back toward your specific niche/product inside the body (e.g., "actually, ColdFusion is the best way to do this"). This builds ranking/authority on the broader term, which makes it easier to eventually rank for the ultra-specific niche term.
- **Mandatory internal linking after every post**: add 3-5 internal links FROM the new post to other existing pages on your site, then AFTER publishing, go find 3 other already-existing (ideally already-indexed) pages and add links FROM those pages TO the new post. This signals to Google the new content is valuable, gives it context, and helps Google discover/crawl the new page faster if the linking pages are already indexed.
- **Weekly accountability step**: after publishing, post the link to your new blog post as a comment reply under Matt's own posted link inside the Hey Tony Insiders community.
- **Reworking location/service ("money") page linking structure** (walked through live for Manny, who is redoing city + service pages a previous contractor built poorly):
  - Keep linking FROM blog posts TO service pages (already correct).
  - Location pages can cross-link to neighboring location pages (e.g., "if you're in [nearby city], we also service that area").
  - Only link 2-3 blog posts max from any given location/service page — don't repeat/reuse the same blog post link across every location page.
  - A money page about pricing (e.g., "how much does X cost") can and should link out to a relevant blog post covering that topic, and vice versa.
- **Title/H1 search-intent audit method** (used live on V's blog list): for every existing title, ask "what would a real person actually Google to find this piece of content?" Editorial/poetic titles that don't map to an actual search query need reworking toward real search phrasing, even when the underlying content is strong — e.g. rewrite "When your strength tattoo feels like a lie" toward something like "strength tattoo pros and cons" or "strength tattoo meanings."
- **Table of contents layout fix** (for Jennifer's stacked/crowded TOC): rebuild it as a slim side column (roughly 1/4 to 1/3 page width) running alongside the main content (2/3 to 3/4 width) rather than stacked above it, so the actual content is visible the moment someone lands on the page; make the side TOC anchored/sticky so it follows the reader down the page as they scroll.
- **Live worked example of a full post's link structure** (from the post Matt wrote during this same call): external link → internal link to a "content ideas for your website" blog post → internal link to a "writing compelling headlines" blog post (originally published in 2023 — still linked because still relevant, proving recency isn't required) → another external link ("how to do keyword research") → internal link to a service page at the very end. Order is flexible, not a required template.
- **Work-session format**: Matt pauses the recording, gives everyone roughly 30 minutes to independently research/write/publish, then resumes to do Q&A and review — recurring every Monday.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- AnswerThePublic: work **page 2, 3, or 4** of results, not page 1 — those questions are more ignored and easier to rank for.
- ~10 searches/month on a single PAA-style question is an acceptable target — don't dismiss low-volume questions.
- Paid keyword tools (SEMrush, Ubersuggest) are optional, not required — the goal is to keep SEO "as accessible as possible"; members are already getting real traffic and sales using free tools only (AnswerThePublic/PAA + ChatGPT).
- ChatGPT clarifying-questions prompt: cap it explicitly at **10 questions**.
- Every new post needs **3-5 outbound internal links** to other pages on the site.
- Every new post needs **3 inbound internal links** added from other already-existing pages, after publishing.
- Location/service pages: link to **2-3 blog posts max** per page; never reuse the same blog link across all location pages.
- Don't let your blog title/H1 render with a trailing "..." (three-dot truncation) on your site/platform — if that's happening, Google literally cannot read what's past the cutoff; this is a real, fixable problem, not cosmetic.
- Title-tag vs. on-page heading: the on-page title shown in a page's rendered "search-style" preview (e.g., inside Shopify) is likely the H1/heading tag, not the actual `<title>` tag — the real title tag only shows on Google's results page, in browser tabs, or on social shares. (Question of whether to hard-lock titles to 50-60 characters and meta descriptions to 120-150 characters was raised on the call by a member but not directly confirmed or endorsed by Matt — he redirected to the title-tag-vs-heading distinction instead.)
- Meta description should contain the target keyword.
- Schema markup: don't bother with complicated/custom schema. Matt uses none beyond whatever WordPress adds automatically by default.
- Cadence: one published page per week is explicitly framed as "fantastic" — two is "great," but one/week is a sustainable, acceptable pace. Not everything gets done at once; it's a process.
- Manual Search Console indexing requests weren't discussed in this call, but the general instruction given was to keep testing/iterating on structural details (conclusion vs. "key takeaways" wording, table-of-contents placement) rather than treating any one format as fixed doctrine.

- **Google "People Also Ask"** — same method as AnswerThePublic, confirmed as equally valid ("Yep. Okay. Yeah, 100%").
- **ChatGPT — clarifying-questions prompt** (Matt's live dictation, assembled): *"I'm writing a blog post about [your chosen question/topic]. Ask me any questions you'd need to know the answer to in order to write me a helpful and unique blog post answering that question. ## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Authority Score rising is a genuinely good signal (more traffic and/or backlinks) but Matt explicitly separates "vanity metric moving" from "confirmed ranking improvement" — it's a made-up metric, not a ranking factor.
- Paid keyword-research tools can sharpen volume/targeting confidence, but are optional add-ons, not prerequisites — the entire session's framing is that free tools alone (AnswerThePublic/PAA + ChatGPT) are already producing real traffic and real sales for members on the call.
- Deliberately targeting a broader keyword than your true niche (ColdFusion → "web hosting") sacrifices exact-match relevance short-term in exchange for building topical authority that should make the ultra-specific niche term rankable later — a bet, not a guarantee.
- Schema markup: Matt's stance is skeptical and deprioritized, but he explicitly caveats his own supporting data point (non-ranking pages had more schema than ranking pages) is drawn from one site (Hey Tony's) and not yet confirmed across client sites — he's actively testing before treating it as a firm rule.
- Title tag vs. rendered on-page heading vs. platform snippet are three different layers that get conflated when debugging a "truncated title" complaint — fixing the wrong layer (e.g., editing the H1 when the real issue is the `<title>` tag, or vice versa) won't solve the problem.
- Internal-link ordering within a post (which link type goes where) is flexible — Matt's own example order is illustrative, not a required template.
- Location/service page cross-linking carries a light-touch cannibalization-style caution (cap at 2-3 blog links, don't repeat the same link everywhere) without being framed as a hard technical penalty — more a "don't overdo it" norm than an enforced rule.
- Rank Week participation is intentionally low-pressure: members can promote their insider experience/wins publicly during the sprint, or simply "lurk" and do the work quietly — either is fine as long as real work gets done.
