# 101 — WDIR Call Replay (09.01.2026)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Multi-division/multi-site client diagnosis** (TrueCraft roofing): ask "does someone who needs [service A] also need [service B]?" If not, don't route Google Business Profile (GBP) traffic to a shared portfolio/holding-company page — point it directly at the one specific division site that needs the ranking help right now.
- **Don't flip-flop a GBP's linked website** — pick one destination and leave it; Google can take **1–3 months or more** to "learn" what a site is about after a link change.
- **Getting a second GBP at the same street address**: you need proof of a physically separate entrance (e.g., a window sticker) to register another business entity's profile there — TrueCraft's construction division got one this way; recommended next for the commercial division too.
- While a division has no GBP yet, **build backlinks to it in the meantime**.
- **Homepage H1 fix**: rewrite a vague tagline (e.g., "Our quality is our reputation") into something that states both the service/industry AND the location — e.g. "Residential Roofing in Cincinnati" — then keyword-research the exact phrasing (e.g. "Cincinnati roof replacement" vs. "roof replacement Cincinnati" vs. "roof replacement, Cincinnati, Ohio").
- **Rename "News" sections to "Blog"** if the content is just blog posts — labeling it "News" signals to Google the content is time-sensitive, which hurts normal evergreen blog content.
- **Flatten URL nesting**: don't structure blog posts as child pages of an "About" page (e.g. `/about/news/the-roof-replacement-process`) — make posts top-level/parent pages so links are short and legible.
- **Audit internal links across sub-sites**: if every division site links back to a low-value hub/portfolio page, that pulls authority away from pages that actually need it. Check the hub's real organic traffic in Semrush first (Matt: "I can almost guarantee it's zero" / "mostly branded traffic").
- **Orphan-page triage walkthrough** (Jill's indigenous-art e-commerce site, 118 orphan pages found):
  1. Open the flagged orphan URL and try to reach it by normal site navigation to confirm it's truly unreachable.
  2. Check for a duplicate — telltale sign is a `-1` suffix (e.g. `.../couch-cushion-covers-1`).
  3. On the admin collections list, use browser find (**Ctrl+F**, not Ctrl+Plus) to search a keyword (e.g. "cushion") and spot the duplicate collection.
  4. If it's a duplicate: delete the orphaned collection outright.
  5. If it's not a duplicate but targets a real search keyword (e.g. "red wine glasses"): don't delete it — link to it from somewhere (nav dropdown, a blog post, another page) so it's no longer orphaned.
  6. Repeat for every flagged URL.
- **Don't build near-duplicate collection/category pages** that only differ by keyword phrasing for the same products — consolidate; treat things like "red cushions" / "black cushions" / "sofa cushions" as product attributes/filters, not separate pages, unless there's a real reason for a dedicated landing page.
- **"Why doesn't this rank" / content-type diagnosis** (Jill's "fly net hats" blog post): take the exact target question and search it in Google yourself. Look at what content TYPE currently ranks (product pages, collections, service pages, blog posts, listicles, comparisons). Match your content's format to whatever format is already ranking — Jill's query returned product/shopping results (Amazon, retailer pages), so the fix is to optimize the product/collection page (not the blog post) and move the useful copy there. If the page is brand-new, don't judge rank yet — wait a couple months and watch traffic per page.
- **Backlink removal, two paths**: (1) contact the linking site directly and ask them to take the link down — they may comply free, ignore you, or demand payment (treat a paid-removal demand as extortion, don't just pay); (2) disavow via Google Search Console.
- **Prefer "dilution" over disavowing**: build more good backlinks so bad ones become a smaller share of the overall profile, rather than risk disavowing a link that might secretly be helping.
- **Low-authority site strategy** (Kirill's moving company): check keyword difficulty in Semrush before targeting a head term. If it's too hard for the site's authority, go after easier long-tail/question keywords first (e.g. "long-distance movers," "how much do movers cost in Toronto," "how much does it cost to hire movers in Toronto"). Answer the question directly in a blog post; give real pricing or embed a pricing calculator (built via ChatGPT/Claude). These don't need backlinks to rank, and ranking them builds overall site authority for harder terms later.
- **Layer neighborhood/sub-area pages** once the head geo-term is out of reach (e.g. beyond "Movers Toronto," build pages for Leslieville, Queen West, Yorkville, North York, Vaughan) — but recognize the site may not have the authority to rank even a sub-area term yet; this is a "keep building," not instant, strategy.
- **If a site has no blog at all**, treat that as a root-cause flag — no topical authority is being built, so the domain can't earn organic traffic on informational queries.
- **Backlink-building resources/process**: use thehoth.com for paid link building (link outreach or link insertion); use AB Newswire for press-release-based backlinks (write the copy with ChatGPT). For local businesses, also get a press release placed through the LOCAL newspaper — sends local relevance signals a national wire doesn't. Check what backlink sources competitors already have (Chamber of Commerce, Better Business Bureau) and go get the same ones.
- **Blog content as the linkable asset**: most sites won't link to a commercial service/product page but will link to genuinely useful informational content — write the blog post, then optionally point paid links (e.g. via Hoth) at that post instead of the sales page.
- **Heat maps — pick the right one**: for local geographic rank tracking, use BrightLocal to show clients where they rank across a map/area for a keyword. For on-page behavioral (scroll/click/mouse-move) heat maps, first ask "what am I trying to accomplish?" — for most local business sites, skip the tool and instead study 1–2 good competitor/category sites and copy where they place phone number, hours, address, and CTA (all visible near the top, no scrolling). Reserve behavioral heat maps for "I'm getting traffic but nobody's converting and I don't know why." Geo rank-heat-maps are worth it when you specifically care about ranking in one sub-area/neighborhood — pair with a direct Semrush check (search "[service] [city]" and see if you show up).
- **Cut down competing CTAs/options per page**: fewer choices convert better than a wall of "get my page audit extension / page audit tool / Watchdog Tool / hire us / get my courses / join the Insiders" links on one page — analogy: Costco stocks only ~3 variations per product category (e.g. ranch, balsamic, Caesar dressing).

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Don't send GBP traffic to a portfolio/holding-company page listing multiple sub-brands — point it to ONE specific, relevant, optimized site.
- Don't change a GBP's linked website repeatedly — Google needs 1–3+ months to "learn" a site after a change; settle on one and leave it.
- A second GBP at the same street address generally requires proof of a separate entrance.
- H1 tags must state both service/industry AND location — a values statement/tagline alone doesn't count.
- Don't label a plain company blog "News" — call it "Blog."
- Don't nest blog posts under other pages in the URL path (e.g. `/about/news/...`) — keep posts flat, top-level.
- Don't link every division/sub-site back to a low-traffic hub/portfolio page — it siphons authority from pages that need it.
- Orphan pages: delete duplicates that serve no purpose; otherwise link to them — never leave a page targeting a real keyword unlinked ("you can't hide that page").
- Don't create multiple near-duplicate collection/category pages for the same products to catch different keyword phrasings — consolidate.
- Match content format to search intent — verify what content type currently ranks for a query before writing/optimizing a page for it; don't force a blog post onto a transactional-intent query (or vice versa), even if it follows an otherwise-correct on-page rule (e.g. H1 = the literal question).
- Don't jump to disavowing suspect backlinks — Matt has never personally disavowed a client's links; he's seen "toxic" links turn out to be helping, and rankings dropped after disavowing them.
- If you must remove a bad link, contact the site first; don't assume you must pay a removal fee someone demands.
- Avoid cheap/low-quality press-release or backlink services — Matt ties Anthony's toxic-domain flags directly to a prior agency's low-quality press-release vendor.
- 100% follow backlinks looks unnatural/suspicious — a natural profile needs a mix of follow and no-follow links.
- A "natural" backlink profile also needs: a unique link profile (not identical to competitors') and a diverse pool of referring IPs/subnets (many different servers, not one network).
- Don't chase head keywords a site's authority can't support yet ("being a hog") — go for easier/long-tail/question keywords first, same logic as with backlinks.
- If a site has no blog, that's itself a likely cause of weak rankings/traffic.
- Advertising directly to a third-party marketplace (e.g. Amazon) means accepting weak/no conversion attribution — treat that spend as a calculated risk, not a measurable-ROI channel.

## Tools, prompts & resources

- **thehoth.com** — paid link-building vendor. Products → Link Building → Link Outreach or Link Insertion. Cheapest tier: **$175 per link**.
- **AB Newswire** — press-release distribution/syndication service; used for backlinks + credibility + AI-visibility ("training AI"). Example package: "single premium PR distribution" — roughly **20 premium placements for about $400 total** (~$20/placement, per Matt's own numbers). Syndicated outlets included Globe and Mail (Canadian, "massive"); a live link went up within a couple hours of submission. Matt's caveat: site UX is "not the nicest to use," but he hasn't seen bad SEO outcomes from it yet and is still testing/rolling it out to clients.
- **ChatGPT** — used to draft press release copy; also floated for building an embeddable pricing calculator ("ChatGPT can make one for you... or Claude").
- **Google Search Console** — where you'd submit a formal backlink disavow (Matt avoids doing this himself).
- **BrightLocal** (brightlocal.com) — used for local map-pack/geo-grid rank tracking shown to clients; replaced Semrush for this specific use because Semrush was "way too expensive."
- **Semrush** — used throughout for: Authority Score, backlink audits (flagging "backlinks from this domain will harm your backlink profile" / link-network patterns), organic traffic estimate for a given URL (to prove a hub page gets near-zero real traffic), keyword difficulty %, and organic keyword/city-combo research (e.g. search "[service] + [city]" to check visibility). Authority Score factors named: natural ratio of follow-to-no-follow links, unique backlink profile, diverse pool of referring IPs/subnets.
- **Answer the Public** (or similar) — referenced as a source for real question-based keywords (e.g. "where do I get fly net hats").
- **Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing)** — Matt's print-on-demand channel for his own book.
- **IngramSpark** — distribution channel to get a book into Chapters, Indigo, Barnes & Noble.
- Hey Tony's own product suite (named as a self-referential "too many CTAs" example): page audit extension, page audit tool, Watchdog Tool, agency hire, courses, "Insiders" community.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **Disavow vs. dilute**: disavowing feels like the clean fix but is risky — Matt has seen it tank rankings when a supposedly toxic link was actually contributing. Building more good links (dilution) is slower but doesn't risk losing hidden positive signal.
- **No-follow links aren't worthless**: a no-follow placement (Globe and Mail via AB Newswire) still carries brand credibility and AI-visibility value even without passing direct link equity.
- **National wire vs. local paper**: AB Newswire buys broad credibility/syndication reach; a local paper buys local-relevance signal that specifically helps local-pack SEO. Local businesses likely want both, not one instead of the other.
- **Behavioral heat maps**: usually unneeded overhead for a typical local-business site (copying competitor best practices gets most of the benefit for free), but earn their cost when you have real traffic and a genuine, unexplained conversion problem.
- **Geo rank-heat-maps**: skip if you don't care about sub-area performance; matter if a business genuinely prefers leads from one specific nearby area over its own city.
- **Portfolio/hub sites**: a real tension between an owner's instinct to showcase "everything the company does" and the SEO/conversion reality that users and Google both want one clear, specific answer. Matt sides with specificity but acknowledges the client-preference cost of pushing back ("he really wanted it this way").
- **On-page rules can still fail intent-matching**: "H1 = the literal question" (Matt's own course guidance) still isn't enough if the SERP's dominant content type doesn't match your format — the intent check should override the generic formatting rule when they conflict.
- **Volume vs. value**: commercial roofing jobs are worth "hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars" vs. residential's higher volume/lower value per job — a business has to consciously pick which side to prioritize per page/channel rather than blur both into one hub.
- **Amazon-directed ad spend**: buys reach and best-seller-list momentum but costs you attribution — a deliberate tradeoff, not an oversight.
- **DIY vs. agency**: traditional publishing (or, by extension, outsourcing to an agency generally) can offer better unit economics while still not delivering marketing results the principal couldn't have replicated themselves — a caution against assuming "professional" execution automatically beats DIY.
