## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Reciprocal internal linking after publishing (core tactic, walked through live):**
  1. Publish the new blog post; grab its live URL.
  2. Go into 3 *old* blog posts that are topically related.
  3. In each old post, find an existing word/phrase that makes sense as an anchor.
  4. Hyperlink that word/phrase to the brand-new post.
  5. Update/republish each old post.
  - This is in addition to (not instead of) the outbound links you already place inside the new post itself while writing it — you need links flowing both directions (old→new and new→old).
  - Rationale (circle analogy): Google already trusts/knows the old pages; when they point to the new page, Google discovers and indexes it faster.
- **Pre-launch/seasonal content tactic:** publish supporting blog content *before* the thing you're promoting is ready (product line, seasonal offer) so it's already indexed and ranking by the time demand hits. Example given: a florist should start optimizing for Valentine's/Mother's/Father's Day/Easter content roughly 6 months out, not a month before.
- **New business location:** don't write a blog post for it — build a dedicated location page instead.
- **YouTube Shorts → blog combo:** once a Short starts surfacing in Google search, write a blog post, reuse the same title as the Short, and embed the video in the post — but give the post a different angle/point of view than the video so it's not duplicate content (e.g., video = ring-style comparison facts; blog post = the in-person customer experience/story).
- **Finding blog topics:** go to FindQuestions.com, or simply write down a real question a customer has actually asked you.
- **Above-the-fold blog page fix (live audit, no redesign needed):** strip clutter that pushes the actual content down — specifically, remove social-share icon rows; make sure a first-time visitor immediately recognizes it's a blog post and knows to scroll down to get to the real content/business.
- **Meta description fix:** give every post a unique, benefit-forward description instead of the site's default/generic one — write it to earn the click, not just to describe the page (e.g., "here are the five things to check for with your AC" beats a generic company blurb).
- **Content-type mixing for topical authority (auto repair example):** alternate make/model-specific "common issues" posts (e.g., "common issues Toyota Corolla") with general service-explainer posts (e.g., "what to expect during AC service") rather than only doing one type.
- **Reverse-engineering an AI citation:** when Google AI Mode / Gemini names your business, ask it follow-ups: "what did you analyze to recommend these companies," and "what search terms did you search for." To build a target list, go further and ask it to name specifics (e.g., "who specifically are the names, phone numbers, mailing addresses of the property managers") — then use that output for direct outreach (personal letters + brochures).
- **Matt's own iteration loop for Rank Week:** after each Rank Week, feed all call transcripts + all chat comments into Claude and ask "what were the pain points, were people struggling today, how did they feel" — use that to decide what to change next time (e.g., adding a mid-session breather).

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Don't publish a dozen blog posts at once on an already-live site.
- Exception: launching a brand-new site for the first time — fine to launch with a batch of ~5–6 posts at once.
- Ongoing cadence: 1–2 posts/week, consistently, is enough. Leanne Jones' (a member's web designer) rule of thumb: no more than 3 posts, ideally 1–2/week — post too many too fast and Google effectively gives you "demerits"/treats it as spam.
- Don't make rapid repeated changes to core site elements (H1, target keyword). One change is fine; let it simmer at least a couple of weeks before touching it again so Google's spam filter doesn't flag the instability.
- Never publish a "coming soon" placeholder page or link — provides zero value to a first-time visitor and signals "this site is under construction."
- **Cannibalization rule:** one page should carry one primary keyword plus its natural secondary/variation keywords — don't spin those secondary keywords off into a separate new blog post; too-similar content cannibalizes.
- Meta descriptions must be unique per page — duplicates get flagged by the audit tool; Google says they're "not" a direct ranking factor but they still drive (or kill) click-through from the results page.
- Don't overthink or chase "perfect" before publishing — a post is never going to be perfect, and perfect doesn't guarantee ranking anyway. Hit publish; revise later if needed.
- Writing a post shouldn't take 3 hours (referenced against a member taking that long) — AI tools improve the more you use them, so long sessions signal something's off in the workflow.
- Sourcing/finding images is a legitimate exception to "don't overthink it" — that step can validly take longer, especially for photographers.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Publishing velocity is context-dependent: a brand-new site launch can batch 5–6 posts at once; a live/ongoing site should throttle to 1–2/week or risk a spam-like penalty.
- Public disclosure vs. tactic longevity: broadcasting a working growth hack (press-release/AI-citation manipulation) bought short-term reach (2M views) but burned the tactic once gatekeepers noticed — Matt is trading reach for durability by keeping current tactics inside the paid group only, "at least for the next couple months."
- Manual teaching vs. tool automation: Matt second-guessed teaching a manual audit method ("counterintuitive," felt like it "wasted two days") when a tool (Money Page Doctor) already automates the diagnosis — but a member pushed back that seeing the diagnostic result itself (why she wasn't ranking) was the actual motivator, not the method used to get there.
- Meta descriptions: not a direct Google ranking factor per Google's own guidance, but still worth writing well because they drive (or suppress) click-through from the search results page.
- "Coming soon" placeholder links: stated best practice is to never publish them, but in practice a member does this occasionally (once or twice, with a personal note to herself to fix it later) without much pushback beyond "I wouldn't even do that" — treated as low-stakes rather than a hard violation.
- Session pacing: adding hand-holding/breaks costs session time but was estimated (via Matt's post-session transcript/comment analysis) to meaningfully reduce newcomer drop-off.
