# Q&A New Insiders — 22.06.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Orphan taxonomy/profile pages created by forms** (e.g. a "find an expert" quiz that auto-generates a public page per answer-combo, like "0-2 years experience"): these get indexed by Google and flagged as orphans (no internal links point to them) even though the site owner never manually created them. Fix by prompting ChatGPT/Claude: "here's my situation, here's my theme, here's what I'm doing, all this stuff — how do I prevent these pages from existing but still have that information on the front end."
- **Content architecture for multi-practitioner/directory sites**: don't write content about each listed expert's individual specialty (spirituality, authoring, leadership, etc.) — pick ONE overarching "vertical" term (e.g. "thought leadership") as the site's umbrella topic, then treat podcasting/speaking/writing/webinars as sub-topics beneath that single vertical. Keeps Google's read of "what this site is about" coherent across a diverse practitioner base.
- **Cleaning up a black-hat/programmatic SEO disaster**: (1) go into Google Search Console and check which of the spam pages are getting clicks/impressions; (2) any getting zero clicks, move to DRAFT status (not delete — keeps the option to restore); (3) because the spam pages are internally cross-linked, drafting them removes the links along with the pages; (4) rerun the site audit tool afterward — it will surface any remaining broken links pointing at the now-drafted pages; (5) resume a normal cadence of 1-2 new blog posts/week. Don't manually 404 the pages, don't set up redirects, don't ping Google to request a recrawl.
- **Platform migration (Wix/Squarespace → WordPress etc.)**: keep every URL exactly identical, plus the same heading tags and same content, even if visual design changes. If a URL structure must change (e.g. `/blog` → `/news`), set up 301 redirects mapping old → new for every page.
- **Product-page keyword strategy for large catalogs (300+ SKUs)**: one page per product TYPE (pizza boxes, cake boxes, burger boxes), not one page per custom variation. Work the "custom" keyword naturally into on-page content/H2s rather than stuffing it into every title. Before assuming "custom" is the driving keyword, do keyword research per top category to find what modifiers people actually search (eco-friendly, wholesale, MOQ, disposable, etc.) — ask: are people searching for custom sizing, custom design, or something else entirely?
- **Easiest first backlinks for a local business with no budget**: join local organizations (chamber of commerce, etc.) that maintain member directories — get a business profile page listed there, that's a backlink.
- **Collection pages with few/near-duplicate SKUs (ecommerce)**: treat the collection page almost like a blog post — write a mini-blog/FAQ block underneath the product grid (e.g., how often people should eat the product) using the same prompt style from Rank Week Day 1, and let ChatGPT/Claude pull from existing owned material (e.g., the owner's book). Structure: products on top, mini-blog + FAQ below.
- **Internal linking rule (corrected from a Rank Week misunderstanding)**: when publishing a NEW blog post, link out from it to 3 OLD blog posts + 1 money page. Then separately go back and edit 3 different old blog posts to add a link into the new post. (Not: link the 3 old posts into the new one only.)
- **Duplicate content across multiple locations** (e.g. identical game description reused for an Anaheim booking page and a Connecticut booking page): fix by differentiating just the title tag and meta description per location (e.g. "Booking — Anaheim" vs. "Booking — Connecticut"); body copy can stay similar.
- **Repositioning/rebranding an existing site without losing SEO equity**: don't rebuild from scratch. Rewrite/simplify existing pages in place, keep old blog URLs live (Google is still crawling them if indexed), and link the new/revamped pages FROM the older already-indexed posts so Google discovers them faster.
- **Podcast-specific microsites**: keep minimal — homepage, booking/"work with us" page, featured guests page, and an embedded always-updating sample (e.g. a Spotify playlist embed). Don't build out per-episode content pages.
- **Diagnosing an orphan product page**: test whether you can reach it from the homepage without pasting the direct URL. If it's only reachable via a buried subcategory nav (not linked from the homepage itself), that's the likely cause. If unresolved, escalate to a more technical call — could be a canonical/duplicate-URL-version issue depending on platform.
- **JavaScript-heavy WordPress site health score**: install WP Rocket, use its one-click buttons to minimize/minify/defer JavaScript. If it breaks something visually, just untoggle that specific button.
- **Fixing red/high-severity site health issues**: batch 5-10 fixes per week rather than trying to clear everything at once — fixing one instance of a systemic issue often auto-resolves it site-wide (e.g., a theme-level setting).
- **Building E-E-A-T around core topics**: publish blog content that directly answers the specific questions your niche audience searches (e.g. a landscape photographer covering specific named locations like Big Sur) so Google associates the site/author with that expertise.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- If an audited page is one you genuinely don't care about (traffic/ranking irrelevant), don't burn time fixing every flag on it — but DO stop public taxonomy/utility pages from being indexable even if traffic-neutral, because they're low-quality pages Google associates with the domain.
- Broken-link flags pointing at social platforms (e.g. LinkedIn) are usually just bots being blocked from crawling — safe to manually validate or ignore.
- Image sourcing priority: **owned/real photos first, then stock images, then AI** — EXCEPT for graphs/graphics specifically, where AI goes to the top of the list ("they make really great graphics").
- New websites: expect **zero visible ranking movement for the first two weeks to two months**. Google needs months of consistency (e.g., weekly publishing) before it trusts a new site isn't a spammer.
- Programmatic/black-hat SEO: **300-500 AI-generated blog posts published in a single day (or even in a month) is unrealistic for any site**, even a publication with a full writing staff — Google reads this as gaming the system. One case had an estimated 3,000-5,000 such pages, ~2,000 of which were actually indexed.
- When purging spam pages: **draft, don't delete**; don't manually mark pages 404; don't set up redirects for them; don't notify Google or request a recrawl. "If your SEO is already terrible, then you can't really do worse."
- Crawl budget: Google gives no official documentation on how it's calculated. Matt's working theory — sites showing real traffic, time-on-page, and multi-page sessions earn progressively more crawl budget; sites with thousands of unvisited pages risk having crawl budget throttled.
- "Ranking" includes **both** the local 3-pack/map pack **and** organic listings — even page 3 or 4 counts as ranking, just poorly. One tends to feed the other.
- Blog cadence target: **1-2 long-form posts per week**, ~1,000-1,200 words called "perfect" length. If only one per week is sustainable, that's fine — prioritize patience/consistency over volume.
- Backlinks: ~10 backlinks is "not a lot" in absolute terms but is meaningful if starting from zero. **Not all backlinks are equal** — an illustrative (non-literal) example: a New York Times backlink might be "worth" 100 units of trust vs. a backlink from "the shop next door" worth 1% — "a hundred 1%'s still doesn't equal the New York Times backlink."
- Don't chase paid/bought backlinks early. Sequence is: write content → optimize pages → THEN pursue backlinks. Backlink/directory training is gated at **Level 5 ("Expert")** inside Insiders — complete earlier levels first.
- Content relevance rule: every piece of content should directly serve the site's core promise. Tangential-but-related topics (e.g. general sugar/nutrition content on a fitness/self-defense site) should be a mention embedded inside an on-topic post, not a standalone piece.
- Site-health error fixing pace, repeated for two different site owners: **5-10 fixes per week**, explicitly framed as burnout prevention — "you'll be astounded at how much progress you make."
- Google **requires written text** to pull data into search results — video/audio can supplement (better UX/multimedia) but can't replace text for search visibility.
- No CMS/platform is definitively "more SEO-optimized" than another: WordPress has the most/easiest options, Shopify comparable, Wix/Squarespace slightly fewer options but still fully rankable.
- GoHighLevel (GHL) as a website host is workable but not Matt's personal recommendation unless already tied into your CRM/other tooling — better dedicated hosting options exist.
- HARO is useful for backlinks/press but explicitly flagged as something that "can be overwhelming" — use it, don't obsess over it.
- Editing/republishing old blog posts alongside a 1-2/week new-post cadence is **not spammy** — it's standard practice, but should be prioritized (see below), not blanket-applied to every old post.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Audit-flag severity depends on business relevance, not the tool's default severity label — but a page being "unimportant" doesn't excuse leaving a public back-end taxonomy page indexable; those are worth fixing regardless of traffic.
- Backlink volume vs. quality: absolute counts (e.g. "10 backlinks") are meaningless without source authority — one high-authority link can outweigh many low-authority ones combined.
- Blogging cadence vs. capacity: 1-2/week is the target, but Matt repeatedly tells people to match cadence to what's actually sustainable rather than force volume — consistency beats speed.
- Video is a valuable UX supplement but not a text substitute, because Google's indexing depends on parseable written content.
- Platform choice is mostly a non-issue for SEO (WordPress/Shopify/Wix/Squarespace all rankable with correct setup) — the real exception is highly templated gallery-style hosts (SmugMug) that structurally create canonical/duplicate problems through their URL patterns; GHL is usable but not ideal purely as a host.
- Rebuilding a brand/niche from scratch vs. revamping in place: rebuilding risks losing accumulated SEO equity (indexed URLs, backlinks, rankings); revamping in place while preserving old URLs and cross-linking new content from them is the safer path even during a substantial positioning pivot.
- Refreshing old content is valuable but should be prioritized — Matt's own agency process for new clients is to check Google Search Console for posts with high impressions but zero clicks and optimize those first, rather than blanket-redoing every old post, "because you could just be wasting your time."
- Tangential-but-related topics (general nutrition on a fitness site, "custom" as a catch-all product attribute) can dilute topical focus and confuse Google's read of site purpose — the fix is folding the adjacent info into on-topic posts, not deleting it outright or giving it standalone posts.
- Programmatic/AI-scaled content: "some people swear by this" even though Matt and most of the community consider it non-viable — the acceptable use of AI (graphics, drafting assistance, interview-style blog generation) is distinguished from the unacceptable use (mass unedited publishing at unrealistic volume).
- Geographic/traffic-source mismatches (Analytics showing a different city than the actual service area) aren't inherently a problem if inquiries/calls are still converting — the actual risk to check for is accidental keyword contamination (wrong city name appearing in titles/meta/H1s), not the traffic-source data itself.
- A directory/marketplace site doing SEO on behalf of its listed experts (to earn backlinks from those experts' own sites) is a different goal than direct organic acquisition for those experts' individual names — Matt is explicit the profile-page approach won't drive new visitors unless the expert is already independently well-searched.
