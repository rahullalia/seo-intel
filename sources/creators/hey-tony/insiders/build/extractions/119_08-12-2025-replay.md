# Optimizing a Money Page (Product/Service Page) — 08.12.2025 Replay (Rank Week Prep)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- Full page-optimization workflow demoed live, in order:
  1. Pick the product/service page you want to rank ("money page") on your own site.
  2. Get a keyword research tool (SEMrush, Ubersuggest, or Ahrefs) if you don't have one.
  3. In the tool, set the **country to the one you actually want to rank in** (not necessarily where you live — e.g., Dr. Karen is in Newfoundland, Canada, but set the tool to United States because 75% of her clients are US-based).
  4. Search your broad head term first (e.g., "natural family planning," "website design"), then scroll the tool's suggestion list to find lower-difficulty variants and long-tail phrases.
  5. Pick a keyword balancing real (even small) volume against low difficulty — long-tail (3+ words) is preferred.
  6. Google that exact keyword; open the #1 and #2 **organic** ranking pages as your competitor benchmarks.
  7. Go to **pageaudit.com**, paste in your page URL, leave "target keyword" blank, click Analyze.
  8. From the Content tab, copy into a tracking spreadsheet: meta title, meta description, H1 tag(s), H2 tags (skip H3s), total word count.
  9. Repeat step 7–8 for both competitor pages (their homepage if that's what ranks, or their specific service page if that's what ranks).
  10. Re-run pageaudit.com a second time on each page, this time entering the actual target keyword, to pull **keyword usage** (raw count of times keyword/variant appears) and **keyword density** (% of total words that are the keyword) for all three pages.
  11. Compare side by side: is keyword in the title tag? In the meta description? How many H1s (should be exactly one)? How does your word count compare to the #1/#2 pages? How does your keyword density compare?
  12. Rewrite your page to close the gaps — see "level plus one" tactic below — then publish new content comparable to what competitors are doing.
- **"Level plus one" tactic**: figure out precisely what the top-ranking competitor is doing numerically, then do very slightly more — not dramatically more. Live example: competitor page used the keyword variant ("web design") **45 times** at **1.76% density** (≈2,563 total words); Matt's plan for his own rewrite = **~2,600 words** (add ~37 words) and use the keyword **46 times** — "I want to do what is proven that Google likes. I just want to do it like 0.0001% more."
- Check **referring domains**, not raw backlink count, for the top-ranking competitor pages — if the actual #1-ranked page for your target keyword has 0 referring domains, that's proof you can rank that keyword without any backlink building.
- Location-page tactic for service-area/local businesses (real estate example): build one dedicated page per city/county, keyword-first phrasing ("Sell your house fast, Delaware, Ohio" — head term first, geography second, not "Looking to sell your Delaware home quickly"), and write **completely unique** body copy per location page (not the same template with only the city swapped) — Matt's diagnosis for pages that aren't ranking is usually "they're not different enough or you're not targeting the right keyword."
- Product-renaming tactic for ecommerce: type the plain physical/functional description of the product into the keyword tool (ignore the cute/branded name), find the phrase with real search volume and low/no competition, and rename the actual product title (not just alt text) to that phrase.
- For informational/non-question search terms (e.g., Brett's book topic "inheriting a house that's paid off"): use the search phrase almost verbatim as the lead-in to the title tag/H1, then append a natural qualifier ("...what to do" / "...everything you need to know"). Keep the keyword as close to the front of the title tag, meta description, and H1 as possible; only add lead-in words if needed for readability, and mirror however many lead-in words the actual #1-ranking competitor uses.
- For ecommerce collection pages lacking content: don't add more copy above the product grid (visitors came to see products) — add an information section **below** the grid instead, structured as H2 questions ("How long does the temporary tattoo last?" / "Can I shower with a temporary tattoo?") answered in natural, human language that naturally reincorporates the keyword a few more times on the page. Explicitly not "FAQ"-labeled — just plain H2s.
- Closing homework (no prompts given this session): write and publish new content on your page that is comparable in depth/structure to what the top competitors are doing.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- Exactly **one H1 tag** per page. Manually pressing Enter mid-headline to force a line break creates two separate H1s — fix by shrinking the container/box so text wraps naturally instead.
- Don't ship a **different H1 for mobile vs. desktop** via responsive show/hide — Matt tried this after copying a #1-ranked competitor and now suspects Google penalizes it as "gaming the system"; flagged as something he may need to undo on his own site.
- Never use a **year/date in an evergreen page's URL or title** (e.g., "best dental services in 2025") — it goes stale as years pass.
- Your **target keyword must appear in the title tag** — called out as missing/wrong on a live example ("White Shimmer Worship Dance Flags" title not containing the actual keyword "White Worship Flags").
- Ideal keyword difficulty range: **0–15/20%**; up to **25% "still possible."** 31% was called "a little bit more difficult than I would like." 100% ("website design," no location, 60,000 US searches/mo) = "never going to rank."
- Long-tail keyword defined as "more than three words long... very specific."
- Zero search volume is not a disqualifier if difficulty is near zero — still worth targeting/optimizing a dedicated page for it.
- **Never guess keywords — always validate with a research tool**, even against your own pride/ego about a product name: "Always follow the keyword research and never guess. Never."
- Match competitor word count and keyword usage/density — don't blow past it. Matt's own page's keyword density of **5% (35 uses)** is called "way too many... insane" versus competitors at 1.76% and 0.7%.
- Local-market sizing rule of thumb: a ~500,000-population city (Hamilton) has roughly **15,000 small businesses** — enough to justify targeting the whole city as the geo-modifier (e.g., "website design in Hamilton"). A 3–4 million population metro (Toronto) is too big to target as a whole — go after a **neighborhood** instead, because people won't travel across a huge city for a local service.
- On ecommerce collection pages, don't stack extra copy above the product grid; put it below.
- Avoid duplicate/near-duplicate content across pages (collection page vs. product page copy, or near-identical location pages) — must be genuinely unique per page.
- If a page already ranks #1, **leave it alone** — don't "fix" it to match best practice. Verbatim: "I don't give a shit if you have 31 heading one tags when you should only have one. If you have 31 of them and you're ranking number one, I'm not touching that page."
- Tool cost/commitment: SEMrush ≈ $150/month (Matt's estimate, "I think"); Ahrefs "light" version ≈ $29/month (per attendee Alex, unverified firsthand by Matt); keyword research tools are an **ongoing** budget line, not a cancel-after-a-few-months audit tool.
- Ubersuggest volume data may be inflated: in Matt's own past 4-way comparison (Ubersuggest vs. Ahrefs vs. SEMrush vs. SE Ranking) on the same keyword, Ubersuggest showed **~3,000** searches/month where Ahrefs showed **750** and SEMrush showed **600** — "it might be grouping stuff together" — still "better than guessing" if it's all you can afford.
- If a keyword shows "N/A" difficulty, refresh/update the tool's metrics (~1 minute) to get a real score rather than treating it as zero.

## Tools, prompts & resources
- **pageaudit.com** — Matt's own free tool. Two-pass use: (1) paste URL, leave target keyword blank, Analyze → pulls Content tab data (meta title, meta description, H1/H2/H3 tags, total word count); (2) re-run with the actual target keyword entered → returns keyword usage count and keyword density %.
- **SEMrush** — ≈$150/month (Matt's estimate); keyword volume + keyword difficulty %, an "update metrics" refresh action for N/A-difficulty keywords, and a referring-domains metric distinct from raw backlinks.
- **Ubersuggest** (Neil Patel's tool) — cheaper alternative; similar keyword-question surfacing to Answer The Public, but per Matt's own comparison test, volume numbers may run inflated vs. Ahrefs/SEMrush.
- **Ahrefs** — "light" version ≈$29/month, mentioned by attendee Alex as a viable budget option (Matt hadn't personally used the light tier).
- **Answer The Public** — free; surfaces question-phrased keyword variants; doesn't give keyword difficulty/volume scoring like the paid tools.
- **characterCounter.net** — manual cross-check: paste full page copy in to verify pageaudit.com's word count.
- **Google (manual search)** — search the target keyword, use the #1 and #2 **organic** results as competitor benchmarks (not paid ads).
- **Rank Week website-audit spreadsheet** (Google Sheets template) — File > Make a Copy before editing (so nobody edits the shared master). Columns used: page URL, focus keyword, title tag, meta description, H1, H2, total word count, keyword usage (count), keyword density (%) — filled out for your page + 2 competitor pages.

## Case studies / examples / results

Diagnosis: 2 H1 tags (different H1 shown on mobile vs. desktop via a responsive hide/show hack copied from a #1-ranked competitor); only **695 words** vs. a #2-ranked competitor's **1,200 words**; keyword density **5.04% (35 uses)** of "website design" — called too high vs. a competitor using "web design" **45 times at 1.76%** density (≈2,563 total words) and another competitor using it **9 times at 0.7%**. Insight: "web design" (not "website design") appears to be the phrasing Google/top competitors are actually optimized around. "Website design in Hamilton" initially showed N/A difficulty; after refreshing metrics, showed **38%**.

Refined to "natural family planning Catholic" = **880 searches/mo, 25% difficulty** ("a great keyword"). Further refined to "Catholic natural family planning online classes" — flagged live as an excellent, highly specific long-tail keyword needing its own dedicated page (no volume/difficulty numbers were pulled on-screen for this final variant).

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Long-tail, low-difficulty, low/zero-volume keywords trade a lower traffic ceiling for a much higher chance of actually ranking and more conversion-ready visitors — a deliberate tradeoff Matt endorses repeatedly (Mark's 0-volume/7%-difficulty location keyword; Karen's niche Catholic-classes phrase).
- Keyword-tool choice is a cost/accuracy tradeoff: Ubersuggest is cheaper but showed meaningfully inflated volume vs. Ahrefs/SEMrush in Matt's own test; pricier tools are more accurate but a bigger recurring cost — reasonable to size the tool to the business (Ahrefs light for a solo owner, SEMrush "Cadillac" tier for an agency serving many clients).
- Local strategy depends entirely on city size: small/mid cities can and should target the whole city as the geo-modifier; large metros need neighborhood-level targeting, since a citywide term becomes both harder to rank and less convertible (people won't cross a huge city for a local service).
- Building dedicated new location/service pages carries a theoretical cannibalization risk, but in Matt's framing the real risk is duplicate/templated copy across those pages, not the multiplication of pages itself — distinct keyword + genuinely unique content resolves it.
- Copying a competitor's apparent "trick" isn't automatically safe: Matt admits trying the mobile/desktop dual-H1 hack after seeing a #1-ranked site do it, but now suspects it's hurting rather than helping his own rankings — a caution against assuming correlation (competitor ranks #1 and does X) equals causation (X is why they rank #1).
- Renaming products for SEO can conflict with an owner's pride/branding attachment to the existing name — Matt's stance is keyword research wins, but he still gets explicit client sign-off/acknowledgment before overriding a preferred name, rather than just doing it unilaterally.
- The "level plus one" approach is proportional matching, not a license to maximize — explicitly not "use the keyword 100 times and write 10,000 words"; the target is a small, deliberate increment over what's already proven to work for the top-ranked page.
