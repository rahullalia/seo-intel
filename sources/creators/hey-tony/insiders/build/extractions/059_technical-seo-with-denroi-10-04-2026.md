## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Nesting a category/folder into a WordPress blog URL** (Varuna, Cornerstone theme): first check the page editor's side panel for a "parent page" selector — theme-dependent, may not exist (Cornerstone didn't show one). If not there, go to the main WP Dashboard → **Settings → Permalinks** → permalink structure, and add the category/parent page there so it prepends to blog post URLs automatically.
- **Canonical flagged "not self-referencing" after already fixing the root cause** (Neary): don't assume the fix failed — check a non-caching tool (Detailed SEO extension) first; if that shows the corrected canonical, the issue is just audit-tool cache lag, not a real problem.
- **Google Search Console walkthrough for a new/confused user** (as demoed live by Elliot):
  1. **Indexing → Pages** report — shows total indexed vs. not-indexed counts; click into an issue category (e.g. no canonical tag, 404, redirect) to see the exact list of affected pages.
  2. **URL Inspection** — paste any single URL to check if it's indexed and, if not, why.
  3. Triage: if the non-indexed pages are low-value/utility pages (privacy policy, cart, etc.), leave them — only chase indexing problems on homepage, service pages, and blog/money pages.
  4. **Sitemaps** (left sidebar, under Indexing) — confirm a sitemap is submitted. Append `/sitemap.xml` to the site's root URL to view it directly; in GSC it should show **"Success"** against the correct count of active pages.
- **Migrating a hand-coded HTML site to WordPress while preserving SEO value** (Jennifer): keep the same URLs wherever possible, keep the same meta titles/descriptions across pages. When choosing what to fix next, prioritize **internal linking** over immediate H1 cleanup — internal linking is what protects long-term indexability once Google recrawls after migration; scrambled/keyword-stuffed H1s can be cleaned up afterward without hurting the transition itself.
- **Diagnosing a spike in "Crawled – currently not indexed"** (Carol): first check if anything major changed on the site around that date. If nothing did, it may be a natural Google-side platform fluctuation, not a real issue. Fix: open the affected URL → **Inspect URL** → **Request Indexing** to force a fresh crawl. For bulk/URL-change scenarios this can take roughly a week to two weeks to fully resolve on its own.
- **Reviving an old blog post stuck as "not indexed"**: change something in the post so it reads as fresh/new to Google — update the copy, tie it to a newer related post, or add a new backlink — otherwise Google keeps deeming it uninteresting and it won't get reindexed.
- **Internal linking as a blanket maintenance habit**: apply it across old blogs, new blogs, and any page you re-optimize — it's one of the clearest signals of site interconnectivity/relevance Google looks for.
- **Squarespace auto-generated category/tag archive pages you can't edit directly**: if a category page shows up in the URL/site but isn't editable from the normal page list, you likely have to go into Squarespace's code/design injection settings to touch it — check there before assuming it can't be removed.
- **Cleaning up excess tags without breaking rankings**: delete a small batch, check nothing broke (rankings/traffic), delete another small batch, repeat — don't mass-delete everything in one pass.
- **Shopify title tag keeps auto-appending the store name after you've already trimmed it**: check **Store Settings → Marketplace / Store / SEO settings** (one of these three) for an option that appends the store name to every title tag, and disable it. If that's not it, look for a shortcode like `[store name]` directly inside the theme's title tag field and delete it manually.
- **Starting a brand-new site the right way** (Marisa): design in Canva first, then build in WordPress (Avada theme in her case). Before building pages, work through: the free Hey Tony intro SEO course, the Insiders community videos on proper SEO/URL structuring, and Matt's book *Get Found* — for URL structure and keyword basics, before writing real page copy.
- **Local business consistency**: use the same navigation across the whole site, and put the target location in the footer, identically, on every page.
- **Local keyword targeting decision**: target the specific town/city first (e.g. Boca Raton, not the broader "South Florida"), then add separate pages for surrounding towns/the wider region afterward once the primary town is covered.
- **Fixing a redirect plugin that "accepts" a 301 but doesn't apply it** (Anya, Rank Math): stop relying on the SEO plugin's built-in redirect manager — install a separate, dedicated redirect plugin instead. Go to **Plugins → Add New** in the WP dashboard, search/install it, then use its own dropdown/interface to manage redirects there instead.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- **Never build out tag pages heavily on any CMS** — WordPress, Squarespace, Shopify, WooCommerce, "anything." Most tags get used only once or twice, which creates "a lot of dead zombie pages on your sitemap" with barely any content and no traffic — a bad SEO signal.
- **Before deleting any tag or category page, always check GSC first** to confirm it isn't ranking. Ranking → leave it alone. Not ranking → safe to remove.
- **Deleting tags in bulk**: do it in small batches, verify nothing broke after each batch — never delete all of them in one shot.
- **Categories are fine to keep** on blog CMS setups (unlike tags) — they help identify page topic to Google "at a very small level."
- **Audit-tool cache lag**: allow roughly **1 to 12 hours** before trusting a "still broken" flag after a real fix; one case had gone **24 hours** and was confirmed to still just be cache, not a real unresolved issue.
- **Non-indexed low-value pages are fine to ignore** — privacy policy, cart-type pages, etc. Only chase indexing status for homepage, service pages, and blog/money pages.
- **URL structure**: keep URLs relatively short — don't pad them with unnecessary extra words.
- **Migration priority order**: internal linking first; H1/H2 cleanup is "definitely an important thing to look at" but can be deferred to after the migration without penalty.
- **Heading hierarchy and wording**: headings should be in correct order and still read naturally for a human — don't keyword-stuff them (flagged example: "fun games, games for corporate" as something you wouldn't actually want people to read).
- **Stale/old content**: refresh it (new copy or a new backlink) or Google will keep treating it as uninteresting and it won't get reindexed.
- **Shopify title tags**: check for a store-level auto-append-store-name setting, or a `[store name]` shortcode in the theme's title field, if the store name keeps reappearing in titles despite manual edits — this is a known behavior in "certain Shopify templates, usually versions of **Dawn**."
- **Rank Math's built-in redirect manager can silently fail** (redirect shows "accepted" but doesn't apply, doesn't appear in the redirect list) — use a separate dedicated redirect plugin instead.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Not every "not indexed" page is a problem: irrelevant/utility pages (privacy policy, etc.) are fine to leave unindexed — the real triage question is whether homepage, service, or blog ("money") pages are affected.
- Audit-tool "still broken" warnings are frequently just cache lag rather than a real unresolved issue — always cross-check a live/non-caching source (GSC or Detailed SEO) before assuming a fix didn't take, especially since acting on a false flag wastes time re-doing work that already succeeded.
- H1/H2 cleanup is "definitely an important thing to look at" but not urgent enough to gate a migration — internal linking was explicitly ranked as the higher-priority fix when time/attention is limited post-migration.
- Spikes in "Crawled – currently not indexed" can be entirely benign (Google platform-side shifts) rather than a site problem — but when it's concentrated in old/stale content specifically, that's read as a signal to refresh rather than something to just wait out.
- Categories vs. tags is treated as a real distinction, not semantics: categories help Google understand page topic even "at a very small level," while heavily-used tags actively hurt by generating thin, low-traffic pages.
- Local keyword targeting is a tradeoff between the specific-town term (lower competition, tighter match to the actual business location) and the broader regional term (what a directory-style competitor appears to rank on) — resolved by pulling real search-volume data (Semrush) rather than inferring intent from what a competitor happens to show up for.
- AI tools (ChatGPT/Claude) were repeatedly framed as good for general orientation and idea generation but explicitly insufficient for decisions that need measurable data — GSC and Semrush were positioned as the actual sources of truth over AI output for indexing status and keyword volume respectively.
- Some diagnoses were left open on the call itself (e.g. Catherine's HTTP/HTTPS audit-tool flag) with a "this looks like a tool error, not a real issue" conclusion rather than a fully confirmed root cause — appropriate given the live Q&A format and limited per-question time.
