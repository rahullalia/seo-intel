# 085 — Content Writing Training with Kira (10.02.26)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Location-page keyword workflow** (done live in Semrush Keyword Magic Tool): enter a candidate location phrase → read Volume + Keyword Difficulty (KD%) together → check your own Domain Authority (Semrush Domain Overview) → pick the variant whose required DA-to-rank is at or below your actual DA, not the highest-volume phrase.
- If the big-city keyword's required DA is far above your actual DA, test cheaper variants in sequence: tried "websites Orlando" (irrelevant results) → "website agency" / "web design agency" (weaker fit) → landed on "Orlando web design company" (lower volume, realistically winnable) as the actual page target.
- Once the big-city page is running, layer in a second, smaller/nearby city as an easier bonus location page (e.g., Cocoa Beach, ~20 searches/month) purely to bank a fast, easy win.
- To pick which nearby city to target at all: ask "what's the closest big city to me?" — target that first even if you don't live directly in it, as long as you're not literally lying about your location (no fake zip code, no "I live in X").
- In the footer, you can still list/link the bigger nearby city name (e.g., "Orlando") even though your dedicated page targets a more winnable phrase like "Orlando web design company" — it's just a link/anchor, not a location claim.
- **Diagnosing a traffic drop**: in Semrush Domain Overview, pull up the organic-traffic graph and the organic-keywords graph together, then line up the date they bend against the site owner's own publishing calendar — check whether they simply stopped writing around that time before blaming the algorithm.
- **Backlink audit workflow** (Semrush): Domain Overview → Backlinks tab → scan "First Seen" / "Last Seen" dates and anchor text → hover the link at the bottom-left of the browser to preview the real destination URL before clicking → sort mentally into legitimate publisher mentions vs. spammy/foreign-language link-farm sites (some links even get silently removed months after appearing, for no stated reason).
- **Getting backlinks without a detectable AB exchange**: use featured.com (HARO's replacement) — browse journalist/blogger questions in your niche, submit a short expert answer with your link; if picked, you get a citation + backlink from their site (sometimes a major publication). Batch it: put 20 minutes on the calendar per day; the dashboard shows whether your pitch got published and supplies the live link once it does.
- **Content-ops batching**: do an entire task type in one sitting across the whole month instead of daily/piecemeal — e.g., all of a month's keyword research done in one batch, not spread week to week — because switching between task types (writing vs. images vs. keyword research) uses different parts of your brain.
- **Batch images separately from writing**: publish the blog post text-only first rather than let missing images block publishing; set aside a dedicated later block (e.g., 30 minutes) to drop images into several already-published posts at once.
- **Content consolidation** (already done by a participant): when you find duplicate/overlapping pages (she ended up with 2 SEO pages + 2 web-design pages after a redesign), delete the old ones and redirect them into the new consolidated page.
- **Post-booking content play** (already implemented by a participant): after someone books an appointment, route them to a "thank you / here's what to expect on our call" landing page that also links out to recommended reading — turns a transactional confirmation step into another content/internal-link touchpoint.
- Auto-generated table of contents on blog posts is called out as helping AI/crawlers parse and read the page structure (not just human navigation).
- Segment your own keyword strategy by which of two buyer situations you're actually in: (1) brand-new-to-SEO / near-zero traffic → prioritize low keyword difficulty even at lower volume, to generate visible momentum fast; (2) already have real traffic but it's the wrong/unqualified traffic → prioritize relevance/qualification over more raw volume.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Keyword Difficulty (KD) is a 0–100 score = roughly the Domain Authority you'd need to have a realistic shot at ranking page one for that term.
- Example: "Orlando Web Design" = 1,300 monthly volume, needs DA ~53 to realistically rank page 1 (stated live on the call, with a brief self-correction from "30" to "53").
- Semrush color convention: red = harder keyword difficulty, green/yellow = easier — always read Volume and KD columns together, never volume alone.
- Don't chase a keyword whose required DA is far above your current DA — that's "good traffic, really hard to rank"; take the easier, lower-volume variant instead while your DA is low, and change the target keyword later once authority grows.
- A location page for a nearby big city is fine even if you don't live there, provided you aren't lying about your actual location (no fake zip code, no "I live in X" claim) — the page's keyword focus and the footer anchor text are just SEO choices, not factual location statements.
- AB backlink exchanges (I link you, you link me, directly) no longer work well — Google can detect the direct reciprocity and discounts it as favor-trading rather than a genuine endorsement of "credible" content.
- ABC backlink exchanges (A→B, B→C, C→A — no direct reciprocal link between any two sites) are what backlink agencies run instead, specifically because they avoid the detectable AB pattern.
- Two segment-specific priorities, restated as a rule: brand-new/no-traffic sites → optimize for low KD first, volume secondary; sites with traffic but wrong-fit leads (her examples: plastic surgery and lawyer clients who get calls for services they don't actually offer) → optimize for lead relevance/qualification, not just more volume.
- Don't assume wide margins + huge hero images are "wrong" just because they force extra scrolling — that's Kira's personal reading preference, explicitly flagged as not a universal rule (some huge, high-traffic blogs use that exact layout and still perform).
- Algorithm-drop risk is specifically tied to AI-generated content getting flagged — genuinely helpful, human-created content tends to "even itself out" after an update.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- High-volume/high-competition vs. low-volume/easy-to-rank is explicitly framed as "a never-ending dance" — there's no universally correct choice; it depends on your current DA and on which of the two buyer-type goals (visibility vs. qualified traffic) you're actually solving for right now.
- Deliberately choosing lower-volume-but-winnable keywords early is as much a confidence/momentum play as a traffic play ("I want you guys to feel that... look at this upward motion") — visible early wins keep a solo operator motivated; the primary keyword target can be upgraded later once DA rises.
- Image-heavy vs. text-block blog layout is a preference/prioritization call, not a hard rule — "none of this is like, this is how you do it... I'm going to prioritize this over this" — with explicit counter-evidence that some huge blogs with millions of views use large images/wide margins and still perform.
- Backlink acquisition trades effort for reliability: passive/reciprocal (AB exchange) is easy but increasingly ineffective and detectable; engineered reciprocity (ABC exchange via an agency) works better but needs third-party coordination; earned PR (featured.com/HARO-style) is the "credible" route but is slower and unpredictable (not every pitch gets picked) versus a guaranteed placement.
- Manually vetting/disavowing spammy backlinks is acknowledged as extremely time-intensive ("people literally spending an entire day going through backlinks") — implicitly not a good use of a solo operator's time without a dedicated backlinks specialist; better ROI sits in generating new legitimate links (featured.com) than auditing old spammy ones.
- Tool numbers (DA, KD, volume) are estimates that vary by platform (Semrush vs. Ahrefs vs. KeySearch) — directionally consistent, but Google Search Console is the more accurate source for actual organic traffic.
