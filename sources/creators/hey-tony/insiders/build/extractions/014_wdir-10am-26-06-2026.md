## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Rank-week page fix (Diana)**: if a page isn't ranking, ask "does this page actually answer anybody's question?" — if not, put the core question front and center at the top of the page (as a heading), then traffic to that page picked up.
- **Location + service page structure (the master rule, given to Marisa the comedian)**: menu = Home / About / Contact / Buy [product] / Services (one page per service, e.g., Comedy, Master of Ceremonies) / Locations (one page per city). On each location page, target only ONE service — whichever is easiest to rank for in that city — then just mention the other service in passing (an H2 tag or plain text, e.g., "I also do emceeing — looking for an emcee? I do that too").
- **Refined location-page rule (given to Ariel, photographer)**: your MAIN/home-base location can have a separate page per service (e.g., "Chicago Newborn Photographer" AND "Chicago Family Photographer"), since that's your flagship market. For every OTHER (satellite/suburb) location, pick only ONE service per location page — never stack multiple services under one satellite location — chosen via keyword research on what's actually easier to rank for in that specific city.
- **Sequencing a full location-page buildout**: finish all location pages first (one page each), each one targeting whichever service is easiest to rank there → only after all location pages exist do you move on to blog content.
- **Auditing heading tags for keyword/topic match**: go through every H1/H2/H3 on the page one at a time and ask "does this support what the page is about?" Anything that's styling, a name/tagline, a CTA ("Inquire Now"), a testimonial quote, or a specific client name (e.g., "Ann and Derek at Lake Simcoe") should not be a heading — demote it to plain text.
- **Diagnosing a page that isn't ranking at all (Prito, wedding photographer, not even page 10)**: check (1) Google Search Console signed up + sitemap submitted, (2) Google Business Profile connected to the site, (3) backlinks/citations — his gaps were mainly thin local citations/directory listings; Matt's read: "probably just a matter of trust."
- **Checking a page's on-page targeting**: look at the actual H1/H2 wording against what the target keyword really is — Prito was writing "romantic storytelling wedding photography in Toronto" when people actually search "wedding photographer Toronto"; rewrite headings to match real search phrasing (Matt's rewrite: "Timeless wedding photography in Toronto").
- **Location content for a photographer without a location page per suburb**: instead of a full page for every small town near a competitive metro, do a "sessions" page/gallery for specific venues you've actually shot at (e.g., "Ancaster Mills sessions"), and use it both to attract search traffic and to show prospective couples a gallery from their actual venue when they inquire.
- **Blog topic ideas for wedding photographers** (insider-tip angle, from Matt's wife's own experience getting traction with this on social): "how to set up your wedding day timeline," "what happens when you fall behind on your wedding day timeline," "10 things to know before your wedding day," "10 things that'll go wrong on your wedding day (and how to fix them)."
- **Fixing a default/theme archive page you can't style (Jennifer's portfolio "Clients" archive)**: use the "Go Full Page" Chrome extension to take a full-page screenshot of how you WANT the page to look, then hand both that screenshot and the existing page's code to Claude Code with a prompt like "here's the default page, here's the code for that, I want it to look like this."
- **Fixing indexing gaps**: internal-link to any page/post that isn't indexed — if a page takes more than 3 clicks to reach from the homepage it's effectively "buried" and Google may never find it. Also drive external traffic to it (social, email list).
- **Manually requesting indexing (GSC)**: Search Console → "Inspect any URL" → paste the page's link → wait for it to retrieve status → if it says not on Google, click "Request Indexing."
- **Deciding whether it's safe to change a URL slug**: check if the page is (a) not ranking, (b) not getting traffic, and (c) has no backlinks — if all three, change the slug freely. If it IS indexed already, you're still "probably safe" to change it.
- **Checking if a specific page has backlinks**: use the HeyKeywords tool's per-page lookup (Matt demoed it live: pulled up a page and it showed the domain's full backlink list but returned 0 for a specific page he knows has links — a current tool limitation); look for a secondary tab/button like "link analysis" for a more reliable per-page number.
- **Med spa CRM pages competing with your own site (AJ/Andrea/Cheney, Zenoti)**: build the same service pages (facials, microdermabrasion, Botox, etc.) on your own domain and try to rank those instead of the CRM's; link FROM each of your own service pages OUT to the CRM's booking page for the actual appointment. If the CRM's pages are already ranking and generating business, leave them alone. If they're competing against your own site, contact the CRM vendor and ask about a noindex setting for their pages, or get a custom domain/subdomain for the booking system so it lives under your own domain.
- **Getting a 3rd-party directory listing to actually help you (Marissa, "The Bash")**: if a directory/booking site is ranking and showing your name (even without sending you the booking directly), reach out and ask them to also link to your own website.
- **Homepage vs. money-page keyword targeting**: if you're not actually trying to rank your homepage for a given keyword, remove that keyword from the homepage title tag (use your name/brand instead) and put the "[keyword] — [name]" pattern on the dedicated service/money page that IS targeting it.
- **Fixing an outdated but still-ranking WordPress site**: don't do a full rebuild/platform change — update the page-builder plugin(s), fix mobile-responsiveness bugs (images cut off/sideways), and delete defunct elements (e.g., a leftover Google+ icon in the footer).
- **Migrating a site to a new builder/platform without losing rankings**: keep the exact same domain, port over identical content, and keep every URL exactly the same — you can change how it looks, but not the URLs or content.
- **Learning an unfamiliar builder/platform (Elementor, WordPress, etc.) fast**: ask ChatGPT or Claude directly — "how do I do this" or "find me a tutorial that does this" — since the course/community doesn't cover every platform's specific UI.
- **Showit users**: Showit has a separate sitemap from its WordPress blog integration — submit BOTH sitemaps to Google Search Console.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- Never build a page for the same city targeting two different services — one location page = one service, full stop.
- Main/home-base location is the exception: it can carry a separate page per service; every other (satellite) location gets only one service page.
- If a page IS ranking — for anything, even a keyword other than your target — driving any traffic, do not touch it.
- If a page is NOT ranking: wait 3 months, then 6 months, doing internal linking with descriptive anchor text in the meantime, before touching the page again.
- If still not ranking after 6 months: redo the Rank Week audit exercise — check who ranks #1/#2/#3, count their word count, note their topics/structure, then match/rework your page.
- Once a location/service page is built and it's driving traffic (even indirectly), leave it alone — don't keep re-optimizing it.
- Money pages should not link out to blog content as "a feature" — link to at most 2-3 genuinely essential resource pages (e.g., "what to wear," "how to calm your baby," "what to expect") rather than any random post. Blog posts should link TO the money page, not the reverse.
- Blog posts don't always need to rank or drive direct traffic themselves — sometimes their only job is passing internal links to the money page.
- A page not indexed, not ranking, no traffic, no backlinks → free to change the slug.
- Sitemaps auto-update and auto-resubmit to Google every time you publish or change a page/post — never manually resubmit a sitemap.
- If content is reachable only via more than 3 clicks from the homepage, treat it as effectively invisible to Google.
- If you're already ranking for a keyword, don't touch that page — reiterated as a rule for the whole group, not just the person asking.
- SERPs shift over time: a page that ranked #1 for ~2 years on 600-800 words can get outranked later once competitors add more content — that's when you redo the audit, not before.
- If you no longer offer a service, remove the page targeting it rather than continuing to try to rank it.
- On Squarespace, "unused JavaScript/unused CSS" audit flags are not fixable — no developer-level access on that platform.
- Delete unused/orphaned pages an audit tool surfaces (e.g., a leftover "squarespace test" page) if nothing links to them and you're not using them.
- Looker Studio is optional reporting/visibility only — it will not help you rank higher; skip it entirely if it's not clicking for you.
- Get local directory listings/citations if a site (especially a competitive-niche one like wedding photography) isn't ranking anywhere and has thin backlinks.
- Connect your Google Business Profile to your website.
- Get everyone's questions in during THIS call rather than saving them for the following (larger, new-insiders) call, so everyone gets help.
- Keep helping other members inside the Insiders community platform — Matt can't personally answer every question right away.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- **Big competitive metro vs. small dispersed market for location pages**: Toronto/GTA is a "massive area" with 40-50 small towns each likely already having its own locally optimized wedding photographer with a Google Business Profile — hyper-local suburb pages are harder to win there. In Florida, where cities run 60-80K people and locals think in regional terms ("South Florida," "Central Florida"), one page per (smaller) city is still worth doing.
- **One-service-per-location vs. topical completeness**: choosing the single easiest-to-rank service per location sacrifices covering every service in every city, in exchange for a realistic shot at ranking each page at all.
- **Linking out from money pages**: total silence (never linking anywhere) isn't the goal either — 2-3 genuinely essential resource links are fine and even helpful; the nuance is restraint, not zero linking, and never treating outbound links as a "feature" of the page.
- **Patience vs. impulse to fix**: the 3-then-6-month wait is explicitly meant to override the urge to keep touching underperforming pages — but that patience has a ceiling: if nothing's happened by 6 months, that's the trigger to actively redo the competitive audit, not to keep waiting indefinitely.
- **"Set and forget" has an expiration date**: a page can hold #1 for years on relatively little content and then quietly lose that position once competitors escalate — long-held rankings still need periodic re-auditing, not permanent trust.
- **3rd-party CRM/booking pages ranking instead of your own (Zenoti)**: if those pages are already the ones generating business, the "correct" SEO move is to leave the messy setup alone rather than disrupt what's working — the noindex/custom-domain fix is only worth pursuing once those pages start actively competing against, rather than helping, the business's own site.
- **Old/dated site aesthetics vs. ranking risk**: an outdated-looking, decade-old WordPress site that's already ranking and converting is explicitly NOT a candidate for a "let's modernize it" rebuild — visual modernization is secondary to protecting existing rankings; migration is only safe if URLs and content are preserved exactly.
- **Backlink-checking tool reliability**: HeyKeywords returned zero backlinks on a page Matt confirmed does have them — a live, acknowledged tool gap rather than a dependable single source of truth; treat a "no backlinks" reading from it with some skepticism and look for a secondary check.
- **Looker Studio's decoupling from ranking**: it's purely a client/self-reporting visibility layer with zero effect on actual SEO performance — safe to skip entirely without any ranking cost if it's not clicking for you.
