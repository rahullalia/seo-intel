## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Diagnosing a location page that "won't rank" (Chloe's case)**: if a keyword-optimization tool is comparing your location page against a local-only competitor's *homepage* (their de facto money page) rather than an equivalent page, recognize that homepage is likely their most-backlinked page on the whole site — you're not on equal footing unless you commit real backlink building to that specific page.
- **Finding a hidden easier-to-rank location target**: pull Search Console's top queries for an existing location page — if a nearby suburb/smaller town name shows up as a top query (Chloe's Atlanta service page had "Duluth, Georgia" as a top query despite the page being built around "Atlanta"), build a dedicated new page targeting that smaller town by name (e.g., "Duluth IT services") instead of only the big-city term.
- **Location + service page architecture for a multi-location service business (Chloe, IT support/cybersecurity, 9 locations)**: pick ONE "home base" city for your core service pages (e.g., "Lubbock IT support," since HQ is Lubbock, TX) rather than trying to rank a bare national term like "cybersecurity services." For every other location, build one page per location targeting exactly ONE service/keyword — do not stack multiple services onto a single location page.
- **Location + service page architecture for a single-practitioner local business (William, Toronto tarot reader)**: build the main city page combining home-base + service (e.g., "Toronto tarot card reading"), keyword-research ~10 service variations (e.g., relationship tarot reading, shadow work tarot reading), then build one page per surrounding suburb/town (Mississauga, Oakville, Hamilton, Burlington, Ancaster, Dundas, Markham, etc. — ~40 suburbs around Toronto), pairing each town with whichever single service keyword has the best volume/lowest difficulty for that specific town. One service + one location per page, not several services stacked on one location.
- **Neighborhood-level pages for geographic authority (Coach D, junk removal, 7 cities incl. Raleigh as capital/strongest city)**: break a city into the named sub-areas people actually search (Toronto example used: "the beaches," Leslieville, Queen West) and build one page per neighborhood+service combo (e.g., "junk removal beaches Toronto," "junk removal Leslieville").
- **Building pages for near-zero-volume hyper-local keywords anyway**: don't skip a location/service page just because the keyword tool shows ~0 search volume — build it, reasoning that historical zero-search doesn't mean zero future demand. If a keyword shows zero volume AND zero difficulty, you can get away with a short page — roughly 400–500 words, no need to go in depth.
- **Fixing a duplicate-H1 issue (Mariana's wedding-ring blog)**: a page must have only ONE H1. If a second heading was styled as H1, convert it to an H2 tag — don't delete the content, just downgrade the tag.
- **Diagnosing "low-difficulty keyword isn't ranking as expected" (same case)**: pull the keyword's difficulty score in Semrush (came back 7 — very low, shouldn't require backlinks to rank), then manually review what the other top-ranking pages for that exact term are doing content-wise, and increase on-page usage of the exact target phrase (she was using it 4x) including placing it in an H2.
- **Deciding whether to remove "toxic" backlinks flagged by an audit (Camilo, Semrush)**: don't disavow/remove by default. Only intervene on backlinks defensively if the site has zero traffic, isn't ranking at all, or appears shadow-banned. Otherwise the fix is adding MORE good backlinks (to outweigh the bad), not subtracting the toxic ones.
- **Troubleshooting low-impression blog posts in Search Console (Jennifer's case)**: first confirm the posts are actually indexed — if yes, that alone is a win. Then check Google Analytics for referral traffic specifically from chatgpt.com/claude.ai/other LLM domains, since blog posts can be doing work (AI citation, internal-linking context for money pages) that raw impressions/clicks don't show. In a highly competitive niche (she named web design/SEO, since so many competitors are SEO-savvy themselves), the fix is getting MORE specific/niche with topics, not assuming blogging failed — e.g., a teammate's "how to add custom icons in Avada" post (and matching YouTube video) still performs well long after publishing because every other tutorial online on that exact task was bad.
- **Sequencing after blogging is "under control"**: move to location pages next — prioritize smaller towns/suburbs over the one big nearby city — while continuing to blog in parallel and interlinking new blog posts to location pages.
- **Choosing your "main" target city after physically moving somewhere smaller (Jennifer: Orlando → tiny community)**: keep the bigger, recognizable city (Orlando) as the primary target rather than switching to the new small town, since the small town alone likely can't support the business volume needed; build separate location pages for the smaller surrounding towns instead.
- **Author-authority tactic**: add a byline (photo, name) linking to a dedicated author page that includes social media links, certifications, and any awards/association memberships — ties content to a verifiable real identity, on top of whatever plugin/theme already auto-generates date + "X minute read."
- **Getting platform-specific how-to answers**: just ask ChatGPT/Claude directly, naming your exact platform/theme (e.g., "I'm using WordPress with [theme]" or "I'm using Shopify"), or ask in the community / the group's web designer (Jennifer) / attend Leanne's calls.
- **Website design-polish approach (Nary's button/paragraph alignment question)**: don't over-index on making design "perfect" on the first pass — pick a consistent approach (e.g., don't mix a clearly off-center CTA button under centered section text in a way that visually reads as accidental) and keep building; layouts differ across mobile vs. desktop anyway, and the whole site will get revised later. Ask Jennifer or attend Leanne's calls for detailed alignment help.
- **Multi-AI blog cross-check workflow (Matt's own experiment)**: developed the same questions in ChatGPT and separately in Claude, then fed ChatGPT's output into Claude and asked it to identify anything the ChatGPT version did better that Claude's own draft missed, merging the best of both. Claude's version was better overall, but a few ChatGPT-sourced elements were worth keeping — cost only "a couple of minutes extra."
- **Fact-checking AI SEO advice**: when AI explains how to rank in AI search, remember it's paraphrasing what humans already wrote online somewhere — not automatically true. Validate against (a) what's actually working for real accounts in the community, (b) human logic ("would I, as a human, respond to content this way?"), and (c) a second reputable source before trusting an untested claim.
- **Capturing tacit expertise as content**: record a subject-matter expert's natural spoken explanation (Matt's hardware-store analogy — a staffer explaining exactly which toilet part you need, why, and how the packaging/sizing works) rather than only writing generic copy; that "train of thought" explanation format is described as increasingly valuable content.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Never block AI crawlers from your website — reacting to falling click traffic this way is called "the stupidest thing" people are starting to do.
- Write content for humans, not "for AI" — explicitly rejected framing content as written to please an AI; AI itself evaluates content based on how valuable it is TO humans.
- Directly answer the target question at the very top of a piece of content (this is what a participant's AI explained back to them when asked directly "how do I show up in AI search/LLMs" and "what is AEO").
- Do more of what's actually working per the group's own tracked results — not more of whatever an AI or outside source claims works.
- Private chats inside ChatGPT/Claude are NOT fed back into public search-ranking or AI-citation systems — if they were, "it would be so easy to game the system." What your own chat history DOES do is build a private context/database of your business that produces better, more specific outputs in future sessions — a different mechanism from public ranking influence.
- AI's citation/recommendation engine draws only from what's publicly crawlable: your website, social media profiles, newsletters — not private AI chat logs.
- Using multiple AI tools (ChatGPT + Claude + Gemini, etc.) does NOT itself improve external AI-search visibility — visibility comes from what's public on your site, not which tool drafted it. Multiple tools CAN improve final content quality via cross-checking.
- Don't disavow or bulk-remove "toxic" backlinks flagged by an audit tool by default — Matt has seen clients remove flagged links, lose traffic, and blame the agency. Only touch backlinks defensively if the site has zero traffic / isn't ranking at all / appears shadow-banned.
- A page must have exactly ONE H1 — a second heading styled as H1 should be downgraded to H2.
- Location pages and service/location optimization are still necessary under the new AI-search model — Google's AI mode runs multiple underlying searches per complex query and still needs that same on-page location/service signal.
- One location page = one service + one location keyword target. Don't stack multiple services onto a single location page.
- Prioritize smaller towns/suburbs adjacent to a big city over the big city itself when picking location targets — easier to actually rank, and per-town searchers are "much more qualified" even if volume is only "one or two clicks a month from each."
- Roughly 15% of Google searches are brand new every single day, and rising (Matt's stated figure) — zero historical keyword volume isn't proof of zero future demand.
- If a target keyword shows zero volume and zero difficulty, ~400–500 words on the page is enough.
- A genuinely low-difficulty keyword (example cited: Semrush difficulty score of 7) shouldn't need backlinks to rank.
- YouTube is described as the second-biggest search engine in the world, owned by/connected to Google, and Google is pulling information from YouTube video transcripts.
- Long-form video/YouTube content is comparatively hard to fake with AI right now (proof you're a real person/business) — though Matt notes "it's getting easier to do that" over time.
- Bing, Yahoo, and other search engines are believed to be "a step behind" where Google currently is on the AI-search shift.
- Google's new AI-mode interface is replacing the 25+-year-old type-a-keyword/blue-links search bar with one that expands as you type and proactively suggests/refines the query, more like prompting than searching.
- For a genuinely competitive keyword (a homepage), a location/service page usually cannot outrank a real local competitor's homepage unless you build backlinks specifically to that page.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **Traffic vs. citation**: falling click traffic is framed as an acceptable, even expected, tradeoff as long as AI citation/mention visibility is rising — but Search Console currently has no way to directly confirm AI citations, so agencies (and Matt himself) will need to do more manual client education about drops that don't reflect an actual performance problem. Matt expects citation-tracking features to eventually reach Search Console.
- **SEMrush-style "AI visibility" scoring**: called out as methodologically weak — the tool has AI generate synthetic prompts, feeds them to another AI, and checks for citation; real users don't type those exact invented prompts, so a low score there doesn't necessarily mean low real-world visibility.
- **Top-of-funnel vs. bottom-of-funnel content** (from Matt's conversation with Edward Sturm): both remain important post-update. If AI trusts and cites your top-of-funnel (informational/blog) content, it's more likely to also recommend your bottom-of-funnel (conversion/service) pages as the actual solution — so you still need both content types, not just one.
- **Using multiple AI tools**: doesn't move external visibility directly, but cross-referencing outputs (e.g., ChatGPT then Claude) can meaningfully improve content quality for a small added time cost — though it can also just create extra work for no gain when the first draft would've been fine, and there's no way to know in advance which case you're in.
- **Location page vs. local competitor's homepage**: usually can't out-rank a genuine local competitor's homepage (their most-backlinked page) without committing backlinks specifically to that page — the practical workaround is retargeting smaller/adjacent towns where you're not fighting a dedicated local homepage.
- **Chasing zero-volume long-tail/local keywords**: looks unproductive by traditional keyword-volume logic but is reframed as forward-looking, given ~15%/day new-query growth — trades unprovable near-term ROI for position ahead of demand not yet reflected in the data.
- **Toxic backlink removal**: audit tools flag them confidently, but removing them carries real observed downside (traffic loss, per Matt's client examples) against unproven upside — the asymmetry argues for leaving them alone unless the site is already failing outright.
- **Staying with a bigger "away" city vs. your literal small-town location** (Jennifer): trades geographic accuracy for practical rankable search volume, since the small town alone may not carry enough demand to sustain the business.
- **YouTube/video as a real-business signal**: currently a strong authenticity signal specifically because it's harder to fake with AI than text — but Matt flags that advantage is eroding as AI video generation improves.
- **Building for AI search vs. building for humans**: explicitly not the same lever — AI evaluates content on human value, so optimizing to please "AI" as a reader directly is the wrong frame; optimizing for genuine human usefulness is what carries through to AI citation too.
