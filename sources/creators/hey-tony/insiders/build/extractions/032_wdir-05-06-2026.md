## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **AI static-ad creative workflow (Diana):** screenshot your current ad sets/ad performance → upload to Manus (Meta's AI agent) → ask it to critique your creative mix (e.g., "I need more static ads, do you agree?") → ask it to write an image-generation prompt for that ad type → paste that prompt into ChatGPT along with an uploaded personal photo → get back a finished static ad.
- **ChatGPT Advertiser Program cross-platform test (Kirill's plan):** retool a couple of landing pages, then run the same creative with roughly equal budget (~$1,000 each) across Facebook, Google Ads, and ChatGPT ads at the same time, starting the following Monday, for about a week to a month; compare results side by side and package findings into a video/report to share back with the group.
- **Recovering blank CRM-generated service pages (AJ/Shani, Collab Med Spa):** audit your booking/CRM platform (Zenoti) for auto-created service listing pages → check whether Google is already indexing them even with blank metadata (it may be) → prioritize your top profit-driving services first → use ChatGPT to write meta descriptions and meta keywords for each → reverify indexing afterward → publish blog posts 1-2x/week and internally link each post to the matching service page so Google recrawls it.
- **Press release build process (for Monday's session):** gather recent customer reviews (screenshots are fine — no need to transcribe them) → upload the screenshots directly to Claude → prompt it to write a press release weaving in ~5 of the reviews around target keywords (e.g., "[Business] is a top-rated SEO agency in [City]") → submit through ABNewswire.com (~$80/release). Matt has already done this successfully for a couple of clients.
- **Reviews-aggregator domain play (Edward Sturm's idea via Matt, untested):** buy "[BusinessName]Reviews.com" (e.g., HeyTonyReviews.com) → use Claude Code with a prompt like "build me this website, here's all the reviews" → ship a one-page site aggregating reviews pulled from Google, Yelp, etc. → theory is AI models pick this up as a positive reputation signal.
- **Live AI-citation visibility test (Matt's own demo):** write a "who's the best [service] in [city]" post naming your business at #2 or #3 with reasons why it should top the list → post the identical content simultaneously to your Facebook business page, X, and LinkedIn company page (skip hashtags on LinkedIn) → check indexing with the `site:` search operator in Google → force/accelerate indexing via primeindexer.com (create a project, paste the URL) → recheck the target AI/Google query days later to see whether the post surfaces or gets cited.
- **Local-AEO content method (answering Victor's "how do we get cited by AI" question):** find a specific, narrow question your customers actually ask (Matt's test case: "best hot sauce pairing with a Pinot Noir") → publish content on your own site that explicitly answers it and names your actual product/service → this is what lets AI Overviews/ChatGPT associate your business with that exact query — general topical presence isn't enough if the specific answer content doesn't exist on your site.
- **Word-count / reviews-plugin audit:** run a page audit, compare your real visible word count against top-ranking competitor pages → if a Reviews plugin (e.g., a Google Reviews plugin) is duplicating testimonial/review content across multiple pages and inflating the count, remove it → target a word count in the same range as competitors rather than a fixed number (example given: 3,000 words on your page vs. 500 on competitors' means trim down toward ~500).
- **Blog pillar/hub structure (Caroline's "how to give a cat a pill" example):** build a hub page/post listing multiple named methods with a one-line description each (e.g., Method 1: the peanut butter method, Method 2: the force-it-down method) → publish a separate, fuller post per method → link from the hub post out to each individual method post. Confirmed: the hub should be a blog post (not a static WordPress Page) so it can link out to other posts.
- **Homepage image-layout fix (Marita):** replace an image slider with a static layout instead — either two photos stacked one on top of the other, or alternating blocks: text-left/photo-right, then text-right/photo-left.
- **On-stage/event photography direction (Glenn's tip, applies to any speaker or performer):** shoot a half-to-three-quarter body shot with the photographer positioned slightly behind the performer and angled about 45 degrees out toward the crowd, so the frame captures both the performer (half the face, mic in hand) and a full view of the engaged audience — instead of a static long shot of just the stage that misses audience reaction entirely.
- **Testimonial page-matching:** put location-specific testimonials only on the matching location page (e.g., Boca Raton reviews go only on the Boca Raton page) instead of mixing testimonials from all cities together.
- **Kill dropdowns for FAQ/service content:** convert dropdown/accordion sections into always-visible H2 headers + plain paragraph text underneath, so AI crawlers and Google can read the content without needing to trigger the dropdown open.
- **Google Business Profile + social:** link all active social media handles to the GBP listing — when someone searches the business name directly, GBP can surface "recent posts on social media" pulled from those linked accounts (observed on mobile).

## Rules / dos & don'ts

- Don't make a bunch of site changes all at once — make small changes and monitor them individually, or you won't know what actually worked (Matt's own admitted bad habit, contrasted with the advice he gives).
- Don't connect Google Search Console to a site that's still being restructured — wait, and instead commit to a firm cutoff date after which no more structural changes happen (Marita chose end of June).
- Don't add not-yet-built pages to the site menu — Google can crawl and judge them as thin/empty before they're ready; keep the menu limited to pages that already exist.
- Keep both a "Contact me" CTA button and a separate "Contact" item in the main menu — not either/or.
- No image sliders — use static images, especially checked on mobile.
- No dropdowns for FAQ/service info — convert to visible H2 + paragraph text.
- Match testimonials/reviews to the specific page's location — don't mix city testimonials together.
- ChatGPT Advertiser Program: no stated minimum ad spend, but you're charged every time spend hits a **$25** threshold.
- No hashtags on LinkedIn — per a podcast tip Matt cited, LinkedIn's system reportedly treats hashtags like title tags and pulls attention away from the actual post.
- Watch out on Reddit — real risk of auto-ban or mod-ban; posts need genuine engagement to get pulled into AI results, simply posting isn't enough.
- Watch out on Quora — risk of being flagged/banned (happened to Matt before), even though Quora content (including years-old posts) performs well in AI-cited answers.
- If a page's word count looks abnormally high, check for a reviews/testimonial plugin duplicating content across pages before assuming it's real content — strip it if found.
- Keep LinkedIn content strictly business/professional-framed — content "has to make sense in the business world," unlike Instagram/Facebook where a looser tone works.
- When citing supporting research/psychology angles in blog content, don't link to direct competitors — use neutral third-party sources instead.

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- Sweeping site changes done all at once (Catherine's full replatform to Shopify) can produce a big win but leave you unable to attribute the cause — Matt names this as his own habit even while advising the opposite (small, monitored changes) to the group.
- Getting ad impressions without conversions (Diana) shows visibility alone isn't the goal — a common, distinct gap worth watching for separately from ranking/reach metrics.
- ChatGPT ad delivery is intermittent and demand-matched rather than keyword-targeted — ads only show when someone's current search matches the sentiment profile collected from your creative; low current competition means advertisers who do show up capture outsized share, but the platform is explicitly early/unstable with no real bidding pressure yet.
- Being an early adopter across every new AI-facing platform is high-effort — Matt flags that needing a presence across Facebook, X, LinkedIn, Reddit, Quora, and Instagram for AI-citation purposes runs against just running a business, calling it "super annoying" and "not trying to be online all day, every day."
- Word count isn't inherently good or bad for SEO — it depends on relevance; the guidance is to benchmark against competitor pages rather than chase a fixed target.
- On-site reviews may add page weight/duplication without necessarily earning user trust ("not sure how much people trust reviews on the website... you can pick and choose") — this sits in tension with the untested theory that a dedicated reviews-only domain might be trusted more by AI systems specifically.
- The same underlying content needs different framing per platform, not just reposting: LinkedIn requires a "resume voice"/professional framing even for a comedian or apparel brand, while Instagram/Facebook tolerate a looser, more personal tone (Heather's example: content fine for a psychotherapist client's Instagram is not appropriate for that same practice's LinkedIn).
- Several tactics discussed are explicitly flagged by Matt as unproven, live experiments rather than settled playbook items — the reviews-only domain idea, and his own real-time Facebook/X/LinkedIn indexing tests ("I'm not saying necessarily [to do this]... I haven't tested this").
- A visibility/traffic drop isn't automatically an SEO failure — Matt uses the "zero-click search" phenomenon to explain a client's declining traffic even when the site's actual search visibility held up, and uses that same reasoning to talk himself out of his own imposter syndrome.
