## Tactics & step-by-step how-to

- **Diagnosing "nofollow" as a hidden cause of orphan pages (Shopify/Wix):** open View Page Source on the site, press Cmd+F (Mac)/Ctrl+F (PC), search "nofollow" to spot `rel="nofollow"` links; cross-check in the platform's link editor for a hyperlink options bar/toggle that sets a link to nofollow — check this specifically on footer links, collection-page links, and brand-page links (not just product links).
- **Triaging a large orphan-page count instead of fixing all of them:** pick your ~10 most important pages, add 1–2 extra internal links to each (from a blog post or elsewhere on the site), monitor how those specific pages perform, then decide whether to dig further into the rest.
- **Working around an H1 hard-wired to the logo (Shopify theme, no developer on staff):** if editing/removing the existing H1 risks being overridden by the theme's universal styling, add a second custom H1 tag via the code editor containing the target keyword — redundant but functional as a stopgap. In parallel, escalate to Shopify support to ask specifically why the H1 is tied to the logo and whether that connection can be removed (the real fix).
- **Auditing why a blog isn't ranking for what it's about:** open the flagged article, Cmd+F the target keyword to count on-page usage/density and check its placement in the H1 and body copy.
- **Structuring long, research-heavy blog posts so Google actually reads the value:** move "key takeaways" and the table of contents to the very top of the post, before the deep content — add a TL;DR block up front rather than after paragraphs of research. Rationale given: Google effectively evaluates roughly the first ~500 words of a post to judge relevance/indexing worth, so front-loading matters more than total depth.
- **Diagnosing GSC "Page indexing" report categories:**
  - "Page with redirect" showing old URLs: if those are old permalinks that already have 301 redirects in place, this is not an error — GSC is just cataloging pages it knows have redirects; no action needed.
  - "Alternate page with proper canonical tag": Elliot did not answer live — said he'd research canonical tags specifically and post a follow-up note in the Insiders group.
- **Verifying whether a ranking-position jump is "real" or noise:** in GSC go to Performance → Queries, scan the clicks/impressions/position columns; Cmd+F a suspected irrelevant keyword to jump straight to its row and see its actual click count before concluding it's a problem.
- **Live competitor keyword-density check (page-audit method):** Google the target keyword, open a top-ranking competitor's page, Cmd+F the exact keyword phrase to count how many times they use it, then compare that count to the client's own usage.
- **On-page keyword consistency fix:** pick one exact keyword phrase (backed by real search-volume research) and use that *exact* phrase — not a variation — in the title tag, meta description, H1, and body copy.
- **Escaping high competition on a broad head term:** replace broad terms ("mechanic near me," "graphic and web designer") with city/neighborhood-modified long-tail versions ("mechanic [City], Texas," "graphic and web designer in [Montreal neighborhood]"); for a city with distinct neighborhoods/boroughs, build a separate location page per serviced area.
- **Service-page build-out:** convert homepage service blurbs into individual dedicated pages, each targeting its own keyword variant with real search volume (example given: "white paper design for [X]").
- **Escalating a stalled Google Business Profile verification:** resubmit once through the standard flow (the red "verify profile" prompt); if it still doesn't resolve, contact Google Business/local-business IT support directly rather than repeating video verification indefinitely — Elliot dropped a link to Google's Business Profile Help Center in the call chat for this.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)

- Tag pages and category pages should be set to **noindex** — "those pages as a whole don't provide any value... they're not your money pages." Generalized rule: "if you have like 50 different tag or category pages, those aren't going to be indexed [anyway]."
- Google crawls roughly the **first ~500 words** of a blog post to judge whether/how to index it — value signals placed after that point may not get weighted.
- New blog posts take time to get crawled/indexed — normal "ebb and flow," not automatically a problem.
- A page with only ~2 inbound internal links can still register as "orphan" in audit tools if those links are set to nofollow (nofollow tells crawlers not to pass through/follow that link).
- Products linked only from a collection/category page normally will NOT throw nofollow/orphan flags — those are fine by default.
- Opening an internal link (e.g., a portfolio gallery image) in a new browser tab has **no effect on indexing** — it's a UX/navigation choice, not an SEO issue.
- Two businesses with different names and different websites can share the exact same physical address and each still get its own separate Google Business Profile verification — identical address alone doesn't block a second listing.
- Zero-click, low-impression rankings on off-topic queries (e.g., #3 position, 3 impressions, 0 clicks) do not damage overall site metrics — "I would not worry about that at all."
- A broad "near me" keyword ("mechanic near me" — 2,200,000 searches/month) is extremely high competition; expect impressions without meaningful clicks/position even with strong SEO effort elsewhere on the site.
- Every individual service should have its own dedicated page — homepage blurbs alone are treated as an SEO gap ("ideally I should have like a page dedicated for every service" — "Yep, absolutely").
- Keep the exact target keyword phrase (not a variant) identical across title tag, meta description, H1, and body copy.
- Working blog recipe (confirmed as effective, not just proposed): topic = "common issues with [a specific year/make/model]" + 3 internal service links + 1 authoritative external link (a manufacturer TSB/service bulletin confirming the issue and fix).

## Tradeoffs & nuance

- **Orphan-page triage vs. fixing all of them:** with hundreds of flagged pages, Elliot explicitly advises against chasing every one — put link-building effort into the top ~10 money pages first and treat the rest as lower priority rather than trying to resolve all ~600 individually.
- **Redundant H1 as a workaround, not a real fix:** adding a second H1 tag to carry the keyword is presented as an acceptable stopgap given a no-developer, logo-bound-H1 situation, but the stated ideal is still getting Shopify support to fully disconnect the logo from the H1 — the workaround is knowingly imperfect.
- **Ranking for irrelevant terms isn't automatically bad:** David's position jump came bundled with nonsensical rankings (shoplifting, head-to-head with national retailers on unrelated terms), but because his actual target blogs kept driving real traffic/authority and the stray terms were zero-click, the guidance was to leave it alone — GSC's aggregate position metric can look distorted by noise without the underlying strategy being wrong.
- **Depth vs. crawl priority in long-form content:** Sankesha's "Deep Brew" article was praised for genuine research depth, but that same depth worked against indexing because the value signals were buried past Google's effective ~500-word attention window — thoroughness has to be paired with front-loaded structure, not treated as sufficient by itself.
- **Broad/high-volume vs. long-tail keywords:** chasing a 2.2M-searches/month term ("mechanic near me") yields impressions but poor conversion due to competition; a lower-volume, location-specific term is explicitly framed as the higher-value target despite the smaller search volume.
- **Uncertainty handled transparently, not guessed:** on the "alternate page with proper canonical tag" GSC issue, Elliot declined to improvise an answer and instead committed to researching it and posting a follow-up in the Insiders group.
- **New-tab gallery links:** a plausible-sounding technical worry (does opening portfolio images in new tabs break indexing/crawl paths?) was ruled out as a non-issue — a reminder that not every UX quirk maps to an SEO problem.
