# Why Doesn't It Rank — 10.04.2026 (Matt Diamante)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Reverse-engineer a competitor's focus keyword**: go to pageaudit.com (free), paste in their page URL, leave target keyword blank, click Analyze. Go to the "Content" section and check word density at 3-word phrases — this reveals what keyword the page is actually built around (Matt's own live example: his SEO page showed "search engine optimization" / "SEO in Hamilton" this way).
- Once you've spotted a competitor's apparent keyword, verify/improve it by running it through a keyword tool (Hey Keywords Tool or Semrush) to check for easier-to-rank variations or longer-tail versions — don't assume their choice is optimal.
- **Primary/secondary keyword system**: use one exact "primary" keyword (the one you actually want to rank for) plus several "secondary" keywords (easier to rank, often longer-tail) worked naturally into the same page's content.
- **URL vs. H1/title split**: put the long, natural-language keyword phrase in the H1 and title tag (e.g., "Bookkeeping services in St. Petersburg, Florida for small businesses"), but keep the URL short (e.g., `site.com/services/bookkeeping`) — don't stuff the full long-tail + city string into the slug.
- Homepage URL = bare root domain only. Build a dedicated service page for each specific service rather than trying to rank the homepage for a specific service term.
- **Hard-to-name niches** (cat behavior consultant, comedian/entertainer, tarot reader): run keyword research across every plausible naming variation (consultant/coach/therapist/trainer/behaviorist, US/UK spelling, etc.), compare volume + difficulty, then commit to targeting only 1-2 of them rather than trying to rank for all simultaneously.
- **Avoiding blog-vs-money-page cannibalization**: money page owns the exact buyer-intent phrase (e.g., "Hydrafacial in Scottsdale"); blog posts cover adjacent educational angles (pros/cons, health benefits, "how long does X take") and only reference the exact money-page phrase once, typically near the bottom as a link back to the money page.
- **Nav/site simplification**: identify the single primary action you want a visitor to take (e.g., "Book"), then move everything non-essential (store, careers, policies, partnerships) into the footer; ~7-8 top-nav items is fine; grouped items (Meet the Team, partnerships, careers) can live under an "About" dropdown.
- **Multi-city service businesses**: don't build a page per city × per service/category (one photographer had drafted 25 cities × 6 event types = 66 pages — flagged as far too many). Instead, target the primary/home city on each core service page, then add one "locations I serve" page or homepage section linking out to the secondary cities.
- **National/virtual service with no single physical base** (e.g., tarot reading across Canada): don't try to rank on a generic non-geo term first. Optimize the homepage for the single largest nearby metro (Toronto, not "across the country"), then build separate location pages for the smaller surrounding cities/suburbs (Mississauga, Oakville, Stouffville, King City).
- **Studio town vs. bigger nearby city** (Algonquin, IL studio vs. higher-volume Schaumburg): keep the homepage targeting the actual studio town, and build a distinct "photography studio" page targeting the bigger nearby city, framed around proximity ("20-minute drive from Schaumburg").
- **FAQ implementation**: mark FAQs as H2 tags; answer the question directly and briefly, then link out to the full blog post for depth; from the FAQ, link onward to the relevant blog post, and from the blog post link onward to the specific product/service page (FAQ → blog → product chain).
- **Fixing "Discovered — currently not indexed" pages in GSC**: don't resubmit URLs manually — increase internal links pointing at those pages, publish blog content that links to them, and build backlinks; keep publishing on a regular cadence so Google actually crawls deeper into the site.
- **De-optimizing an over-stuffed collection/category page**: count how many times the exact-match keyword appears; if a competitor's equivalent page uses it far less (with less overall copy), trim the page rather than add more.
- **Competing against giant competitors on a head term** (department stores, Adidas): concede the broad head term and shift the page's target to a descriptor-first long-tail variant (e.g., "navy leather ballet flats" or "silver ballet flats" — modifier before the product name) instead of the bare category term.
- **Systematic optimization order for large catalogs**: list every candidate keyword with its search volume and difficulty, sort by highest volume + lowest difficulty, and start optimizing pages in that order.
- **(Untested/exploratory) GSC keyword-refresh loop**, described from a friend's fully-automated AI blog system, which Matt is considering trialing on a side project (not on Hey Tony or client sites): every ~2 weeks, per published piece of content, pull Google Search Console data for that URL, identify keywords it ranks for on page 2-4, and rewrite/add that language into the existing content to push those long-tail terms higher.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- **Max 2 high-quality blog posts/week** — that's what Matt's agency runs for clients with good results; technically you could publish 5/week, but quality is the limiting factor, not capacity.
- **Do not let AI fully auto-write-and-publish blog content unsupervised** — Matt explicitly: "I do not recommend this because it's a slippery slope if you just let AI do whatever."
- Testimonials can be moved higher on a page (e.g. right under the hero) if it helps conversion — no fixed rule against it.
- Prefer the more specific/local term people actually say over a broader regional label when both are viable (Boca Raton over "South Florida").
- Nav menu items / logo text should **not** be marked up as heading tags.
- **Both phone numbers and email addresses must be clickable** on the site for mobile usability.
- Just because a competitor uses a keyword doesn't make it the right one — verify independently.
- You do not need to repeat the exact focus keyword throughout a page — use it once as primary, plus natural secondary variations; copy must read like a human wrote it, never awkward from stuffing.
- Location/city name **should** be in the title tag on money/location pages.
- **Do not cram the full long-tail keyword + city into the URL slug.** Matt's own SEO page ranks #1 in Hamilton without "Hamilton" anywhere in the URL, H1, or H2 tags — proof you can rank for a hard local keyword without spamming the URL.
- On Squarespace, optimizing images is basically the only speed lever available — there's little else you can do on that platform.
- Site speed only matters relative to competitors: if everyone's equally slow, it doesn't cost you; if a competitor is meaningfully faster and everything else is equal, they'd "theoretically" outrank you — but Matt has "seen slow websites outrank fast ones," so it's not deterministic.
- Blog posts can mention the exact money-page keyword phrase, but generally only once, near the end, as the link-out — not repeated throughout.
- **FAQ count: 3-4 per page/topic is the general rule/max.** 12 FAQs on one page is "too many" — split onto a dedicated page and keep each FAQ relevant to whatever page it's on.
- If pages with a footer-only blog link aren't getting indexed, move the blog link into the top navigation menu.
- **Domain Authority (Semrush/Ahrefs-style scores) is "a made up metric."** A low score (example cited: DA 7) isn't proof you're failing — sites with low DA do rank well. The only real lever to raise it: more organic traffic + more/better backlinks.
- Sending Google Ads traffic to dedicated landing pages does **not** hurt organic SEO.
- A generic, reused-every-month "specials" page is fine for SEO — won't hurt rankings and will likely surface for "[business] specials" searches.
- If a keyword returns zero/near-zero volume across every variation tried in a keyword tool, that's a real signal that people genuinely aren't searching that exact phrase, not a tool failure.
- Ranking for a keyword nobody searches is pointless, but roughly **15% of all searches every day have never been searched before** (Matt's cited stat) — so don't over-index purely on existing measurable volume.
- If a keyword-suggestion tool outputs something nonsensical for a service page (example: "cat fighting help" suggested for an inter-cat-aggression service), don't force that literal phrase into the H1 — rephrase naturally (e.g., "Stop your cats from fighting") while keeping the underlying topic.
- The "money page doctor" tool is meant for analyzing money/service pages (it infers what keyword a page is currently optimized for) — don't necessarily apply its output the same way to blog posts.

## Tools, prompts & resources
- **pageaudit.com** — free; paste a URL, leave keyword blank, click Analyze, check the "Content" tab's word density (3-word phrase view) to reverse-engineer a page's real focus keyword.
- **Hey Keywords Tool** (Matt's own tool) — pulls keyword volume directly from Google Ads (the underlying source everyone uses), whereas Semrush/Ahrefs/Ubersuggest each apply their own undisclosed adjustment/estimation rules on top of that same data, producing different volume/difficulty numbers per tool. Matt: "they are basically like making up the volumes." Accuracy is expected to improve as more users feed the tool's database. Discount code: the old one had expired; Matt recreated a fresh Stripe coupon live (50% off, restricted to the lower/base-tier product) — worth checking if a code is still live/valid.
- **"Money page doctor"** — analyzes existing on-page content and reports what keyword/topic the page currently reads as being optimized for.
- **Semrush** — used for a free-trial keyword/competitor audit; also the source of the Domain Authority-style score discussed as "made up."
- **Ahrefs** — named alongside Semrush/Ubersuggest as another tool with its own differing keyword volume/difficulty estimates.
- **ChatGPT** — used alongside the keyword tool to build a "strategic use of keywords" list (Lori) and to help generate keyword-phrase candidates (Neri: "bookkeeping services in St. Petersburg, Florida for small business").
- **Rank Week** — Matt's separate flagship training, referenced by multiple attendees (Regine, Gareth, Gillian) as prior training completed before/around this call.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Blog cadence is a quality/quantity tradeoff, not a hard technical cap: Matt's agency runs 2/week for clients successfully, but he notes a friend's fully-automated daily AI-written-and-published blog is still working (getting recommended in Perplexity) — yet he still won't recommend that path due to quality-control risk, and is only considering testing the GSC-rewrite loop on a side project, not on Hey Tony or client sites.
- Keyword-tool volume/difficulty numbers are inherently inconsistent across vendors because each applies its own undisclosed adjustment on top of the same raw Google Ads export — cross-tool comparisons are unreliable, and Matt frames his own tool's improving accuracy as a function of growing user data rather than a fixed advantage.
- Whether to use a competitor's exact focus keyword or a variation is explicitly a judgment call — "it can definitely be a variation," "you have to make that determination yourself" — not a hard rule.
- Site speed's ranking impact is conditional/relative rather than absolute, and Matt explicitly caveats it with a counter-observation ("I've seen slow websites outrank fast ones") — a poor PageSpeed score isn't an automatic penalty, especially on a locked-down platform like Squarespace where little beyond image compression is possible anyway.
- Over-optimization is a real, distinct failure mode alongside under-optimization: Gareth's page repeating its keyword 41 times is flagged as likely hurting, not helping — there's a ceiling on keyword density, not just a floor.
- Pillar-page/silo architecture is criticized as often being more about consultant billing/scope than SEO necessity, but the underlying content isn't dismissed as worthless — Matt's fix was folding it into existing collection/category pages rather than treating it as a separate page tier.
- Head-term vs. long-tail is a volume-vs-winnability tradeoff: against department-store-scale competitors, concede the broad term and shift to descriptor-first long-tail phrasing that can realistically rank, rather than sinking more content into an unwinnable page.
- Location targeting is a two-way tension resolved case-by-case: sometimes stripping a location off the homepage is wrong (William's tarot site — the location needed to go back in) and sometimes cramming every serviced city onto one page is wrong (the 66-page multi-city example) — the operating rule is "target your single biggest/most relevant local market on core pages, spin out true secondary markets to their own linked pages," but which market counts as "biggest" and how many deserve their own page is judged per business.
- Zero recorded search volume doesn't necessarily mean a keyword/page is worthless — countered by the ~15%-novel-query stat and by Carol's real booking from an unmeasured search phrase — particularly relevant for very local or very new query patterns.
- Store/e-commerce pages that aren't core to a service business (Nancy's talks-booking site store, Coco Rose's candles/face-masks) are treated as acceptable to deprioritize from SEO entirely and route through non-organic channels (existing newsletter list) instead of optimizing everything on a site equally.
