## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **30-day SEO plan** (spreadsheet shared in chat, organized by Week 1/2/3/4, same structure each week with a different page):
  1. On the "blog posts" tab, grab **4 questions** from findquestions.com (Matt says rankweektraining.com is "the same thing" and may load faster) or from Rank Week training content.
  2. Grab **4 money pages** (if you don't have 4, use however many you have — e.g. Matt himself only had 2 on heytoni.ca "services").
  3. Each week: **publish one blog post** that links to **3 other blog posts + 1 money page**.
  4. Run the audit tool (heytoni.ca, discount code **"June 2026"**) and **fix 5 serious/red-flagged issues** — the tool has embedded "how to fix" tutorial videos per issue, just click through.
  5. **Fix the week's money page title tag + meta description** — use ChatGPT, Claude, or "an LLM of your choice."
  6. **Add 3 internal links to that money page** from other pages on the site.
  7. Repeat weeks 2–4 with a new money page and a new blog post each time. If you have 46 product pages, "don't worry about it, just do four" and keep working down the list — don't try to do everything at once.
- If your Monday-written post isn't indexed yet: **link to it from 3 older blog posts** to help it get picked up. If you don't have other posts yet, write one post/week and interlink them all.
- Diagnosing a "page seems to not be crawling fully" problem: paste the URL into ChatGPT, Claude, and Google and ask **"how many words are on this page?"** — re-run it once (sometimes there's a transient glitch), but if it consistently returns far fewer words than the real page (and title/meta come back "server not verified"), the host/site is blocking bots — contact your host and ask why (often a firewall on cheap hosting to save server costs).
- Split combined service pages into one page per distinct service (people searching one don't care about the others).
- For e-commerce with many SKUs: audit collection pages + your top-selling products first (not the full catalog) — add real content (like a mini blog post) underneath the product grid on collection pages, since most competitors leave them bare.
- Rotate which money page gets linked from each new blog post (e.g., a 10-service business cycles through its 10 offers); for a mixed collections+products catalog, alternate: one week link to a collection, next week to a product.
- Homepage strategy: don't try to make the homepage rank or cram every service into it — use it as a **funnel to money/service pages**, and keep a separate, full services page that lists everything in depth.
- FAQ strategy for near-identical product pages: write **different, product-specific FAQs** for each page (what carat, what metal, how to clean/store this specific item) — imagine what a customer holding that exact item in front of you would ask; never duplicate the same FAQ block across every page.
- Local/service-area SEO build-out (real estate example): pick your **top 20 neighborhoods** first, build a "realtor in [neighborhood]" page for each with unique text per page (no duplication), and separately publish "hidden gem" content about each area (e.g., best coffee shops nearby, photographed on your phone) — this speaks to relocation buyers' lifestyle concerns, not just listings. Pace the build-out (e.g., 1–2 pages/week for a 200-neighborhood list) rather than trying to finish all at once — and you can **stop once a subset is already ranking/converting**, you don't have to finish the whole list.
- Location page structure: put location pages under a dedicated **Locations page**, not nested inside a specific service page.
- Word count for local pages: 400–500 words is fine if the term isn't competitive; if it is, audit who's currently ranking and mimic their approach/depth (can go up to ~2000 words) — but always prioritize organizing it so an actual human reader can consume it and decide to hire you.
- International SEO: do keyword research **in the target country/language** rather than assuming your home-market research transfers — Matt's own agency ranks broadly across the US while based in Canada.
- Targeting an overseas/lower-cost-market client base (e.g., India-based agency selling to US/UK): don't chase generic head terms (they return local-intent results); go long-tail with qualifiers your buyer actually cares about (e.g., "website designer in India who speaks English and works 9 to 5 Eastern") and state those reassurances explicitly in your page copy.
- AEO (answer engine optimization) = "it's all just SEO" — there's no separate playbook; ranking well on Google raises your odds of being surfaced in AI answers. Practical version: add FAQs, structure content in answer/Q&A format.
- Auto-embedded "recent posts" block (3–5 previous posts shown under a new one) in an email/CMS template is a free, automated internal-linking mechanism if your tool supports it — no extra manual work required.

## Rules / dos & don'ts
- Blog cadence: **1–2 posts/week**; Matt's own top-paying clients get 2/week. Don't go beyond what you can sustain — inconsistent or excessive volume risks quality dropping and Google flagging the site as spammy.
- Every distinct service = its own page. Never combine multiple services onto one page.
- Never duplicate the same FAQ set across multiple product/service pages — each must be unique to that specific page/product.
- Don't target zip/pin codes as keywords — "people aren't typing in zip codes," they type city names.
- Don't try to rank a whole broad region/market at once — "pigs get fat and hogs get slaughtered." Take a slice, win it, then expand.
- The #1 reason people don't end up ranking is **giving up too early** — Google intentionally makes it take time, specifically to filter out spammers who quit the moment results aren't instant.
- Schema markup: not worth the time — explicitly placed outside the "20% of effort that drives 80% of results."
- Carousels hurt: people don't click through them, and per Ariel's Insiders takeaway, they can quietly eat into/"cannibalize" a page's effective word count/content signal without you realizing it.
- Reporting a competitor who uses your trademark in their Google Ads copy to Google does nothing — Google doesn't police this. The actual remedy is legal (attorney, cease-and-desist) — not legal advice, consult a lawyer.
- No country tag needed in the URL unless the site is genuinely multi-language or has different regional pricing/currency.
- Email sends: cadence/consistency matters far more than time-of-day. Pick a day and stick to it; sending too often (e.g., 10 emails/week) trains people to unsubscribe.
- Zero/low measured search volume is not automatically a reason to skip a keyword (see Carol case + the 15% stat below).
- You don't need to finish an entire planned page set (e.g., all 200 neighborhood pages) if a subset is already producing rankings/business.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Low/zero measured search volume isn't a hard "skip" signal — Google Trends measures relative momentum, not absolute volume, and ~15% of daily Google searches are entirely new queries, so a "no volume" term can still be worth targeting.
- Blog cadence is a quality/consistency tradeoff: pushing past what you can sustain (beyond ~2/week) risks both diluted quality and a spam flag from Google — better to run a lower, sustainable cadence than to burn out and stop, since stopping early is the actual failure mode, not a modest pace.
- Long-tail/qualified terms beat broad head terms for both local and international targeting — broad terms (generic "website design," "top realtor in Maryland") return local-intent or giant-competitor results and are effectively unwinnable; specific, qualifier-rich long-tail terms convert and are actually rankable.
- Word count for local/location content is competition-dependent (400-500 words when uncompetitive, up to ~2000 when matching aggressive competitors) — but human readability/organization matters more than hitting any specific count, since the actual goal is getting a person to read it and hire you.
- You're not obligated to complete an entire planned page set once you're winning with a subset — but you also shouldn't try to build them all simultaneously; pace deliberately (1–2/week).
- Whether to invest in SEO at all is conditional on where a business already converts — Matt told Joan that thriving via Instagram/events/referrals is a legitimate reason to not prioritize organic SEO spend, since "everybody runs a business in a different way."
- Expired domains aren't uniformly bad — safe to buy-and-redirect an exact-match expired domain to your main site, but risky as a new primary domain due to invisible legacy penalties from prior ownership/backlink history that don't show up as an obvious "shadow ban."
- NotebookLM podcast embeds are a plausible engagement/SEO upside (time-on-page, alternate content format) but Matt explicitly flagged this as untested by him, not confirmed — a genuine open question, not an endorsement.
- Homepage-as-funnel vs. homepage-as-in-depth-service-page is a judgment call depending on how buried/prominent secondary offerings currently are on a given site, not a fixed rule.
- Scaling unique FAQs across many near-identical SKUs (e.g., jewelry) is labor-intensive, but Matt frames the effort as justified because it's in service of selling more product, not just an SEO technicality.
- Traffic from outside Google (email opens, social clicks back to a blog post) functions as a trust/authority signal to Google, not just a nice-to-have distribution channel — so newsletter cadence/consistency has an SEO dimension beyond relationship-building.
