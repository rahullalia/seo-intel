# Web Design for SEO with Leanne — 15.04.2026 (Leanne Jones, Leanne Digital)

## Tactics & step-by-step how-to
- **Hero section template** (repeated for nearly every reviewer): big image + H1 headline + short sub-text describing it + one call-to-action button, all visible without scrolling — visitor should be able to act "without even having to scroll, without even really having to think."
- **Split H1 trick**: your real (SEO-friendly, often plain/boring) keyword phrase stays inside the actual `<h1>` tag but can be styled visually small; a punchier, non-SEO "impactful headline" sits next to/below it styled bigger (as an H2). Leanne's own site: H1 = "digital marketing services" (styled small), H2 = an impactful tagline styled large.
- **Home page framework** for service businesses (given to Katya): Hero (H1) → trust-building elements (either folded into the hero or placed just below it) → "who we are"/about-us section → services section. Rationale: visitors don't know you yet, so introduce yourself before pitching.
- On homepage, keep the "who we are" bit to a short blurb and link out to a full About page for the deeper story, rather than putting the whole bio on the homepage.
- **E-commerce homepage structure** (given to Catherine): hero section (image + CTA linking to latest release/seasonal collection) should NOT carry the H1. Put a real, keyword-targeted H1 + a short paragraph of body copy directly below the hero instead — this way the rotating promotional hero can change constantly while the H1 stays fixed.
- Category/collection pages get the more specific, competitive keyword (repeated in the category title, every product title, and body copy underneath); the homepage H1 stays broader/durable.
- **Fixing "wall of text" pages** (Victoria on Wix, David/Chuck's Garage on Kukui): the fix is white space, not less text.
  - Add margin above/around text blocks (demoed live: added 50px margin above a section in Wix's inspector panel — immediately more readable).
  - Left-align long blocks of body copy; reserve center-alignment for short blurbs only.
  - Align the CTA button to match the text alignment (e.g., left-align button under left-aligned text) for cohesion.
  - Increase line-height (space between lines) to add breathing room.
  - Add white space on the sides of text blocks/columns, not just top and bottom.
  - Fix letter-spacing where characters sit too close together (found on Victoria's site between an "A" and "S") — adding letter-spacing made it "breathable."
  - Use a column-based grid to organize content instead of letting elements "float" (Leanne personally uses a 4-column grid). Demoed a 3-column layout in Figma (image / body copy / second text block).
- **Lengthening a page without it feeling long** (given to David for hitting a word-count target):
  1. Two-column text layout — break one long block into column 1 / column 2.
  2. Alternating image/text rows — image-left+text-right, then swap to text-left+image-right, repeating down the page.
  3. Subtle background color/shade shift between sections as a visual cue that a new section/paragraph has started.
  4. FAQ accordions — collapsible content adds word count without visually looking like more text on the page. If reusing this on the homepage, write **new** text rather than duplicating the FAQ copy already used on another page (e.g., the Contact page).
- **CTA hierarchy fix** (fishing guide site): when a hero has two competing buttons, keep one dominant (solid/bold) and demote the other visually (e.g., transparent/outline style) so only one clearly reads as "the" action.
- **Low-res/no-good-photo workarounds** (Pavitra, interior design):
  - AI upscaling tools to fix low-resolution images: Image Upscaler, Upscale Media, Canva's built-in upscaler (Pavitra confirmed Canva's "works pretty well").
  - Stock photography is an acceptable substitute for a hero image as long as it's descriptive/relevant to the business — real photos are ideal but not mandatory.
  - Layout alternative when you lack one great high-res photo: layer a smaller, higher-resolution inset photo of someone performing the service over a blurred/zoomed background image (e.g., a blurry shot of the office), rather than forcing one big photo.
- **Exact hero image sizing recipe**: in Canva, create new design → Custom size → enter 1440 (width) × 740 (height) px.
- **WordPress Pages-vs-Posts fix** (AJ): if blog content was mistakenly built as Pages instead of Posts, and the pages are already ranking, leave the existing URLs alone — going forward, use WordPress's actual Post function for new blog content rather than migrating and risking a redirect/URL change.
- **Single-word inline styling** (Caroline, Astra Pro/Gutenberg): wrap the target word in a `<span>` tag and apply a custom style to that span via code; ask ChatGPT to generate the span/CSS if you don't code.
- **Getting backlinks**: (1) sign up for Chamber of Commerce listings and business directories — Leanne's own About page links out to a directory she's listed in, and that listing is itself a backlink; (2) write guest posts on other people's websites/blogs with a link back to your own site embedded in the post.
- **Platform-blocked-by-CMS workaround**: ask ChatGPT directly, e.g. "I'm using this platform, how do I add in another section," when the CMS/template won't let you expand a layout (used for David's Kukui platform).
- Review queue in these live-critique sessions runs strictly in the order links were posted in the chat (first posted, first reviewed) — Leanne works top to bottom and doesn't always reach everyone.

## Rules / dos & don'ts (keep every threshold, number, specific)
- Keep the H1 **consistent** long-term — don't swap it every time a seasonal promotion changes; let the rotating hero/promo area do the seasonal messaging instead.
- "Swimwear" alone judged too broad/competitive a keyword to realistically rank for; get more specific (Catherine's own example: targeting by **cup size**).
- Do **not** embed an Instagram feed on your homepage — "it just slows down your website." Traffic should flow Instagram → website, never the reverse. Exception: makes sense if you're specifically an influencer.
- Don't stack multiple trust-builders (photos, star ratings, org logos) all at the very top of the page — it reads as "busy"/overwhelming; place them further down in a simple row instead.
- Sans-serif fonts = "down to earth," easy to read, good default for most sites; serif fonts (with tails) = more luxurious feel — match the choice to brand positioning (Leanne recommended sans-serif/Open Sans for an outdoorsy fishing-guide business specifically because it isn't a luxury experience).
- Two competing CTA buttons hurt conversion: "if people have too many choices, they sometimes just choose not to make any choice."
- Shopify **always** puts its H1 tag in the site logo "for some reason" — flagged as a known platform quirk; a workaround exists (not detailed).
- If a platform/template genuinely can't support adding text or headings at all (e.g., Pavitra's legacy Squarespace gallery template), that's the threshold to switch templates or platforms — don't keep trying to force-fit content.
- If existing content is already ranking, don't restructure URLs just for tidiness — the redirect/ranking risk outweighs the cosmetic benefit.
- Word-count numbers cited for Chuck's Garage (automotive, Kukui platform): started around 800 words, currently at roughly 1,000–1,200 (both figures given in the call); Matt's guidance target is **~2,000 words**; competitor site cited at roughly **2,000–2,200 words**.
- Recommended hero image dimensions: **1440 × 740 px**.
- Example margin fix: **50px** added above a text section transformed readability.
- Leanne's personal grid system: **4-column grid**.
- Backlinks: want authentic, genuine backlinks — avoid building "a little network of just people just backlinking with one another." A prior weekly (every-Tuesday) reciprocal blog-linking arrangement between Jessica's group was flagged by Matt directly as something to stop doing.
- Stock images are fine for a hero as long as they're descriptive of the business; not mandatory to use only real photos, but real photos build more trust.

## Tradeoffs & nuance
- Stock photography is acceptable (not a compromise to be embarrassed about) as long as it's descriptive of the business — but real photos of the actual team/business are still positioned as the stronger trust-builder when available.
- Whether to fix WordPress Pages-built-as-blog-posts is a tradeoff between backend/architectural cleanliness and SEO risk from changed URLs/redirects — Leanne came down on the side of leaving already-ranking content alone and only fixing the process going forward.
- On reciprocal backlinking, Leanne explicitly declined to give a definitive yes/no to Jessica's specific accountability-group swap idea and deferred to Matt/Denroy as the backlink specialists, while still stating the general principle (want authentic links, not a closed network) — signaling this is a genuine gray area rather than a clean rule.
- A longer page isn't inherently bad — length is fine as long as it's broken into digestible, well-spaced chunks; the "problem" attendees kept perceiving as "too much text" was almost always actually a spacing/whitespace problem instead.
- Two CTA buttons aren't forbidden outright — the fix is establishing visual hierarchy (one dominant, one demoted) rather than removing either one.
- Center-aligned text isn't wrong in general — it works for short blurbs but becomes hard to read at the scale of full paragraphs, where left-alignment is preferred.
- Platform constraints get different responses depending on severity: a minor limitation (Shopify blocking a single title edit, Wix not letting a box move) gets a workaround or is left as a known issue, whereas a platform that blocks all text/headings entirely (Pavitra's legacy Squarespace template) crosses the threshold into "switch platforms."
- Brand color choice isn't always the designer's call — the salmon/pink color on the fishing-guide site was the client's decision tied to the salmon-fishing theme, and Leanne worked within that constraint rather than overriding it, only adjusting the supporting palette (text color) around it.
