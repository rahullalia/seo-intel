A site map is a file that's hosted on your website that users don't really ever see. But it tells Google which pages and Google and other search engine which pages and files you think are important on your website. And it also provides valuable information about these files, for example, when a page was last updated, or any alternative languages or versions of the page. So don't worry. Sitemaps can be generated automatically when using any major website builder. And even if you're not using any major website builder and you're doing it on your own, there are sitemap generators that you can find out there. It's super simple to do. But if you're using something like WordPress or Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, all of those, generate your schema markup automatically. So you don't need to worry about that. Google actually lets you upload your sitemap directly to their search platform. So that's something that's super important as part of your SEO strategy. So when submitting a sitemap, it can help decrease the amount of time it takes Google to index and rank your website. So if your pages aren't getting indexed on Google within, you know, a week or two weeks or three weeks, it depends on how often you update your website. But if they're not getting ranked, let's say within a month, then you need to go in and check that your sitemap has been submitted to Google Search Console, which we'll have a whole tutorial video about coming up. You don't need to worry about that. So sitemaps, just keep that in the back of your mind. It's something that's super important that tells search engines what all the pages are on your site, how they're connected, and how they're related to each other.
