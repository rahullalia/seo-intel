# Duplicate Tags

Every page on your website should be unique, and it should be about a unique topic, meaning that you shouldn't be talking about that same topic anywhere else on your website. You can reference those topics, but you shouldn't have an entire page about the same topic. If you do, you'll have multiple pages with the same title, tags, meta descriptions, or H1 tags, and Google won't know which page is the right one for users, so it may not show any of them. You're essentially plagiarizing yourself. And Google very much frowns on any kind of plagiarism, especially if you're taking it from another website. But if you're taking it from your own website, I mean, nobody's going to say anything, but Google's going to know and they're going to basically say, you know, we can't rank you any higher than we already are because we don't know. You have two pages with similar content on it. You know, which one's the more accurate one? Which one do people like better? And at that point, you'll be cannibalizing your own search rankings. So what you can do in that instance, again, I'm going to have a whole video on the how to's for all of these. These are basically just descriptions and filling you in on what all of these terms mean and what all the elements are for SEO. But essentially, if you have two pages with similar content, maybe you did it by accident or you forgot you wrote a blog post years ago about this, a similar topic. You can take both those pages and merge them into one longer form piece of content and tell Google like, hey, this page that used to be here is no longer here and it is now this page that exists over here. So that's how you would deal with if you had, you know, any duplicate tags or anything like that on your website, or you could just go and update the tags and make sure all the content is unique on every single page.
