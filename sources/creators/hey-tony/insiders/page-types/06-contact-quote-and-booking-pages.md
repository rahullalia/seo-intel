# Contact / quote / booking pages

*Part of the HeyTony page-type research (`page-types/`). The rules every page inherits are in `00-rules-for-every-page.md`; the citation key, sources, and file map are in `README.md`.*

The contact page is a conversion page, not a ranking page. It ranks only for your business name plus "contact", "call" or "get in touch", it is allowed to be thin, and Matt and Leanne judge it entirely on whether the right people call, book, or submit the form. Booking and appointment pages are treated the same way: the service page is the money page, and it sends the visitor to the form or booking tool; the form itself is not the money page. Quote forms are the exception that spreads: Matt wants a short one on every product and service page as well as the dedicated Get a Quote page.

## What it is for and what it targets

- Leanne: "the contact us page isn't a page that we're like, striving super hard to rank high for. It's. We mostly want to make sure that it is optimized for converting the right customers." [call 045]
- The contact page ranks only for brand plus "contact / call / get in touch"; do not try to optimize it for the main keyword. Contact pages are not ranking targets (Marisa, confirmed by Matt); the playbook lists them as not keyword-targeted and not expected to rank. [calls 052, 021; playbook III 3.2]
- Contact, booking and appointment pages are not money pages because "those pages don't rank"; send SEO traffic to the service page, which then links to contact. A service page CTA into a contact or intake form "is the right way to do it... That counts as money page. The contact form doesn't." The booking page is not the money page; the money page can link to the booking page or an external booking platform, and a third-party booking tool is explicitly not your money page. [calls 075, 074, 114; playbook III 3.1]
- The opposite framing also appears: "Your contact page might be a money page", and booking and reservation pages are money pages ("It could be your booking page. Airbnb reservation page. Sure."). See the disagreement note. [calls 058, 025]
- The only KPIs Matt cares about: "people clicking on the booking page and filling out the form or buying the thing." [call 026]
- Booking friction loses the lead even if you rank. Matt, searching for a therapist: "their website doesn't let me book a thing online. So there's a barrier there. I have to call somebody. And then when I call them they're like, yeah, you have to do an intake. We have to set up a time for that... I go to the next person on the list." Ranking number two or three with easy booking can beat number one. [call 005]
- Contact pages are allowed to be thin: "Another example of a page that could have low content is your contact page... This page is okay to not have a lot of content. Google's going to index this as your contact page." [course: low-word-count-how-to-fix]
- Define the conversion first: "what do we want them to do? Do we want them to fill out a form or do we want them to call you?" Decide the one conversion action and make it the dominant button; the electrician's answer: "I want people to fill out this form over anything else." Bill (caravans) prefers phone calls over email, so his site pushes people to ring and leave a message rather than submit forms. Think from the prospect's point of view: "they might want to book a call, they might want to just call. They might want to fill out a contact form and get more information." [calls 106, 060, 024, 124]
- A booking page alone is not enough: a fertility coach with "booking page, general info page, no specific service pages, and a pop up lead magnet" needs dedicated service pages before anything can rank. [call 119]
- Forms used by existing customers (security-document request forms) should stay indexed if customers Google them: "that's a navigational keyword." A candidate application page (nanny agency) can stay noindex while the "work with us" page is indexed. [calls 054, 068]
- A podcast website's page set: "a homepage, a booking, Work with us page, featured guests, and then maybe like a sample of the podcast" (embedded Spotify playlist). [call 017]
- The book: on service pages, "Tell people what to do next. Book a call? Fill out a form? Don't leave them guessing." Free quotes count as a lead magnet; sticky banners on product or service sites offer "something like '10% off your first order' or 'Get a free quote.'" If someone lands on a service page "but don't fill out your contact form," trigger a follow-up email. GBP reports "How many calls, messages, or bookings you're getting directly from Google." No email covers contact pages. [book ch. 40; book ch. 46; book ch. 47; book ch. 35; email 2026-08-04]

## What goes on it, top to bottom (the structure)

- Leanne's full contact page build (Figma, mock massage therapy clinic), in order: (1) hero / banner, cohesive with the rest of the site so it "makes people feel like they are traveling on the same website", H1 simply "Contact us" (no keyword chase), plus a short bio about booking with the clinic; (2) phone number, email address, hours, socials; (3) the contact form; (4) testimonials; (5) the map; (6) optional accreditation logos and an FAQ block; (7) footer. [call 045; playbook IV Web Design]
- The form goes as close to the top as possible: "We want to have our contact form as close to the top of the page as possible... so people don't have to scroll." Above the fold means what is visible before scrolling (her newspaper analogy). On a Google Ads landing page: "we really want the contact form as close to the top as possible and our call to actions just right at the top. So nobody has to do any scrolling before they can like get to actually purchasing." [calls 045, 016]
- Put the highest-priority contact method above the fold: "If you wanted, like, someone to call you the most, then we probably want to highlight your phone number" at the top and put the form below. [call 045; playbook IV Web Design]
- Reassurance line beside or under the form (Leanne): "I respond within 24 hours of every form inquiry," "a real person will review this," or "Amanda is going to review this and she'll get back to you, kind of personalize it so it feels real." [call 016]
- Testimonials on the contact page reduce hesitancy: "they might have some hesitancy when they reach a contact form, like, this is the next step. And so we want to help reassure them." "You do want to make sure that you're using real reviews." [call 045]
- Map: a screenshot of the map uploaded as an image and linked to the Google Maps URL, not a live embed (HeyTony's site does this). Details under Visuals. [call 045]
- Accreditation / association logos "would help increase trust in your customers." [call 045]
- FAQs on the contact page answer booking questions ("how often should I get a massage? Do I need to bring anything? What can I expect"): "Once people feel kind of reassured that all their questions are answered, that typically helps increase conversion rates." Simple FAQs can sit on the contact page; the curriculum offers them as the optional word-count lift: "Google knows that contact pages don't typically have a lot of words on it, but if you wanted to, you could add some FAQs here to increase that word count." Chuck's Garage's contact-page FAQ accordion is fine, but its text must not be duplicated on the homepage. [calls 045, 007, 055; course: low-word-count-how-to-fix]
- Form length is a lead filter: "if you're getting too many, ask more questions. If you're not getting enough, remove some questions." For high-ticket B2B add "what's your budget?"; to maximize volume you might not even request a phone number. The short inline quote form is name, phone, email, short message. [calls 045, 013]
- Useful intake fields: a "how did you find us?" field on a booking form surfaced "I found you on the Internet." A Gravity form with a pre-ticked "subscribe" box on a surveyor's booking form feeds a Mailchimp list that gets each new blog post; that weekly email traffic to posts is a positive "traffic from other sources" signal. [calls 053, 020]
- A thin contact page that is not indexing (Kai, realtor): "there isn't really anything on this page that kind of is doing much besides the contact"; add "even like a short paragraph underneath the get in touch so it's like contact me like I'm your Muskoka realtor... just adding a little bit more value and context." [call 047]
- Skip filler instructions: on the magician's "book now" page, visitors "are already on the contact page. Like complete the form below. Like, why do you even need to say that?"; show the form on the left or right and beside it call / email / WhatsApp. [call 063]
- Local businesses: include the information people are looking for. Two dentist sites compared: one had "request appointment" at the top but no hours, no location, no phone ("I can't just call these guys"); the better one had phone number, text, email, address, contact us and book consultation at the top and "Open Monday to Saturday." "For most local businesses, it's like, include the information people are looking for and you should be good." An old bin rental site with phone number, accepted payment methods (Visa, MasterCard, cash), sizes, and an "order disposal bin" button was judged enough. [calls 101, 105]
- A form page still needs an H1: on a sign-up form page with no H1, add a text block at the top set to Heading 1 ("sign up form page"), "I always have that at the top." [course: no-h1-heading-multiple-h1-headings-defined]
- HeyTony's own contact page: heytony.ca/contact has a form that routes to the team (the example of how to inquire about done-for-you SEO); its headline was cited in passing as "Ready to rank on Google." [calls 013, 114]
- Keep intake information in one place: Elliot's "What to expect in your first call" block repeated on every page is duplicate content. [call 010]
- A praised intake flow (Ash, cleaning): enter your information, get a clean for about $155, or learn more. A praised competitor contact pattern: clear locality, short copy, "contact us button right there." [calls 113, 109]

## Layout and design

- Form on the left or right with call / email / WhatsApp beside it; the hero must match the rest of the site. [calls 063, 045]
- Button contrast is the first conversion fix: if you get traffic but few contact form fills or contact page visits, "The first thing I would try changing would be your button colors and try to pick a button color that has enough contrast." Button styling that passed: accent color with enough contrast (white on teal failed, dark navy text on teal passed), border radius 16px. A CTA button should not have the same font color as body text. [calls 028, 115, 038]
- One action, one color: too many CTAs and colors dilute the action ("Call now for a complimentary consultation," "download your report," and a blue button all competing on Scott's site); the CTA should be "a different color than everything else on the screen." [call 109]
- Identical-looking buttons must not go to different places. Neri's bookkeeping site had one button to a 15-minute Calendly consultation, one to the contact page, and "Schedule call" to the same Calendly, which confused Glenn: "If they go to the same spot, why are they labeled differently?" Suggested labels: "click to book now" / "book a 15 minute consultation". [call 038]
- "Contact us" as a nav item in the same color as everything else on every page does not read as the action; make it a button, or relabel it "Get a free quote" / "Book a free call." [call 103]
- Mobile: phone numbers and emails must be clickable ("if people are on mobile... how are they gonna write it down?"); pest control location page: "Call us. This should be clickable. If you're on mobile, it needs to be clickable. That's just a conversion thing." Tap-to-call everywhere; for a mobile-heavy audience anchor a labeled clickable number at the bottom of the screen; an icon-only floating button with no visible number tanked call conversions. The book's mobile checklist: "Can someone call you with one click? Can they find your contact page or checkout easily?" [calls 058, 070; playbook III 3.4; playbook IV Mobile; book ch. 20]
- Speed: a "Book now" button that lands on a slow-loading contact page hurts (that contact page also had a blurry video). A photographer's embedded scheduler (connected to her CRM) "was really taking up my speed"; she replaced it with a button linking out, despite the advice that booking should be "as easy as possible." A Google reviews plugin "can slow down your website, which can negatively affect your SEO"; test with Google PageSpeed Insights and only remove the plugin if it is slowing the page. [calls 103, 019, 045]
- Testimonial styling on the contact page: plain text in a box with a quotation icon in the brand color, reviewer name bolded, and stars (yellow like Google, or brand color); no plugin needed. Pasted review text works the same for Google as a plugin, and more review text raises word count. [call 045]

## Visuals

- Replace the embedded Google Map with a screenshot image (WebP) linked to the Google Business Profile / Google Maps listing: the official embed triggers "this is slow" in Google's own PageSpeed test, and "people aren't coming down here and looking at this, they're just going to click on the map that will open Google Maps". "You can just upload an image of the map. Like, take a screenshot of the map and upload that into your website and then make this whole image a link." Confirmed on HeyTony's own site. [calls 070, 045; playbook IV Web Design; playbook IV Images; playbook V 4.4]
- Accreditation and association logos as a trust row; real-review testimonial boxes with stars. [call 045]
- Do not leave a blurry video on the contact page. [call 103]

## Length, keyword density, and on-page SEO

- Thin is fine and expected; audit flags (orphan page, low word count) on contact, thank-you, ad landing, category/archive, and funnel pages are routinely intentional and fine to ignore. [course: low-word-count-how-to-fix; playbook III 4.1; playbook III 4.6]
- The H1 does not need the keyword; label it "Contact Us." Optional lifts: a short contextual paragraph (Kai), FAQs, pasted review text. [call 045; playbook IV Web Design; call 047; course: low-word-count-how-to-fix]
- Booking pages duplicated per location: differentiate on title tag and meta description by city; low-stakes booking pages for two cities (escape room, Anaheim versus Connecticut) can differentiate on title plus meta alone ("Booking - Anaheim"). [call 017; playbook IV Canonicals; playbook VI 3.3]
- External booking systems (Zenoti, med spa): put the service content on your own site, link to the booking page from each service page, and noindex or custom-domain the external booking pages if they compete. [call 014]
- Search forms that create "?query=" URLs produce canonical noise; the static page is what should rank. [call 110]
- Agentic crawlers now fill out mock contact forms and click menus (Denroy), so interactive elements are seen. [call 015]
- NAP and address: keep NAP identical across every listing and present and crawlable in the site footer; make the on-site business address a clickable CID link rather than plain text (these links exist for the search engine, not human click-through); do not publicly list a home address in schema or GBP for a remote business (safety). [playbook V 4.4; playbook V 4.5; playbook V 4.3; playbook V 4.1]
- Open Graph "type" can describe "is it an article, is it, you know, contact page". [course: open-graph-issues]
- Webinar registration confirmation pages: set to draft once the webinar is over. [call 050]

## CTAs and conversion

- Forms are a ranking signal: people completing a form are "showing Google that people are taking action on your website and that's good for ranking." Embedded (third-party) forms still count: "I think that's fine. Like, just have like a redirect to like a thank you page or something." Form fills from out-of-area visitors still count as positive signals even if you decline the work. [calls 013, 026]
- Thank-you redirect and page: redirect form submissions to a thank-you page; Jennifer's post-booking page says thank you, here is what to expect on our call, plus optional extra reading. Thank-you pages reached only after a form submission are legitimate orphan pages. [calls 013, 085; course: orphaned-page-no-internal-links-point-to-it]
- Quote forms on product and service pages: keep the dedicated Get a Quote page (full form) and add a short form (name, phone, email, short message) on each custom-product page, because conversions signal Google and it removes a click; a "Get a quote" button already sits in the header. Top-ranking pages often have the contact form on the service page itself; one shared quote form fed from every money page and connected to the CRM is fine (electrician, Framer). [calls 013, 105, 073; playbook III 3.4]
- Booking widgets and external systems: Leanne's hero CTA "Book a massage appointment" links to the Contact page, or to an external booking platform (Jane app) if you use one; a Calendly link right next to the price on a sales page is better UX than sending people to a contact form; you can link every blog post to the booking page and even embed the booking form on those pages; sending a "book an appointment" CTA to a separate landing page is fine, and a "Book an appointment" CTA on a service page does not break the one-topic rule. [calls 036, 052, 116, 007; course: every-page-1-main-topic]
- The "Call Brett" critique (Brett's probate page): a "Call Brett" button plus a phone number right beside it is "the same call to action there twice"; a button that instantly dials is off-putting ("If you click a button, you're like, oh, shit, I'm already calling this person"). Replace it with "a contact button here that brings them to a form", or "get a free quote, contact, book a call... Because people might not be ready at the moment. And if they are, they can click on your phone number." [call 124]
- CTA wording names the action and the service: "Book a massage appointment", "Request a quote", "contact a Vancouver roofer", never generic "contact us"; "Contact Us and Book Today are still a little bit vague of what we want people to do", "Start your corporate video today" is clearer; "Start your logo design" ("giving them the quickest way to do that is the best way to convert them"). The book: CTA buttons ("Book a Call," "Schedule an Appointment," "Get a Free Quote") are conversion tools, not SEO links: "that kind of text is vague. It doesn't tell search engines anything specific about the page it links to... Use buttons to guide users toward the next step in their journey, and support those buttons with clear text links nearby when it makes sense for SEO." [calls 083, 082, 098, 115; book ch. 9]
- Hyper-specific calls to action (contact, get a quote, read more reviews) should be buttons, which can be styled, animated and made to stand out; in-content links should be anchor text. [call 086]
- CTA placement on every page: a CTA at the top and bottom; the hero CTA visible before scrolling on desktop and mobile; every page ends with a CTA section ("book an appointment with us"). Leanne's logic: top, "if someone's landing on our website, we want to convert them as soon as possible. So I always like including a call to action right at the top", and bottom, "as they're scrolling down, they get more and more convinced... when they get to the bottom... now we want to convert them." Her bottom CTA block links straight to booking, with the phone number as the secondary button if you want calls too. [calls 008, 067, 115, 079]
- Flow examples: the movers homepage ends with "request a quote" before the FAQs and final CTA, and every service box links straight to booking; a solid service site has "your call to actions like right at the top here. So that makes it really easy for people to contact you right away", plus a button under the hero text linking to the free quote; HeyTony's service-page pattern is a "get a free quote" button that brings the visitor to the form; service pages end with "contact us for more information. Contact us for a free quote."; location pages need at least a "go book an appointment" link; a sales page (Maritza's book) gets a contact form at the bottom: "I think we definitely want to make sure that we have like a contact form." [calls 083, 106, 070, 001, 002, 028]
- Therapy practice: the action is "book an appointment", so the menu and the above-the-fold area should push booking, not the career link; put "Therapy available seven days a week", the phone number and the email on the other side of the header. [call 070]
- Scroll-to-section CTA: give the contact section an ID (for example id="contact") and link buttons to "#contact" so the visitor jumps down without loading a new page; "this works for every website" (Elementor, Divi, Showit, Squarespace, basically any builder). Maritza had a separate, different form on the contact page from the homepage form; either approach is fine, the scroll-down approach just avoids loading a new page. [calls 045, 106; playbook IV Web Design]
- One form per page: "I wouldn't have two contact forms on one page... unless it's like a really long page." If the footer already carries a form (Matt had recommended a footer form on every page), don't duplicate it in the body of the contact page. [call 045; playbook IV Web Design]
- Header and footer: have both a "Contact me" button in the header and "Contact" as a menu item ("Should I also have it in the menu? Yes."); the button at the right of the menu on Maritza's site was "perfect". Footer contact elements for a local service business: a contact button, phone number, email address, plus a list of cities served; adding "contact us" in the footer "might help them with conversion". [calls 032, 042, 081]
- Blog to conversion: "Have several call to actions on that blog page. Whether they're buttons, just general anchor text. Create as many opportunities for a user to go from the learning side of things to your more transactional, more conversion based page." In a post, "if you're looking for a wedding photographer in Hamilton, give us a call" with the words linked to the contact or booking page; the CTA at the end of a post should send people to the contact page, not "leave us a note in the comment section." [calls 068, 077, 103]
- Off-site routes into the contact page: every YouTube video description gets "want us to play at your next event, contact us here" with a link, because people find you on YouTube, Google and LLMs; a social "link in bio" can point to the contact page. [call 042; book ch. 17]
- Email capture on a tool or result page: "if you want to know when your size is in stock, sign up here" or "10% discount off your first order... we'll email you your suggested size." [call 033]
- Answer the phone: "somebody needs to answer that phone" (towing lead gen); missed or unanswered calls hurt local rankings (Google reads it as a bad user outcome). [call 109; playbook V 4.1]
- Fewer options convert better: the cat site's appointment tiles linked to different calendar bookings; Matt still pushed for fewer options and problem-led copy. Make the next step obvious for lead-hungry businesses (Kira). [calls 034, 092]
- Keep the navigation on sales and checkout flows: Google indexed Marla's sunset-cruise checkout page while the sales page had its navigation removed; "add the navigation menu back in... is this website trusted? This is just one page. Like what if they wanted more information? What if they need to contact you?" [call 113]
- Campaign landing pages can send CTAs straight to a tool ("start your claim against Porter Airlines", "check eligibility" into the claim tool). [call 072]

## Links, indexing, and where it sits in the site

- Contact belongs in the main navigation: "you should have your contact page in your navigation menu so that people can find it." HeyTony's nav is Work, Services, Blog, Contact. The book: "Your main navigation menu should feature your highest-priority pages, like your services, products, contact page, or blog", and nav and footer must include "a contact page (because people need a way to reach you)"; the subdirectory is /contact/. [course: low-word-count-how-to-fix; course: internal-linking; book ch. 9; book ch. 27; book ch. 6]
- Contact pages are exempt from the external and internal link minimums. [playbook III 2.12; call 112]
- Social media links belong "either on your contact page or on your footer or something. Or you could even put them at the top somewhere. But don't link out to all your social media" elsewhere. [call 066]
- A contact page named "Inquire" returned 404 in GSC only because the wrong URL (/contact) had been submitted. [call 061]
- Abandoned form pages with a random-number URL and no purpose should be deleted rather than linked. [course: orphaned-page-no-internal-links-point-to-it]
- Lead-magnet and intake pages: build the GoHighLevel form on the website (not inside the CRM) so the page can be optimized and found via search, with internal links plus external links to the LinkedIn company page; internal-link to a lead magnet page from blog posts ("even if it is an orphan page... Now it's not orphaned"); lead-magnet quizzes and internal-only pages may be deliberate orphans, paired with a noindex tag. [calls 054, 104; playbook IV Crawl Depth]
- When pitching local publications, use their contact form or contact page and "look for any press or media" contact. [call 029]

## Don'ts

- Don't optimize the contact page for the main keyword or treat the booking tool as the money page. [calls 052, 075; playbook III 3.1]
- Don't embed a live Google Map. [call 070]
- Don't put two contact forms on one page. [call 045]
- Don't use an instant-dial button as the only CTA, and don't put the same CTA twice side by side ("Call Brett" plus the number). [call 124]
- Don't label the CTA generically ("contact us", "Book Today") or leave "Contact us" as a plain nav item in body-text color. [calls 083, 098, 103]
- Don't use identical buttons that go to different destinations. [call 038]
- Don't leave phone numbers or emails unclickable on mobile. [calls 058, 070]
- Don't duplicate contact-page FAQs, intake blocks ("what to expect"), or review text across pages. [calls 055, 010]
- Don't remove the navigation from a sales or checkout page. [call 113]
- Don't let a scheduler widget or reviews plugin slow the page. [calls 019, 045]
- Don't write "complete the form below" filler on a page that is already the form. [call 063]
- Don't rely on a booking page plus a lead-magnet pop-up with no service pages. [call 119]

## Where the sources are thin or disagree

- Money-page status: calls 075, 074 and 114 and the playbook say contact, booking and appointment pages are not money pages ("those pages don't rank"); call 058 says "Your contact page might be a money page" and call 025 calls booking and reservation pages money pages. The emails class "product or pricing pages" as money pages but never mention contact. Both positions are recorded. [calls 075, 074, 114, 058, 025; playbook III 3.1; email 2026-08-04]
- Forms everywhere versus one form: Matt recommended a footer form on every page; Leanne's rule is never two forms on one page, so the footer form replaces the body form on the contact page. [call 045]
- Booking widgets: booking should be "as easy as possible", yet an embedded scheduler was removed for speed; no rule is given on which booking tools to embed versus link out to (Jane app, Calendly, Zenoti are all mentioned as link-out targets). [calls 019, 036, 052, 014]
- Nothing specifies a word count, title tag, meta description, image count, or the full field list of a quote form beyond name, phone, email, short message. The curriculum covers contact pages only through audit-fix lessons; the book only through navigation and CTA chapters; no email covers them. [course: low-word-count-how-to-fix; book ch. 9; email 2026-08-04]
