# Transcripts slice H (073-081)

Scope: nine HeyTony Insiders calls from late Feb to mid Mar 2026: the five-day Rank Week 2026 public sprint (days 1-4 in this slice), Kira's content-writing workshop, two "Why Doesn't It Rank" (WDIR) reviews, a Monday work session, and Leanne's web-design-for-SEO session. Speaker is Matt Diamante ("Tony") unless noted. Every bullet is a claim actually made on the call; member examples are labelled with the business type.

## 1. Homepage

- Homepage is "click zero" in crawl depth; everything linked from it (nav menu or any link on the homepage) is one click away. Pages you want ranked should be three clicks or fewer from the homepage because "the harder it is for a human to find your page, the less likely Google will care about it." [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- Matt's own homepage layout as a crawl-depth tool: a bar with his services (each one click away), his most recent blog posts at the bottom (one click), and category links (SEO, Web Design, etc.) so all posts in a category become two clicks. He said you can also simply link to important pages from the homepage; "I don't recommend putting everything in dropdowns like this or in toggles." [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- Local business: link to your Google Business Profile from the homepage or footer. Matt's homepage has a map that, when clicked, loads the GBP with the service area. [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- The homepage can be your money page. Stack Electric (Hamilton electrician, friend of Matt's) ranks number one for "Hamilton electrician" with the homepage: contact info right there, information about what they do, what areas they service, residential and commercial, blog posts at the bottom. Matt: "is this a page that I'm like, this is designed to convert? Not necessarily, but they're doing well." If you have no service page, audit the homepage. A restaurant's money page would probably just be the homepage. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Money Page Doctor finding for a Washington DC wedding photographer: "Competitors ranking for this keyword are primarily homepages", i.e. the ranking page type for that transactional keyword is the homepage, so that is the page type to optimize. [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Video background hero (Styles, video production company, homepage is money page, Vimeo clip looping): it tanks page speed; on a slow connection it probably never loads; on mobile it is not sized properly and gets cut off while people scroll past to find information. "Is that video appealing to you, or is it appealing to a person looking to hire you?" [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Kira to Faye (Oil Can Grooming, Shopify, beard oil and hair styling brand rebuilding B2B to B2C): homepage H1/H2s were brand lines like "road tested" and "take the road less traveled", so the site ranks for nothing. Pick one primary commercial keyword for the homepage, here "beard oil" (high volume, not insanely hard), and put it in the H1/H2s; keep brand personality but pair it with the keyword, e.g. "best hair pomades for guys: tools for the trade". [076_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-03-26.txt]
- Homepage word count (Kira relaying Matt): Google the homepage's primary keyword, look at the top two to three competitors' word counts and compare; don't write words just to hit a number ("if the competitors are 2500 or 3000 words but it's all fluff... use your best judgment"). [076_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-03-26.txt]
- Leanne's homepage template (massage therapy clinic, Winnipeg), section by section: (1) hero: the first thing people see, make it impressive and tell them what to do next, focus keyword "Massage therapy in Winnipeg" in the hero headline, a short description of services, and a CTA button in a colour that stands out against the background so the eye goes to it; (2) an H2 intro section that also includes the focus keyword ("Winnipeg Massage Therapy"), introducing who you are and who you work with (busy professionals, athletes, desk workers, people recovering from injury) so a visitor resonates ("I'm a desk worker, I got back pain right now, I'm going to book"); (3) services section listing each individual service with a link to its child page; (4) trust: testimonials, Google reviews, or video testimonials; (5) "how it works" process section, because a first-timer has no idea what the next steps are and certainty drives conversion; (6) FAQs, which answer questions before people reach out and are "a great way to boost up your word count without it just being more and more text"; (7) a CTA section at the very bottom, on every page. Every H2 is its own section, and sections are visually separated with different background colours "like a slide deck". [079_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-05-03-2026.txt]
- Diana (AI readiness consultancy) told her partners the site needed "a CTA at the top that's really clear about what it is that we are trying to get people to do" plus the right focus key phrases; Matt called it a boss move. [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt]
- If the homepage, about page and service pages clearly show what you offer (Lorena, online sex therapist), you may not need to "rank" for the service term; people arriving via blog posts see the services and contact you, provided there is "a CTA, a call to action." [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Multiple product lines on one site (Monica's daughter: baby accessories, pacifier-chain materials, crochet/macrame course): keep one website but lay the homepage out so people are not confused about what action to take, e.g. three clear options like "buy/shop", "build your own", "materials". [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]

## 2. Service pages (parent/overview and individual child service pages) and money pages in general

- Definition: "A money page is a page on your website that makes you money. It's a service page or a product page, a page that somebody goes to when they are looking to buy or to hire you." Examples given: dentist: dental implants page; wedding photographer: wedding photographer or luxury wedding photography packages page; realtor: homes for sale in Scottsdale; hat store: baseball hats collection or a product; hot sauce company: ghost pepper / Carolina Reaper product pages; plumber: 24-hour emergency page; personal trainer in Vancouver; car accident lawyer; garage door company: a "garage door installation Scottsdale" page; psychotherapist: couples counseling or PTSD therapy pages; shop page, rental page. "Service page equals a money page." [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Money page is about intent, not checkout: "People are ready to spend money when they visit that page." The CTA (get a free quote, book a call) can send every money page to the same form; that is "what a money page should do." Electrician (Samir, Framer site): money pages are EV charger installation, ceiling fan, home renovation pages, each with "get a free quote". [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- The service page is the money page, not the contact form it leads to: "you have a service, sex therapy for couples... That is the money page. That's the page where they click on the link and then you make money" (Lorena). Linking the service page CTA to a contact form or intake form "is the right way to do it." [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- One keyword per money page; "a keyword can be a whole sentence" ("custom home builder Burnett County"). Every page on the site should be focused on one keyword/phrase. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Money page keywords are shorter-tail; a long-tail question can be captured with an FAQ section on the services page where the exact question appears (Kasha, migraine coach). [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Never write "near me" on your website: "That is the sign of somebody who doesn't know how to do SEO." Google knows where you and the searcher are; you need local indicators instead, so the keyword is service plus city, e.g. "dental implants Hamilton", and the page is about that. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Separate page per service (Paro, magician in Kent): "Magician Kent", "Wedding Magician Kent", "Corporate Magician Kent" are separate pages; on the general "Magician Kent" page mention "I do all kinds of events from weddings to..." and link each mention to its dedicated page. A visitor searching "magician for wedding" landing on a page that also talks birthday parties and corporate events "doesn't really make sense for them". [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Portrait photographer who sells oil portraits from photos (Brianne): two service pages, "portrait photographer/photography Birmingham" and "oil painting portraits Birmingham"; bring people in on one and upsell the other. Don't mix wedding photography and portrait painting on one page. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Blog vs service page is decided by intent (Phil, snow removal): "how to do snow removal in a parking lot" = blog post; wanting to hire someone = service page; "the content is written differently depending on what the intent is"; a product page (snowblower) states specs ("great for up to 2 feet of snow, blows snow 50 feet") rather than arguing why you need one. [077_rank-week-2026-day-1.txt]
- Money Page Doctor: if you submit a blog post for a commercial keyword it flags "blog post detected... consider creating a dedicated service page for this commercial keyword." Other flagged gaps for an "AC repair in Charlotte" page: keyword not in title, H1 or meta description; content thin ("consider adding more words to match the competitor"); low keyword density ("the exact keyword isn't used but components are present... consider using the full keyword naturally"); keyword mismatch (competitors target other keywords, which is free keyword research). [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Page audit inputs for a money page: word count, keyword occurrences, keyword density (pageaudit.com Content tab), title tag, meta description, H1/H2/H3 (extension "Copy All Headings"), mobile and desktop PageSpeed. Stack Electric example: 829 words, keyword 3 times; PageSpeed 53 mobile / 79 desktop; overall score 76% while ranking number one, so "don't obsess over the score." A competitor checked: 933 words, keyword once, 0.11% density. Zero occurrences means the keyword is not on the page at all, which is a problem; a low word count is also a problem. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Competitor gap analysis on eight dimensions: title tag, meta description, H1, H2/H3 structure, word count, keyword density, page speed, trust signals, each with a priority (do first / this week / nice to have). Core rule: "what your competitor one is doing to rank number one is what Google likes"; "mirror that and slightly outdo them." [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Over-optimization: "if you were using the keyword three times and they're only using it once, that might be considered keyword stuffing to Google for that specific keyword. If you don't have enough words on your page but you're using the keyword more times, that might be considered keyword stuffing." Prompt output quoted: "Higher density does not equal better rankings. Google increasingly interprets heavy keyword repetition as over optimization." Matt has had team members over-optimize pages and wonder why they don't rank. [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]

This page is supposed to make you money"); H1 similar but "the competitor's version reads more natural"; word count close, gap is topical coverage: "write another 150 to 200 words" under a suggested H2; page speed: use next-gen image formats or shrink image file size (same display size), compress images, remove unused scripts, lazy load. ## 3. Location / city / service-area pages

- Best is a Google Business Profile per area if you have a physical location; otherwise build location pages. [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- URL form: subfolder, not subdomain: yoursite.com/irvine, not irvine.yoursite.com. [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- Don't generate pages for every city ChatGPT lists; run each city keyword through a keyword tool ("North Hollywood electrician"), find the easiest ones and build those pages first. [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- "Pigs get fat and hogs get slaughtered": a custom home builder targeting 20-30 counties should choose five and start there; a dumpster rental company with 16 county pages should perfect one first ("I don't want you doing the work twice"). [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- One city per page: "If I land on a page about bathroom remodeling in Hamilton, I don't want to see another city mentioned there... that also confuses Google." Franchisee with one landing page listing several cities should make individual location pages. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- What goes on a location page (Angela, wedding photographer an hour from the bigger city): "a Toronto wedding photographer page that talks about how I am a wedding photographer, I shoot a lot in Toronto. Here are some of the Toronto events I've done. Here are the venues that I've shot at", plus blogs about venues/locations in that city. Don't change the page that already ranks for your home city. [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Landscaper (Brock, Newmarket, 145 five-star reviews, not showing for Aurora): the GBP won't help outside its city; location pages on the site "will definitely help". Subleasing an office for a second GBP was mentioned but "technically you shouldn't do that." [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Expansion three hours away (Scott, NYC to Albany): consider a separate website because Albany visitors will wonder "are they coming out?"; location pages on the existing site are the alternative; set up a GBP there. [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Multi-location dental group (Gleb, six clinics, one site, one general dental implants page plus a page per clinic): separate websites per clinic would let each rank for more keywords; think like a patient who wants a dentist within five km, "why is that information all on the same website?" [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Multi-location service (Lux): step one is location pages; then blog posts that link to the specific location pages; citations and a GBP per location if possible. [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt]
- Footer and locations (Varuna's client, local SEO, no walk-ins, wants a "clean" footer): local signals (NAP, navigation) in the footer matter because Google "looks for that to determine if it's a local business"; Matt would add a "Locations" page listing the four locations, each linking to its own dedicated location page. [081_wdir-replay-27-02-2026.txt]
- Internal links on location pages (Manny, revamping city and service pages): link from blogs to the location/service pages; location pages can link to neighbouring location pages ("if you're actually in [city next door], we also service that area") and to other service pages; don't link a ton of blog posts from them and "don't link to the same blog post from all the location pages"; two to three relevant blog links is fine. [080_work-session-02-03-2026-replay.txt]
- Melissa (web designer ranking number one for "website design in Ludlow"): to eliminate competitors, build location pages for the surrounding towns and cities; "that's a lot of work... could be very beneficial." [081_wdir-replay-27-02-2026.txt]
- Location pages for bilingual or remote services: a state-by-state money page strategy (Peter, Medicare broker, Mid-Atlantic) was deferred to a one-on-one look rather than generic advice. [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]

## 4. Product, collection, category pages (ecommerce)

- Collection vs product page intent (Jorg, Lari Design, ski T-shirts on Shopify): Googling "ski T-shirt" returns collection pages (and Amazon collections), so "people are looking for a collection page and you're trying to optimize for a product page"; optimize the ski T-shirts collection instead, and make a collection per keyword (e.g. "T shirts for skiing" and "apres ski T shirts"). [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt]
- Same review: put the keyword at the beginning of the title; the collection's heading order was wrong (H3s first, the H1 at the bottom) so add "Ski T shirts" / "T shirts for skiing" as the H1 at the top; the number-one competitor ranks with a plain "T shirts" collection, one H1, barely any text ("our complete T shirt collection for women and men"), so "this should be easy for you to rank"; the title showed 60 characters but the tool flagged it too long. [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt]
- Don't confuse Google with anchor text: linking "premium quality ski T shirts" from the collection to a blog post about "what makes a good T shirt" tells Google the wrong thing. [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt]
- Product naming: nobody searches "1027" (inside joke about skiing until 10:27 then apres ski); name products for what people search ("funny apres ski T shirts"). Parallel example: a bong shop renamed a product "octopus bong" because people searched that, and did the same across products; let keyword research dictate product names, including very specific greeting cards ("single Mother's Day e-card"). [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt] [074_rank-week-2026-day-3.txt]
- Product imagery on collections: competitors show shirts in context (snow, ski cabin, snowplow in background); Google can now read what's in a picture, and realistic context helps conversion, so "look at what these guys are doing that I'm not." [078_wdir-06-03-2026.txt]
- Kira: collection pages can rank with "two sentences at the top" but thin ones should be enriched: why wear a padel shirt, benefits, differences vs a pickleball or regular sports shirt, history of the sport, what to pair it with (shorts, sneakers, with credible external links to where to buy), and FAQs at the bottom ("Frequently asked questions: paddle shirts" is "a sneaky little way of including the keyword"). "I always like to think about pages as, like, we want it to have as much information as a blog post." [076_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-03-26.txt]
- Product names on a Shopify collection should render as H2/H3 tags; Faye's theme didn't, and H1/H2 tags were mixed; fix requires editing theme code (a member, Muhammad Assad, had done it for Jorg). [076_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-03-26.txt]
- Product pages: reserve the category money keywords (e.g. "hair pomade", "best hair pomade for guys") for the product pages and use the phrase a few times on the page; a competitor brand name can be a secondary keyword ("compared to Suavecito pomade, our pomade is..."). Product copy that is punchy but never repeats the words "beard" or "hair" won't rank. [076_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-03-26.txt]
- Collection/product word count: same rule as homepage, compare to the top two to three ranking competitors. [076_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-03-26.txt]
- Shop page, product page, collection page, rental page all count as money pages. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Product gallery at the bottom of every blog post (three-product store): "Not necessarily... I would link to one product that is relevant to that blog." [073_rank-week-2026-day-4.txt]
- Shopify blog: use the native blog, no app needed; a blog is one column, picture, text, text, picture, text. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Affiliate/product sellers: add blog pages with real reviews, pros, cons, video if possible. [075_rank-week-2026-day-2.txt]
- Google Merchant Center flags (Samantha, bookstore): shipping speed and return policy are matched against competitors; either match what competitors do or change business fundamentals (e.g. raise price and offer free shipping, "people like free shipping"). [081_wdir-replay-27-02-2026.txt]

## 8. FAQ pages and per-page FAQ blocks

## 9. Blog index and blog post pages (page structure, length, images, headings, CTAs; not topic selection)
