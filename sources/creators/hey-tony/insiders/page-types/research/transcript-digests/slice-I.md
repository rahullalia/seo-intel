## 1. Homepage

- Leanne's homepage wireframe (service business default, adaptable for products), in this section order: navigation bar, hero (photo that fits the service + H1 + short supporting copy + CTA button), trust-building elements, "about / introduce ourselves" section, services section, testimonials, "how the process works", FAQ, final call-to-action section, footer. [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- Write the homepage copy before designing: "the purpose of layout and the design process is to help communicate a message. And so if you know your message first, it makes it a lot easier" to design around it. Leanne writes the copy with Matt's techniques (keyword research first, then a ChatGPT prompt she shares in the resource folder) and only then lays it out. [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- The hero is "one of the most impactful pieces to your website design": it captures attention and must tell people what to do next so you "capitalize on as many sales as possible". The hero message is the H1 and the H1 is the focus keyword; she cut a longer headline down to just the focus keyword "massage therapy in Winnipeg". [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- Hero mechanics from the live build: full-width hero box with a horizontal stock photo (Pexels), a brand-colour overlay or gradient so the text is readable, do not cover the subject's face with the words, supporting text under the H1 that "further explains what our business is doing", then the CTA button ("Book a massage appointment") in a contrasting secondary colour (yellow on green) and made bold, "because the quicker someone can click on the button, the more likely they are to book". Vertically centre the hero content so there is equal space above and below. [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- Hero photo must reflect the business: she rejected a couples-massage photo because "I would want to make sure, like, do they even offer couple massage therapy". Pick photos whose colours echo the logo palette. [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- Trust-building elements go either directly in the hero or right below it: groups you belong to, awards, or Google review rating. Her example: five star shapes plus bold text "Rated 5 stars on Google reviews" placed inside the hero, "this already feels a little bit more of a trustworthy website just having these stars here". [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- "Your homepage can be as long as you want it to be. Just the longer it is, the less likely people are going to reach the bottom of it. So you always want your most important messages near the top." [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- About / intro section of the homepage: an H2 that contains the focus keyword again (H2 smaller than H1, 40 px in her mock), a short intro paragraph, then the audience's pain points as four boxes (busy professionals, athletes, desk workers, people recovering from surgery), each with an icon (running shoe, computer, heart) "for people that are just skimming our content", rounded corners, on a near-white tint of the brand colour so the section reads as its own block. "What I like to do with every section is add a CTA, a button. Because somebody can be convinced by this." [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- Services section on the homepage: new H2, white background to separate it from the tinted section above, go-to layout is "text on one side, image on the other side and then reverse it"; with four services you can also do four boxes each with an image or an icon. Service names are H3s (32 px in her mock) and each service needs a link or button so someone can book or read more right away, "lots of CTA captures as many people as possible". [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- For a coaching business with programs: "I probably would create an individual page for each of your services. And so your homepage would kind of briefly describe that service and then you would have a button that would link to that page... that would be great for SEO too, because then you can add more content and try and target that keyword." The homepage "is really kind of quickly giving them the rundown of our business". [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]
- After services: testimonials (Google reviews, personal reviews, or video reviews) "to prove what you've done"; then "how the process works" in three steps (book an appointment, personalized assessment, targeted treatment session); then FAQs "if people had some more questions"; then the final CTA section, which every page needs (see section 14). [083_web-design-for-seo-with-leanne-18-02-26.txt]

## 2. Service pages (parent/overview and individual child service pages) and money pages in general

- Metal roofing page (Vancouver roofer ecoexteriors.com, reviewed by Denroi, treated as both a service and a location page): title tag "Metal roofing in Vancouver" is good; meta description had the keyword only at the very end, "keywords, especially in your meta description, have much more weight to Google when they are earlier"; heading structure H1 then H2s was right. [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page: heading "Metal roofing is the last roof you'll ever need" "leans a bit more on over optimizing... reads very awkwardly... redundant with the word roofing or roof"; reword to "Why a metal roof is important in Vancouver". The keyword "does not have to be exact, but it has to be related": "metal roofing in Vancouver" vs "Vancouver metal roofing contractor" are the same intent to Google. [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page: "not that many instances in your heading tags that mention the location Vancouver", so change headings like "Benefits of metal roofing" to "Benefits of metal roofing in Vancouver" or "...for homes in Vancouver", "really emphasize the location since this is a location page". [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page, CTA copy: "never use generic call to action text, contact us. This should always be replaced with whatever you do", e.g. "contact a Vancouver roofer" or "contact a professional roofing company"; generic "contact us" tells readers where they go but "for Google, AI or any crawl bot, they wouldn't be able to tell" (descriptive anchor text). [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page, thin section: the colour-options sentence should be "double or triple the length" to raise keyword and word count, plus a toggleable FAQ accordion (what is the most common roof colour, does roof colour matter, brown or black, does roof colour affect temperature) which "really pads up the keyword density and helps this page rank for more keywords while associating it more to the location". [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page, tables: a comparison table that explains cost "always works fantastic for Google", e.g. your cost vs a generic competitor, or a table comparing metal roof colours and temperature. [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page, bottom section: "at the bottom of all of your location pages or service pages have an extra section that is just there to internal link to any of these other services", e.g. heading "Are you looking for other roofing services?", relist every service with a one-sentence description and a link. [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Linking direction: "Your blog posts should always be linking to at least one service page. But your service pages do not always have to link to blog posts. If they do, the best chance to do that is in your FAQ section." [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Same page: make the business address on the page a clickable link to the Google Business Profile CID link (maps URL ending in ?cid=number, found with the Pleper tool); CID links are permanent and "have slight more benefits than just the share button". [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Michelle's situation: new blog posts were ranking but the main pages (homepage and metal roofing) sat on page 10; Denroi's fixes above are the prescription for getting money pages moving. [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Web hosting company's new "SEO" page (Elementor redesign) showed crawled-not-indexed: "build internal links to these. Sometimes these need 10, 15 even. Especially since this page is going for a very difficult keyword, just SEO." Optimize for something easier like "hire SEO expert in [City]" or "hire SEO services"; the H1 "Discover your website's true SEO potential" is "an extremely difficult keyword to rank for". [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- "Internal linking will do so much for getting your unranked pages to start ranking." [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Proof from the HeyTony site: the SEO services page "we have not changed... in four years... went from position 13 to number one with only internal links. Every single time we would publish a new blog... there is one link to this SEO service page", telling Google "this is our top converting page. This is our priority page." [086_technical-seo-with-denroi-12-02-2026.txt]
- Blog-to-money-page ratio: from each blog post, "one internal link on that page going to the money page, and then 2 to 3 linking to other blog posts", because the money page "may not be necessarily linking to other pages on your site" so Google stops there, while the other blogs carry more links; pages with the most links pointing at them look important. ## 3. Location / city / service-area pages

- Why they exist: "location pages are much more important for service based businesses than they are for product based businesses." Each city has different keyword difficulty and volume (Toronto vs Hamilton), so "to go for both, we'll need two separate pages", one optimized for "SEO Agency in Toronto" and one for "SEO Agency in Hamilton". [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Where to link them: if you don't want locations in the main menu, the footer is fine "as long as those pages are accessible somehow"; they are higher intent pages. Example site driveagency.com lists cities in the footer, each linking to "Atlanta Digital Marketing Agency", "Miami Digital Marketing Agency" pages; copying that layout is "a good strategy". Add the city list as a fifth footer column or a separate footer section; first confirm search volume for city + keyword (higher population usually means some volume). [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Not for everyone: a nationwide/worldwide web hosting service gets no benefit from location pages "unless you're going for a very specific area". [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Location page content: on the Vancouver metal roofing page, put the location in headings ("Benefits of metal roofing for homes in Vancouver"), add location FAQs, a cost/comparison table, and the "other services" link section at the bottom (full detail in section 2). [082_technical-seo-with-denroi-26-02-2026.txt]
- Nearest big city: Jennifer (web designer now an hour from Orlando) can make a location page for Orlando: "you're not technically saying you're in Orlando, you're just saying web design Orlando... You're just making a location page on Orlando." Keyword: "Orlando web design" is 1300 volume but ~53 difficulty (needs DA ~53+, she had 4), so target "Orlando web design company"; small nearby towns like "Cocoa Beach web design" (~20 searches) are easy wins to build momentum. "In the footer, you can still name it like Orlando. You don't need to call it the keyword you're shooting for in the footer because it's just a link." [085_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-02-26.txt]
- Jennifer also relayed Matt's earlier advice to a Toronto web designer whose footer listed Toronto, Orlando and another far city: "why are you trying to diversify so far out? Go micro with the cities." [085_content-writing-training-with-kira-10-02-26.txt]

Build them as pages like the Toronto service page, not blog posts. [088_wdir-replay-06-02-2026.txt]
- Same review: footer "our areas of service" listing all location pages plus an "areas we serve" block with links on the homepage; "just make sure all those pages are 100% unique. They mention local landmarks, they mention local things"; the structure can be a template but the content must differ. [088_wdir-replay-06-02-2026.txt]
- Local signals inside blog posts (Jackie, Denver hairstylist): in every blog post add a line like "if you're looking for a hair stylist in Denver" with those words linked to the local service page, keep name, address and phone in the footer, and link to the Google Business Profile ("look at all our amazing reviews"). National traffic still helps because it shows Google you hold interest on the topic. [095_work-session-01-19-2026.txt]
- Two GBPs (commercial vs residential roofing) are fine when they serve two audiences; each should point to an optimized page, not an unoptimized portfolio landing page. [094_wdir-replay-23-01-2026.txt]
