## 1. Homepage

- Homepage vs landing page mindset (Leanne): the homepage is for casual visitors, it should "introduce them to the brand and kind of give them the vibe of our business." A hard-sell, conversion-optimized layout on the homepage "might kind of give someone a bit of an ick, like, oh, why are they trying to sell me already, I don't even know you yet." Save the sell-first structure for paid landing pages where people "already want to buy." [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- Leanne's baseline homepage wireframe: hero with photo or video, add text in the hero carrying the H1 and a call to action, then the brand story, then key features (or services), with "a little bit of story in each" section. Reviewed for an event-venue owner (Squarespace): leave the hero photo/video, add hero text with the H1 and a CTA, then "the history ... it all started at blah, blah, blah ... kind of like an emotional story" because it "builds some emotional connection," and rename "services" to "key features" of the venue. [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- Where a personal or founding story goes: for a unique venue with personal history, "I would include that story right in your homepage," not on a separate landing page. [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- Trust and testimonials belong on the homepage too: Leanne includes testimonials on the homepage and then relevant ones on each service page. [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- Homepage keyword focus, live review of a Toronto wedding photographer (Showit site): title/H1 "Toronto wedding photographer plus filmmaker" targets two keywords, "so are you a wedding photographer or a filmmaker? ... pick one." Toronto is very competitive, so get more specific (luxury weddings, barn weddings). [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Same review, heading hygiene: there were three H2 tags at the very top; "Inquire now" was an H2 and "should not be an H2 tag"; the page targeted "romantic storytelling wedding photography in Toronto" when "people aren't looking up romantic storytelling, they're looking up wedding photographer ... you're confusing Google"; "Wedding photography effortlessly refined" as an H2 is "way too long"; "Timeless wedding photography in Toronto" is fine; "featured in", "Ann and Derek at Lake Simcoe", "Explore signature work" as H2s are "way too many H2 tags here that have nothing to do with your main keyword." Test for every heading: "does this support what the main page is about?" "Hi, I'm Prito" could become "Hi, I'm Prito, a wedding photographer." A testimonial line ("Most couples tell me we felt so comfortable with you") is "great to have" but not as a heading. Also: a giant blank section was left where a video was deleted, a playing GIF may be slowing the page, and "how does it look on mobile?" Root cause: "people ... use their heading tags as style versus what they're meant for." Fix: use the keyword more on the page and study the Toronto wedding photographers who rank. [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Homepage title tag for a newborn photographer: if the homepage is no longer the page targeting "Chicago Newborn Photographer," drop that from the homepage title and use the brand ("Ariel Ross Photography"); put "Chicago Newborn Photographer" plus the name at the end on the money/service page title instead. Competitors doing keyword-first homepage titles is fine, but only if that is the keyword the homepage is going after. A keyword tacked onto every page's title could hurt; on the homepage alone "it's probably fine." [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Homepage as a funnel: a multi-genre photographer can make the homepage "act almost as like a funnel to all those other pages," but "I would still have like a main services page with like all the different types of photography," and "you're not going to talk about all of your services in depth on your homepage." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Ecommerce homepage: "you're not trying to necessarily get your homepage to rank. Your money pages, your collections and your products are where you want to focus your attention. And blog posts as well." Forcing FAQs onto an ecommerce homepage was called "a bit of a force fit" by the member; Matt redirected effort to collections and products. [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Homepage keyword choice for a new site (natural weight loss coach): Google Trends shows popularity, not volume; use a keyword tool (hey keywords, Semrush, Ubersuggest) to check volume and difficulty; zero volume does not mean don't target it, "15% of all searches done on Google every day are brand new." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Location links on the homepage: a photographer's homepage can carry a "locations I service" block with clickable city names leading to each location service page (homepage itself not trying to rank for those). [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Listing main product categories on the homepage (not just in the menu) helps orphaned or buried product pages get found; Matt asked "can you get to those orphan pages from your home page without going directly to that link?" and suggested the homepage list the main categories. [017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]
- Homepage listing all blog categories reduces crawl depth (Denroy): clicking a category shows all posts in it, "instead of having page 10 of pagination." [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- If a blog question keyword shows the homepage in Google instead of the post, it's because the homepage mentions it or features the new post; "give it time, that will filter out." [019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- Cannibalization worry: a comedian feared her homepage (about her comedy) and her comedy service page would compete; she resolved it by making the service page about process ("how it works, how you can hire me, what we can do for you"). [011_wdir-10am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Service pages on a homepage: "if it's showing up on your homepage, you're either mentioning that on your homepage or you have that text on your homepage somewhere." Homepage with a lead-magnet hero box that was an H1 caused competing H1s; fix below under section 15. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- Don't send paid ads to the homepage (Leanne): "a lot of people when they run Google Ads, they just send people to their home page and I feel like that's kind of missing out on a lot of leads." [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- General 404 URLs can be redirected "straight back to your homepage" (Denroy). [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]

## 2. Service pages (parent/overview and individual child service pages) and money pages in general

- What a service page is (Elliot): "your service pages are the very specific things that are about your business ... built specifically around, hey, this is what we offer. This is how we offer it. This is why you should choose us. That is going to provide the information, and that is going to be more on the conversion side of things." Blogs add context around those topics and link to them. [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Overview vs individual service pages (Leanne): "the overview service page lists out all of our different services ... more for navigation to get to the individual service pages. And that's when we can really go hard with our SEO for the individual service pages." She has prompts for both page types in her shared drive. [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- One page per core service, built over time (Denroy): "Create a hub page that links to every single one of your service pages. You don't need to make all your service pages and launch them all at once." A long services list is "totally fine" for visitors and "helps with the SEO." [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Split bundled services: a pet sitter with one page covering overnight care, pet sitting and dog walking should make each a separate page: "if somebody's coming for pet sitting, they don't care about the dog walking part ... people looking for dog walkers, looking for something a lot more specific." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Same rule for sub-services: a "custom jewelry" service should become separate pages for custom engagement ring, custom wedding ring, custom promise ring: "if you want to rank for something you need a page about that topic." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- You don't need 25 services: three or four service pages is fine; keep linking blogs to those rather than inventing "content pages." [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt, 012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt]
- What counts as a money page: a telehealth GLP-1 business with men's, women's and military pages: "your men's, women's and military pages are your money pages ... link to those." Don't add extra content pages now; focus on blog posts. [012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt]
- Service page structure modeled on heytony.ca's service page: it explains the process, "how it works, how you can hire me, what we can do for you ... this is how we do it. You're going to call us, we're going to speak with you." A comedian copied that pattern for her comedy service page. [011_wdir-10am-03-07-2026.txt]
- H1 for a service page: Matt's SEO service page H1 should be "SEO in Hamilton or Search Engine Optimization in Hamilton or Search Engine Optimization Agency"; his actual H1 "just says Search Engine Optimization. It doesn't say in Hamilton," so it is "not even optimized properly." H1s should be the keywords you want to rank for but "make sense in English ... for a person to consume," not "gobbledygook." The H1 shows on the page; the title tag is the blue text on Google. Use the Page Audit extension to check title and headings. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Money page word count example (hey Tony money page analyzer): Matt's SEO page has 2,400 words, the average competitor 4,400, "so I need to beef my words up." But check where you rank first: "if I'm ranking number two in this, I don't want to touch that." [018_insiders-welcome-call-22-06-2026.txt]

## 3. Location / city / service-area pages

- One service per location page: "with location pages, I don't believe in doing, like a bunch of the same location for different services. I just choose one that is the easiest to rank for and I go after that." On that page, mention the other service with "maybe have a H2 tag somewhere or just text that says, like, I also do emceeing." (Comedian with two services, 12 Florida cities.) [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]

- Photographer with newborn and family services: on the main city page "all of those services can be for that location," but for a suburb like Skokie "I wouldn't have a family one and a newborn page. I'd only pick one ... and then pick a different location to do the other one." Decide by keyword research on which is easier to rank in that specific city. Each location page gets its own original content. After building them, "you wouldn't touch them again ... leave them for three to six months," write blogs that link to them with anchor text like "Skokie newborn photographer." [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Small cities are worth a page each: Florida cities of 83,000, 66,000, 58,000 people: "am I doing the right thing by creating a page for each city? Yes, yes." [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]

If super competitive, audit who ranks and mimic; 2,000 words is not too much "just make sure it's formatted in a way where if a person's reading it ... they can actually consume it." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]

## 4. Product, collection, category pages (ecommerce)

- Collection pages "are one of the most underutilized types of page when it comes to E commerce and ranking." On a collection page with only four SKUs, "you're almost going to write a mini blog post underneath those four skus": how often people should eat it, etc., plus FAQ questions underneath. Structure: "products on top and then FAQ blog below." Use the Rank Week blog prompt ("I'm writing a collection page for ... I need to have some FAQ questions underneath it"). [017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]
- Jewelry store: audit collections and the top 10 products first; "your collection pages can be very, very, very valuable. Most people don't optimize those." A collection page that is "just pictures of products" needs, underneath all the pictures, "almost like a blog post, but more information about those products." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- FAQs on product pages: yes, "as long as you can have different FAQs for each product." Care/cleaning/storage FAQs that would be identical across products are a problem ("you don't want the same FAQs on every single page"). Use product-specific questions: "what carat is this ring? ... if you had that ring out and a customer was talking to you, what kind of questions would they ask about that specific piece." Worth the work "to sell more product." [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Collection vs blog cannibalization: a "natural granola" collection page and a blog "health benefits of natural granola" don't cannibalize; the blog targets a longer keyword. A blog on the same topic as the collection would. [012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt]
- Product descriptions: a former ad copywriter's descriptions were "overwritten in terms of poetry, but not well enough in terms of SEO." Matt: "people just want to know, what the hell do you sell? What does this thing do right? What features do I get? Like, they want bullet points, they want easy to understand." [012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt]
- Product H1s: products get H1s too (a Shopify/GemPages theme had coded H3 instead of H1 on products, being fixed product by product). Each product needs a different name/H1; don't name products with city names; use descriptive names people search ("white beaver fur felt hat," not invented names): "it's really easy to rank for nobody's looking up" and "describe what it is and that will help you rank for that keyword." [012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt, 013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Name the product after the keyword: a heart-shaped vibrator should be named "heart shaped vibrator" ("that's what I would name the product. And then you can have like the actual name, whatever it is"). Support with a blog like "inconspicuous/discreet vibrators" listing your products, "or even a collection page where you have those listed," and a "[popular competitor product] alternative/alternatives" page listing your products. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Every product needs a unique meta description and unique headers; with 100 engagement ring designs, vary them ("18 karat gold engagement ring" vs "14 karat gold engagement ring") so none are identical; each page optimized around one long-tail keyword; keep an Excel sheet of keywords per page (Denroy, with Jorg). [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Standard/shared product descriptions across 1,000 doormat designs: "They should be different. Cannibalizing myself. Okay." Long-unsold designs: "just make a sale category and put things on sale and just get rid of them." [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Custom packaging, 300 product pages: use "custom" in content and titles where people search it, but research sub-intents (eco friendly, wholesale, sizes); an H2 like "custom pizza box designs include ... here's how we do it." Do keyword research for the top categories (pizza boxes, burger boxes). [017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]
- Use the words people search, not your preferred term: UK "yoga rug" seller should use "mat" in the H1 ("organic cotton yoga mat") and can say "rug" in the description; "your job is not to train people ... use the terms that people actually use" and copy competitors' naming since "they've done the keyword research." [019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- One product, one page: don't carry two product names (woodchip boiler / biomass boiler) on one page; choose one; comparison goes in a blog. [019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- Category descriptions: a member used the money page audit tool to rewrite her category descriptions (counted as a win). [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Menu depth for products: "if you have to click more than two times to get to that page, it's probably hidden. Those products are hidden." Drop-down menus themselves don't hurt SEO. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Orphan product pages (custom private server): make sure they're reachable from the homepage within the subcategory path; list main categories on the homepage; could be canonical/URL variants, ask Denroy. [017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]

## 15. General page rules that apply to every page (length, density, images, headings, speed, mobile, readability, AEO/answer-first) when framed as page-building guidance

- One keyword per page: "Every page is supposed to go after one main topic or one main keyword"; Yoast's "each focus keyword needs to be unique" is correct; the cannibalized-keywords scanner flags pages competing for the same term. [018_insiders-welcome-call-22-06-2026.txt, 019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- Every page you want ranked needs "a title tag, a meta description, and a singular heading one tag" (Denroy); missing title/meta "just tells Google that page its content isn't as important." Fix red audit issues first, yellow later; duplicate or multiple H1s must be fixed. Batch: "Fix 5 to 10 a week." [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt, 017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]
- Headings: H1 is the most important tag, "tells Google what the page is about," usually the biggest text at the top; H2s are subtopics, H3s under them, back to H2; "you can't go from an H2 to an H4 ... You shouldn't be using H6 or H4s anyways"; keep sequential. Don't use headings for styling ("oh, I want my text bigger ... then you end up having 10 on the page or none at all"). Updating H1s gave one member a 30% traffic increase. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- H1 and title should match: "the title and the H1 tag should be basically the same," with the focus keyword plus a few extra words so it isn't "keyword stuffy" (Leanne's H1 for "website design"). Squarespace sets heading sizes globally; accept a big H1, it "should be kind of big and impactful"; don't use headers just for text size. [016_web-design-for-seo-24-06-2026.txt]
- Meta description length: "aim for between 120 characters and 150"; ChatGPT tends to write them too long (Gemini kept to length better). Unique meta description on every page, even for near-identical products. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt, 015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Alt text on images is part of the rank report card (Matt's own page was flagged for missing alt text and a too-long meta). [018_insiders-welcome-call-22-06-2026.txt]
- Answer first: put the answer to the page's topic "right at the top" because you can't know how deep crawlers go; a page that "doesn't answer anybody's questions" should lead with the question. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Word count and density: match the SERP, not a fixed number; over-optimizing (2,000 words vs competitors' 500, keyword 30 times vs 10) can stop a page ranking; don't repeat keywords all over; "even in the headers and product names, everything should be different always, as well as the meta. On every page." [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Images every 200-300 words on content pages; own photos first, then stock, then AI (AI first for graphs/graphics); webp. [012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt, 017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]
- Readability: write for a human ("if a person's reading it ... organized in a way that they can actually consume it"); no walls of text; bullets for product facts. [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt, 019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt, 012_work-session-29-06-2026.txt]
- Mobile: check how every page looks on mobile; a 10-year-old WP Bakery site was "not mobile responsive, getting cut off" and needed plugin updates, not a rebuild. [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Speed: GoHighLevel and Squarespace give no control over CLS/TBT or unused JS/CSS; on WordPress use WP Rocket to minify/defer JS (toggle off if it breaks something); remove heavy widgets and GIFs; cheap hosts that block bots hide your content from crawlers and LLMs. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt, 017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt, 019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt, 020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Schema: "doesn't matter ... not worth the time," it's the 80% of effort that yields 20% of results. [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Open Graph: enable via plugin, low priority. [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Audit score targets: "don't aim for perfection. A hundred percent on the Page Audit tool doesn't mean you're necessarily going to rank first ... above 80%" is fine; a 92/100 rank report card with two fixes is normal. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 018_insiders-welcome-call-22-06-2026.txt]
- Ship then iterate: "I'd rather you put out crap than just wait around and, like, strategize"; Matt's own SEO page "the way it's laid out, is good. I just need to optimize it properly." [011_wdir-10am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Replicate what works: analyze a page that performed ("this formatting that I did, or I put the button here") and copy it to another service or page. [011_wdir-10am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Redesigns and migrations: keep every URL that gets traffic or backlinks exactly the same, keep content, headings and structure the same; the look, colors and branding can change; 301 anything that must move; a site-wide rebuild makes Google "adjust a bunch of" rankings. Moving platforms (WordPress to Wix etc.) keeps the domain, so URLs can stay. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt, 017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt]
- Quick-win targeting: pages ranking positions 11-20 can move to page one with "small optimizations, add a paragraph, add an H1 tag." [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Don't touch pages that rank; wait 3-6 months on new pages; internal link with descriptive anchors; change slugs only on pages that are not indexed, not ranking, with no backlinks. [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt, 019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- Internal links: 3-4 per page; every new post links to three other posts and one money page and gets three inbound links; the week's money page gets three inbound links from other pages. [019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt, 020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Conversions and engagement as signals: forms completed, time on site, visits from other sources (email, social, even people clicking from the Insiders call) all tell Google the business is legitimate and can raise crawl budget. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt, 017_q-a-new-insiders-22-06-2026.txt, 020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Never duplicate content across pages (Denroy: "the number one thing"); related but distinct languages, locations, or products need distinct titles, metas and copy. [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Accordions/toggles are safe to index; agentic crawlers interact with menus and forms. [015_technical-seo-with-denroi-25-06-2026.txt]
- Accessibility and sequential headings go together; Lighthouse accessibility 100 is achievable. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- AEO is SEO: answer-based content and FAQs help, but ranking on Google is what gets you into AI results; social posts and GBP activity also feed AI overviews. [020_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt, 011_wdir-10am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Don't link back and forth between your own sites much; a couple of cross-links is fine; treat multiple sites as separate SEO projects. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
- Backups before editing theme files or PHP (Croc Web, BlogVault, UpdraftPlus); Wordfence for security. [014_wdir-10am-26-06-2026.txt, 019_work-session-22-06-2026.txt]
- If you're hiring a designer, ask for a Loom tutorial on updating pages yourself; beginners can use Wix or Squarespace, WordPress for more control. [013_wdir-11am-26-06-2026.txt]
