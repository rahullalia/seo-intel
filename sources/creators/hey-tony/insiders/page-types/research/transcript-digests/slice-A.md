## 1. Homepage

- The homepage must plainly say what the business does. Emmanuel (cosmetics manufacturer, eight-figure business) audited his 10-year-old site and "realized my homepage didn't even say that" (that they make cosmetics). Matt: a website "can be a massive driver of business if you just tell people what you do." [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- Gabrielle's brother's new HVAC site: the only mention of what he does ("heating and cooling") was inside the logo image. Gabrielle: "google can't read that. Like, they don't even know you exist." Fix is to put the service in real text on the page. [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]
- Pick ONE keyword for the homepage and build the headings around it. Denroy to Yvonne's weight-loss clinic (Chandler, Arizona): "what keyword do you want this homepage to rank for? If you could only rank for one keyword, what would it be?" Their hero heading "Lose weight, keep it off, tighten tone contour" "does not say weight loss Chandler. That does not say weight loss Arizona." Even though they already rank first, adding keyword plus location to the heading "will cement that number one position and possibly even push you to a further reach." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Same review: secondary headings should carry the keyword too, e.g. "Three pillars of health" becomes "Three pillars of weight loss treatment." "Really have that keyword as often as you can. Especially since you are working in the medical field where Google does treat this a little bit differently from other sites." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Which service gets the homepage: "If you offer multiple services, your primary service, the one that makes you the most money will be your homepage. Every other service can have its own separate page." Medications or things you prescribe or sell get another page. "One keyword per page is the general guideline you will always follow on any site." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Ellie's Shopify yoga-rug store (Healing Textiles, UK) homepage review by Denroy: there was an empty H1 (a stray space or line break) to remove; the real H1 "Ancient artisans, modern luxury" contained no keyword. He suggested a keyword-led H1 such as "outdoor yoga rugs" and "optimizing the homepage around that." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Same review, heading by heading: "Authentic toxin free yoga products is good. This is a great description of what you sell." The heading "healing power of Ayurvastra" is "a heading that I don't feel is very necessary for your homepage" for such a new site; if you keep a heading without the keyword "make sure the content below it does" include "yoga rug." "Natural botanical dyes" becomes "natural and botanically dyed yoga rugs"; "Our best sellers" becomes "our best selling yoga rugs." "You just want to have your keyword as many times as possible to get the ball rolling. Especially since you are such a new site." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Prito (Toronto wedding photographer) had a homepage title tag "Toronto wedding photographer / GTA wedding photography." Matt: "that's two keywords... Every page should rank for one keyword... you're trying to go for the whole pie. You're not even getting a slice." For the homepage "I would just do Toronto wedding photographer"; cover the other cities with separate location pages. [006_wdir-10am-10-07-2026.txt]
- Multi-location business: "your main website is going to be for your biggest location or the location that is doing the best," with location pages (and possibly sub pages) for the others. [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]
- Leanne's standard homepage wireframe (shared from her Drive): hero image; trust-building elements; introducing ourselves; highlighting products or services; testimonials; how our process works ("This helps ease people's anxieties on what to expect next from working with you"); FAQs; final CTA. "I like to include a call to action at the bottom of every page and at the top just to make sure it's as easy as possible for people to reach out to us." [008_web-design-for-seo-08-07-2026.txt]
- Joan's Shopify homepage review (Leanne): fonts "a little bit small"; move the main image above the bullet points and make it the hero, "almost full screen," because "we want our hero to really be impactful." Recommended hero size 1440 by 720 pixels, exported as WebP so it does not slow the site. "What can slow your website down is if you have like a slider right at the start here or videos. But having one big image, as long as it's sized correctly, it won't slow down your website very much." [008_web-design-for-seo-08-07-2026.txt]
- Same review: instead of stark white, a very soft gray or soft sage-green background is fine; logo should be SVG (ideal) or a PNG without the white background box; make the navigation bar transparent over the hero. [008_web-design-for-seo-08-07-2026.txt]
- Joan reported that after Leanne's class she "changed my homepage... and I have updated several of my money pages" and "got two orders yesterday" worth over $600, which she attributed to that. [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]
- Should you simplify the homepage? Matt to Joan: "Maybe, maybe not. It depends if you're ranking or not. If you're not ranking, you can try expanding it or simplifying it. If you are ranking, leave it the [expletive] alone. Don't touch it." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Having six product collections linked on the homepage is fine ("That's great"); being told the homepage was "too busy" for that "doesn't sound too busy to me." But take the collections out of carousels: "people aren't going to go to that." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Crawl-path rule stated as "if I can't get to it from your homepage, Google's not going to index it." The navigation menu counts as the first click; a page that needs four or five clicks from the homepage "is buried." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Realtor with an IDX feed (Linda, Naples): since listings are identical everywhere, "your biggest thing is going to be blog posts and probably your homepage" for differentiation, plus local perspective content (neighborhoods, coffee shops, bike shops, where billionaires live). [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- The homepage URL is the safe link target for a guest post or backlink while a site is being rebuilt "because your homepage URL is not going to change." [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- Simple FAQ items can live on the homepage (see FAQ section). [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Homepage and money pages should each still carry at least one external link (see section 15, external links). [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]

## 2. Service pages (parent/overview and individual child service pages) and money pages in general

- Definition used throughout: "location pages are money pages, product pages are money pages, services are money pages, collections are money pages." A money page "should be able to convert or book," or at least link to a booking or contact action. [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]
- HVAC example (Gabrielle's brother, Albany NY per 004): the site had no money page at all ("you have to contact him in order for him to tell you what he can do"). Matt: one page per service, e.g. furnace installation, furnace swap, furnace repair, AC install, AC swap, AC repair. "On those service pages, we just kind of describe what that is and then, like, contact us for more information. Contact us for a free quote." "It doesn't have to have the price on it necessarily on the service page." A service page "is the money page." [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- Parent/child structure for the same HVAC site: "You can have a button that says services, and then on that services page, you can have like furnace, ac, whatever, like different categories of them... And then you could have the different services for each of those." [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- Service page length: "Look at who's ranking number one, two, and three. See how many words they're using. See what kind of information they're using... Look at what they're doing and then provide a little bit more information." The copy "should have his expertise, experience, any... examples of stuff that he's done... that makes it truly unique that other people can't write about as well." [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- Over-optimizing a money page is a real failure mode (Shiloh, Costa Rica pickleball vacation rental with "a ton of information"): "you might only need 400 words for that page. If the person ranking number one, two and three have 4, 5, 600 words, you might not need that many words. And you might have 3,000 words on your page." "More isn't always better." Over-optimizing is "one of the biggest pitfalls with SEO... using the keyword too many times. You're providing too much information." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Same call: "if you create pages that are similar, then they're going to compete for one another... and they're not going to rank"; a page that is not indexed, gets no traffic and no impressions "is useless. Why do you have that page? Get rid of that page." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- URL structure: `domain.com/services` is the main services page "that shows you all the different services," and `/services/bathroom-renovation` tells Google "we offer a service that is bathroom renovation." Do not flatten it to `/services-bathroom`: "The adding the word service in there doesn't help you." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Get more specific with pages as you grow: Emmanuel (cosmetics manufacturer) plans pages like "hair care shampoos" rather than only "we make shampoo," to "start to get a little more specialized." [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- One page optimized for one keyword works: Sarah (Kingston, Ontario agency) "optimized my one page specifically for website design in Kingston" during Rank Week, kept blogging weekly, and now ranks page one for "marketing agency in Kingston" (fifth spot) against a crowded field. [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Kelly targeted "narcissistic abuse recovery program" for her brand new program page (zero keyword difficulty, about 150 searches a month), did the press release, and was "already at the top" the day after posting. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Service page layout (Leanne reviewing Kelly's "social media training" page): a clear call to action in every section: "I do recommend having some sort of call to action because we just want to make it as easy as possible for people to get to the thing that we want them to do." Center the hero text and put the button directly under it; add top margin so each content section reads as its own block; make the button the brand yellow; fade the decorative yellow swoosh because it was "competing with the text"; body copy in a softer black at about 18px; a "What's included" list laid out as one column per bullet point; testimonials condensed to similar lengths, highlighting "the really nice part." [008_web-design-for-seo-08-07-2026.txt]
- Calls to action on a money page may send people to a separate booking landing page: "totally fine to leave site." The only hard rule is "you never want to external link to a competitor for any reason." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Money pages (and the homepage) should still have one external link each. Examples Matt used on his old website-design service page: "featured in" links, his Chamber of Commerce profile, Trustpilot, Glassdoor, Yelp, "I'm a real business look," plus source links for any claim ("according to this study" linked to the study). [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- A registered trademark's listing on the USPTO site can be used "intermittently maybe on money pages or on collection pages... or even in blog posts as an external link" that still talks about you. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Internal linking into money pages: every weekly blog post links to one money page (plus three other blog posts). [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt] [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]
- Do not repeat the money keyword as the topic of blog posts (cannibalization). Kathy (website management services): blog about a different topic such as "website design in 2026" and link "website management" to the service page; "That's not cannibalizing, because the topic of that page is different than the topic of your money page." Only use the money keyword in a blog title if the post is genuinely about it ("how do you choose a website management service"). [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Sarah (ecommerce) had "collection pages, product pages, blog pages, all aiming for the same keywords" and is re-doing them to one keyword per page. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Backlinks to a specific page: "backlinks lift up your site as a whole. But if you look at who's ranking number 1, 2, 3, 4, if they have five backlinks to that specific page, you'll need to get five backlinks to your specific page." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Do not reuse identical blocks on every money page (Elliot's betrayal-therapy pages repeated "the people you work with," "what to expect in your first call," a references list and a "written by" block on every page): "There's so much content that is duplicated on this page." Matt: "on your own website you don't want to repeat the same words on every page." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt] [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]
- A page about a specific neighborhood can be built as a service page: Victor (DC movers) added a service page "with my niche expertise about a very specific neighborhood of Washington, D.C." and hit the top of the AI Overview within two days ("very unique, very useful, and very specific... a lot of value and research"). [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- Every page (service pages included) "could have their own FAQs," shown as plain question-and-answer text, not toggles (see FAQ section). [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]
- If a page is already ranking, do not touch it even if tools show no backlinks or "no difficulty": "if I go in and, like, muddy the waters, then, like, I might lose that ranking." [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- Pages you cannot reach in two or three clicks from the homepage or nav are "buried" and are unlikely to be indexed. [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Robert (voice actor) is building "genre pages and landing pages and different things for the services for the new website," repositioned around what local customers look for rather than generic "voice actor" terms. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]

## 3. Location / city / service-area pages

- What a city page looks like (Matt to Cigar, Vancouver web designer): one page per target area (North Van, Burnaby, Surrey, downtown Vancouver). First check which variation people use in each area ("web design" vs "website design," used interchangeably). H1 "website design in Burnaby"; H2 like "Premier website design in Burnaby"; then local specifics: "Burnaby has over, I don't know, 15,000 small businesses and only X percent of them have a website. But those who do have a website and who have like a properly mobile optimized website... are getting more business." Mention local businesses you built sites for, or if none yet, local sites whose design you love ("if you're looking for something similar, you can always contact us"). [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- Length: look at the three main competitors for that keyword in that city, "see the length of their local page and then write the same or a few extra words, maybe 100 or 200 words" (Michael recalling Matt's rule; Matt confirmed). [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]
- Location pages are money pages. They should convert or book, "not necessarily right from that page, but like, at least have a link saying like, here's, you know, go book an appointment." They can also link out to a relevant blog post. [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]
- Avoiding duplicate content across city pages when the service is identical (Michael, GLP-1 telehealth): talk about local things that help the reader in that place: nearest hospitals if side effects happen, local grocery stores with GLP-1 friendly foods, the farmer's market, local gyms (or partner with them), a local hike, "a nice route for you in your neighborhood" for a 5k walk. Florist example: the funeral homes and wedding venues in each area, "So it's totally unique content every single time." [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]
- Bilingual location pages: yes, make a Spanish version of each local service page, "Just make sure it's actually translated and do the keyword research again, but in Spanish... almost the exact same content, but you're using the keyword in the way that it makes sense." - Multi-location therapy practice (Mariah, six locations incl. - Do not build location pages for places you are not based in or do not specifically serve (Kathy, online consultant in central Florida): "Unless you're actually based in that location. I wouldn't." ## 4. Product, collection, category pages (ecommerce)

- Collection vs category: "a category page is WordPress. It's for blog posts... A collection page is on Shopify. That can be a collection of different products... You can rank, and you should rank your collection page, but not your category pages." [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- Category pages (blog) will never rank; Shopify collection pages will (repeated to Jelly). [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Keyword frequency in product descriptions (Denroy to Joan, Shopify): no set number. "If they only mentioned it once, you likely will only need to do it twice. If they mention it 20 times, you'll likely have to do it 21... You don't want to over optimize your page by going 20 extra keywords above them. That's redundant." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- The keyword should lead the title tag and the meta description: "The sooner that keyword appears in your title, in your meta description, in the content as a whole, the more important Google and crawl bots will view that keyword." [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Two H1s on a Shopify product page (Kate, goldsmith, GemPages): the product title (the short one shown on the collection page, "easier to read for consumers") and a padded custom H1 both rendered as H1. Denroy: keep the more abbreviated one as H1, keep the other text on the page but demote it to H2 or regular text (via product.liquid or manually); two headings back to back with no text between is fine; "Only heading one is limited to just one per page. Heading two, you can have as many as you" want. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- "You may also like" related-product blocks (often four products) count as internal links. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- 400 diamond products fed in from a vendor with only a spec table, "no header, there's no text" (Kate): research what people actually search ("Canadian diamonds," "ethical diamonds," maybe "engagement diamond") and name products accordingly; each product needs a different H1 and the unique shape/carat/clarity specs are "enough to make that a unique keyword"; put the bigger keyword on collection pages ("ethically sourced Canadian diamonds") while individual diamond pages may just be the stats plus "diamond." Do 10 (or 20) a week so you learn before touching all 400. [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Kate had all products done in French and English before moving on to collection and landing pages and the blog. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Cannibalization across collection, product and blog pages all targeting the same keywords (Sarah): fix by giving each page one keyword, redoing collections first, then products, then blogs. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Shopify speed checklist (Denroy to Joan): CDN set up, images served as WebP, image lazy loading enabled, CSS and JavaScript deferred; old oversized images still on the server should be deleted. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Shopify speed: Matt has a Fiverr vendor (roughly $100) who speeds up Shopify stores specifically; "the only time you're going to hear me say, go to Fiverr." [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Huckberry is Matt's and Leanne's reference ecommerce site "for just how to lay out your products" (and its journal/blog). [008_web-design-for-seo-08-07-2026.txt]
- Shopify blog CSS identical across posts is not a duplicate-content problem; same TEXT on every page is. [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- A product/ecommerce "category" rule also applies: no tags, 5 to 10 categories (see section 9). [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Collections linked on the homepage (six of them) are good; take them out of carousels. [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]

## 8. FAQ pages and per-page FAQ blocks

- One FAQ page vs FAQs on each page (Yaniv, rebuilding with his designer): "Every page should not should, but could have their own FAQs. Don't put them in a drop down though. Don't do the toggles. Just have them like question answer underneath. Question answer underneath." Accordion design? "No, no, I think Google and AI are less likely to look at that than if you just have it on the page because you're making more work for them." [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]

[007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]

## 9. Blog index and blog post pages (page structure, length, images, headings, CTAs; not topic selection)

Blog post structure and on-page
- The weekly post is generated with the second prompt: "Write this as a clean WordPress ready blog post using my expertise, experience, opinion, stories, et cetera. Use H2 and H3 headings only. Indexing and maintenance
- You do not have to request indexing for each new post; Google will find it via internal links; request manually only if it is still missing after a couple of weeks or a month. [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]

- "Discovered, currently not indexed" posts: check internal links (not orphaned), keyword competition, that the post is in the XML sitemap (plugins sometimes skip new posts), then request indexing. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Refreshing an old indexed post vs writing new: an old URL may already have backlinks and internal links and Google may recrawl it faster, so refreshing can save time, but a new dedicated page is also fine. [005_wdir-11am-10-07-2026.txt]
- New sites: publish at least two blog posts a month (Denroy); Matt's standard is one per week (two per week for agency clients). [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt] [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]

## 15. General page rules that apply to every page (length, density, images, headings, speed, mobile, readability, AEO/answer-first) when framed as page-building guidance

Keywords, headings, titles
- One keyword per page, always. [006_wdir-10am-10-07-2026.txt] [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt] [009_work-session-guest-post-06-07-2026.txt]
- Heading hierarchy: H1, then H2, then H3, "If you need to, you can always come back to H2"; like a book with title, chapters, subheadings; headings must "appear in order"; the H1 should be first (Prito's Showit site had all H2s above the H1; Kajabi printed the blog name above every H1). Only one H1 per page; unlimited H2s; two headings back to back are fine; one H1, eight H2, five H3, seven H4 was "totally fine." [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt] [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt] [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Visual size of the H1 does not matter: "The styling does not matter. The H1 does not have to be the largest text." Only invisible text is a problem. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Keywords in heading tags are weighted more than in paragraphs; headings must not be generic ("learn more," "maybe you often"); use the focus keyword, a variation, or a local keyword in them. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Put the keyword as early as possible in title tag, meta description and content. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Keyword density: match the top ranking pages plus one; never 20 more. Matt: traditional "use the keyword X times in these places" is not what we do; content should read naturally with keyword variations. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt] [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt] [002_wdir-11am-17-07-2026.txt]
- Two spellings that show the same search volume ("wood chippers" vs "woodchippers") are one keyword; use the correct one. [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Title tags auto-appending the business name (Squarespace, Yoast): remove in Squarespace title code or Yoast > Settings > content types > posts/pages > delete "site title"; Google usually strips it anyway, it is mostly an audit warning. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt] [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Meta descriptions: set in Yoast, which overrides the page builder (Elementor) description; always set one. [007_technical-seo-with-denroi-09-07-2026.txt]
- Title tag and meta description are "the billboard saying click me instead of your competitors": rewrite them on pages ranking bottom of page one or page two to lift CTR. [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt]
- Find the query with the most impressions for a page in Search Console and "add that on your page one more time." [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Text inside a logo or image is invisible to Google; state what you do in real text. [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt]

Length, duplication, thin pages
- Length is set by the top three competitors for that keyword, plus a little; over-long, over-optimized pages get outranked by 400 to 600 word pages. [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt] [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Never repeat the same text blocks across pages (business info, sections, references); identical CSS/format is fine. [001_work-session-20-07-2026.txt] [004_work-session-13-07-2026.txt] [010_wdir-11am-03-07-2026.txt]
- Denroy: "As recently as last year, Google has started punishing sites that have too much white space in their content. So if a user scrolls down and just sees this, nothing's here.
