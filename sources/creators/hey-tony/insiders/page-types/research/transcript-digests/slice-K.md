# Transcripts slice K (108-116)

Scope: nine HeyTony Insiders call transcripts, December 2025 (Rank Week days 4 and 5, two Monday blog work sessions, two Friday "Why Doesn't It Rank" audits, Denroi technical SEO open forum, and two Leanne design calls: the SEO page makeover and the Elementor blog-post template build). Every bullet below is a claim, number, rule, or example actually stated on a call, with the transcript it came from in brackets.

## 1. Homepage

- Homepage hero must say what you do and where, in text, not in a graphic. Michelle's couples therapy homepage (GoDaddy site, Manhattan) read "experienced psychotherapist for individual and family therapy" with her address buried at the bottom of the page. Matt: "if you're a local business, like, I want to see exactly where you do that service." His rewrite: H1 "couples counseling in Midtown Manhattan" or "couples therapy in Midtown NYC" (Midtown Manhattan: difficulty zero, about 20 to 40 searches a month; "Times Square" and "couples counseling NYC" at 590 a month were judged too hard), with a sub-line underneath like "trusted provider of personalized couples therapy, Midtown." Swap counseling for therapy if the keyword research says therapy is easier. [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- "Couples counseling is like going to the gym. The relationship gets strong" was judged not captivating as a headline; the fix is the service plus the neighborhood, not a metaphor. [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Trevor's "We Film Events" homepage: the hero text ("Vancouver live streaming and video production services for events") was baked into a photo. "Google can't see the photo, right? They can't see the words in the photo. I mean, they can, but it doesn't really count." Use live text. [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Same site: the logo appeared twice in the hero (once in the nav, once as the hero image). "Why do we need this twice? This is wasted real estate." The hero should instead tell people what you do: "hire us for your blank, or like we do blank... We are a dentist. We will fix your teeth. We are plumbers. We will fix your leaks." [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- The primary CTA button belongs above the fold: "Get a quote" or "Book a free call" is "the button that people want to click right before scrolling. Because you can't depend on anybody scrolling anywhere." The site's "Watch" button was the wrong primary action. [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Homepage keyword: one keyword per page. Trevor's homepage tried to rank for both live streaming and video production while separate pages already existed for each; "you need to choose one" and push the others to their own pages (live streaming, event video for meetings and AGMs, conference recording, virtual events, each its own page). Suggested homepage keyword: "Vancouver video production services." [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Model homepage praised live: Ash's Cincinnati cleaning site: "Cincinnati's trusted house cleaning for busy families... You're saying where you are. You're saying what you do. You have call, book in 60 seconds. Hell yeah." Later: "Ash's website was really great set up where you could put in your information. You can get a clean for 155 bucks or whatever, or you could learn more. It had exactly what he did." [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Jessa's intimacy-course site: the homepage was ranking number two for "online intimacy course for couples" while the actual course page was not; the homepage mentioned "course" only six times and every competitor result was a course page. Advice: make the course page the homepage (WordPress: Settings > Reading > set the static homepage to the course page), monitor for a couple of weeks, and if rankings drop "put it back to the way it was. Don't delete any pages." The menu Home button will then go to that page. "SEO is all about testing." [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Mike Peterson's Boston towing rank-and-rent homepage: "Boston Towing, full local towing service near you." "The words near you, that doesn't do anything... when I see that, I'm like, that is like amateur SEO." Do not add "near me" or "near you" to pages; Google localizes results by the searcher's device. Also flagged: no Google Business Profile, no address on the site, no directory listings, and the homepage keyword "Boston towing" (difficulty 57) when "tow truck Boston" (29), "tow truck Boston MA," "Boston tow truck company," and "tow truck service Boston" were easier. "You need your address on the website somewhere to show that you are actually local." [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Scott's Organizing Agency homepage: too many competing colors and CTAs (green, purple-pink, blue, a "download your report" button, "call now for a complimentary consultation"). "Typically, when you come to a website, there is a call to action that is a different color than everything else on the screen... What's with all the colors?" A site that sells clutter-free organizing "does not look organized." His top competitor (The Model Home) was praised for lots of space, few words, clear services, and a contact button right there. Matt's recommendation: a website redesign focused on clean service pages. [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- "Should my homepage rank for anything? Not necessarily." HeyTony's homepage is broadly about digital marketing while individual service pages carry SEO, Facebook ads, Instagram ads. For a wedding photographer and videographer, the homepage can be broadly "wedding photographer and videographer in [area]" while the money pages target individual services (elopement, engagement shoots, proposal shoots). [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Evie (skincare and Reiki studio, NJ): "I fix some things on my homepage. Not even on my money page, just on my homepage. And I am literally ranking number three when I was, like, all the way at the bottom." [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Shopify homepage H1 audit (Monique, bee venom skincare): the theme had an empty H1 plus extra H1s coming from plugins and a pop-up ("25% off your first order"). Fix in Online Store > theme editor > theme.liquid, search "page.title" or "h1"; put the homepage keyword ("The Best Bee Venom Distilled in Nature's Lab") in the one H1. Rule: "one heading one tag per page. And that should be the very first heading." [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- A single-property vacation rental: it is fine for the homepage to be the page that ranks ("Casa Luna Azul," five pages). Then add content (blogs or extra pages) so the domain ranks for more keywords. [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- A homepage that is "about us and like short story, very, very little keyword specific sentences" is not a money page (Victor, moving company). [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Hidden landing pages are invisible: "if I go to your homepage and I can't get to those pages from your homepage, they're invisible" (Aaron, Brazil: homepage plus unlinked product landing pages, blog on a subdomain). Everything should live on the main domain for a cohesive experience. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- A one-word domain without the keyword is fine for the homepage: "squigglesquad.com" did not need to become "squigglesquadhandwriting.com." "Some people have theories that exact match keywords matter, but I don't think that it plays that big of an impact." [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Homepage map embed advice (applies to any page with a map): see section 14. [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]

## 2. Service pages (parent/overview and individual child service pages) and money pages in general

- Definition: "Your money pages are all the individual services or all the individual products you have." A booking page on another platform is not the money page; the money page is optimized for the keyword and can link out to the booking platform. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Money page = a high-intent page: "a page that somebody has a high intent. They're coming to that page because they are looking for that service." For a mover: home furniture movers, home appliance movers, apartment movers, "hire movers," even route pages (moving from New York to Washington) if keyword research supports them. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- One page per service: Scott (organizing) had every residential service on one page. "You should have a different page for every service... would it be more useful to have a page specifically about closets?" Structure: overview pages (commercial, residential, moving) that link down to "what do you need help with organizing?" with garage, bathroom, laundry room, kitchen, each an individual page. "Every page is about one keyword or should be focused on one keyword. And right now you don't have a page focused on bathrooms." The links from the overview page can be H2s or paragraph text "as long as those words are linked to the other page." [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- "What if I just have a services page? ... this is a wake up call that you should have individual pages for different keywords that you want to rank for." A plumber is not just "plumbing services": drain repair, drain snaking, new pipes each get a page. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- HeyTony's own poorly optimized example (Instagram ads page): "The only thing down here is get in touch. And the get in touch goes to my contact page." A money page needs more than a single generic link. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Weekly money page tasks from the 30-day plan: fix the title tag and meta description on a money page ("This takes five minutes"), add three internal links to a money page, and have each new blog post link to one money page. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Order of work: "I would fix my money pages first... as much as possible. And then I would focus on writing new blog content. But I wouldn't let fixing the money pages stop me from writing blog content." At the agency: two deliverables per week, e.g. one new blog post plus "fixing a service page or fixing a product page. Right. Like rewriting the whole thing because it doesn't rank." [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt] [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Internal link math for money pages: HeyTony's SEO services page "was ranking on page two for like a year and all we did was build internal links to it and it started ranking number one." If a page already ranks 2 or 3 leave it; if it is bottom of page one or on page two, add internal links. [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Link minimums that apply to money pages too: "one external link per page and three to five internal links per page. You can do more for sure. But that's like the minimum." Contact and about pages are the exception. [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Money page to money page links are fine "if it makes sense" (web design page: "if you're looking for SEO services, we also do that as well"), but not forced (Instagram ads page should not push a new website). "Do it in moderation and just do what makes sense." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- The keyword must actually be on the page. Manny's "installing landscape lights in Montreal" page (built by a prior developer) did not contain "landscape lighting Montreal" (50 searches, difficulty 11, "you don't even need backlinks to rank"). "The reason why you're not ranking, Manny, is because the keyword's not even on the page." [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Same site: the page linked to the homepage using the anchor "landscape lighting," telling Google the homepage is the landscape lighting page; service pages should link to the matching city pages instead. URLs were keyword stuffed and included "near me" and a category slug ("landscape-lighting-installer/near-me-montreal-quebec-landscape-lighting"). Matt's URL: "/landscape-lighting-montreal." "Get rid of the near me, get rid of the categories... You don't need that stuff." "Whoever you hired to do this is a hack." [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Same site: every service and city page looked the same ("Different picture. Three or four paragraphs. Recent projects."). "It's hard as a user to really disseminate what page I'm on... make it obvious that they're on that page." Breadcrumbs on the site were praised. [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Insync IELTS coaching page (Chennai, India): competitors ranking with zero backlinks had "lots of text"; his page had little. Competitor details worth copying in spirit: face to face, online and hybrid classes, "10 to 20 years experience, trainers assistant for test registration," mock tests, the full phrase "International English Language Testing System" (he never used the full acronym expansion). His page opened with "35% off sign up right now": "It's fine to have a call to action at the top here. But this page starts with. There's not a lot of text... give me more information." Also the breadcrumb bar was hidden on scroll ("you're hiding text on the page immediately. Google doesn't like that"). Advice: "Revamp it and then look at who's ranking number one, two, three for any of these keywords and just try to do more of what they're doing." [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Scott's competitor service and area pages: "very clean... There's a lot of space. There's not a lot of words so far. And like, it's very clear as to what they do... She is basically one or two sentences on every service page or area serve page" and a "contact us button right there." [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Leanne's Winnipeg logo design service page makeover (full section list, top to bottom): hero with H1 plus one CTA button; H2 section "where Winnipeg logo design meets storytelling"; H2 with three H3 services in a three-column grid with icons; H2 "why does logo design matter more than you think" as text left, image right; H2 process section with H3s as image left, text right; about me section (text and photo); a centered-text section for visual variety; FAQ accordion; a closing call-to-action section. "My rule of thumb when I'm designing, every H2 tag should be its own section." [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Her audit of the old version: headings out of order (no H1, no H2s, stray H3s and H5s), HeyTony audit score 71, keyword density too high ("keyword stuffing and it's basically gibberish to the average person"), vague headline ("designed to make your business stand out... What are we designing?"), "not compelling copy" ("Our company does logos"), meaningless headlines, generic stock photos on a logo page ("you expect to see some examples of logos if you are on a logo design page"), big wall of text, too much centered text, oversized image without alt text. After: audit 96, PageSpeed 97 to 100. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Dynamic search-result URLs (anything with "?" or "=" after the last slash, e.g. an ATV inventory search page) get canonical warnings and "typically have a harder time ranking because the content is constantly changing." If you want it to rank, build a static page at a clean URL with the same search shortcode plus static content ("paragraphs, text blocks, FAQs"). [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- "Crawled, currently not indexed" on a page you want ranking: "create more internal links to it." Sample or irrelevant pages: delete them. [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- H2 count on a page: "heading two tags. You can use as many as you really feel necessary. Just don't go too overboard to the point the current rank one has 50 and then you're going for 200." "If they have five heading two tags, go for seven, eight." Five or ten is fine. [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- Do not hide service or collection page copy behind "read more" dropdowns: "It's better if it's not in a box. Right. Because Google is looking at that and the same as a person. Well, if this information is so important, why is it hidden in a box?" "You need to convince your client to allow you to put more content on that page and work with their website designer so that they can make it look good." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Duplicate-ish money pages (three bundles of the same product): "I would let keyword research dictate what I do." If the keywords differ, keep three; if not, merge. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Competitors who copy a category page with different text for different keywords and the same products: "if it's unique text, that's fine." Matt's stance: "if somebody has two heading one tags and they're ranking number one, I'm like, I'm gonna add two heading one tags and just try it out. Because I have nothing to lose because I'm not ranking." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Competitor outranking you with 8 H1s and a phone number in an H2 (Eric, Key West charters): then look beyond on-page: backlinks, anchor text, internal links, blog volume, topical authority. "On page SEO is very important, but sometimes they're ranking for other factors." [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Priority order when a page is not ranking: "the most important is having helpful content on your website... you can't optimize if you don't have content." Then internal linking as you write, then backlinks ("getting one or two backlinks to that page will push you over the edge"). [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Service page with an easy keyword in another language (Thomas, web design, 2% difficulty, 100/month): go after the bigger keyword first; "your location. Maybe you can mention that on the page or maybe not." Adding a city into a long keyword makes you more likely to rank there but no more likely elsewhere, and "we don't want to stuff keywords." [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Seasonal or event-driven money pages: "Six months out, that's when you want to start doing SEO for those pages" (San Jose furniture rental page ahead of the Super Bowl). [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Intent check before building the page type: "If they're case studies, yours should be a case study. If it's a blog post yours should be a blog post. If it's a service or a money page, yours should be a service or money page." If all results for "podcast production" are how-to guides, a service landing page will struggle; write the guide ("Don't be scared to show people how to do it themselves"). [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Live streaming example of the same rule: top results were platforms (Vimeo etc.), so "maybe this isn't the right keyword for you." "If the top results are a service and you're trying to get a blog post, like, you're probably not going to rank for that... if it's a blog post and you're trying to rank a service, it's probably not going to happen." [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Page speed comes after content: "don't even worry about your page speed at this point. Like, you gotta fix your pages first... If your content is equal or better than everybody else's content and you're still not ranking, then I would say, okay, look at your page speed after that." [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- A blog post and a page "look the exact same to Google... It's just content." Service pages usually chase harder keywords, which is why they rank slower. [108_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-22-2025.txt]
- Joyce (K-beauty affiliate, cannot sell on her own site): build brand comparison or alternative pages ("company X versus company Y," "alternatives," "dupe") as pages, not hidden from the menu but placed "in the footer maybe," to pick up branded traffic. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Wedding business: money pages should carry "a highlight of the best work in that category" and also link to case study pages; do both. [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Blog posts can all link to the booking page; "You could even put your booking form on those pages somewhere if you wanted to." But no two pages on the same keyword: a deep clean blog post plus a deep clean service page is cannibalization. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]

## 3. Location / city / service-area pages

- Expanding a cleaning business to nearby cities (Fairfield to Hamilton, Ohio): "create location pages for those separate cities... Just make sure they're unique and they talk about the individual areas." Expect three to six months. You probably will not enter the map pack there without a Google Business Profile in that area; expanding the GBP service area is possible but "might dilute" your current map pack. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- Furniture rental serving San Francisco, Las Vegas, Phoenix, Los Angeles from an LA base: "If you are servicing different areas, absolutely. You can have location pages for them." [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- City plus neighborhood pages (Coach D, car recycling, 21 areas): one keyword per page; "car recycling, neighborhood... That's like the full keyword." The service part can stay the same across pages, "but the neighborhood is what changes the keyword." Follow keyword research per area (maybe "we buy junk cars" in one, "sell my car fast" in another). "That's not cannibalization." Agency example: pizza delivery Hamilton, Ancaster, Dundas, and possibly "Detroit style pizza" where that variant is easier. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- HVAC: two clients, every service for every city would be about 200 pages. "You need 100% original, my friend." "Pigs get fat and hogs get slaughtered... that is being a hog, right, you're likely not going to rank for all of those. It might hurt the site." Pick one keyword per location page, usually the main service (the $350 job, not the $100 add-on). "Could they take on all the work?" [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Small towns: "is it worth building out a page if there's no volume there?" Then local nuance from the members: in DC, Maryland and Virginia people identify with their town (Potomac, Bethesda), so area pages make sense; start with the more expensive areas. [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Model area page (The Model Home, Washington DC): headline names the city, "one or two sentences," lists specialties (closet makeovers, kitchen reorganizations, decluttering) and a contact button. "I come here. It says Washington, D.C. I know I'm in the right locality." [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Scott kept his area-served pages under About Us with an old template; Matt's reaction was to redesign and build proper service pages first. [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- City pages in the footer (Manny): fine, but link each service page to the matching city page, simplify URLs to "/landscape-lighting-montreal," and make each city page visibly distinct from the next. [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Vacation rentals (Isla Mujeres): "go for the long keyword vacation rental in city and then just mention things in that each location, build those location pages out... one keyword per page." [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- Multiple rental properties: differentiate pages by bedrooms and oceanfront versus ocean view; each property at a different address can also have its own Google Business Profile. [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Blog posts as internal-link sources for location pages: "you don't need to include more than one location per blog post... Alternate services and locations." Fifty or a hundred internal links in one post makes it unreadable and looks unnatural. [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Put the location in the H1 of a local money page ("couples counseling in Midtown Manhattan"); "near me" and "near you" do nothing. [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt] [109_wdir-19-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Product businesses (interior design products): location pages only if you "service specific areas or you're in stores or boutiques." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Seasonal service in two countries (Ibiza summers): "I would have two pages set up... they're both in different countries or different cities," and a GBP can exist per business location. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]

## 4. Product, collection, category pages (ecommerce)

- Shopify collection pages are "the biggest missed opportunity when I look at a Shopify store." Layout: "at the top you could have a sentence or two, and then obviously the products in that collection. Underneath that you can have paragraphs. You can almost write, like a blog post with pictures and all this kind of other stuff." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Accordions on technical product pages are acceptable "if people are using it... as long as it's not, like, every piece of information you're trying to hide." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Product page reviews and keyword density (Andreas, 15 to 20 reviews on one product): "Keep the reviews there, because that'll help you sell more. It shows that people are buying it." For density: "look at the word count of the actual, like, what your competitor is doing, minus, if they have reviews, try to match their word count, and then try to match their keyword density and just pretend like your reviews don't exist on that page." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Hundreds of near-identical variant pages (steel corner guards and kick plates, every size a page, Wix): keep pages that rank and get traffic; "do keyword research to be like, what sizes are people looking up the most and make sure I have those specific pages"; consider a category page with a filter for the rest. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Blog post outranking your own collection page (Catherine, silk nightwear, ranking 10 for a keyword where competitors show product pages): first "adjust the blog post to feature some of your products"; if that fails, scrap the post and optimize the collection page, or redirect the post to the collection (you will lose some of the backlinks the post earned). [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Shopify title tag and meta description: "if you can put in the title tag and meta description, that's all you really need. Like, you don't need Yoast for that." [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Shopify EN-CA duplicates of every page and product: check Search Console for which variant ranks; for blog posts keep one and redirect; look for a geolocation plugin creating them; "a lot of Shopify, WordPress, almost any site is still case by case basis." [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- Keyword density on a product page was "too high" on Leanne's tool for her own page; same lesson as service pages: stuffing reads as gibberish. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Product business with 2.2 million products: "just choose five. Choose five of your most popular ones" as the first money pages to internally link. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Golf product (cart seat towel, page five on Shopping): fix meta tags, optimize product pages, and write blog posts that link to the product pages ("the best kind of golf cart seat towel," "what materials absorb the most"), plus organic social. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Tag pages (WordPress) are "dead pages... duplicate content from the blog post... no unique content." HeyTony never uses them; use categories. [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- Old blog "posts" that were really 10 photos and two lines (tour company): delete if not indexed, no traffic, no backlinks; update or keep if there is value. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]

## 6. Contact / quote / booking / intake pages

- The booking page is not the money page; the money page can link to the booking page or an external booking platform. [114_rank-week-day-5-replay.txt]
- You can link every blog post to the booking page and even embed the booking form on those pages. [116_rank-week-day-4.txt]
- Contact page does not need the internal/external link minimums. [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Praised intake flow: enter your information, get a clean for about $155, or learn more (Ash, cleaning). [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Search forms that create "?query=" URLs produce canonical noise; the static page is what should rank. [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]

## 15. General page rules that apply to every page

- One H1 per page, the first heading, containing the target keyword; "It's basically the title of our page. Like if it were a book, that would be the title of the book." Duplicate desktop/mobile H1s with identical text are tolerable. Everything under it is H2/H3. Designers often style visually and forget tags; Elementor lets you style and then set the right tag. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt] [110_technical-seo-with-denroi-2025-12-18.txt]
- Headings as sections: every H2 is its own section; three H3s under an H2 suit a three-column grid; alternate layouts for skimmability ("left align text, center align text, 3 column, and then text image, image text, text image, and then center align"). [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Heading sizes: about 48px H1, 36px H2, smaller H3; increments of 5 or 10; depends on font ("Some fonts look really big"). Body 16px minimum. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt] [111_blog-post-templates-using-elementor-in-wordpress-18-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Text alignment: "With centered, aligned text, it's a little bit harder for the human eye to read it... It's much easier on our eyes if text is left aligned." Use centered sparingly. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Real text, never graphics, for headings and copy (also scales for mobile; check mobile font sizes after designing). [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt] [113_why-doesn-t-it-rank-12-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Images: size to the container; a page's content width is about 1200px so a half-width image needs about 500 to 700px (her example was 500 by 375); JPEG unless transparency (PNG); convert to WebP (Free Convert or a plugin); always add alt text that uses the focus keyword but also describes the image for screen readers. Oversized images were the top PageSpeed flag. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt] [112_work-session-writing-a-blog-post-12-15-2025.txt]
- Whitespace: add padding in ems (she used 4 to 10em bottom padding) so text never butts against section edges; "a little bit more breathing room." Icons above service columns make them "easier to digest." Left-align inside columns. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Paste copy into the code editor tab so Google Doc styling does not carry over. [115_seo-design-wtith-leanne-10-12-2025-replay.txt]
- Keyword density: neither too high (stuffing) nor hiding the keyword; benchmark against the competitor's word count and density. Word count on Boston's .gov list was "not good... just a table" yet it ranked on authority, so authority can beat on-page.
