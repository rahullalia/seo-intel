Source: nine HeyTony Insiders call transcripts, 10 June 2026 to 19 June 2026 (two WDIR Friday Q&A calls, a Monday work session, Leanne's web design for SEO call, Denroi and Elliot's technical SEO call, and the four Rank Week replays, days 1 to 4). ## 1. Homepage

- The homepage is the "zero click page": the visitor has clicked nothing yet. Anything linked from the homepage navigation or body (about page, blog index, services page, product page) is one click; a blog post reached from the blog index is two clicks; a post linked only from another post is three clicks. "If it takes four clicks or more to get to a page on your website, Google doesn't really care. The harder it is for a human to find your page, the less likely Google will actually care about it." [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt]
- The homepage can be your money page. "Your Money page can be your homepage if you'd like." Matt's own site: "I don't even have an about page on my website. Everything's on the homepage." [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt] [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Marisa (comedian, Boca Raton): her booking page is her homepage for Boca Raton, with other location pages for other cities. Matt confirmed the about page does not compete with the homepage for keywords: "the difference between your homepage and your about page is so slim because your homepage is like, here's about the business, here's what I do, all this stuff. And then your about page is like, here's a little bit more about me." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Do not keep revising the homepage. Jason (Atlanta photographer) admitted he revises his homepage "like every other week" and is stuck on page three. Matt: "don't mess with your homepage. Cause every time you change it, Google has to go back and be like, okay, where does this belong?" Get it "looking pretty good and leave it alone." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Counter-example for patience: Matt's own "SEO in Hamilton" service page was built, not heavily optimized, and left untouched; it reached number one after about a year purely from ongoing blog posts building topical authority, internal linking and anchor text. [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Homepage for a multi-country business (Benjamin, UK web design agency with a Wyoming office): optimize the homepage and main pages around the UK keyword ("Web developer in UK is your main keyword. That's your homepage, that's your main services page") and build entirely separate pages for each US area rather than trying to make the homepage serve both. [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]
- Anil (disability tax credit service): "your homepage would be like, here's the service that we do." The business name equals the government program name; Matt suggested optimizing for the forms people need help with (step by step, video guide), even building a page where people type their info and it fills out the government form for them, with bigger text blocks for visually impaired users. [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Leanne's go-to homepage wireframe, "works for most websites", in order: (1) Hero: headline with an impactful message, a call to action button, and an image or graphic that relates to the business; (2) trust building elements "either within the hero or right below it" because "we really want to make a strong impression when someone lands on our website"; (3) an About Us section right below the hero, "imagine we're introducing ourselves to our customer": what we do, how we do it, who we serve; (4) outline of services or products; (5) testimonials: "other people have had great experiences working with us, you probably will too"; (6) a How It Works / process section to ease the anxiety of people who have never hired this kind of business; (7) FAQs, "good for Google and it's also good for your customers to kind of answer those lingering questions that they might have that might stop them from reaching out"; (8) a call to action at the very bottom, "so if someone scrolls all the way to the bottom of the page, they have something to do next instead of ... they might just leave the website because we didn't tell them what they should do next." [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]
- Leanne's build order: set up branded styles first (matching the Figma file), then build the homepage first, then the other pages. Copy comes before layout: "I always start with the copy first ... work out your SEO copy first and then you can figure out what kind of layout will help convey that message stronger and help you make sure that you're not having like a big wall of text." [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]
- Leanne on a Wix section she was building (massage business): a four-column "who we help" section (desk workers, athletes, etc.) whose purpose is "to make all of my potential clients feel seen and heard that we do offer services like directly for them", followed immediately by a call to action button. "I think we could use a little bit more text in the hero here." [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]

## 2. Service pages (parent/overview and individual child service pages) and money pages in general

- Definition: a money page "is a page that makes you money ... realistically, it's a product or service page." Examples given: dentist = Invisalign page; roofer = residential shingle/roofing page; realtor = "sell your home" / "buy a home" pages; a booking page; an Airbnb reservation page; a photographer's service pages; each ecommerce product; Matt's website design page ("Talking about how we do website design, and you can contact us"). "All you need is one." "Barbara, service page is 100% a money page." [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Leanne's framing: Matt's "money page" means your core offerings you make the most money from; she calls a single-offer page a "sales page" or "landing page" and the homepage can also be a money page. She promised a separate wireframe for sales/landing pages because "it's a little bit different than like the standard homepage or a standard service page." [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]
- Break combined services into a page per service line. Anthony (NJ commercial trash compactors and related lines) had "a homepage and then I had like all services combined"; on Matt's recommendation he built "a page for each service line", fixed all the H1 heading tags, then built location pages for each major service line. [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Steph G (psychotherapist, Ohio): have a page for each service specialty (Matt had told Francois the same), target the city (Columbus) rather than the whole state, and go after specific therapy types per page: couples therapy, marriage counseling, PTSD therapy, CBT, anxiety therapy in Columbus; "there's going to be one that's easier and you'll start getting calls for those keywords." Her plan: blog posts in support of that page first. [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Every page targets one keyword. Tom (realtor, Virginia Beach, "homes for sale in Virginia Beach" page with IDX): do not also write a blog post for that keyword: "If you have the exact same keyword on two different pages, Google looks at them and goes, well, I don't know which page to rank. Maybe I'm not going to rank either of them." Blog instead on "best areas to live in Virginia Beach" and link to the homes-for-sale pages. [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Target keyword for the audit: what you think people type to find that page, for local businesses city plus service ("Hamilton Electrician", "Movers Toronto", "Recording Studio Oakland CA", "Health Performance in Austin", "ADU Bay Area"). Get more specific where possible: "Is it Movers Leslieville, Movers Queen West." [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- The money page audit spreadsheet columns: the LLM output for the page (structure, H2/H3 tags, counts), the title tag (meta title, "the blue text that appears on Google") and the meta description ("the text that you see underneath that on Google"), repeated for the top three organic competitors (skip ads/sponsored, Reddit, Yelp, Instagram). [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Competitor example from the Page Audit extension: for "Hamilton Electrician" the competitor's density for the exact phrase was zero because they wrote "Electrician in Hamilton"; keyword occurrence 20, keyword density 0.3 for that variation. Variations count. [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Why a worse page outranks you: "What your competitors are doing is something that Google likes ... we need to mirror that and slightly outdo them." Don't take the LLM's word count as gospel; if third place has 500 words, 600 can do; include missing information (even a price range). [024_rank-week-day-3-replay-17-06-2026.txt]
- Match the page type Google ranks for the keyword. Sarah (UK): her product page vs. competitors' collection pages ranking; "Google wants to see a collection page. I'm going to optimize my collection page for that keyword rather than that product page." Rocky: "if they have a product page ranking, you want to optimize your product page ... if it's a money page, then optimize yours for a money page. If a blog post is cannibalizing that, maybe change that blog post around or consider removing it." [024_rank-week-day-3-replay-17-06-2026.txt] [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt]
- Don't rip up a money page that isn't ranking. Ken (private investigator, "cheating spouse" page; ChatGPT produced "thousands of words of pseudo me"): "make some small changes to the page and wait a couple weeks. Update a couple paragraphs or add a couple paragraphs or add like a couple FAQs. Don't make like a crazy, crazy switch." [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt]
- Lead magnet on a money page: Ken's idea of a short five-question tick-box questionnaire that gives his Amazon book for free: "As long as you're getting their contact information. That's a lead magnet. That's fantastic." Conversions on the page are "one of the most important user signals." [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt]

## 3. Location / city / service-area pages

- You change the entire content for each location page, not just the city name: "You're changing the entire content each time. It is a lot of work, but if it makes you money, then it's worth it." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Florist client example of what to write on each location page: same day delivery in that place ("if you're ordering from [city], within six hours or 12 hours, we'll have flowers out to you"), a bit about the place, geographic specifics such as "if you're attending a funeral in blank, here's where we'll deliver flowers within X amount of hours", listing local funeral homes and wedding venues per page. [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Photographer version (Ariel): list the parks/locations you shoot at and talk about each: "it's really great from the months of May until September ... in the middle of the summer it's going to be a lot hotter ... here are some things that you can do to prepare." This belongs on a location page, not a blog post, "because that's like static information that you want people to know and you want to rank for the money keyword." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- To generate location page content, reuse the blog interview prompt: ask yourself per location "what do you like the most about this location? How's the lighting in this location? What if it's rainy? Can we still shoot here?" then turn it into a page. Pace: "Just start with one ... do one a week. And if there's 12 in three months, you have all the pages done." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Competitors doing near-duplicate location pages with only the city swapped: "if they're doing that, then it's super low competition if you come in with new and different content." Try unique pages for three or four months; if they don't rank, "then you can go back and be like, can I do something simpler? ... Maybe you can make them all the same." A podcast guest reported duplicate pages ranking in low competition, "I don't think that works for everybody." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Locations hub: competitors list 12 locations at the bottom of pages; "if they're doing it, try it, pick one page to do it on." Matt's preference: "have a button in your footer and your header that says locations ... it goes to a page that has all the different locations", grouped (e.g., Chicago suburbs), each linking to its own location page. [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Denroi's location page build (web developer, Wyoming): "Web developer in Wyoming, there's your title tag, there's your heading one. Down that page you'll have headings: Why should you hire a web developer in Wyoming? Why your Wyoming business needs professional web development. How a web developer can help businesses in Wyoming. Really build out that page for the service and keyword and repeat that process for every single area." Then "create blog posts or a main hub page that links to every single one of those location pages, making absolutely sure that a user that lands on that hub page can tell those pages are in the US for the US, not for someone in the UK." [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]
- URL structure for local services (Gabriel, armed security guards): drop the "services" parent; use /security-guard/toronto/ (service type slash location) and optimize the page for that keyword. "Having the services parent is the unnecessary one there ... it extends the URL ... too redundant with the service type." Hyphen (security-guard-toronto) is also fine, but a slash makes categorizing easier "if you are building out dozens or hundreds of location pages ... over 30 pages." [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]
- Navigation: location pages "don't have to be" on the main navigation, "However, we always recommend the most important location pages be somewhere in your navigation, whether that's the main header navigation or in the footer." [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]
- "Secret" geo pages that cannot be reached from the homepage (Aaron's previous SEO trainer's "secret pages"): "if those are secret and you can't get to them from your homepage, Google can't see those." [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- A location page should be tied to a Google Business Profile where possible. Pavitra: the location page tied to her GBP ranks, the other doesn't; moving Florida to Dubai means switching the GBP address (re-verify, keep reviews) or creating a second profile (loses reviews). [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]
- Expanding to new markets (Scott's roofing client, markets three hours away): step one, GBPs in those areas (otherwise no maps, no local reviews, no local signals); step two, "make sure you have a page on the website for those locations ... a dedicated landing page for them. So that's a location page"; step three, local press, local backlinks, chamber of commerce, sponsorships. [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt]
- Tom (consultant, Ontario and New Jersey, targeting NYC): have a GBP in both areas and "location page on your website for both of those areas"; reviews get split between profiles. Maybe start with one market. [024_rank-week-day-3-replay-17-06-2026.txt]
- Opening a new location: "I wouldn't say do a blog for that, David. I would just say make the location page." [027_work-session-15-06-2026.txt]
- Blog geo metadata (Squarespace): set the blog's location to Dubai only when the blog content actually mentions Dubai (a "service in Dubai" post); generic posts like "what is a web developer" need no location. [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]
- Victoria (cleaning company coaching): instead of one "how much does it cost to start a cleaning business" page competing with Stripe and Wix, build state/province versions where people actually search that way ("in Ontario", "in BC", "in Ohio"), because rules, taxes and suppliers differ, with real screenshots of costs and first-hand expertise. [024_rank-week-day-3-replay-17-06-2026.txt]

## 15. General page rules that apply to every page (length, density, images, headings, speed, mobile, readability, AEO/answer-first) when framed as page-building guidance

- One focus keyword per page; every page is about one specific topic; the same keyword on two pages confuses Google. [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt] [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt] [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt]
- Keyword variations are semantic and interchangeable ("photography versus photographer ... use them interchangeably"); three or four uses of the phrase in a post is fine; competitors rank with density around 0.3 using a variation. [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt] [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
- Over- and under-optimizing both exist; calibrate against what ranks 1, 2 and 3 (word count, topics, page type) and slightly outdo them. [022_rank-week-day-4-replay.txt] [024_rank-week-day-3-replay-17-06-2026.txt]
- H1: one per page. Marisa's Avada site showed two H1s because she duplicated blocks to restyle mobile; use each block's responsive settings (e.g., font size 100 px desktop, 50 px mobile) instead of duplicating, because duplicates load everything twice and Google sees "the same paragraph two times in a row." A different hero background image on mobile (text on top, photo below) is fine "as long as the text stays the same." Rebecca fixed her 2016 Avada site's mobile text running to the screen edge with ChatGPT; Matt: paste the block code into Claude code and say "make this mobile friendly in the same blocks without duplicating the content." [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt]
- Title tag: about 55 to 60 characters before Google truncates, especially on mobile. Meta description: "quote unquote, don't matter for ranking," but a unique, compelling description drives the click; duplicate meta descriptions across pages are bad. [026_rank-week-day-1-replay-15-06-2026.txt] [027_work-session-15-06-2026.txt]
- Heading structure: blog posts use H2 and H3 only; location pages use the keyword as H1 and question-style H2s ("Why should you hire a web developer in Wyoming?"). [026_rank-week-day-1-replay-15-06-2026.txt] [023_technical-seo-with-denroi-18-06-2026.txt]

- Fonts: more than three fonts slows the site; two is fine; serif headline plus sans serif body gives contrast; Times New Roman and default serifs look unstyled/dated. [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]
- Colors: avoid pure white and pure black (use off-white and near-black navy, "a little hint of color elevates your design"); subtle alternating section background colors (sandy beige, tan); pick palettes on Pinterest ("navy blue color palette"); CTA buttons in a high-contrast color (gold) with bold, readable 20 px text; remove box shadows and text shadows. [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]
- Layout rhythm: text left/image right, then reverse in the next section, "adds some more visual interest and differentiates each section so people know that this section will be only about this topic." [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]

- Mobile vs desktop: B2B sites (interpreter) can prioritize desktop where traffic shows it; Squarespace only offers desktop and mobile breakpoints; always test responsiveness. [028_web-design-for-seo-10-06-2026.txt]
- Unique content on every page; no hidden text ("if it's hidden, it's hidden from Google ... they'll be like, well, these people clearly don't want me to see it"); no hidden/secret pages. [026_rank-week-day-1-replay-15-06-2026.txt] [021_wdir-19-06-2026.txt] [025_rank-week-day-2-replay-16-06-2026.txt]
