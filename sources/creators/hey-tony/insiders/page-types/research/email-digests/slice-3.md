## Tips by email

### 2026-05-14, This will help you rank fast (19e26a910bfb89e7)

- Core concept: "information gain". "A big thing we've been focusing on at HeyTony when doing SEO is something called 'information gain'". He says it is "something that Google absolutely loves" and that you may already be doing it without realizing.
- Why it matters: "Because of all the AI slop out there, Google is looking for new, unique content." "AI is only really able to reiterate content that exists. It can't come up with new ideas or concepts." "Google is looking for information that is NEW and different."
- The anti-pattern: "If you go to ChatGPT and say 'write me a blog post about TOPIC' it'll spit you out a great piece of AI slop - an iteration of content that already exists.. just reworded." "Google.. and PEOPLE hate that."
- What to put on the page instead: "We want to hear from YOU - the expert. Your views, opinions, personal stories, failures, etc. That's what Google & AI are recommending right now."
- Rule: "Most people use AI to replace their thinking... Don't do that."
- The method: "Instead - have ChatGPT interview you to get your perspective and thoughts." "I guarantee you'll have a different outlook than your competitor down the street. And that makes your content completely YOU."
- P.S.: Rank Week is coming up in June; promise is showing how to "get SEO done in just one hour a week" for people struggling to get SEO done or spending too much time on it.

### 2026-05-09, SEO in 1 hour a week... it's easier than you think (19e0d27ab6e706fb)

- Framing: small business owners wear "about 12 different hats", SEO sits on the to-do list "between 'redo the website' and 'finally hire that VA'", and the typical pattern is "wrote 3 blog posts in January and then... life."
- The one rule, quoted as "the only SEO advice that actually matters in 2026": "The people ranking right now are the ones answering their customers' questions. Consistently. On their own website." "That's it. That's the whole thing."
- What it is NOT: "Not schema markup. Not 47 backlinks from guest posts. Not the perfect keyword density."
- Voice and tone of the content: "Just... you. Sharing what you know. The same way you'd explain it to a customer standing in front of you."
- Why blog posts beat social: "Think of it like social media, except instead of a TikTok that disappears in 3 days, a blog post answering a real question keeps ranking for years. Still pulling in leads next year. Same effort. Way better return."
- Step list, verbatim:
  1. "Open your inbox. Look at the last 10 questions a customer asked you... That's your content calendar for the next 2 and a half months."
  2. "Pick one. Answer it on your site. Like you'd explain it to a friend over coffee."
  3. "Do it next week. And the week after."
- Cadence rule: "Do it once per week."
- Math implied by step 1: 10 questions = 10 weekly posts = about 2.5 months of content. P.S. version: "go open your email and pull the last 5 questions a customer asked you. There's your next month of content, sitting there already."
- Diagnosis of the real blocker: "It's never been about not knowing what to do. It's about doing it when you're already maxed out."
- P.P.S. plugs HeyTony Insiders for personal help.

### 2026-04-16, Google will delete your reviews if you break these rules... (19d97448feadb1a1)

- Context: "Google just dropped some massive updates to their reviews guidelines... and people are pissed."
- Matt's TLDR, verbatim:
  - "you can't give an incentive to leave a review"
  - "customers can't leave staff names in reviews"
  - "you have to ask everyone for reviews. Not just happy customers"
  - "you can't ask for reviews while someone is at your business"
  - "you can't ask them to leave a specific review or mention a product or service"
- Google policy text as quoted in the email (verbatim, full):
  - "Contributions to Google Maps should reflect a genuine experience at a place or business. Rating Manipulation includes Incentivized or Biased Reviews, which are not allowed and will be removed from Maps."
  - "This includes:"
  - "Content that has been posted due to an incentive offered by a business - such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services."
  - "This includes content posted following requests for revision or removal of a review or rating in exchange for an incentive."
  - "Content exhibiting unusual volumes or patterns of review contributions that are indicative of efforts to manipulate a place's rating"
  - "Content that is based on a conflict of interest. A conflict of interest may include current or former employment, a contractual or consultory relationship, or other professional or personal affiliations that demonstrate a conflict of interest (such as industry competitors, familial relationships, etc.)."
  - "We do not allow merchants to:"
  - "Offer incentives - such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review or revision or removal of a negative review."
  - "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers"
  - "When soliciting reviews, merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises, nor should they request that specific content be included."
  - "This includes:"
  - "Merchants requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews"
  - "Merchants requesting that staff solicit reviews that include specific content, including content that identifies a staff member."
  - "We do allow merchants to:"
  - "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."
- Practical implications for review generation (these are the rules as Matt summarized them, not added advice): no review incentives of any kind, no staff quotas, no scripted review content, no "mention our X service" asks, no on-site review requests, no review gating (ask everyone, including unhappy customers).
- P.S.: Matt wrote an SEO book called "Get Found!", "written in plain english at an 8th grade reading level - if you're struggling to understand SEO - I wrote this for you!"
