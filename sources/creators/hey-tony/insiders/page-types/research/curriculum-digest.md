# Curriculum digest (74 lessons)

Source: all 74 plain-text transcripts in `hey-tony-insiders/curriculum/` (starter, newbie, intermediate, advanced, expert, auditFixes, heyKeywordsTutorials, level0-understandingSeo, startHere). Every bullet is a claim, rule, number, step, or example taken from a lesson; the lesson file is cited in brackets. Verbatim quotes are in quotation marks. Prompts and procedures are copied in full.

## 1. Homepage

- The homepage should be about the business name AND the main keyword you want to rank for. On the Lakeport Dental example: "this homepage should be about Lakeport Dental. But in my opinion, I'm like, yeah, welcome to Lakeport Dental. I would also have in here like, you know, St. Catherine's Dental, you know, the main keyword that you want to go for. Because if people are looking up Lakeport Dental, they're going to find you anyways because that's the name of your business." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Homepage title tag: put the brand name at the END of the title tag, the keyword first. Lakeport's title "Lakeport Dental, top rated St. Catherine's dentist office" should be flipped: "Lakeport Dental, which I would actually put at the end of your title tag, top rated St. Catherine's dentist office." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- A homepage that talks about several cities is NOT one topic. Lakeport's homepage talks about St. Catharines (a city), Niagara (a different city and region, "Niagara Dental Services"), and Lakeport Dental, with no H1 tag: "this is a really good example of a page that isn't just about one topic." Keep the homepage to one city / one main topic. [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Homepage word count: "yes, even your homepage should have 2,000 plus words." Pages with approximately 2,000 words tend to rank 1 to 3 or 4. [level0-understandingSeo__word-count.txt]
- The HeyTony homepage has about 1,500 to 2,000 words but does not look word heavy on a quick scroll. Its structure: some intro content, services listed, a testimonial, then FAQs. [level0-understandingSeo__word-count.txt]
- Technique to get homepage word count up without a wall of text: hide content in sliders or dropdown menus. The "our approach to growth marketing" section loads more content under each item when clicked; the FAQ section lists services with more info under each ("Do we do organic social"). "If you're looking for a way to increase the word count on your homepage, I highly suggest doing DropDowns or an FAQ section where you can, you know, give a lot more information." [level0-understandingSeo__word-count.txt]
- Homepage layout goal: "when somebody comes to the site, we want to make sure that they can find the information they're looking for quickly." [level0-understandingSeo__word-count.txt]
- Homepage URL is the one page that does NOT carry a keyword: "URLs should include the focus keyword for that page. Except the homepage that's not going to have your URL is just going to be whatever your website is, dot com or ca or co or whatever." [level0-understandingSeo__urls.txt]
- Homepage services list uses keyword anchor text to each service page: "We offer the following digital marketing services. Website design. That's. This is anchor text... website conversion optimization, that's a link to that page, local SEO that links to that page." Never "learn more" or "click here". [level0-understandingSeo__anchor-text.txt]
- Homepage internal links on HeyTony: the words "neutral vodka" link to that case study; "web design" links to the web design portfolio category; "search engine optimization", "local SEO", "Facebook ads", "Instagram ads", "TikTok ads" each link to that service page, "using those specific words or using images relating to those words." [level0-understandingSeo__internal-linking.txt]
- Every page including the homepage should link to 3 to 5 other pages within the content: "every time they go to a page or linking to like three to five other pages within the content. And that goes for your homepage, that goes for service pages or product pages." [auditFixes__dead-end-page-has-no-outgoing-links-how-to-fix.txt]
- The homepage does not count as a click for crawl depth; any page should be reachable from the homepage in 3 clicks max. [level0-understandingSeo__crawl-depth.txt]
- Google indexes one video per page, "whether it's a blog post or your homepage or whatever it is." [level0-understandingSeo__video.txt]
- Keyword density example implies the homepage / main page should use the keyword several times naturally ("our blueberry pancakes are made with...", "the best ways to enjoy blueberry pancakes"), 3 to 5 times in the example before even writing body copy. [level0-understandingSeo__keyword-density.txt]

## 2. Service pages and money pages

- One service = one page = one main keyword. "Think of every page as a silo." People google "cosmetic dentistry St. Catharines", "dental implants", "dental veneers", "dentures", "preventive dentistry": "they're Googling one of those things. And that allows us to create a page to rank for that specific keyword or that main topic." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Dental implants page example: main keyword "dental implants in St. Catharines"; the H1 "high quality dental implants in St. Catharines" is fine, but the page had no meta description ("which is an issue"), and the heading tags said only "Dental implants": "although this is about one specific topic, they aren't including the full keyword anywhere. So that is likely going to impact their ranking." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- "in the URL, which is very important, you should have the keyword that you're trying to rank for. So dental implants. St. Catherine's." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- White fillings page as the model service page: "all this content is mentioning white fillings. We're not talking about dental implants... So they have Q&As, they have FAQs there. Book an appointment, that's fine. But this page is all about white fillings." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Do not have more than one page about a topic: "that's what we call cannibalizing... Google's not going to likely not going to be able to find that specific page on your website because it's conflicted." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Service page missing H1 example (3D Dental Imaging St. Catharines): title tag "3D Dental Imaging", no meta description, only H2 tags. "heading one tags tell Google and people what the page is about. And heading two tags support that main topic. So these are supporting a topic that doesn't exist." Put the H1 at the top: "3D imaging and CBCT dental scans in St. Catharines Ontario. Like, I would just make that the heading one tag." [3-intermediate__3-fixing-audit-issues-light-keyword-research.txt]
- A service keyword with zero search volume is still worth a page: "this could actually be a really good opportunity for us because there's no keyword difficulty... if that's a service that you offer, you can still rank for that." [3-intermediate__3-fixing-audit-issues-light-keyword-research.txt]
- Do keyword research for every page: "you should do this for every single page on your site," searching the page's keyword to find the main keyword and secondary keywords. [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Easier service keywords beat the city-wide head term: "dentist St. Catherine" is 1,000 searches and very hard; "emergency dentist" or "pediatric dentist" are "way easier to rank for"; "wisdom teeth removal" 880 searches, easy. "I'd rather go for that easier keyword." [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Never put "near me" in your keyword or on the page: "if you are local and you see near me in there, don't include near me in the keyword. That's not going to help you. That just looks spammy as hell. You're just going to use like Sedation Dentistry." [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Don't use neighborhood/area names like "Garden City Dental" or "Martindale Dental" as keywords; "choose keywords that make sense for your business, that aren't crazy difficult to rank for." [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Service page keyword method (plumber in Hamilton): pick the keyword you want to rank for even if difficult (KD 60), then look for variations to use as secondary keywords on the same page: "plumber in Hamilton, maybe plumbers in Hamilton, Ontario". [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-research.txt]
- Split out distinct services into their own pages: "Emergency plumber. I would make that its own page because that is a term people are specifically looking for." "24 hour plumber Hamilton" also flagged as a good keyword. [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-research.txt]
- Spy on the top 10 for your service keyword: "you can look at how many times you're using the keyword, where they're using the keyword, what information they have on that page. So you can use that information to dictate maybe what you have on your page. Because this is what's ranking. That's what Google likes. Maybe do something similar." Check if ranking pages have backlinks; a #1 page with zero backlinks means an easier keyword. [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-research.txt]
- Keyword gap lists give "ideas for blog posts, for service pages"; commercial intent "are probably somebody investigating to buy". [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-gap.txt]
- Model service page structure (HeyTony local SEO page): "it's all about local SEO, how it works, you know, some case studies and stuff like that. And then, you know, we have FAQs on this page as well." [level0-understandingSeo__anchor-text.txt]
- Service pages need at least one external link too: HeyTony's Facebook ads service page links to a Facebook ads support page, "we're linking to, you know, learn more about Facebook ads there... some of our best practices are taken from Facebook." [level0-understandingSeo__external-linking.txt]
- Service page H1 format example: "Website design in Hamilton, Ontario". "The heading one tag is the main topic of that page." The H1 is "usually the biggest text on the page" (Matt is testing a smaller H1 on that one page and it ranks #1, but "typically you only want one heading, one tag per page"). [auditFixes__no-h1-heading-multiple-h1-headings-defined.txt]
- Service page URL pattern praised: sociallyinfused.com/services/website-design, "That's simple, that's succinct. You're telling search engines that it's a service that you provide by having services there and the services website design." [level0-understandingSeo__urls.txt]
- Service page word count: the agency optimizes "about a thousand to fifteen hundred words on every single page"; minimum 500. [auditFixes__low-word-count-how-to-fix.txt]
- Service/money pages get ranked by internal links from blog posts: "every time you write a blog post, you're going to link one time to a service page. Or if you offer products online, you're going to link to a product page and you're going to use the keyword to link to that page that you want that other page to rank for." This alone ranked a competitive page #1 after it sat for a year. [4-advanced__1-the-power-of-blog-posts.txt]
- Service pages should have a featured image set (HeyTony's main services page was missing one, "We should probably have an image there"). [auditFixes__open-graph-issues.txt]
- Open Graph type on a service page showed "article", which is wrong; fixing it requires manual schema saying "this page is actually a service page". [auditFixes__open-graph-issues.txt]
- Schema can tell search engines the page type: "is it a service page, is it a blog post, is it an article, is it a product page". [level0-understandingSeo__schema-markup.txt]
- Content gap on money pages is referenced: "we looked at content gap, analyzing what your competitors are doing that you're not on your money page." [5-expert__backlink-gap-analysis-and-link-building.txt]
- Competitor service pages can be monitored for title tag, word count, meta description, pricing and link changes with Watchdog. [5-expert__5-bonus-spy-on-your-competition.txt]
- Every page, including service pages, must link out to 3 to 5 other pages in the content; product pages can do "here's some related products". [auditFixes__dead-end-page-has-no-outgoing-links-how-to-fix.txt]

## 3. Location / city pages

- A different town gets its own location page: "Plumber in Stoney Creek. So Stony Creek's actually a different location. I might make a location page out of that." [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-research.txt]
- Local businesses include the city in the keyword: "if you're a local business, you probably want to include that local keyword in there like Hamilton or your city." [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-research.txt]
- Right local keyword shape: "wedding photographer Hamilton" or "elopement wedding photographer Hamilton", not "photographer". [2-newbie__1-what-are-keywords.txt]
- Start local keyword research by typing "your industry and then where you're based" into Google and pulling the #1 site's organic keywords. [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Do not mix cities on one page: St. Catharines and Niagara on one homepage is two topics. [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Location pages should follow the service + city heading pattern: "Website design in Hamilton, Ontario" as the single H1. [auditFixes__no-h1-heading-multiple-h1-headings-defined.txt]
- Title tag examples that lead with service + city: "Hamilton Web Design, your local web design company", "Top Web designers in Hamilton 2022 reviews", "Massive Web Design Hamilton Web Design & SEO", "Web Design website Development Hamilton", all with "the focus keyword right at the beginning or very, very close to the beginning." [level0-understandingSeo__title-tags.txt]
- Meta description examples for a city keyword: "Hamilton's award winning web design and development agency" (web design near start, Hamilton as another keyword, web design repeated); "Massive Web Design is an established Hamilton web design firm that specializes in creating unique imagery for web design." [level0-understandingSeo__meta-descriptions.txt]
- "near me" is never part of the on-page keyword: "you will rank for that in your local areas, likely, if you are giving Google local signals" but "dentist near me" is 90,000 searches country-wide and "really hard to rank for". [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Blog posts are the opposite: "I would typically avoid using any location names in here... Avoid these, don't use those or anything with the location unless it's your location. But these are going to be blog posts and you do not need to have your location in your blog post title." [heyKeywordsTutorials__find-questions.txt]
- Local SEO signals that back up city pages: Google Business Profile plus citations (directory listings on Google Maps, Yelp, Apple Maps, Instagram, local directories) with correct business name, phone number, address, link to website, and services. [level0-understandingSeo__local-seo.txt]
- Track local keyword variants in Looker Studio by filtering all queries containing the city word, e.g. "Hamilton", to see "whether people are searching for SEO or digital marketing, marketing or website design or any variation." [5-expert__1-tracking-your-growth.txt]
- Keyword research must be set to your country: "if I search this in the United States, it's going to be very different than if I search this in Canada." [heyKeywordsTutorials__keyword-research.txt]

## 4. Product, collection, category pages

- Blog posts should link to product pages with keyword anchor text: "if you offer products online or products, you're going to link to a product page and you're going to use the keyword to link to that page." [4-advanced__1-the-power-of-blog-posts.txt]
- Product pages convert from blog internal links: Matt spent $30,000 after a blog post about "can you start a community on Slack" linked to a product page; "if you have internal links and you have products like under a hundred bucks, people are probably going to be a lot more likely to buy that or fill out the form." [4-advanced__1-the-power-of-blog-posts.txt]
- Product pages should link to related products to avoid dead ends: "on a product page it'll be like here's some related products like that keeps Google on your website, that keeps people on your website." [auditFixes__dead-end-page-has-no-outgoing-links-how-to-fix.txt]
- Product reviews are a ranking factor: "especially on E commerce sites, so on your products you should definitely be asking your customers to leave reviews once they've received the product"; "Google will look and say this product has you know, 5,000, you know, five star reviews. It must be a good product so we should show it on top." Needs "a system to monitor that". [level0-understandingSeo__reviews.txt]
- Product schema: product description, pricing, "how many reviews your products have, or what's the average review of your product". [level0-understandingSeo__schema-markup.txt]
- If you sell online products, do keyword research on "whatever that main product is that you sell". [3-intermediate__2-finding-keywords.txt]
- Outranking Amazon on product topics: "you can ignore Amazon because they're going to have shit SEO anyways. And they're only ranking because they have that product and they have so much authority. But we can actually outrank Amazon by having better content." [4-advanced__3-creating-a-blog-outline.txt]
- Category pages with low word count are acceptable if you do not need them to rank (HeyTony's Facebook ads case-study category, reached via Work > filter). To improve: add words at the top, "here's some of our work that we've done on Facebook ads. Here's the strategy we take." More items in the category also add words. [auditFixes__low-word-count-how-to-fix.txt]
- Category pages do not need featured/OG images "because these aren't getting indexed anyways." [auditFixes__open-graph-issues.txt]
- A portfolio/work category page tells search engines its topic via the anchor text pointing to it: "work category, web design... now Search engines know that this is about web design." [level0-understandingSeo__internal-linking.txt]
- Blog categories: "I would recommend having 10 categories max... Never set it to uncategorized because that's just, you know, a catch all and that shows that you're lazy." A post may sit in two relevant categories (SEO and website). [4-advanced__4-uploading-publishing-your-content.txt]
- Category page H2s change by themselves as content is added (seen in Watchdog diffs). [5-expert__5-bonus-spy-on-your-competition.txt]
- Every page is its own unique topic; do not let two pages share the same title, meta or H1 (duplicate tags), or Google "may not show any of them." [level0-understandingSeo__duplicate-tags.txt]

## 5. About / team pages

- Nothing in the curriculum addresses about or team pages specifically. Only the universal rules apply (see section 15): one H1 matching the page topic, title tag and meta description present, at least one external link, 3 to 5 internal links in the content, a featured image, no dead ends.

## 6. Contact / quote / booking pages

- Contact pages are allowed to be thin: "Another example of a page that could have low content is your contact page... This page is okay to not have a lot of content. Google's going to index this as your contact page." [auditFixes__low-word-count-how-to-fix.txt]
- "you should have your contact page in your navigation menu so that people can find it." [auditFixes__low-word-count-how-to-fix.txt]
- Optional word-count lift: "Google knows that contact pages don't typically have a lot of words on it, but if you wanted to, you could add some FAQs here to increase that word count." [auditFixes__low-word-count-how-to-fix.txt]
- Contact sits in the main nav alongside Work, Services, Blog on the HeyTony site. [level0-understandingSeo__internal-linking.txt]
- A form page still needs an H1: on a sign-up form page with no H1, add a text block at the top, set it to Heading 1 ("sign up form page"), "I always have that at the top." [auditFixes__no-h1-heading-multiple-h1-headings-defined.txt]
- Abandoned form pages with a random-number URL and no purpose should be deleted rather than linked. [auditFixes__orphaned-page-no-internal-links-point-to-it.txt]
- "Book an appointment" CTA on a service page is fine and does not break the one-topic rule. [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Thank-you pages reached only after a form submission are legitimate orphan pages. [auditFixes__orphaned-page-no-internal-links-point-to-it.txt]
- Open Graph "type" can describe "is it an article, is it, you know, contact page". [auditFixes__open-graph-issues.txt]

## 7. Pricing pages

- Nothing in the curriculum teaches how to build a pricing page. Adjacent points:
- "how much does SEO cost" is a high-intent question: "they're probably really close to pulling the trigger and hiring an SEO agency. And they're like price shopping." Write content for it. [2-newbie__3-writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt.txt]
- Verify AI pricing claims before publishing: "hourly 20 to 200 an hour for SEO? It's probably not 20 to 200 an hour, right? Project based. We don't do any." Remove anything that does not make sense. [2-newbie__3-writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt.txt]
- Competitor pricing changes can be watched with Watchdog ("if they're changing their pricing, if they're doing anything"). [5-expert__5-bonus-spy-on-your-competition.txt]
- Pages with prices or packages found in backlink-gap lists are often paid directories; "you might not want to do all these." [heyKeywordsTutorials__backlinks-gap.txt]

## 8. FAQ pages and FAQ blocks

- FAQ dropdowns are the recommended way to raise word count on the homepage: "if you look down here at the FAQs, we did the same kind of thing. So we listed out the services that we offer there. Do we do organic, social, you know, each one of these has more information." [level0-understandingSeo__word-count.txt]
- FAQs can pad a thin contact page. [auditFixes__low-word-count-how-to-fix.txt]
- Service pages carry Q&As / FAQs (white fillings page; HeyTony local SEO page "we have FAQs on this page as well"). [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt] [level0-understandingSeo__anchor-text.txt]
- "Turning your FAQs into blog posts. This is one way to create content ideas for your website." [level0-understandingSeo__internal-linking.txt]
- Question-style H2s can win rich snippets: "when you use H2 tags and when you're answering questions, Google might show whatever your, you know, first paragraph is or that relevant information on a page to the user right in the search results... it could help you jump right to number one." [level0-understandingSeo__heading-tags.txt]
- Sources of real questions: answerthepublic.com (3 free searches/day with an account, 1 without; use list view; copy three questions) [2-newbie__2-how-to-find-keywords-your-customers-are-searching-for.txt]; HeyKeywords Find Questions scans Reddit, Quora, Facebook and People Also Ask and clusters them (manicures: "why do some nail salons charge more for manicures?", "what should I look for in a clean, high quality nail salon?", "how long do I typically wait at a good nail salon?"), "most of these are really easy to rank for." [heyKeywordsTutorials__find-questions.txt]
- Looker Studio "content optimization ideas" quadrants (why / what / how / can): if a question like "why can't I post on Instagram" has 1,500 impressions, "maybe I should write a blog post or add that to the page that's ranking for that keyword." [5-expert__1-tracking-your-growth.txt]

## 9. Blog index and blog post pages

### Blog index
- Do not hide the blog: the dentist site "are actually hiding their blog... last time they posted was 2018." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- Blog belongs in the main navigation (Work, Services, Contact, Blog). [level0-understandingSeo__internal-linking.txt]
- The main blog page should have a featured image because "somebody might share it." [auditFixes__open-graph-issues.txt]
- The main blog page naturally carries many images, one per post card. [level0-understandingSeo__images.txt]
- Max 10 blog categories, never "uncategorized". [4-advanced__4-uploading-publishing-your-content.txt]

### Why blogs (strategy that shapes the page)
- Seven reasons: increased traffic and trust; internal linking opportunities; unlock harder keywords; organic backlinks; the avalanche / compounding effect; consumer trust and recognition; topical authority ("Google wants to see experts in a specific field"). [4-advanced__1-the-power-of-blog-posts.txt]
- "I'm not saying to go out and write like hundreds of blog posts. That's going to come off as spammy. It's a long game with SEO." [4-advanced__1-the-power-of-blog-posts.txt]
- Every blog post, like every page, is about one main topic; bad examples: "could you be ruining your teeth with these five bad habits? Terrible keyword. No keyword there. Five reasons to get back to school. Dental checkup. Terrible topic. Nobody's looking that up." [3-intermediate__4-every-page-1-main-topic.txt]
- A blog post's main title is the exact question people google. [2-newbie__3-writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt.txt]
- Blog post H1 example: "How long should a URL be for SEO?" "That is the main topic. That is what this page is about. And that's what your heading one tag or your H1 tag should be." [auditFixes__no-h1-heading-multiple-h1-headings-defined.txt]

### First blog post procedure with ChatGPT (verbatim) [2-newbie__3-writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt.txt]
- Pick one of the three questions copied from answerthepublic. Prompt 1: "I'm writing a blog post about [insert one of the three questions]. Ask me questions you'd need to know to write the most complete blog post about this topic. Make sure you ask questions about my expertise, experience, opinions, case studies, etc. So that the blog post is truly unique and me."
- Answer its questions by dictating (voice button) or on your phone in the same conversation: "number one, you know, this is the answer to that question. Number 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10."
- Always start with an outline, never the whole post: "do you want me to turn them into a structured blog draft now? Yes, please. Or you could say, you know, write me a blog outline with basic details filled in." "I always start with an outline because I don't want it to write or Chat GPT to write an entire piece of content. That's going to be garbage."
- Copy the outline into a blank document, remove anything wrong or that does not make sense (e.g. "hourly 20 to 200 an hour for SEO?", "Fiverr slash upwork"), optionally leave notes, re-upload: "using the notes in this, like now you can write this piece of content." Or: "I updated the draft. Here's the draft. Please write a blog post using that draft."
- Read the result, check accuracy. For any section that is off: "this doesn't sound like me. It should be more jokey and sarcastic" and re-paste that section. Work "one section at a time... highlight a section, paste it back in, tell it literally what you want it to say instead. Or like, hey, you forgot X, Y and Z."
- Copy the final into a blank document and "remove all these lines that go across here because that is very obviously chatgpt."
- Paste onto your website "as a blog post with the main title of the blog post, the exact question that people are googling."
- Funnel logic inside the post: top-of-funnel question ("when was hot sauce invented") can end with "check out our hot sauces"; "you can help lead them down the funnel with your content."
- "don't go crazy and do a bunch of these. Just do one." Post the link in the community.

### Finding the blog keyword [4-advanced__2-finding-keywords-for-your-blog-posts.txt]
- Use keyword gap / content gap against a big industry leader (Neil Patel), not the shop down the street: "the bigger the competitor you could put in here, the more keyword opportunities you're going to find."
- Filter KD 0 to 30; a newer site should look for 14 or under, even 0 to 15.
- Pick a keyword you can genuinely write about ("What is Open Graph", 320/mo, KD 25).
- Open the keyword, read keyword variations / related keywords, and collect 5 to 10 secondary keywords: "I would choose maybe like five to 10. I wouldn't necessarily go any more than that. Just because you don't want to have too many keywords on the page, you're not going to be able to use them." Export CSV with keyword, volume, difficulty.
- Zero-difficulty variations are worth including even if you never rank for the head term: "I'm still getting 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. So that's 140, 150 potential visitors."

### Blog outline procedure (verbatim steps) [4-advanced__3-creating-a-blog-outline.txt]
- Put keywords in a sheet: the top one is "the primary keyword", the rest "secondary keywords".
- In the keyword tool, open the primary keyword, set the country, scroll to who ranks 1 to 5. "Referring domains is how many unique sites that are linking to you, and backlinks are how many times those sites are linking to you." "I want to see that I can rank on the first page with one, maybe two backlinks or zero backlinks. If you find one that's ranking, you know, number one, and it has zero referring domains here... that's going to be a really good opportunity for you."
- Open the top three or four results. Ignore YouTube; Medium is fine; ignore Amazon.
- Install the free Page Audit Chrome extension (pageaudit.com > browser extension).
- On each top page: click Page Audit, go to heading tags, copy ALL heading tags, paste into a blank document. Repeat for every top page, even if some headings are irrelevant ("mobile app or charity").
- Note that short content can rank: later.com's post was "really short" and on page one.
- Clean up: delete anything that doesn't make sense (consent cookies, privacy centers, "share this article"); keep anything related ("Open graph. How to test and debug", "Open graph tags for meta and Twitter"); keep "Want help setting up" type sections if you can offer that.
- Structure the cleaned list: Introduction; main sections as H2 ("basic open graph metadata"); sub-points as H3; finer points as H4 "or just a bulleted list".
- Add extra heading tags for information the competitors missed.
- Paste the keywords at the bottom as part of the ChatGPT prompt: "This is the primary keyword: [keyword]" and "These are secondary keywords: [list]". "This is for ChatGPT to use."

### Uploading and publishing procedure (verbatim steps) [4-advanced__4-uploading-publishing-your-content.txt]
- Copy the ChatGPT content under the outline in your document and keep it: "It's actually a good idea to keep a copy of all of this stuff because if the site, if your site goes down for some reason, or if, you know, an intern goes in and deletes a bunch of... stuff by accident, you have backups."

- Remove the word "introduction" from the body ("obviously it's. The introduction is the beginning of the blog post").
- Double check everything, fix spacing, "Make sure that anything that should be a heading two tag is a heading two tag. And then same thing with heading three tags." "Final thoughts" is allowed but can read as AI.
- Save draft. Preview: "Always preview it before you publish it." Fix spacing; turn code examples into code sections so they stand out.

"we want to show Google that we're not just making stuff up"; "you want at least one of those on every page of your site. And it's fine if it's at the top. I prefer it to be at the top. ### Keyword density
- Definition: "the amount of times you're using that keyword in relation to the total amount of words on that page... 10 times on a page that has a thousand words, your keyword density will be 1%." [level0-understandingSeo__keyword-density.txt]
- "there's no steadfast rule for what percentage of keyword density you should have"; 10% is "crazy high" but if that page ranks #1 "they're doing something right". Case by case. [level0-understandingSeo__keyword-density.txt]
- Using the keyword once is too little: a blueberry pancakes page would naturally use it 3, 4 or 5 times in headers alone. - Variations count toward density: "blueberry stacked pancakes or pancakes with blueberries... blueberry pancake versus blueberry pancakes. That's Totally fine." [level0-understandingSeo__keyword-density.txt]
