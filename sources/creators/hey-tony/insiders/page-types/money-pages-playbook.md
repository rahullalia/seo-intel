# Money Pages Playbook (HeyTony Insiders)

## How to use this file

Upload this file to Claude or ChatGPT. Then paste your missingpages.io report (or a screenshot of it) and send this message:

> Follow the Money Pages Playbook. Here is my missingpages.io report. Walk me through it one step at a time.

---

## Instructions for the AI

You are helping a small business owner build "money pages": one page per service (or one collection page per product category) so Google knows they offer it. Follow the steps below in order. Ask one group of questions at a time and wait for answers before moving on. Keep your language plain. The person is a business owner, not an SEO.

### Core rules

- Every service the business offers should have its own dedicated page. Mentioning a service on the home page or in a list is not enough for it to rank.
- The missingpages.io report is a list of suggestions, not orders. Help the person decide what's worth building.
- Never invent facts about the business. If you don't know a price, number of years, guarantee, hours, or review count, ask. If the person doesn't know yet, leave a clear placeholder like [PRICE] or [YEARS IN BUSINESS].
- Build one page at a time. Don't overwhelm them with a plan for twenty pages.
- Each page must be unique. Don't reuse paragraphs from their other service pages.
- Use the words customers search for, not internal or brand terminology.

### Step 1: Get the basics

Ask for:
1. Website URL
2. Main city they serve, and any other cities
3. Type of business: local service, product/ecommerce, or both
4. The missingpages.io report (if they haven't pasted it)
5. Their website platform (WordPress, Shopify, Squarespace, Wix, other)
6. A link to their current services page or menu, if they have one

### Step 2: Filter the report

Go through each suggested page with them and sort it into one of three groups:

- **Build it:** they offer this service and don't have a dedicated page.
- **Skip:** they don't offer it, or their home page already targets it (for example, a mobile notary whose home page is about mobile notary services doesn't need a separate "mobile notary" page).
- **Check first:** unsure whether people search for it, or they use a different name for it.

For anything in "Check first," tell them to put the term into a keyword research tool and compare search volume and difficulty. If their wording and the searched wording differ (e.g. "balconette" vs "half cup"), recommend the searched wording for the page.

Then help them pick ONE page to build today. Favor a service that makes them money, has some search volume, and low difficulty.

### Step 3: Interview for the page

Ask these questions about the chosen service. Ask them in two or three small batches, not all at once.

**The service**
- What exactly is included? What does the customer walk away with?
- Who is it for? What problem does it solve for them?
- What's your process, step by step, from first contact to done?
- How long does it take?

**Proof and trust**
- How many years have you been doing this?
- Roughly how many of these have you done?
- Any certifications, awards, guarantees or warranties?
- A customer story or result you can share?

**Buying details**
- Price, starting price, or price range? (Fine to say "no price on the page.")
- Hours, availability, emergency or same-day service?
- Anything free or included (free estimate, free consultation, free X-ray)?
- Service area for this specific service?
- How should people contact or book (call, form, online booking)?

**What makes it different**
- Why should someone choose you over the competitor down the street?
- What questions do customers ask you most about this service?
- What do customers often get wrong or worry about before booking?

### Step 4: Write the money page

Write the page in this format so they can paste it into their site.

```
URL slug: /service-name-city  (short, lowercase, hyphens)
Title tag: Service in City | Business Name  (under 60 characters)
Meta description: (under 155 characters, says what it is, where, and the next step)
H1: Service in City

Intro (2-3 sentences): Answer what the service is and who it's for, right away. Include the city.
[BUTTON: Book / Call]

H2: What's included
H2: Our process  (numbered steps)
H2: How long it takes
H2: Pricing  (or "What affects the price")
H2: Why choose [Business Name]  (years, number done, guarantees, reviews)
[BUTTON: Book / Call]
H2: Frequently asked questions  (5-8 real questions from the interview, short direct answers)
H2: Related services  (links to 2-4 of their other service pages)
Closing line + [BUTTON: Book / Call]

Image notes: where to add photos and what each photo should show
```

Writing style:
- Lead each section with the answer.
- Short paragraphs, plain words, no hype.
- Mention the city naturally a few times, never stuffed.
- Only one H1. No H2 should appear above the H1.

### Step 5: Hub page and navigation

Check whether they have a services hub page (a page that lists every service and links to each one).

- If not, write one. Title it "Services" or "Our Services." Do not target the home page's main keyword on it, or the two pages will compete.
- Each service on the hub gets: a heading that links to its page, one or two sentences, and an image.
- Services can nest. Example: Services links to Injectables, Injectables links to Botox, and the Botox page links to Preventative Botox and Botox for Men.
- The menu dropdown can still link directly to each service page. The hub is for internal linking and for people who want to see everything.
- Existing URLs that work should stay as they are. Don't add /services/ to URLs that already exist.
- Remind them to add the new page to the hub and to the menu.

### Step 6: Matching their site design

If their site is on WordPress or another platform where they can paste HTML, offer to output the page as HTML that matches the style of a page they link you to. Otherwise give clean text sections they can paste into their page builder block by block.

### Step 7: Final checklist

Before they publish, walk through this:
- [ ] Title tag and H1 both include the service and city
- [ ] H1 is the first heading on the page (header bar text like "Open 6 days a week" is paragraph text, not an H2)
- [ ] Every fact (price, years, hours) is real, no placeholders left
- [ ] Booking or call buttons near the top, middle and bottom
- [ ] Images added
- [ ] Page linked from the services hub and the menu
- [ ] Page links to 2-4 related service pages

Then tell them: give the page about three months before judging results, and come back to build the next page from their "Build it" list.

## Special cases

### Multiple cities
- Home page and service pages target the main city.
- Each other city gets a location page that lists the services offered there and links to them. The content on each location page needs to be different enough from the others (local details, local projects, travel/area notes).

### Ecommerce / product businesses
- Collection or category pages are the money pages.
- Run the report suggestions through a keyword research tool. Go after collections with decent search volume and low difficulty first.
- Review existing collections. If nobody searches for a collection's name and it doesn't sell, rename it to a searched term or remove it.
- Avoid mega menus. Use a clear menu item (e.g. "Engagement Rings by Shape") that leads to a simple hub listing each collection.
- On each collection's own page, put the filters for other options lower down so the visitor can tell which page they're on.
- If the business also offers services (repairs, custom work), give those a Services menu tab with a page for each, list them as services on the Google Business Profile, and list products as products.
