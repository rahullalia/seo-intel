# Portfolio, gallery, and case-study pages

*Part of the HeyTony page-type research (`page-types/`). The rules every page inherits are in `00-rules-for-every-page.md`; the citation key, sources, and file map are in `README.md`.*

## What it is for and what it targets

- Built for conversion and proof, not to rank: "the portfolio is not necessarily something that is going to rank. It's more for conversion," and "in 99% of cases" these pages aren't found through Google on their own. HeyTony's own /work and case-study pages rank only for the branded term "hey Tony" [calls 078, 035, 071].
- The job is proof of work: "if you say I'm a photographer but I can't see your work, then that's really not saying much" [call 011].
- Exception: a gallery or venue page can rank if it targets a real keyword instead of a proper noun, e.g. venue pages titled "Art Gallery of Hamilton wedding photography examples," never named after a client, nobody searches "Sarah and Michael's wedding" [calls 096, 112].

## What goes on it, top to bottom (the structure)

- Case studies live on the money page itself as proof, plus their own archive (HeyTony runs separate custom post types for work, services, testimonials, and case studies) [calls 117, 014]. One project page: project name as H1, a real-volume keyword as an H2, internal links to related projects, a closing CTA ("ready to get more traffic, fill out the contact form") [calls 047, 112].
- Gallery-style pages, each job as its own page with one image and a short paragraph, can carry most of a site's clicks and impressions [calls 013, 074].

## Layout and design

- Show the work before the praise: move video or photo proof above testimonials so visitors "see what they're getting into" first [call 032].
- Don't repeat photo sections down the homepage; move the bulk of images to a dedicated gallery page in the nav instead [call 049].
- A "portfolio" page that only lists divisions is "useless" for SEO and pulls the GBP link away from money pages; don't point GBP at an unoptimized portfolio site either [calls 101, 094].

## Visuals

- Real photos beat stock, and stock runs "about the same" as AI here; real job photos (one HVAC client uses 172,000) beat stock outright [calls 067, 051].
- Crisp embedded video with sound is the real showcase, an autoplaying compressed background video "isn't really doing you a service" [call 103]. Host on YouTube over Vimeo unless you're a filmmaker ("the world's second largest search engine") and turn off related-video clutter and autoplay [call 042].
- Gallery images stay under 1000px wide except banners, source under 2K resolution [playbook IV Images]. Carousels are a liability, Matt dislikes them, and Wix-style galleries "take too much juice" [calls 020, 064].

## Length, keyword density, and on-page SEO

- Titles, alt text, and captions matter more here since the photography or project is the work itself, but don't force a keyword onto every image, just describe what's in it [call 065].
- Thin, image-heavy pages can take copy above the grid toward 400 to 500 words, or stay thin if only someone searching that exact project will ever find it [call 010; course: low word count].
- Never use a graphic as the H1 (Google can't read baked-in text); logos, signage, and animation should never carry heading tags either, one audit found 13 H1s firing from exactly that [playbook III 2.4, IV Heading Structure].

## CTAs and conversion

- Case studies close with a direct ask: "ready to get more traffic, blah, blah, fill out the contact form" [call 112].
- Proof sections on money pages, "websites they've designed," real-world results, and case studies, function as trust proof, not decoration [call 081].
- Link a specific portfolio piece from inside a related blog post ("we just did this for [Children's Hospital]") rather than hoping the portfolio page gets found on its own [call 078].

## Links, indexing, and where it sits in the site

- Internally link related pieces to each other and request indexing manually, these pages rarely get found organically on their own [call 047].
- A default "clients"/"portfolio" archive competing with a hand-built portfolio page causes duplicate metadata and canonical conflicts; style and keep the default archive, or 301 it and confirm nothing still links to it [call 014]. A renamed portfolio section whose canonical tags weren't updated leaves old URLs 404ing while canonicals still point at them; fix per-page in the SEO plugin, never by hacking permalinks [call 007].
- Archive dead pages (old listings, discontinued projects); keep only what generates traffic, ranks, or holds backlinks, 301 the rest [calls 047, 074].

## Don'ts

- Don't expect a portfolio or case-study page to rank on its own, it's a conversion and proof surface first [calls 078, 035, 071].
- Don't title project pages after people's names or one-off proper nouns nobody searches [call 112].
- Don't let a default archive template compete with a hand-built portfolio page for the same URL space [call 014].
- Don't use a graphic as an H1, and don't lean on carousels for photos or reviews [playbook III 2.4; call 020].

## From the calls: live examples

- Katya, a Quebec graphic designer: 12 project pages indexed, 39 not; the fix per page was a real-volume keyword, an H1/H2 structure, and internal links between related projects [call 047].
- A vehicle-wrap company's 25-year-old site had every past job as its own portfolio-style post; the rebuild turned those pages into service pages, keeping only the ones already earning traffic, rank, or backlinks [call 074].

## Where the sources are thin or disagree

- Whether to add text to a thin, image-heavy project page is situational: add copy "if there's just a lot of pictures," but equally fine to leave it alone if "that's not going to be a page that is bringing new people into your site randomly" [call 010].
- Whether a case study belongs on a Page or a Post is decided by search intent, not a fixed rule: one framing wants it to "stick out and maybe be pages on your website and not posts" [calls 112, 116], while another calls a client's session posts "basically case study pages" without moving them out of the blog [call 066].
