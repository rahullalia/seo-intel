# Location, city, and service-area pages

*Part of the HeyTony page-type research (`page-types/`). The rules every page inherits are in `00-rules-for-every-page.md`; the citation key, sources, and file map are in `README.md`.*

A location page is a money page built for one service in one place: "Location page is mainly for targeting one specific service plus one specific city area. So you can optimize for that keyword and capture high intent searches. Give an example SEO agency in Toronto versus SEO agency in Hamilton." Matt's verdict on the format is blunt: "Location pages work. We do them for our clients when they call for it." The whole method comes down to picking the right city and service pair by keyword research, writing genuinely local content for each one (never a template with the city swapped), tying each page to a Google Business Profile where one exists, putting them behind a visible Locations hub, and publishing them at a steady one-or-two-a-week pace until the business is full. [calls 066, 070]

## What it is for and what it targets

- Service businesses, not product businesses: "location pages are much more important for service based businesses than they are for product based businesses." Each city has its own keyword difficulty and volume (Toronto vs Hamilton), so "to go for both, we'll need two separate pages", one for "SEO Agency in Toronto" and one for "SEO Agency in Hamilton". A product business builds them only if you "service specific areas or you're in stores or boutiques"; a nationwide web host gets nothing from them "unless you're going for a very specific area". [calls 082, 116]
- They are money pages: "service + city combos" sit in the money-page bucket with services, industries served, and product or pricing pages, and get the same audit, tag rewrite, body rewrite, and incoming-link discipline. "Those are called location pages. And absolutely you guys should be doing those, but you make sure that you do service those areas." [email 2026-08-04; calls 002, 117]
- Without the page you will not rank: "If you have specific pages for each location, that's how you rank for those. And if you don't, then it's very, very difficult." An "areas we serve" list or a map graphic of Colorado "doesn't do good"; "if you don't have a page for the keyword you're trying to go for, like, you're not going to rank for it." [call 061]
- Adding a location creates a whole new keyword: "Chicago newborn photographer versus Evanston newborn photographer, those be totally different pages." "We buy junk cars Dallas" and "...Houston" never cannibalize "as long as we're not shooting for the same keyword and that the content doesn't feel the same"; "car recycling, neighborhood... That's like the full keyword", the service part can stay the same across pages "but the neighborhood is what changes the keyword. That's not cannibalization." [calls 069, 107, 116; playbook VI 2.1]
- One page = one keyword = one service = one location. Never build a page for the same city targeting two services ("you're not even getting a slice"), and never combine cities: "If I land on a page about bathroom remodeling in Hamilton, I don't want to see another city mentioned there... that also confuses Google"; a franchisee landing page listing several cities should be split into individual location pages. [playbook III 3.5; call 075]
- One service per satellite city, chosen by keyword research: "with location pages, I don't believe in doing, like a bunch of the same location for different services. I just choose one that is the easiest to rank for and I go after that." Mention the second service in passing, "maybe have a H2 tag somewhere or just text that says, like, I also do emceeing." Photographer version: in Skokie "I wouldn't have a family one and a newborn page. I'd only pick one ... and then pick a different location to do the other one." Tarot version: "relationship tarot reading Oakville", "shadow work tarot reading Markham", "It's just keyword research." HVAC version: one keyword per location page, usually the main service (the $350 job, not the $100 add-on). [calls 014, 038, 116]
- Home-base exception: the flagship market can carry a separate page per service ("Chicago Newborn Photographer" and "Chicago Family Photographer"; on the main city page "all of those services can be for that location"). A multi-location therapy practice keeps the main site on the biggest location, builds "therapists in Vienna, Virginia" listing the services offered there (PTSD, CBT), and adds sub-pages for individual services only if volume justifies it ("you possibly could just rank with like therapist Vienna"); medication management and testing get a mention on the location page "for now". [playbook III 3.5, VI 2.2; calls 014, 004]
- Which phrasing per city is also research: "web design" vs "website design" (used interchangeably, check each area), "counselor" vs "therapist" per area, "we buy junk cars" in one city and "sell my car fast" in another, pizza delivery Hamilton / Ancaster / Dundas with "Detroit style pizza" where that variant is easier; if "tarot reading / reader" carries the most volume, the location pages may be versions of the homepage keyword. [calls 005, 004, 116, 038]
- Do not build every location x service combination: 25 cities x 6 event types = 66 pages is "too many pages" and "crazy... You're not going to be able to say a hundred percent new things on each one of those pages. Most of them probably won't get indexed." Each location page gets a section listing the event types whose buttons go to the generic event-type pages ("comedian for HOAs in Florida"); build a combo page only when research shows people search "Boynton Beach HOA comedian", and "expect maybe five of them total." A ~200-page HVAC service x city matrix: "You need 100% original, my friend... Pigs get fat and hogs get slaughtered... that is being a hog... It might hurt the site." Also ask "Could they take on all the work?" Comedian with 9 to 11 cities: "I wouldn't make like a bazillion pages" (no page per client type per city). [calls 049, 058, 116, 067; playbook VI 2.2]
- City size sets the unit. A ~500,000-population city (Hamilton, ~15,000 small businesses) justifies targeting the whole city; a 3 to 4 million metro (Toronto) is too big, go neighborhood ("junk removal Leslieville", "landscape lighting Montreal"); "If I was a dentist... I'm not trying to go after the entire city of Toronto. I'm trying to go after a neighborhood"; a patient wants a dentist within five km; a ~2 million metro (Cincinnati): "make location pages for the suburbs" based on what people search; Montreal "boroughs" work as location pages; DC realtor: "the top 20 neighborhoods", "Realtor in neighborhood 1, realtor in neighborhood 2"; Toronto tarot: roughly 40 suburbs, one page each; realtors break pages down "by neighborhoods", east/west, community names, "by the beach", because volume is lower but far easier for a low-authority site. [playbook III 3.5, VI 2.2; calls 119, 075, 071, 044, 020, 038, 121]
- Neighborhood pages in a big city need authority first: "movers Toronto" is 62% difficulty and the North York / Leslieville / Queen West / Yorkville pages are "a really great keyword" but need blog content and topical authority before they move. [call 101]
- Zip and PIN codes: no, "people aren't typing in zip codes", they type the city name. County pages only when the keyword tool shows the county is searched ("Indian River" had almost nothing, so no page); a mortgage broker with pages for all 11 cities in his county should not extend to all 94 counties unless data shows viability: "if there's a county that has population 500 and 85% of them are searching mortgage broker near me... it would be viable"; if nobody searches it, "it adds kind of like a waste of space on your site." [calls 020, 103, 039]
- Spell it the way people type it: "St." not "Saint" for Port St. Lucie (all the volume is on "St."), "Boca Raton" not "South Florida", "Cincinnati" not "Ohio", specific cities not "GTA"; "Orange county, it is very broad", so page per city ("sign language interpreter Irvine"). Elliot's post-update nuance: keep location keywords very specific, "medical sign language interpreter Orange County" or "...Irvine County, California." [calls 103, 058, 071, 006, 040, 039; playbook III 3.5]
- Prefer small towns and suburbs over the big nearby city. "Stouffville is such a small place. Nobody is probably going after that keyword"; "think about it as a user... I'm getting married in King City. I'm looking for a King City photographer"; "Toronto wedding photographer" is "multimillion dollars to rank for", so "narrow down to specific cities... to the point of neighborhoods even." Toronto has ~50,000 small businesses, so a Mississauga agency pushing a Toronto page "might just dilute everything"; Vaughan, King City, Stouffville "might have no search volume... 10", but "nobody's going after all those small ones." "The ones that we've had the most success with is we go after the small towns"; a florist delivering to a town of 5,000 next to a big city: "I would rather go after that keyword." Florida cities of 83,000, 66,000, 58,000: "am I doing the right thing by creating a page for each city? Yes, yes." Expect "one or two clicks a month from each of those, but they'll be much more qualified." [calls 006, 007, 088, 038, 014]
- Why the big city is hard: a location page usually cannot outrank a genuine local competitor's homepage (their de-facto money page, most-backlinked page) without dedicated backlinks; Toronto competitors also get local publication links; around Toronto "40 or 50 small towns" each have a wedding photographer with a GBP "only optimizing for there", so outside-the-city pages "might be a little bit more difficult." Alternative for photographers: venue pages you have shot ("Ancaster Mills sessions") that double as galleries. [playbook VI 2.2; calls 004, 014]
- Counter-case: a photographer who moved to a suburb was told to target "wedding photographer Hamilton", not the tiny town, "because Hamilton's a much bigger place"; a web designer an hour from Orlando can make an Orlando page ("you're not technically saying you're in Orlando, you're just saying web design Orlando"), targeting "Orlando web design company" because "Orlando web design" is 1,300 volume at ~53 difficulty (needs DA ~53+, she had 4), with small wins like "Cocoa Beach web design" (~20 searches) to build momentum; a studio-town photographer adds a separate page for the bigger nearby town (Schaumburg). [calls 096, 085, 058]
- Zero-volume local keywords are still worth a page when difficulty is near zero: "15% of Google searches are brand new every single day"; "somebody might look up that keyword in that small town and then you're the only one"; higher population usually means some volume, so confirm city + keyword first; national KD can mislead (a term can show KD 88 nationally with no single city carrying that volume). "Sell your house fast Delaware Ohio" had 7 difficulty and the #1 result had zero referring domains: "this tells me I can rank without getting backlinks." [calls 038, 117, 082, 119; playbook III 3.5, VI 1.2]
- Don't take the city list from an AI: run each city through a keyword tool ("North Hollywood electrician"), find the easiest, build those first; decide from data, "not necessarily based on what Claude's saying." [calls 073, 039]
- Too small or too new to rank at city level: target state or province keywords first, then build pages for the top 10 to 20 highest-population cities (West Virginia agency: "the top 10 places population wise in the state"). Cleaning-business coaching: instead of one "how much does it cost to start a cleaning business" page fighting Stripe and Wix, build state/province versions ("in Ontario", "in BC", "in Ohio") because rules, taxes, and suppliers differ, with real cost screenshots. [playbook VI 2.2; calls 051, 024]
- Nationwide or virtual business with a local GBP: "would we create a local page on the website specifically to St. Louis? Yeah, for sure", then suburb pages "one a week"; remote bookkeeper: "Start with the area closest to you... Bookkeeper in Detroit. And then you can have your service pages mention you operate or service all of Michigan or all of the US", because the website ranking for the city and the GBP "feed back into each other"; optimize around the city you can win (St. Petersburg) and mention you serve Florida and nationwide; a virtual tarot service still optimizes the homepage for the home city and adds surrounding cities; furniture rental serving SF, Las Vegas, Phoenix from LA: "If you are servicing different areas, absolutely"; "Northwest Lien" going national keeps the domain and should "create a location page and see if that drives any traffic"; a US and UK agency makes service location pages rather than one page for both countries. Kira's caution: if you are not hyperlocal, the GBP (which forces a service area) may not be the top lead priority. [calls 013, 012, 068, 030, 058, 114, 105, 106, 092]
- Only for places you are based in or genuinely serve: "Unless you're actually based in that location. I wouldn't." A real office or "a virtual address" legitimizes "SEO for businesses in Hamilton"; check whether people search "central Florida" or specific towns. [call 004]
- "Near me" is never the target: not in the keyword, URL, or copy, "the sign of somebody who doesn't know how to do SEO"; "you can't put near me on the page to rank for it", Google uses the searcher's vicinity, and if "near me" is already your top query, leave the page alone; "near me" and "near you" do nothing; "dentist near me" is 90,000 searches country-wide and "really hard to rank for"; "near me is probably going to be the most broad SEO keyword on planet Earth", and a location keyword like "Mechanic Killeen, Texas" (ranking 6th) is "significantly better for your SEO strategy overall." Support proximity with real signals instead: explicit service-area names, a visible address, GBP, and citations (BrightLocal, about $200 one time, into Yelp, Bing, Apple Maps, Facebook, 411, Yellow Pages). [playbook II 2.5, III 2.3, VI 2.1; calls 013, 113, 109, 044, 096; course: finding-keywords]
- Tie each page to a Google Business Profile where possible: Pavitra's location page tied to her GBP ranks, the other does not; best is a GBP per area if you have a physical location, otherwise location pages; a GBP "won't help outside its city" (Newmarket landscaper not showing for Aurora) but location pages "will definitely help"; you probably will not enter a new city's map pack without a GBP there, and widening your GBP service area "might dilute" your current map pack; subleasing an office for a second GBP: "technically you shouldn't do that." Physical address beats service area: "If you don't have physical address, you can have a service area, which is still helpful, but it's not as efficient." [calls 023, 073, 074, 114, 052]
- Expansion recipe: step one, GBPs in the new areas (otherwise no maps, no local reviews, no local signals); step two, "a page on the website for those locations... a dedicated landing page for them. So that's a location page"; step three, local press, local backlinks, chamber of commerce, sponsorships. Two regions (Ontario and New Jersey/NYC): GBP in both plus a location page for both, reviews get split, maybe start with one market. One GBP per location, each linking to its own location page; a temporary second location (one year minimum) still gets its own GBP; a seasonal business in two countries (Ibiza summers) gets "two pages set up" and a GBP per location; rental properties at different addresses can each have a GBP. [calls 022, 024, 117, 105, 114, 109]
- GBP hygiene that backs the pages: each service-area page linked from the matching GBP service category; GBP description first line says what you do and where; two GBPs (commercial vs residential roofing) are fine for two audiences if each points to an optimized page, not a portfolio landing page; with four GBPs (Clermont, Orlando, Naples, Fort Myers), choose one as the main location of the website; NAP identical everywhere. [calls 005, 056, 094, 070; playbook V 4.4; book ch. 18]
- Separate website instead of location pages when the market is far or very competitive: Albany, three hours from NYC, because visitors wonder "are they coming out?"; a six-clinic dental group ("why is that information all on the same website?"); a Botox clinic expanding to another state; otherwise pages per area ("Hamilton Botox", "downtown Hamilton Botox", "Dundas Botox Clinic"). [calls 074, 075, 117]

## What goes on it, top to bottom (the structure)

- The governing rule: "You're changing the entire content each time. It is a lot of work, but if it makes you money, then it's worth it." The structure can be a template, the content cannot: "just make sure all those pages are 100% unique. They mention local landmarks, they mention local things." [calls 021, 088]
- Opening build (Vancouver web designer, one page per area: North Van, Burnaby, Surrey, downtown): H1 "website design in Burnaby"; H2 like "Premier website design in Burnaby"; then local specifics: "Burnaby has over, I don't know, 15,000 small businesses and only X percent of them have a website. But those who do have a website and who have like a properly mobile optimized website... are getting more business"; then local businesses you built sites for, or, if none yet, local sites whose design you love ("if you're looking for something similar, you can always contact us"). [call 005]
- Denroi's heading skeleton (web developer, Wyoming): "Web developer in Wyoming, there's your title tag, there's your heading one. Down that page you'll have headings: Why should you hire a web developer in Wyoming? Why your Wyoming business needs professional web development. How a web developer can help businesses in Wyoming. Really build out that page for the service and keyword and repeat that process for every single area." [call 023]
- Pest-control heading pattern: H1 "Fort Myers Pest Control" ("You can repeat the Heading 1 tag as a Heading 2 tag sometimes"); H2s "Why choose us in Fort Myers", "Kind words our customers say in Fort Myers", "Fort Myers Pest Control Services"; the individual services under that are H3s carrying the city ("Pest control in Orlando"). Denroi's Clermont pass: the location appeared only in the H1 and one other heading, "for the rest of this page you don't mention the location at all. So this is an easy optimization you can do across every single location page": "Why choose Infinite Solutions" (you rank for your brand anyway) becomes "Why choose us for pest control in Clermont"; "Kind words from our customers in Clermont"; "Our Clermont pest control services"; buttons "Learn more about pest control in Clermont", "Learn more about spider control in Clermont". [calls 070, 072]
- Vancouver metal roofing page (service + location): title "Metal roofing in Vancouver"; headings changed from "Benefits of metal roofing" to "Benefits of metal roofing for homes in Vancouver", "really emphasize the location since this is a location page"; the thin colour-options sentence made "double or triple the length"; a toggleable FAQ block (most common roof colour, does colour matter, brown or black, does colour affect temperature) that "pads up the keyword density and helps this page rank for more keywords while associating it more to the location"; a cost comparison table ("always works fantastic for Google"); and a bottom section "Are you looking for other roofing services?" relisting every service with a one-sentence description and a link. [call 082]
- The minimal model (The Model Home, Washington DC area page): headline names the city, "one or two sentences," a list of specialties (closet makeovers, kitchen reorganizations, decluttering), and a contact button: "I come here. It says Washington, D.C. I know I'm in the right locality." [call 109]
- Comedian/entertainer version: each city page lists the types of clients you work with, "a little bit more detail about the location and how to book you", plus a section of event types whose buttons go to the generic event-type pages. [calls 067, 049]
- Local content recipes, by business. Newborn/family photographer: "you need to make sure that you're mentioning things that are local to that area", monuments, parks, wedding venues, "Use local landmarks", the hospitals where babies are born; the parks you shoot in and what each is like: "it's really great from the months of May until September... in the middle of the summer it's going to be a lot hotter... here are some things that you can do to prepare." Wedding photographer: "I shoot a lot in Toronto. Here are some of the Toronto events I've done. Here are the venues that I've shot at." Florist: "here are some funeral homes near us that we deliver to. Here are some hospitals, here are some high school or event spaces", same-day delivery windows ("if you're ordering from [city], within six hours or 12 hours, we'll have flowers out to you"), "if you're attending a funeral in blank, here's where we'll deliver flowers within X amount of hours", the wedding venues per page, "So it's totally unique content every single time." GLP-1 telehealth: nearest hospitals if side effects happen, grocery stores with GLP-1 friendly foods, the farmer's market, local gyms (or partner with them), a local hike, "a nice route for you in your neighborhood" for a 5k walk. Auto repair (10 towns in a 25 to 30 mile radius): the specific car struggles in that town, backing a boat into the lake, Texas heat, neighborhoods, streets, lakes, parks. Mortgage: this town's four events this year, loan programs for living there. Electrician: the different utility company in Richmond vs Langley vs Vancouver, whether electricity is more expensive, which permits are needed. Real estate finance: "each city is going to have different bylaws", plus local partner referrals (best mortgage broker or realtor there). Nanny agency: "there's a lot of parks in the area, if you're comfortable we can take your kids to the park". Betrayal therapy: Alberta oil-field work schedules, Ashley Madison in Toronto. Landscaping: the town's trees, bugs, landscaping, then "here are some of the projects that we've recently completed in small town" linking to the project pages (the projects help the location page rank). Junk cars (Kira's team method): "Oh, you're in Dallas. Like, let's talk a little bit about Dallas... Buying a junk car in Houston is helpful because Houston has this and that and Dallas has that. Now we have differentiated pages." [calls 070, 021, 074, 002, 039, 117, 019, 071, 010, 107]
- Remote or non-delivery businesses anchor in local hooks the same way (nearest hospitals, grocery stores carrying your product, local gyms and trails). [playbook VI 2.2]
- How to generate the copy: reuse the blog interview prompt per location, "what do you like the most about this location? How's the lighting in this location? What if it's rainy? Can we still shoot here?", then turn the answers into the page. [call 021]
- Testimonials from that area only: Boca Raton testimonials on the Boca Raton page, not mixed ("Yep"); reviews on a location page should come from that location's own GBP ("ask which Google Business Profile the reviews come from"), under a city-bearing heading ("Kind words our customers say in Fort Myers"). [calls 032, 070; playbook III 3.4]
- Services on the page: link only the services relevant to that town (if the content only supports 10 of the 36 services, link those), keep a note so every service is covered across the set of pages, and weave the links into the content rather than "three links at the bottom... related" (acceptable only if there is no alternative); links should be "somewhat high quality" where they expand the knowledge. [call 039]
- Neighborhoods inside a town page: a town page can list its neighborhoods; a DC mover got a "neighborhoods we serve in Washington D.C." section with links to local neighborhood news sites. [playbook VI 2.2; calls 049, 061]
- FAQs: location FAQs belong on the page (see the roofing example), but never repeat identical FAQ text verbatim across location pages. [call 082; playbook III 2.10]
- The locations hub page: "have a button in your footer and your header that says locations ... it goes to a page that has all the different locations", grouped (Chicago suburbs together), each linking to its own page; Leanne's version is a "Locations served" overview plus one page per city. A hub that is "just like, a page of links. It's useless. People don't really need that" gets rebuilt as a hierarchy: the top page lists the two counties (Suffolk, Nassau) with the top towns under each (Town of Hempstead, North Hempstead); clicking a town opens that town's own page, which lists the places within it; "You're probably going to be creating like 10 or 20 pages." The hub must make it obvious which country the pages serve ("a user that lands on that hub page can tell those pages are in the US for the US, not for someone in the UK"). [calls 021, 067, 049, 023; playbook VI 2.2]
- What is a blog post instead: "we offer this service in this town or the city, that can be a page"; informational or comparison content is a blog post. Community content for a realtor (best wineries, coffee shops, fruit farms, festivals, what it's like to live in Port Dalhousie, walkability, retiring there) "they're all blog posts", written factually from your own point of view "like a tour guide", not static community pages; "hidden gems" and "the five best coffee shops in this area" posts with phone photos in front of each. Static information you want to rank for the money keyword (the parks you shoot at) stays on the location page, "because that's like static information that you want people to know and you want to rank for the money keyword." Opening a new location: "I wouldn't say do a blog for that, David. I would just say make the location page." Support a city page with "best venues / best entertainers in Dallas" posts, never generic "about Dallas" blogs. Realtor lifestyle pages like "what does it mean to get divorced in North Potomac" are a separate neighborhood-page type. [calls 019, 020, 021, 027, 054, 005]

## Layout and design

- Same ordering fix as a homepage: move "who we work with" and the design approach higher, with pictures, and keep it scannable so a human "can actually consume it" and decide to hire you. [calls 043, 020]
- Keep the site shell identical across location pages: a set of landing pages with a differently coloured menu made "This site looks like a different site." [call 010]
- Per-city navigation helps: competitor Massey gives each city page its own navigation listing that city's services, each service (spider control) with its own page; "A lot of people get lazy... they don't have unique navigations for each city. And it does help." Suggested build: a Locations button, five to seven location pages, each listing landscape / irrigation / pest control with deeper pages under them. [call 070]
- Templated blocks are the tell: two pest-control pages looked identical at the top, only the "why choose" block and services section differed, the reviews widget was the same, and the services block was a shortcode outputting the same headings on every page: "If this is the same or similar on every page, that's not good." The review widget was also "either not showing up as text on the page or it's slow." [call 070]
- Do not park area-served pages under About Us on an old template; redesign and build proper service pages first. [call 109]
- No references list at the bottom ("Don't do references like this. Just internal link or external link throughout the content"). [call 010]
- Mobile basics for a local page: "Can someone call you with one click? Can they find your contact page or checkout easily?"; a clickable phone number. [book ch. 20; call 070]

## Length, keyword density, and on-page SEO

- Word count: "You can do four or five hundred words. If it's not competitive, you don't need a lot of information"; "if there's no volume and no difficulty, you could probably get away with four or five hundred words." If competitive, audit who ranks and match their depth; 2,000 words is not too much "just make sure it's formatted in a way where if a person's reading it ... they can actually consume it." Michael's rule (confirmed by Matt): look at the three main competitors for that keyword in that city, "see the length of their local page and then write the same or a few extra words, maybe 100 or 200 words." [calls 020, 038, 002; playbook III 3.5]
- Too long is real: Elliot's AI-spammed location pages were each "an incredibly long piece of content" (4,000 to 6,200 words) with identical sections repeated on every page. [call 010]
- Title tag and H1 lead with service + city: "Website design in Hamilton, Ontario" as the single H1; title examples "Hamilton Web Design, your local web design company", "Top Web designers in Hamilton 2022 reviews", "Web Design website Development Hamilton", all with "the focus keyword right at the beginning or very, very close to the beginning"; meta examples "Hamilton's award winning web design and development agency" and "Massive Web Design is an established Hamilton web design firm..."; a cash home buyer's H1 "Looking to sell your Delaware home quickly without hassle" becomes "Sell your house fast, Delaware, Ohio" or "We buy houses in Delaware, Ohio"; "couples counseling in Midtown Manhattan" in the H1; "Interior designer in Wellington, Florida offering residential commercial services" as a title. The location in the H1 is what tells Google the page is different ("if in my one tag I put that specific like Skokie newborn... then it's like oh, kind of knows this is the keyword"). [course: no-h1-heading-multiple-h1-headings-defined, title-tags, meta-descriptions; calls 119, 113, 043, 050]
- Meta description: keyword near the start, not the end ("keywords, especially in your meta description, have much more weight to Google when they are earlier"). The keyword "does not have to be exact, but it has to be related": "metal roofing in Vancouver" and "Vancouver metal roofing contractor" are the same intent; avoid awkward over-optimized headings ("Metal roofing is the last roof you'll ever need" became "Why a metal roof is important in Vancouver"). [call 082]
- City, state, or neighborhood in the keyword: the homepage keyword attaches to the state; city pages use city + state or city only depending on volume ("we buy junk cars Dallas" 210/mo, KD 9, vs 20/mo with "Texas"); neighborhood pages use keyword + neighborhood + city, not the state. [call 107]
- URL: service slash location, /security-guard/toronto/, and drop the "services" parent ("Having the services parent is the unnecessary one there ... it extends the URL ... too redundant"); a hyphen form (security-guard-toronto) is fine, but the slash makes categorizing easier "if you are building out dozens or hundreds of location pages ... over 30 pages"; "/landscape-lighting-montreal" style simplification; subfolder, not subdomain (yoursite.com/irvine, not irvine.yoursite.com). [calls 023, 109, 073]
- Uniqueness rules: similar titles across the set ("sell your house fast in Franklin County, Ohio", "...Portsmouth, Ohio") are fine "but make sure that all the text on the rest of the page is completely unique... - They should convert or book, "not necessarily right from that page, but like, at least have a link saying like, here's, you know, go book an appointment"; they can also link out to one relevant blog post. [call 002]

## Links, indexing, and where it sits in the site

- Nest location pages under a standalone Locations hub, never inside one service page: "I would have a locations page and then have the locations there"; "right in your navigation, you would say locations or service areas and just start listing each of those different areas"; a Botox clinic's pages "should be in your navigation menu, like locations or something like that." [playbook VI 2.2; calls 020, 097, 117]
- Header or footer: location pages "don't have to be" in the main navigation, "However, we always recommend the most important location pages be somewhere in your navigation, whether that's the main header navigation or in the footer." The footer is fine "as long as those pages are accessible somehow" (driveagency.com lists cities in the footer, each linking to "Atlanta Digital Marketing Agency", "Miami Digital Marketing Agency", copying that is "a good strategy"); add the list as a fifth footer column or its own footer section; footer link text can be just the city ("you can still name it like Orlando. You don't need to call it the keyword you're shooting for in the footer because it's just a link"); "our areas of service" in the footer plus an "areas we serve" block on the homepage. Footer local signals (NAP, navigation) matter because Google "looks for that to determine if it's a local business." [calls 023, 082, 085, 088, 081]
- Homepage links index faster: link new local pages directly from the homepage ("areas we serve"); footer-only links index slower. [playbook VI 2.2]
- Menu depth: one "service area" dropdown ("I want to see one drop down. You could have 10 on here if you want to"); a menu nesting cities, counties, and dumpster sizes under each was "way too much going on... how do you even do this on mobile?... I can't even click those. So why are they there?"; Denroi's version is a Locations dropdown listing cities, hover a city for a side dropdown of "comedian in [city]" services; services and locations can both live in nav dropdowns. [calls 103, 068, 040]
- City lists at the bottom of pages: "It is good. - Never hide them: "if those are secret and you can't get to them from your homepage, Google can't see those"; "If we hide pages, we hide them from people, we hide them from Google" (a competitor's "areas we serve" button led to unlinked clones identical except the city name); Elliot's raw-HTML landing pages were unreachable from the homepage and so would not be indexed. - Blogs feed location pages: write blogs that link to them with anchors like "Skokie newborn photographer"; "you don't need to include more than one location per blog post... [calls 014, 112, 095, 007, 063]
- Links out of a location page: cross-link neighboring location pages ("if you're actually in [city next door], we also service that area") and other service pages; cap blog links at 2 to 3 per page and "don't link to the same blog post from all the location pages"; service and location pages "do not always have to link to blog posts. - Link the page to the GBP: use the CID link (whatismycid / Pleper; maps URL ending ?cid=number, permanent, "slight more benefits than just the share button"), anchor text like "movers in D.C." works, or make the address clickable to the CID; the content checklist's version: every page links to the GBP with the GBP target keyword as anchor. - GBP posts as links: when location pages sat a year without ranking, add links to them in GBP posts whose copy mentions the service and the location, and build more internal links from blog posts that mention the area ("the more internal links you create... the more Google will see that page as important for your site"). ## Don'ts

- Don't swap the city into a template: "You need 100% original, my friend"; a reviews widget or services shortcode that renders the same on every page is the giveaway. [calls 116, 070; playbook VI 2.2]
- Don't mention other cities on a city page, and don't keep Ancaster on the Hamilton page ("that page won't have Ancaster in it. It'll have Hamilton, it'll have Stony Creek"); St. Catharines and Niagara on one page is two topics. [calls 075, 069; course: every-page-1-main-topic]
- Don't inject your city into broad blog posts: David added "in Killeen" to broad-ranking posts on Claude's advice and traffic fell from 40,000; Matt: change them back (reverting all at once is fine, give it 1 to 2 months). Blog posts "cast a big net" nationally, "and as you get traffic from outside of your area, that's going to help you rank inside of your area because that builds authority"; "I would typically avoid using any location names" in blog titles unless it's your location. [calls 004, 095; playbook VI 2.6; course: find-questions]
- Don't use "near me" anywhere on the page or in the URL. [playbook II 2.5]
- Don't build pages for places you don't serve or aren't based in, and don't diversify far away: a Toronto designer's footer listing Toronto, Orlando, and another distant city drew "why are you trying to diversify so far out? Go micro with the cities." [calls 004, 085]
- Don't build the location x service matrix "just in case"; don't take the city list from ChatGPT or Claude; don't chase zips or unsearched counties. [calls 049, 116, 073, 020, 103]
- Don't hide pages or leave them orphaned; don't nest locations under a service page. [calls 117, 025, 020]
- Don't create a hub that is "just a page of links"; don't stop at an "areas we serve" list or map graphic. [calls 049, 061]
- Don't rewrite a page that already ranks for your home city to chase a new town; make a new page. [calls 074, 038]
- Don't put "Why choose [brand]" headings on a location page ("You will rank for your brand name no matter what you do"); put the city in the heading instead. [call 072]
- Don't bolt a references list onto the bottom; don't change the menu colours per page. [call 010]
- Don't go statewide when your city has more work than you can handle ("shooting a shotgun"); "don't just create a page to create a page." [calls 071, 066]
- Don't publish a placeholder blog while waiting for the budget to build the city pages. [call 065]
- Don't sublease an office to fake a second GBP ("technically you shouldn't do that"); don't widen the GBP service area without expecting map-pack dilution. [calls 074, 114]
- Don't write generic "about Dallas" blogs as support content. [call 054]
