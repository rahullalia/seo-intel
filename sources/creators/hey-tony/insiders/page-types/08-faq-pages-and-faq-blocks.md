# FAQ pages and per-page FAQ blocks

*Part of the HeyTony page-type research (`page-types/`). The rules every page inherits are in `00-rules-for-every-page.md`; the citation key, sources, and file map are in `README.md`.*

FAQs in Matt's system are blocks, not a page: three or four questions tied to the one focus keyword of the page they sit on, written as plain question-then-answer text, answered in the first sentence, and linked onward to the fuller blog post or the money page. A central FAQ page is discouraged because "what are we ranking for?"; each real customer question that needs more than a paragraph becomes its own blog post instead. FAQs are content, not money pages, and Matt's testing puts FAQ schema and accordion formatting far below the quality of the answer itself.

## What it is for and what it targets

- One FAQ block per page, tied to that page's keyword. Leanne: "I'd like to include an FAQ on every page because it's a good way to include answering questions on that focus keyword of the page because every page should have one focus keyword... If you have all your FAQs on one page, what are we ranking for?" [call 028; playbook III 2.10; playbook IV Conversion elements]
- No central FAQ page: "I would have FAQs on all of your pages, but have them relevant to the topic of the page" (deep tissue massage FAQs at the bottom of the deep tissue massage page). "We don't want to have the same FAQs on each page. We want to make sure that each page has unique text on each page... Google doesn't like that." Yaniv's rebuild: "Every page should not should, but could have their own FAQs." [calls 045, 004]
- An FAQ section on a service page lets you place a long-tail question exactly while the page targets the shorter keyword; an accordion on a thin section "really pads up the keyword density and helps this page rank for more keywords while associating it more to the location". [calls 074, 082]
- FAQs are content pages, not money pages, in the email taxonomy; each real customer question is a blog post, not an FAQ line: answer it "Like you'd explain it to a friend over coffee." [email 2026-08-04; email 2026-05-09]
- FAQ or blog post? Denroy's test: "can the question be answered in one paragraph or does it need more information? Does it need visuals? Will someone benefit from having longer form content? If the answer is yes, turn it into a blog. If the answer is something simple, well, you can keep it as an FAQ on your homepage, service or contact page." [call 007]
- "Turning your FAQs into blog posts. This is one way to create content ideas for your website." Matt's own blog has a post on FAQs turned into blog posts (suggested as an internal-link target for Joyce, who built an FAQ page). A FAQ question can be expanded into a blog post if its keyword differs from the page's keyword; each standalone FAQ question can become its own post. Recurring customer questions (summer camp: "do we have to pick our kids up between sessions") are worth a blog post even if answered everywhere: it saves support time, and sending people that link brings non-Google traffic that builds trust with Google. [course: internal-linking; calls 108, 092, 065; playbook V 7.3]
- Conversion purpose (Leanne): on landing pages "I like to include our FAQs, kind of like handle objections, just answer any questions that people might have just to help reassure them that this is the right choice"; on funnel pages "FAQ section. I always love including FAQs. Answer people's biggest concerns and questions. This also helps reassure people and reduce anxiety." On the homepage an FAQ section eases anxieties about booking or buying, "it's also great for SEO because now you can answer people's questions. And that's what Google wants to see", and "it's a good point to add to increase your word count without it being a big wall of text." [calls 016, 067, 079]
- Expectation-setting: FAQ-style Q&A on a tour page pre-empts problems ("Am I guaranteed to see a wolf during a wolf tour in Yellowstone? No, we can't control the animals..."); summer art camps: "FAQs looks great", another way to give parents information while "putting all those keywords in". [calls 084, 072]
- Money pages, collections, products: the book wants an FAQ section on every service page ("Common questions (Yes, add an FAQ section.)"), "FAQs for common customer questions" on product pages, and "Include FAQs that real customers ask." on category pages. Ecommerce: FAQ blocks on collection and product pages for long-tail / AI context and conversion; start with collections; "products on top and then FAQ blog below"; product-page FAQs are fine if unique per product; FAQs on an ecommerce homepage are not where to focus (prioritize collections and products). [book ch. 40; book ch. 38; book ch. 39; calls 041, 017, 020]
- Contact page FAQs answer booking questions and lift conversion and word count (detail in the Contact chapter). [calls 045, 007; course: low-word-count-how-to-fix]
- Adding a couple of FAQs is one of the "small changes" to make to a non-ranking money page; Laura (Hire Dragons) keeps adding FAQs to current pages, which is fine, but expect temporary ranking wobble. [calls 022, 029]
- AEO: a member "started adding FAQs in our content and... optimizing our content as per the answer base"; Matt: "answer engine optimization... it's all just SEO. If you rank on Google, your odds of being recommended in the AI results are a lot higher." His study of 100+ AI Overviews: "it's not about formatting, it's not about like FAQs or schema or anything like that. It's more, does your content agree with the general consensus that AI overviews is already talking about... and then do you have your additional expertise underneath that?" The emails: "It's not about schema markup or advanced strategies." [calls 020, 029; email 2026-06-18]
- Question-shaped headings win snippets: "when you use H2 tags and when you're answering questions, Google might show whatever your, you know, first paragraph is or that relevant information on a page to the user right in the search results... it could help you jump right to number one." The book: "When your headings align with commonly searched questions or phrases, they can boost your chances of landing in rich snippets (aka rich results) or AI-generated overviews in search results... putting your page front and center, even above the traditional #1 spot." [course: heading-tags; book ch. 7]

## What goes on it, top to bottom (the structure)

- Count: 3 to 4 per page. "If you have 12 FAQs, that's too many... typically it's like three or four FAQs for a specific topic"; Matt: "FAQs should be limited to like three to four questions maybe. But I haven't been using them in blog posts." If you have 12, split them onto a dedicated page or spin each into a blog post. [calls 058, 091; playbook II 4.7; playbook III 2.10; playbook V 7.3]
- Questions are H2 tags with paragraph text underneath: "These should be like H2s and then just paragraph text underneath"; "Matt recommends using H2 tags for the FAQs" (Elementor lets you set accordion titles to H2 or H3). Write plain question then answer, no literal "Q:" / "A:" prefix; in the AI markdown view of a page it is just question then answer ("I don't even know if you need Q and A") so Google and AI "can pull from that". [calls 032, 058, 115, 094; playbook III 2.10]
- Answer-first, then link: keep answers short, answer right away, then "if you want more information, check out our full blog post about this topic"; the blog links on to the product or service page (the FAQ to blog to money page chain). "we like if... you have, like, a brief description of the answer, or, like, you have the answer, and if somebody wants to learn more, they can click over to, like, the full piece of content." Linking to a money page is fine; if the answer lives on the money page, link with a hashtag anchor URL that scrolls to the answer. Homepage FAQ entries: a question, a couple of sentences, then a link to the full blog breakdown. "there's no way to know how far they crawl on every page, which is why I like to put the answer to whatever the topic is right at the top." [calls 058, 043, 021, 013; playbook III 2.10]
- Homepage Q&A block (Denroy): short, focus keyword plus location in every question header, internal links under each answer to the pages you want to rank. [call 030]
- Write questions the way people ask them ("How long do temporary tattoos last?", "Do temporary tattoos come off in the shower?"), not forced to contain the full keyword every time; on a collection page Matt prefers question-style H2s over a labeled FAQ block: "Would you do FAQs under the guise of question answer? I wouldn't do FAQs, no. I would just do like a heading two tag with the question... and then" the answer. Shopify: FAQs on collection pages first (broadest reach), as plain H2 questions (not labeled "FAQ") in natural language that reincorporates the keyword ("How long does the temporary tattoo last?"), built as the native collapsible-row component; product-specific FAQs for near-identical SKUs. Kira: FAQs at the bottom of collection pages with the keyword in the FAQ heading. [calls 119, 076; playbook VI 3.2]
- Don't stuff keywords into FAQs, "make sure that you're actually answering the question." [call 058]
- One-line accordion answers are useless: expand them and include the keyword, or do not hide them in dropdowns at all. [call 097]
- Lists where they fit: dumpster page "prohibited, what is allowed, what's not allowed, that's fine. You could probably do bullet points." [call 105]
- Blog posts: Matt's content engine ends each blog with FAQs; Christopher's format is a short video answering the question at the top, a quick summary under the video, the article, FAQ questions at the bottom; a "what does a contractor do" post can use FAQ-style H2s such as warning signs / "what does a contractor not do." But don't stack "Key takeaways", "FAQ" and "Conclusion" as three endings that restate the same thing; keep key takeaways separate from the FAQ block, and address the FAQ points in the content or keep one closing block. [calls 034, 039, 107, 081, 094; playbook V 7.3]
- Tony on "10 FAQs at the bottom of every blog post helps show up in ChatGPT": "No. Like what's going to be helpful for a human? Just listing FAQs at the bottom after your call to action does not help SEO. You could have some FAQs in there. But realistically is that gonna be helpful to a human being? Probably not. But if it's like an FAQ style piece of content, that might." [call 069; playbook II 4.7]
- Sourcing the questions: Google's People Also Ask (click a question and close it to reveal more; pick four distinct questions such as "What is hot sauce made of," "What is the healthiest hot sauce", not near-duplicates); answerthepublic.com (3 free searches a day with an account, 1 without; use list view; copy three questions); HeyKeywords Find Questions (scans Reddit, Quora, Facebook and People Also Ask and clusters them; manicures: "why do some nail salons charge more for manicures?", "what should I look for in a clean, high quality nail salon?", "how long do I typically wait at a good nail salon?"; "most of these are really easy to rank for"); Looker Studio "content optimization ideas" quadrants (why / what / how / can: if "why can't I post on Instagram" has 1,500 impressions, "maybe I should write a blog post or add that to the page that's ranking for that keyword"); the last 10 questions in your inbox and Findquestions.com; Reddit-sourced questions; questions customers ask in person, on social, by email or on a sales call; Marcus Sheridan's model questions ("How much does a fiberglass pool cost?", "Fiberglass vs. concrete pools, which is better?", "What are the problems with fiberglass pools?"); record yourself answering "the questions your customers ask you all the time," transcribe, and turn it into content. [call 114; book ch. 36; course: how-to-find-keywords-your-customers-are-searching-for; course: find-questions; course: tracking-your-growth; email 2026-05-09; playbook II 1.3; playbook V 6; book ch. 2; book ch. 14]
- Competitor comparisons inside FAQs (Botox vs Dysport) may be what helps a competitor rank; Matt: "Sure, maybe", and framed comparison as separate consideration content. [call 048]

## Layout and design

- Placement: near the bottom, before the final CTA. Leanne's homepage and funnel wireframes put FAQs after the process section and just before the final CTA; the movers homepage goes "request a quote", then FAQs, then final CTA; a service-page FAQ is built near the bottom before the final CTA; Marita's homepage ends with FAQs before the footer (approved); the electrician homepage has "our FAQs at the bottom here" among things done right; Mark (Squarespace consulting firm) has FAQs "towards the bottom of every page"; FAQs can sit low on the page. Counter-note: Melissa's FAQs were "pushed to the bottom" under too much content while the ranking competitor surfaces questions higher, and Gail's bottom-versus-top question was framed as a CRO / design test, not an SEO rule. [calls 008, 016, 028, 083, 115, 058, 100, 023, 081, 024]
- Plain text versus accordion, Matt's side: Yaniv's rebuild: "Don't put them in a drop down though. Don't do the toggles. Just have them like question answer underneath. Question answer underneath." Accordion design? "No, no, I think Google and AI are less likely to look at that than if you just have it on the page because you're making more work for them." Matt elsewhere: "having drop downs like this... can we just not do drop downs anymore... just have all of these open basically"; "when AI and Google's crawling your site, they're not necessarily opening up all the dropdowns"; "if you're technically hiding text in toggles, it's not going to be as effective" (the hey Tony site uses lots of toggles, menus and side-swipes that he wants to move away from; "it's fine in some cases, but not in every case"); after testing dropdown Q&As: "It's better if it's not in a box... if this information is so important, why is it hidden in a box?"; "I don't like all the toggles and pop downs"; on a pet store site: "let's open up these FAQs. Great." The playbook: never hide FAQs behind an accordion for genuinely important SEO content; supplementary technical detail (product specs) inside an accordion is fine, and accordions are acceptable to pad thin word count where the expanded content is genuinely substantive. [calls 004, 032, 034, 116, 091, 068; playbook III 2.10; playbook IV Conversion elements]
- Plain text versus accordion, the other side: Denroy: "I've used accordion toggle menus on over 100 sites now. They all rank." Google does a visual crawl, then a raw HTML crawl where it sees toggled content, plus agentic AI crawling that clicks into menus. Leanne's balance: SEO blogs say keep FAQs open for the crawler; about five can be open without overwhelming; with ten long ones use an accordion; four FAQs can be laid out in two columns; accordions add word count without visual bulk; her own service page FAQ is an accordion. The curriculum recommends FAQ dropdowns as the way to raise homepage word count: "if you look down here at the FAQs, we did the same kind of thing. So we listed out the services that we offer there. Do we do organic, social, you know, each one of these has more information." [calls 015, 028, 055, 115; course: word-count]
- Enclosed / boxed FAQ formatting repeated across Shopify blog posts is fine as long as the FAQ text itself differs per post. [call 001]
- A FAQ link inside a section should become a button. [call 060]

## Visuals

- FAQ sections do not need a photo; "as long as you have some photos and it's not just like a huge wall of text." [call 100]
- No other visual guidance exists for FAQ blocks; the relevant rule is that they are the word-count lever that avoids a wall of text. [calls 067, 079]

## Length, keyword density, and on-page SEO

- Too many FAQs can hurt: Jill's category-page FAQs added about 1,000 to 1,750 words to a page that competitors win with a few hundred; remove them (draft) and spin each question into a blog post that links back. Compactors page: "why are there so many FAQs on here?... You don't even need to have FAQs... if you're trying to get to a word count, sure"; the top competitor's FAQs "are not really long, which is good." A FAQ block at the bottom of a collection page plus quotes plus text may be too much; check density, not just volume. Long FAQ sections on treatment pages are fine. Ask first "is this page ranking?" before judging a page's FAQs. [calls 094, 105, 058, 092]
- Unique text per page: never repeat identical FAQ text verbatim across pages (not the contact-page set again on the homepage); on service and location pages ask "are FAQs necessary on this page? Are they unique on this page compared to your other pages?" Counter-note: on one call the same FAQ block repeated on every page "is probably fine" while identical review text "is likely showing up as duplicate content on your site." [calls 045, 055, 061, 020, 096; playbook III 2.10]
- The keyword placement checklist includes "an FAQ" as one of the places the keyword should appear; question headers on the homepage carry focus keyword plus location. [playbook III 2.6; call 030]
- Static FAQs are among the "paragraphs, text blocks, FAQs" that a dynamic page lacks and needs. [call 110]
- FAQ schema stance: Matt: "I haven't found that they're any more or less useful than using them or not using them"; deprioritized by his own testing (after a year, no measurable difference); skipping it carries no penalty; only worth the manual dev effort for high-competition keywords. Denroi: "You will not be punished for not doing this... The impact of it is very minimal. So if you're going for a high competition keyword, do it. If not, it's not the end of the world." Yoast's schema toggle covers standard schema on every page type; FAQ schema for blog FAQ sections needs third-party tools "which can help. However, schema is a bit lower priority than content"; a member's Acumatica AI Studio blog hit page one and an AI citation within eight hours using good content, an AI-style FAQ and answering the question in the first paragraph: "In the past schema was much more important. Nowadays, not as much." Elliot's counter-view: "I would recommend definitely having schema for FAQs because that obviously is really productive." CJ was fixing FAQ schema "so that I can do better there to answer some of the other questions that are coming up on search." [call 074; playbook I 2; playbook IV Schema; playbook VI 4.4; calls 023, 072, 047, 043]
- How to add FAQ schema if you want it: describe your on-page Q&A to Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini, ask it to generate the schema, and paste it into the page's code-injection block. [call 023; playbook IV Schema]
- The bot-flood side effect (Carolina, tiny one-person niche site): hand-added FAQ schema snippets at the bottom of each page "turned my site into catnip for AI bots" (100,000+ legitimate bots from Apple, Meta crashing shared hosting; on another telling 150,000 visits a day versus about 40 normal, needing Cloudflare and a new IP); Google AI started recommending her. The fix: upgrade hosting (about $30 a month cloud / VPS plus a WAF), ask Claude for a snippet that blocks the AI / spam bots, do not block the AI crawlers you want, never strip the schema that earned the visibility, keep publishing two posts a week; whether Cloudflare's human check hurts SEO was still to be researched. [calls 034, 047; playbook IV Schema; playbook V 7.4]
- When auditing why a competitor outranks you, check whether they use a "summary at the very top... key takeaways at the bottom", plus internal links. [call 091]
- Competitors "adding so many FAQs" is a CRO / design test, not an SEO rule. [call 024]

## CTAs and conversion

- FAQs sit immediately before the final CTA so objections are handled before the ask; "Once people feel kind of reassured that all their questions are answered, that typically helps increase conversion rates." [calls 016, 028, 045]
- Each answer carries an onward link (blog post, money page, or hashtag anchor) so the FAQ block is a conversion path, not a dead end; homepage answers link to the pages you want to rank. [calls 058, 043, 030]

## Links, indexing, and where it sits in the site

- The FAQ section is the best place for a service page to link out to blog posts; the chain is FAQ to blog to product / service page. [call 082; call 058; playbook III 2.10]
- Anchor text to a FAQ page: "One of the most common questions about pest control", link the whole phrase, because it points to a pest control FAQ page. [call 072]
- A dedicated FAQ page, where one exists: the cat-behavior competitor keeps a few answers on the page and links "to read more FAQs, click here" to a dedicated FAQs page; the book puts a standalone FAQ page in the footer ("Your footer... is where you can link to secondary, but still important, pages. Things like frequently asked questions (FAQs), policies, support pages, or location-specific content live here."); Joyce built an FAQ page. [calls 034, 108; book ch. 9]
- Two pages on the same topic (a "guide" and an "FAQ" for mechanics liens in California) is a cannibalization question to investigate. [call 081]
- Schema "Clarifies what kind of page it is: Whether it's a blog post, product page, service page, recipe, or FAQ"; rich snippets can show "FAQs". [book ch. 22]

## Don'ts

- Don't build one central FAQ page in place of per-page blocks ("what are we ranking for?"). [calls 028, 045]
- Don't exceed three or four per page; 12 is too many. [call 058; playbook III 2.10]
- Don't hide important answers in toggles or dropdowns (Matt's rule; see the disagreement note). [calls 004, 032, 116]
- Don't repeat the same FAQ text on every page. [call 045; playbook III 2.10]
- Don't stuff the keyword into questions and answers instead of answering. [call 058]
- Don't bolt 10 generic FAQs under the CTA of every blog post and call it AEO. [call 069]
- Don't stack TL;DR, Conclusion, Key Takeaways and FAQ as redundant closers. [call 081; playbook V 7.3]
- Don't write one-line hidden answers. [call 097]
- Don't strip FAQ schema that is earning AI recommendations because bots arrived; fix hosting and block bad bots instead. [playbook IV Schema]
- Don't use FAQs to force word count onto a page that competitors win with a few hundred words. [call 094]

## Where the sources are thin or disagree

- Accordion versus open text is the biggest disagreement in the whole set. Matt (calls 004, 032, 034, 116, 091) and the playbook say keep them open because crawlers and AI may not open toggles; Denroy (calls 015, 082) says accordions rank on 100+ sites and Google's raw-HTML crawl sees them; Leanne (calls 028, 055, 115) uses accordions when there are many or long FAQs and builds her own service-page FAQ as one; the curriculum's word-count lesson recommends FAQ dropdowns. All four positions are recorded above. [calls 004, 032, 034, 116, 091, 015, 082, 028, 055, 115; playbook III 2.10; course: word-count]
- Central FAQ page: Leanne, Matt and the playbook say per-page blocks, not one FAQ page; the book lists a standalone FAQ page among footer links; the playbook itself says to split 12 FAQs onto a dedicated page; the cat-behavior competitor and Joyce have one. [calls 028, 045, 034, 108; playbook III 2.10; book ch. 9]
- Duplicate FAQ blocks: never repeat FAQ text (calls 045, 055, playbook) versus "probably fine" for FAQs, not for reviews (call 096). [calls 045, 055, 096; playbook III 2.10]
- FAQs in blog posts: Matt "hasn't been using them in blog posts" and rejects 10 FAQs under the CTA, yet his content engine ends each blog with FAQs and Christopher's format is endorsed. [calls 091, 069, 034, 039]
- FAQ schema: Matt and Denroi call it minimal or unmeasurable; Elliot recommends it "definitely"; Carolina's case shows it can both win AI recommendations and flood a site with bots. [calls 074, 023, 047, 034; playbook IV Schema]
- Placement: bottom-before-CTA is the default everywhere, but Melissa's competitor surfaces questions higher and Matt treats top-versus-bottom as a CRO test. No word count per answer is given beyond "short", "a couple of sentences", and "not really long". [calls 081, 024, 058, 021, 105]
- Thin: no title / meta guidance for a FAQ page, no image guidance beyond "no photo needed", and no email covers FAQ layout; the emails only classify FAQs as content pages and push questions toward blog posts. [email 2026-08-04; email 2026-05-09]
