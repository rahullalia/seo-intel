# Pricing pages

*Part of the HeyTony page-type research (`page-types/`). The rules every page inherits are in `00-rules-for-every-page.md`; the citation key, sources, and file map are in `README.md`.*

Matt's pricing page is usually two things at once: a page called "Pricing" in the navigation that answers the question "how much does X cost", and a price range or "starting at" figure on the service page itself. The emails class "product or pricing pages" as money pages; on the calls Matt says a page that answers "how much does it cost" is a blog post in his setup, not a services page. Either way the target is the searcher typing "[service] pricing" or "how much does [service] cost", and the rule is transparency: name the number, the range, or the starting point, or you are "getting left out entirely."

## What it is for and what it targets

- "If anybody doesn't have a pricing page on your website, add a pricing page, because people are also always looking for pricing... that's more commercial intent. They're looking, they're comparing... if you think you're going to convince somebody to hire you over your competition without giving them the price, it's going to be a bit tough." Oliver's "Ayahuasca cost" page and retreat price calculator are the example. [call 054]
- Call it "Pricing": nobody Googles "boudoir investment"; they Google "boudoir photography pricing." "Just call it pricing. Call it what it is." People search "[service] pricing"; brand plus "pricing" ("Spotify pricing") is the book's example of a navigational keyword. [call 049; playbook III 3.4; book ch. 3]
- Pricing pages are money pages in the emails ("product or pricing pages... Anything someone lands on ready to buy"), and the book calls a pricing page a high-intent page: "If someone visits a high-intent page on your website, like a service, product, or pricing page, but doesn't convert," retarget them. [email 2026-08-04; book ch. 47]
- On the calls, a page answering "how much does it cost" is a blog post in Matt's setup, not a services page. Caroline (cat behavior consultant) should put a "Pricing" tab in the top navigation that links to a page answering "how much does it cost to hire a cat behavior consultant" (that question is the URL and the topic), and on it break down "what goes into it, maybe your credentials... why you cost X amount of dollars per hour per service." [call 052]
- Cost questions are the best blog questions: "how much it costs" is one of the best because customers literally ask it at expos; "you can bet your ass they are going to Google and typing in that exact same thing." "how much does SEO cost" searchers "are probably really close to pulling the trigger and hiring an SEO agency. And they're like price shopping." Cost questions ("how much does a brand photographer cost") are the blog topics most related to hiring you; "How much do professional headshots cost" is a fine post; "how much is an ayahuasca retreat" (110 searches a month) should cover high, mid and low ranges plus locations, mirroring what the #1 result covers. [calls 051, 102, 065, 088; course: writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt]
- Marcus Sheridan's Big Five, prescribed as blog posts: "How much does your product or service cost? What factors affect your price? Is your product/service worth the cost? Cheap versus expensive options"; "Some of the highest converting blog posts I've ever seen are price related". "How much does SEO cost" was the Rank Week Day 1 exercise, and price is the first thing a visitor to a money page probably needs ("Probably price, how long does it take"). [call 108; playbook II 4.3; calls 120, 117]
- Transparency strategy (from Marcus Sheridan's Endless Customers): "be the most transparent business in your industry." Matt published "How Much Does SEO Cost in 2025? (The Brutally Honest Guide)" to "address ALL of your questions about how & why SEO costs what it does" and folds every reader question back into the post. Write the posts that answer pre-sales questions "even if there's no SEO value to them", e.g. "What SEO Software & Tools Do We Use at HeyTony?" and the SEO cost guide. A "how much does SEO cost" post "is good to have on your website just from a client perspective", and Matt wants to show up in AI results for it, seeding LinkedIn posts with a short answer and the link. [email 2025-06-22; email 2025-06-30; calls 102, 099, 096]
- List prices or ranges even as a premium provider: "if you don't have any prices, you're getting left out entirely" from price-shoppers who will shop regardless. Nuance: a luxury provider whose buyer decides through an in-person or call experience can validly hide prices; the test is your buyer's actual decision process. [playbook VI 5.1]
- On money pages: "I know you don't like talking about price on your website, because it depends. But stuff like that, you can give a range." A service page "doesn't have to have the price on it necessarily"; describe the service and invite a free quote. Ariel no longer wanted specific package prices on her headshot page (new system); removing that packages/prices section is fine and will not hurt. [calls 024, 001, 035]
- Adjacent pricing pages that earn their place: a med spa payment-plans page ("we get asked a lot this question") stays in the main nav; a monthly specials page is fine and will show for "[business name] specials"; a telehealth military page exists "because we have special pricing for military personnel" and is treated as a money page. [calls 058, 012]
- The book on service pages: "What it costs (or a general price range) (If you're not ready to list prices, at least address the cost.)"; the chosen HVAC company listed "Hourly rates and pricing info"; transactional pages must "Make it obvious what you're offering, how much it costs, and how they can get it." [book ch. 40; book ch. 3]

## What goes on it, top to bottom (the structure)

- Matt's own "How much does SEO cost" page, in order: a too-long-didn't-read at the top ("quality SEO typically costs between 1700 and $3500 a month. For most small businesses, it takes this long. Cheap SEO often costs more in the long run"); a breakdown by option (Fiverr, freelancer, small SEO agency, mid size, enterprise); what Redditors say it costs in various situations; a cost breakdown (studio rental, camera gear, hourly for a photographer, in the photography analogy); and "here's what you get with those different price points." [call 051]
- The same model given to Jeff (web designer whose pricing post felt like a humble brag): a TL;DR right at the beginning, the different options, what people on Reddit say (with screenshots), an SEO cost breakdown, what you get at $1,000 / $3,000 / $10,000 / $50,000, and results; answer the reader's real questions (basic five-page site, template versus custom, animation or not). [call 074]
- The TL;DR answers the price directly: the range per month, 6 to 12 months to results, cheap SEO costs more. [call 094]
- "Tell them your prices... you can say starting at, you know, whatever the price is, and then break it down based on the different tiers you have or if you wanted to add this on." [call 051]
- Packages must be concrete: the string quartet's "packages and pricing" copy ("explore a variety of live music services and packages") is "very vague"; lay out quartet / trio / duo / single with rates per event type, ideally on their own pages. [call 061]
- Pricing calculators: "write that blog post, give them the actual pricing or give them like a pricing calculator... ChatGPT can make one for you, or Claude... calculators that you can just embed on your website" (movers, "how much do movers cost in Toronto"); Oliver's retreat price calculator. [calls 101, 054]
- What is included goes with the price: the Yellowstone tour page shows $5,000 to 6,000 but not what is included or group size; Melissa's competitor shows a payment plan (50% deposit) and "what's included" on the service page as proof elements; the top cat-behavior competitor lists a "payment method" block near the bottom of the page. [calls 084, 081, 034]
- A cost or comparison table on a service page "always works fantastic for Google". [call 082]
- Value framing: state a value like "$100 of value, free," but "don't make it unrealistic... I have to back that up" (is it normally sold at that price, or are bonuses creating the value); on a PR-services ad landing page, frame the cost against hiring a full-time employee ("the cost might seem high... but it's actually way less compared to hiring someone full time"); an agency page saying "Saves you thousands for just 25 bucks a month. Here's what you get" still needed to state what the service actually is. [calls 005, 016, 105]
- Real evidence: cost content should show "the real screenshots of what it costs" (Victoria's state-specific cost pages); Bill's brochure-style site offers "information, get price list and stuff like that." [call 024]
- Send pricing seekers to the pricing page rather than stuffing packages into the service page: ceramic coating page CTA "Book an appointment", then "go to the pricing page, for example". [call 066]

- HeyTony's "How much does SEO cost in 2026" post uses a chart, Reddit screenshots, a bulleted cost breakdown ("here's what you're paying for"), and videos; "it makes sense to have more images depending on the content." Real screenshots of actual costs are the evidence layer. [calls 102, 099, 024]

## Length, keyword density, and on-page SEO

- Meta description: Jennifer's "WordPress website design and pricing" page was missing one; add it (unlike legal pages). [call 078]
- No year in the URL; change the year in the title each year ("How much does SEO cost in 2026"). [call 074]
- Keep the numbers current: Matt updated his prices but not the blog post, and clients demanded the old price; "if you're changing your pricing, make sure that you're updating it everywhere". He would write "these are our prices" rather than "as of March 2026". Leads quote the page to his sales team, so it has to stay current. [calls 065, 051]
- Verify AI-written pricing claims before publishing: "hourly 20 to 200 an hour for SEO? It's probably not 20 to 200 an hour, right? Project based. We don't do any." Remove anything that does not make sense. [course: writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt]
- AI Overview citation pattern for price claims: state the consensus figure first, then pivot: "The average kitchen renovation costs $50-75,000 in [area]. However, we can do it for $25,000." Writing only "we do it for $25-35K" will not get cited. [playbook II 4.7; playbook V 7.2]
- Schema: prices (and reviews) need a third-party schema tool beyond Yoast's standard schema; manual JSON-LD Product-type schema can carry prices if you choose to add it (paste into the header); schema can expose "product pricing" in rich snippets; GBP should include "services, pricing". [call 072; playbook IV Schema; book ch. 22; book ch. 18]
- Different pricing in different currencies is one of the only reasons to use country-tagged URLs; location pages where only the fee differs are still separate pages, "just change up the wording." [calls 020, 106]
- No backlinks are needed for a keyword like "how much do movers cost in Toronto". [call 101]

## CTAs and conversion

- Put the price on the CTA where possible: for the lien-filing CTA, "file your lien today, $10", so the shortcut is obvious. [call 081]
- A Calendly link right next to the price on a sales page beats sending people to a contact form. [call 052]
- Retarget visitors who hit the pricing page and do not convert. [book ch. 47]

## Links, indexing, and where it sits in the site

- Navigation: a "Pricing" tab in the top nav (Caroline); "Packages and Pricing" in the header (Regina); the payment-plans page in the main nav (med spa); "pricing or the book now" is a normal nav item a visitor clicks and must stay on the same domain and look. The book: nav and footer "can also include links to pricing, case studies, or anything else that directly supports conversions." [calls 052, 042, 058, 114; book ch. 27]
- A pricing money page can link to a relevant blog post and vice versa. [playbook IV Internal Linking]
- Pages with prices or packages found in backlink-gap lists are often paid directories; "you might not want to do all these." [course: backlinks-gap]
- Competitor pricing changes can be watched with HeyTony Watchdog ("if they're changing their pricing, if they're doing anything"; before/after diff; "Updating pricing" is one of the tracked changes). [course: bonus-spy-on-your-competition; playbook VI 1.4; book ch. 34]

## Don'ts

- Don't call it "Investment" or any euphemism. [call 049]
- Don't hide prices entirely unless your buyer genuinely decides in person or on a call. [playbook VI 5.1; call 054]
- Don't write vague package copy ("explore a variety of live music services and packages"). [call 061]
- Don't leave stale prices anywhere once you change them, and don't date them "as of March 2026". [call 065]
- Don't put the year in the URL. [call 074]
- Don't publish unverified AI price ranges. [course: writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt]
- Don't write a pricing post that reads as a humble brag (Jeff). [call 074]
- Don't claim unrealistic value you cannot back up. [call 005]
- Don't show a price without what is included (Yellowstone's $5,000 to 6,000 with no inclusions or group size). [call 084]

## Where the sources are thin or disagree

- This is the thinnest of the four page types. The curriculum does not teach a pricing page; the playbook has no layout, table, or length guidance and says so; the book only asks that cost be addressed on service pages; the emails give the transparency rule and the money-page classification. No word count, H2 list, image count, or table format exists for a standalone pricing page; everything structural comes from Matt's own "How much does SEO cost" post. [course: writing-your-first-blog-post-using-chatgpt; playbook III 3.4; book ch. 40; email 2025-06-22]
- Blog post or money page: Matt says a "how much does it cost" page is a blog post in his setup, not a services page; the emails class "pricing pages" as money pages and "guides" and "comparisons" as content pages. Both are kept. [call 052; email 2026-08-04]
- Show prices or not: call 054 says everyone should add a pricing page; the playbook allows luxury providers to hide prices; Ariel was told removing her package prices would not hurt. [call 054; playbook VI 5.1; call 035]
- Dating the page: no year in the URL but the year in the title, versus writing "these are our prices" rather than "as of March 2026"; the two are compatible but come from different calls. [calls 074, 065]
