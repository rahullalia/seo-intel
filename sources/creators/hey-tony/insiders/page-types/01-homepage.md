# Homepage

*Part of the HeyTony page-type research (`page-types/`). The rules every page inherits are in `00-rules-for-every-page.md`; the citation key, sources, and file map are in `README.md`.*

In Matt's system the homepage is "click zero": the page most visitors land on first, the page most backlinks point at, and "the handshake of someone's website." Its first job is to say plainly, in live text, what the business does and where it does it, then give the visitor one obvious next action. Whether it should rank depends on the business model. A local or service business points the homepage at its single most profitable service plus its home city, and the homepage is often the money page itself. An ecommerce, national, or multi-product site treats the homepage as a branded orientation page and pushes ranking onto collections, products, service pages, and blog posts. Either way the homepage targets at most one keyword, never two, never several cities, and no blog post is ever allowed to chase the homepage's keyword.

## What it is for and what it targets

- Local or service business: the homepage targets [primary service] + [home city], e.g. "dentist in Hamilton," "Residential roofing in Cincinnati," "Massage therapy in Winnipeg," "Bookkeeper in [city]." "If you're a dentist in Hamilton, your homepage, you want to try to rank for dentist in Hamilton because that's what people are searching." "Homepages are fine, especially for a service based business because like your Dumpster rentals, that's probably the keyword that you want to go for with your homepage." [playbook II 2.4, III 3.2; calls 103, 083, 068]
- The one service that makes the most money is the homepage keyword; every other service gets its own page. "If you offer multiple services, your primary service, the one that makes you the most money will be your homepage. Every other service can have its own separate page." Things you prescribe or sell get another page. "One keyword per page is the general guideline you will always follow on any site." [call 007; playbook III 3.2, IV Governing Rules, VI 2.2]
- The homepage is the home-base city; other cities get location pages that do not repeat the home city. Comedian: "my homepage is going to be Boca Raton... on locations, I'm not going to have Boca Raton. I'm just going to have the others." Tony: "My priority is my homepage. My money page... my homepage is going to be my location. Boca Raton." Denroi: start with your own city because it is the easiest to rank and it ties to the Google Business Profile address. [calls 049, 066, 068, 021, 038]
- Multi-location: "your main website is going to be for your biggest location or the location that is doing the best," with location pages (and possibly sub pages) for the others. A UK agency with a Wyoming office keeps the homepage on the UK keyword ("Web developer in UK is your main keyword. That's your homepage, that's your main services page") and builds entirely separate pages for each US area. A photographer keeps the homepage keyword where the studio actually is (Algonquin IL) and builds separate pages for the bigger town nearby (Schaumburg), optionally a studio page saying it is a 20-minute drive away. [calls 004, 023, 058]
- National, virtual, or online-only with no physical base: still pick one location. Optimize the homepage for the single largest nearby metro, or the easiest-to-rank city ("Lubbock IT support"), say "I am a service based provider located in blank but I serve people all over the world," and spin out location pages. "It's a lot harder to rank if you're trying to target all of the United States." Tarot reader told to put Toronto back on the homepage instead of the national term: "pigs get fat and hogs get slaughtered. You're trying to eat the whole pie and you're not going to get a piece." A bookkeeper goes for "Bookkeeper in [city]" even if she serves the state or nation because she lacks domain authority for broad terms; service pages can say you serve all of Michigan or the US. [playbook II 2.4, III 3.2; calls 067, 038, 058, 068]
- When the home market is too big (Chicago, Orange County, Toronto): check difficulty and volume first (Semrush). If the big-area keyword is viable go for it; otherwise "pick your localized area and specialize in that on your homepage" (Irvine, Chilliwack) or a niche (luxury weddings, barn weddings instead of plain Toronto wedding photographer). Chicago newborn photography is "a really hard line," so "build at least a small locational aspect first and then broadening out to Chicago." The Chilliwack photographer's homepage ranks for her niche in Chilliwack while location pages chase Vancouver, West Vancouver, Abbotsford. [calls 040, 047, 014]
- Playbook variant for businesses too small or new to rank at city level: target state-level keywords first, then build pages for the top 10 to 20 highest-population cities. [playbook VI 2.2]
- Ecommerce, content, and multi-product national sites: do not try to rank the homepage for a keyword. "Stop trying to rank the ecommerce homepage for one keyword." "You're not trying to necessarily get your homepage to rank. Your money pages, your collections and your products are where you want to focus your attention. And blog posts as well." "Are people typing in funny T shirts, apparel and drinkware? No... The idea here is that you can get them in through a collections page or a blog post or a product." Leanne: "I wouldn't focus so much on trying to rank your homepage... I would focus on trying to rank your product pages and your collection pages." The homepage "just needs to make a visitor understand what the site is once they land from an entry page." [playbook II 2.4, III 3.2, VI 3.1; calls 020, 103, 100]
- Branded ecommerce homepage treatment: H1 = brand name, title tag = company name + focus keyword, meta description that explains the store ("Laulu Design is a boutique online store that offers funny T shirts, apparel and drinkware"; "Just Laulu Design isn't enough to really rank, really explain what the store is"). Forcing FAQs onto an ecommerce homepage was "a bit of a force fit." [calls 086, 020; playbook II 2.4]
- Exception for single-line product stores: pick one primary commercial keyword and build the H1/H2s around it: "beard oil" (high volume, not insanely hard) for a grooming brand, "outdoor yoga rugs" for a yoga-rug store, "healthy pet snacks / healthy pet food / organic pet food" for a pet store. Matt: "I always say the homepage doesn't necessarily need to rank for anything," though for a pet treat store it could. [calls 076, 007, 068, 066]
- Umbrella homepage with money pages underneath: HeyTony's homepage is broadly about digital marketing while service pages carry SEO, Facebook ads, Instagram ads. A wedding photographer/videographer can be broadly "wedding photographer and videographer in [area]" while money pages target elopement, engagement, and proposal shoots. A multi-genre photographer uses [location] + "photographer" and lists family, wedding, headshots lower as services; the homepage can "act almost as like a funnel to all those other pages," but keep "a main services page with like all the different types of photography" because "you're not going to talk about all of your services in depth on your homepage." Leanne: "I'm not really trying to target a main keyword for my homepage, but I'm targeting the keywords that I want to rank for in my services pages." "Should my homepage rank for anything? Not necessarily." [calls 112, 067, 020]
- Startups and aggregators: "your homepage very rarely will ever be your main money page. This is just one of the many landing pages"; optimize for something broad ("flight delayed") or the brand name and build one page per keyword. A multi-property site with a search bar may be a "gateway" or navigational page rather than lead capture. [calls 072, 105]
- The homepage IS the money page when there is no dedicated page: "Your Money page can be your homepage if you'd like." Matt: "I don't even have an about page on my website. Everything's on the homepage." "For a lot of especially local or service-based businesses, that will be a money page." Stack Electric ranks #1 for "Hamilton electrician" with its homepage; a restaurant's money page "would probably just be the homepage"; a single-property vacation rental (Casa Luna Azul, five pages) ranks on its homepage, then adds content so the domain ranks for more; a two-sided marketplace's money page "might be your homepage." If you have no service page, audit the homepage. [calls 022, 021, 038, 075, 110, 118; playbook III 3.1]
- Competitor homepages are what you are up against: Money Page Doctor for a DC wedding photographer reported "Competitors ranking for this keyword are primarily homepages"; for "website design in Hamilton" positions 1 and 2 were competitor homepages, and Matt audited his service page against them because "those are the pages that are ranking." Because "your homepage usually has the most amount of backlinks anyways," in a competitive city a location page probably will not outrank a local competitor's homepage. [calls 074, 118, 038]
- Never target the same keyword on the homepage and a service page: "you either have to choose your homepage or have a dumpster rentals page... you don't necessarily need a services separate page for that." Roofing homepage takes the broad "Roofing Vancouver," the metal roofing page takes "metal roofing Vancouver." If the homepage already ranks, "leave it as is" and build a separate service page for the new focus; if you do re-target a ranking homepage, rewrite the old copy "so they're not competing with each other." [calls 096, 097]
- Nary's bookkeeping homepage optimized for "bookkeeping services": "Your homepage is acting like your service page right now, right?... that is your intent for this page. I would keep that here," and do not also build a bookkeeping service page on the same keyword. [call 052] (see the conflict note at the end)
- If the homepage outranks the intended money page, stop fighting it: "your homepage is now your money page. And try adding that keyword a couple more times on that page or talking about the topic a couple more times," and point future blog links at the homepage. Jessa's course site: homepage #2 for "online intimacy course for couples" (it mentioned "course" only six times) while the course page ranked nowhere and every competitor result was a course page; option: set the course page as the static homepage (WordPress Settings > Reading), monitor a couple of weeks, and if rankings drop "put it back to the way it was. Don't delete any pages." "SEO is all about testing." Playbook: swapping a sub-page in as homepage is "a little bit risky"; mitigate by monitor-and-revert, never by avoiding the test. [calls 054, 113; playbook III 3.2, I 3]
- Homepage cannibalizing a service page: Google "tries to find one page per website that makes sense for that keyword" and will not rank one site in positions 1 to 4; change the homepage only "if you're fine with losing that ranking and making it up on another page," otherwise "one page could just not show up, or both pages could be harming each other." Member tip: paste both pages into Claude and ask what to change on each. William unpublished the interfering Toronto location page; the homepage jumped to 7, then 9, then dropped: "Give it 30 days... optimize it, leave it, stop fucking around with it." [calls 037, 065, 052]
- A blog post's keyword is never the homepage's primary keyword. If a post's question keyword shows the homepage instead of the post, the homepage mentions it or features the new post: "give it time, that will filter out." The Money Page Tool reports which of your pages ranks #1 for a keyword, exposing when the homepage steals a service page's term. [playbook II 2.4, III 2.13, II 3.3; call 019]
- About, contact, and category pages do not compete with the homepage: "the difference between your homepage and your about page is so slim because your homepage is like, here's about the business, here's what I do... And then your about page is like, here's a little bit more about me." A comedian resolved a homepage-vs-service-page overlap by making the service page about process ("how it works, how you can hire me, what we can do for you"). [calls 021, 088, 011; playbook III 3.2]
- When a different page ranks (a SaaS features page on page 6 instead of the homepage), check what type of page ranks 1, 2, 3: "that might match the intent better than what the other page you're trying to get to rank." [call 022]
- Keyword too competitive for now ("bookkeeping St. Petersburg" was "too strong for us"): "leave it how it is. Write blog posts and just forget about optimizing your homepage for now," and fill out the Google Business Profile completely. [call 043]
- Intent check: a nanny agency's "professional nanny placement" read as aimed at nannies seeking work, not families hiring ("find a trusted nanny" vs "hire a nanny in San Francisco"); a keyword blindly translated from French had to be checked in both languages. "If you're like, I hope something ranks, it's likely nothing's gonna rank." [calls 068, 034]
- Choosing the keyword: Google Trends shows popularity, not volume; use a keyword tool (hey keywords, Semrush, Ubersuggest) for volume and difficulty; zero volume is not a veto because "15% of all searches done on Google every day are brand new." Prefer the easier variant: "tow truck Boston" (difficulty 29) over "Boston towing" (57); "Midtown Manhattan" (difficulty zero, 20 to 40 searches a month) over "couples counseling NYC" (590 a month, too hard); "Sugar Land ultrasound" (30/mo) or "3D 4D ultrasound" (20/mo, very easy) over an H1 phrase that returns nothing in Semrush. [calls 020, 109, 113, 096]
- Brand and domain: branded/navigational searches find the homepage; a "supergeneric" business name that overlaps established companies "might be worth considering a more unique name." A one-word domain without the keyword is fine (squigglesquad.com did not need "squigglesquadhandwriting.com"): "I don't think that it plays that big of an impact." [book ch. 3; call 114]
- Order of work: "look after your homepage first to make sure that it's optimized well, then make your service pages right away, and then expand to the location pages." "Start with your homepage, get your homepage up, and then start building the other pages," and submit to Google once the homepage and a couple of pages exist, even before location pages. New member on Shopify: "optimize your homepage first, just as like a starting point" with the Page Audit tool. Leanne builds branded styles first, then the homepage, then other pages. [calls 040, 066, 106, 028]
- Do not use the homepage for a why-doesn't-it-rank diagnosis: "the homepage can rank for a bunch of different things. I want to look at one specific page." [call 124]
- Realtors with an IDX feed: since listings are identical everywhere, "your biggest thing is going to be blog posts and probably your homepage" for differentiation. [call 005]

## What goes on it, top to bottom (the structure)

- Leanne's wireframe, taught on every web design call and the default for "most service based businesses" (product sites swap services for product categories): (1) navigation bar; (2) hero: photo or graphic that fits the business + H1 + short supporting copy + one CTA button; (3) trust-building elements, inside the hero or right below it; (4) about / "introducing ourselves"; (5) services overview, each linking to its own page; (6) testimonials; (7) how it works / process steps; (8) FAQs; (9) final CTA section; (10) footer. Matt on an electrician site built this way: "It kind of follows exactly what I talked about... for what makes a good homepage design." [calls 008, 016, 028, 045, 055, 067, 079, 083, 100; playbook IV Web Design]
- Hero = "the main attraction of your website... the first impression people get," "the handshake of someone's website." It must be impactful and "help direct the customer into what they should do next." A site with no hero banner "makes me feel like I'm on one of the interior pages." [calls 055, 060]
- Hero contents: the H1 (the focus keyword in plain language, "a description of what our website is about, our business"), sub text that "further explains what our business is doing," and the CTA button, all visible "without even having to scroll, without even really having to think." Example hero: H1 "bookkeeping services in [city]" + appointment CTA + cut-out photo of the two owners. [calls 055, 067, 083, 060; playbook IV Web Design]
- Hero must say what you do and where, in text: "hire us for your blank, or like we do blank... We are a dentist. We will fix your teeth. We are plumbers. We will fix your leaks." The local version: "if you're a local business, like, I want to see exactly where you do that service" (H1 "couples counseling in Midtown Manhattan" with a sub-line "trusted provider of personalized couples therapy, Midtown"). The model: "Cincinnati's trusted house cleaning for busy families... You're saying where you are. You're saying what you do. You have call, book in 60 seconds. Hell yeah." [call 113]
- Hero trust signal: 5 gold stars in Google's color + "Rated 5 stars on Google" hyperlinked to the real Google review link, placed once prominently; or "helped over 10,000 people with pain," awards, "as seen in" logos, logos of reputable clients. "This already feels a little bit more of a trustworthy website just having these stars here." Put "rated five stars on Google" in the hero instead of "welcome" ("welcome... doesn't really add much to the user experience"). [playbook IV Conversion elements; calls 083, 067, 045, 036, 060]
- Trust row below the hero: about 3 of star rating, years in business, happy clients, licensed/insured, association logos (Leanne's own: 5-star Google rating plus chamber of commerce / organization logos right below the hero); move trust badges below the hero if they make it busy. [playbook IV Web Design; call 055]
- Hero extras: a pre-header to communicate a niche faster ("on site / live calligraphy"); a top bar carrying a 10% coupon or "free shipping over $50" instead of "we love pets," and stop it scrolling so attention stays on the button; a hand-drawn arrow for playful brands. A lead form in the hero when you want maximum inquiries (Leanne did it for an electrical site, not for her own personal site: "it's important to kind of introduce myself before I go straight into the contact form"); Matt reportedly said hero lead forms convert more now. [calls 060, 045]
- Hero H1 is one short statement: "Your Vancouver Roofing Experts. Great. That's all you need," not that plus "Trusted Vancouver roofer since 2008" in the same tag; a big marketing headline can be an H2 while the SEO H1 is styled smaller (Leanne: "This here is actually my H1 tag. It's much smaller... it's not like a really good selling feature. So I styled this smaller"; "Digital Marketing Services in Winnipeg" as H1 with the emotional hook as H2). [calls 096, 045, 055, 067]
- Keep the H1 fixed while promos rotate: a lingerie store's hero can promote seasonal products but the H1 never rotates; on Shopify (which ties the H1 to the logo) put the H1 plus a line of text directly below the hero so the hero can change and the H1 stays put. [call 055; playbook IV Conversion elements]
- About / intro block right under the hero: "imagine we're introducing ourselves to our customer": what we do, how we do it, who we serve; H2 carries the focus keyword again ("Why choose Winnipeg Massage Therapy Clinic"); a short intro paragraph; then audience-identifier boxes with icons (busy professionals, athletes, desk workers, people recovering from surgery) so a visitor thinks "I'm a desk worker, I got back pain right now, I'm going to book"; then a CTA. Even a graphic designer whose shoppers "don't care who owns the store" keeps "this little blurb on your homepage and then link to learn more about you," because for a service "people working with you" is the product. [calls 028, 055, 079, 083, 067]
- Story belongs on the homepage when it is the brand: for a venue with personal history, "I would include that story right in your homepage" ("it all started at blah, blah, blah... kind of like an emotional story" that "builds some emotional connection"); a boudoir photographer's "my letter to you" should sit next to a photo of her. A long About block is not too long "because we also want to have a good enough word count for SEO"; break it with bullet points if it looks heavy on a phone. Matt himself has no about page; Elliot: under the about blurb "have a button down here that goes to... the about page." [calls 016, 049, 045, 021, 050]
- Services block: an intro to each service with a button or keyword-anchor link to its own page ("We offer the following digital marketing services. Website design... local SEO that links to that page," never "learn more" or "click here"); icons or text-left/image-right rows; service names as H3s; for a coaching business "your homepage would kind of briefly describe that service and then you would have a button that would link to that page." No maximum number of services: list the core ones and push the rest to a mega menu or dropdown ("It be about organizing it so it's not too much information"). A philosophy-led homepage keeps its philosophy but adds "an extra section for your services." A comedian's services block is the types of people who book you (corporate, HOAs, 55-plus communities, activity directors, club bookers), each card clicking through to its own page. A homepage with "a blurb for each service" but no service pages needs the pages. [course: anchor-text; calls 083, 067, 058, 044]
- Product sites: list the main product categories or collections on the homepage itself (not only the menu): six linked collections is "great," not "too busy," but not in carousels ("people aren't going to go to that"); "a hero section and it links directly to Shop now. And so every section they talk about a different product"; multiple product lines get three clear options ("buy/shop," "build your own," "materials"). [calls 010, 017, 100, 074]
- Testimonials: on the homepage, then relevant ones on each service page; Google reviews, personal reviews, or video reviews "to prove what you've done"; they can sit directly under the hero ("I think that would definitely help with conversion") or after services; show them once, not twice; you do not need all of them, and hidden text is not an option; link each on-site review to that exact review on the Google profile and add a "read more reviews" button; keep one scrolling testimonial section, not a scrolling plus a static one. A comedian moved video clips above testimonials because "people want to see what they're getting into." [calls 016, 083, 058, 049, 021, 086, 060, 032]
- How it works / process: three steps (book an appointment, personalized assessment, targeted treatment; or get a quote, plan your move, moving day) because a first-timer "has no idea what the next steps are" and certainty drives conversion; "This helps ease people's anxieties"; put a contact button underneath. [calls 008, 079, 083, 058]
- FAQ block: "good for Google and it's also good for your customers to kind of answer those lingering questions... that might stop them from reaching out," and "a great way to boost up your word count without it just being more and more text." On a small site put the service and the location in every FAQ header ("Why should you hire a bookkeeper in St. Petersburg?"); on a big site put internal links under each answer aimed at the pages you want to rank and adjust the toggles as new pages publish. FAQs can point to core blog posts ("when's the best time to do a maternity shoot?" then a couple of sentences and "here's the full breakdown") as "a permanent fixture on your homepage," but not for every post. Write new FAQ text, do not reuse the contact-page FAQs. HeyTony's homepage FAQ lists services with more info under each ("Do we do organic social"). [calls 028, 079, 030, 021, 055; course: word-count]
- Locations block: "areas we serve" / "locations I service" / "locations I perform at" text with a clickable city name per location page, on the homepage itself, not only the footer ("that will help you get indexed a lot faster... Look at all this space here"); "Neighborhoods we serve in Washington D.C." is great; mention the city and greater area and keep the address on the page; a site that never said where it was based had to add Port St. Lucie / West Palm / Treasure Coast. [calls 014, 088, 058, 061, 096, 103; playbook VI 2.2]
- Blog block: the most recent 5 to 10 posts via the platform widget (book) or "have three of them. You don't need this big background with a photo" (Matt on a long homepage); plus links to blog categories, 10 categories max, so every post is two clicks from home; articles can be grouped into process sections (improving, selling) that link to categories. Putting the blog on the homepage to look less spammy is fine. [book ch. 27; calls 049, 073, 028, 055]
- Final CTA: "I always end every page with a call to action section," "so if someone scrolls all the way to the bottom of the page, they have something to do next instead of... they might just leave the website because we didn't tell them what they should do next." CTA at the top and the bottom of every page. [calls 028, 067, 079, 083, 008]
- Footer hand-off: NAP (name, address, phone) present and crawlable, linking to the GBP via the CID link, because Google "looks for that to determine if it's a local business"; link to the GBP from the homepage or footer (Matt's homepage has a map that loads the GBP with the service area). [playbook V 4.4; call 073]
- Order variants Matt approved: comedian mock: hero, testimonials, three embedded YouTube shorts, about blurb with read-more, "types of events" cards, how it works (+ contact button), FAQs, footer, plus a locations section; Stack Electric: contact info right there, what they do, areas served, residential and commercial, blog posts at the bottom; HeyTony: "level up your digital marketing... trusted by these known brands," services bar, a testimonial, FAQs, recent posts and category links at the bottom; the cat-behavior competitor worth copying (structure, not look): CTA near the top, certifications at the top instead of the bottom, featured-in logos linked out, step one / two / three, her story, FAQs linking to a separate FAQs page, services, payment method, Google reviews. [calls 058, 075, 105, 073, 034; course: word-count]
- Content that belongs lower or elsewhere: an intro block that "sounds like a blog post" ("The problem with most national park trips") moves down or becomes "why you should go on a Yellowstone national park tour," then offerings, then testimonials; maternity content on a Chicago newborn homepage "strays away a little bit from the purpose of this page"; "the homepage... you want it to cover a lot. But... offer like, opportunities to like, click on another page to add more depth"; brand lines that do nothing for SEO or the reader ("every woman is uniquely beautiful") and an "is boudoir right for me?" section ("if they're already on your website, they know that they want this") get cut; repeated galleries consolidate into a portfolio page in the nav; a mid-page inquiry form is redundant when buttons already go to it. [calls 097, 050, 049]
- Talk about the reader's specific problems, not options: "Does your cat hide when anybody comes over? Is your cat fighting? Does your cat scratch the groomer?"; "the more options you give people, the less they're gonna do." [call 034]
- Catalogs and feeds: do not embed four PDF catalogs; "have a picture of the front of the catalog that links to the catalog," opening in a new tab. Seasonal money pages come off the homepage and menu off-season ("Google still knows about the URL, just don't have it on the menu"). [calls 022, 021]
- Squeeze-page sites: keep the squeeze page as a separate page and "make your homepage about what you do," linking to blog posts from the homepage: "If you want Google to find your blog posts and your website, you need to show them that you have multiple pages." A one-page site is "absolutely terrible for SEO"; "you need more than one page for it to rank." [calls 026, 061; book ch. 9, 16]
- Text inside homepage modules counts: tour product cards' text is picked up by Google toward what the homepage ranks for; a hosting homepage with comparison tables "has plenty of content... This is a great indicator for me for why this page is the one that is indexed." [call 097]

## Layout and design

- Copy first, then layout: "the purpose of layout and the design process is to help communicate a message. And so if you know your message first, it makes it a lot easier"; "work out your SEO copy first and then you can figure out what kind of layout will help convey that message stronger" without "a big wall of text." Wireframe or mock up first (Figma, Canva, pencil) to surface bad ideas and lock image sizes; Leanne checks the mock with the HeyTony headline tool so the keyword sits in the H tags, and later copy edits go straight into WordPress. The wireframe doubles as a guide for "how much copy to write." [calls 028, 083, 055; playbook IV Web Design]
- One section = one H2 = one topic, "like titles in an essay"; alternate background tints section to section "like a slide deck," so each reads as its own block (near-white tint of the brand color for one section, white for the next). Reuse, do not rebuild: duplicate a working section, flip which side the image sits on, swap the background. [calls 067, 079, 083; playbook III 3.3, IV Web Design]
- Section rhythm for long pages: two-column text/image rows, "text on one side, image on the other side and then reverse it... you can kind of repeat that for a long time without it looking too boring," or four boxes with icons; the problem with a 1,200-word homepage that felt overwhelming "is not the text... it's the amount of space around the text." "Okay if it's a longer scroll" once the text is broken up with white space. [calls 055, 083]
- Most important messages near the top: "Your homepage can be as long as you want it to be. Just the longer it is, the less likely people are going to reach the bottom of it"; "As people scroll down to your page, less and less people see the bottom of your page. So it's most important that we put most of our effort at the top." Push the hero CTA up if a big screen cuts it off. [calls 083, 098, 096; playbook III 3.3]
- Hero proportions: the header/contact block occupies roughly 1/6 of the page, not half; shrink oversized logos (one ate a third of the screen); never show the logo twice (image + text) in the header, and never repeat the logo as the hero image ("wasted real estate"). Leanne does not like an oversized hero and likes "a little bit of a hint that people can scroll down," while still making the hero "almost full screen" and "really impactful" when the image is strong. [playbook IV Web Design; calls 113, 055, 008]
- Same wireframe, any audience: "I find this layout works really well for basically most service based businesses. The thing that will make it stand out is just your branding... the fonts and the imagery and the colors." Rounded corners and rounded fonts read friendly; hard lines read serious (cybersecurity); serif for a classier brand, a standard sans-serif for a hardworking trades brand; script or rounded fonts "to symbolize creativity" for an art studio; script can elevate a temporary-tattoo brand for grown-ups. [calls 045, 067, 083, 100]
- Fonts: no cursive ("Any friction is friction... maybe they didn't learn cursive. And especially the younger generations"); body at least 16 px; small fonts get bumped up; swap an unstyled serif body (Times New Roman, "looks like no styling was done") for a sans serif like Open Sans, keep a serif headline if it matches the logo, body text in subtle gray rather than navy, add line height and spacing; headline sizes are about usability, not fixed numbers. [calls 049, 100, 008, 028, 055, 058]

## Length, keyword density, and on-page SEO

- Word count, old rule: "yes, even your homepage should have 2,000 plus words"; pages of about 2,000 words tend to rank 1 to 3 or 4; HeyTony's homepage runs about 1,500 to 2,000 words without looking word heavy. [course: word-count]
- Word count, the rule the calls use (the playbook says the calls supersede the 2,000 figure): Google the homepage keyword, compare the top two or three competitors, and sit just above them without writing fluff. "If somebody's ranking with 500 words, do 600 words"; "if they're at 2,000 words, you can put your word count at 2005"; "if the competitors are 2500 or 3000 words but it's all fluff... use your best judgment"; "What is more important is if the content is actually adding value or adding depth." Do not treat ChatGPT's word count as "the bible": one competitor may have 3,000 and number three 500. Chuck's Garage went 800 to 1,000 to 1,200 and was told it needs about 2,000 against competition at about 2,200. [playbook II 1.7, III 2.5; calls 024, 050, 076, 055]
- Over-length is real: a boudoir homepage at roughly 10,000 words ("2,000 words max out on this page") with about 2,000 words inside heading tags alone and duplicated mobile/desktop H2s had to be broken into separate pages (maternity boudoir, bridal boudoir); a cat-behavior homepage at 2,000 words with dropdowns lost to a competitor at 896: "You can do less. I'm giving you permission to do less... I think you just did too much," and the trimmed content moves into a blog post. Counter-data: a one-page, 35-word, no-meta, no-H2 vibe-coded site "ranking #1 for a ton of keywords," a data point about niche intent, not a recipe. [calls 061, 034; playbook III 2.5; email 2026-08-11]
- Measuring: audit tools inflate homepage word counts with currency selectors, review widgets, pop-ups and mobile duplicates; "just highlight all of your content and then paste it into any word count or character counter." [call 064]
- Raising count without a wall of text: dropdowns, sliders, or an FAQ/accordion section ("If you're looking for a way to increase the word count on your homepage, I highly suggest doing DropDowns or an FAQ section"; "accordions are great for increasing our word count without it looking like there's more text on the page"); add words to the FAQs later; keep it findable ("when somebody comes to the site, we want to make sure that they can find the information they're looking for quickly"). [course: word-count; calls 055, 083]
- Keyword placement, the 90% rule: title tag, meta description, H1, at least one H2, the content at least twice, the URL if you can. "The most common reason a page doesn't rank is because it doesn't include the keyword they want to rank for.. and even if it does, it's usually mentioned once!" The exact keyword appearing once on a med spa homepage was "an opportunity right away"; a Montreal designer's exact keyword appeared once, in the footer. [email 2025-06-19; calls 035, 044]
- Density: use the keyword often and naturally, more on a new site or in medical niches ("Really have that keyword as often as you can. Especially since you are working in the medical field"; "You just want to have your keyword as many times as possible to get the ball rolling. Especially since you are such a new site"); the course example uses the keyword 3 to 5 times before body copy; benchmark density against the #1 and #2 results and never stuff. "Content on the page is number one"; schema, meta and title matter less. [calls 007, 068; course: keyword-density; email 2025-07-05]
- Consistency of form: pick one spelling and use it in title, meta, H1 and H2s, variations only in body copy ("Med Spa Scottsdale," spaced, because it is searched more than "MedSpa"); Google treats "St. Petersburg" and "St Petersburg" as one keyword, only audit tools need the exact string (run two reports); Google treats a variation as helpful but "the exact phrase throughout is better." [calls 035, 030, 044]
- Title tag: keyword first, brand at the very end: "Lakeport Dental, which I would actually put at the end of your title tag, top rated St. Catherine's dentist office"; "Your Moving Men - trusted local movers in D.C." becomes "Movers in Washington D.C." + "free quotes" + brand last; "Welcome to Teton Excursions" becomes "Yellowstone Tours" first ("People aren't going to Google and typing in welcome to Teton excursions"); "Homepage Prime Services Fort Smith" ("What the hell? What is that? What do you do?") becomes "Same day dumpster rental in Fort Smith"; "Real 24k gold currency to protect your future" fails because the keyword is unclear ("Are you buying it? Are you selling it? Is it gold bars? Is it coins?"). The playbook states the homepage title as brand name + focus keyword with every other page keyword-first (see the conflict note). Under 60 characters; Shopify auto-appends the store name site-wide, so change that setting "with caution." [course: every-page-1-main-topic; calls 061, 097, 096, 124, 041; playbook III 2.1, IV Title Tags; email 2025-07-03]
- Title when the homepage is not targeting a keyword: use the brand ("Ariel Ross Photography") and put "Chicago Newborn Photographer" + name on the money page; a keyword-first homepage title is fine only if that is the keyword the homepage is going after; a keyword tacked onto every page's title could hurt, on the homepage alone "it's probably fine." [call 014]
- One keyword in the title, never two: "Toronto wedding photographer / GTA wedding photography" is "two keywords... you're trying to go for the whole pie. You're not even getting a slice," so "I would just do Toronto wedding photographer"; "wedding photographer plus filmmaker": "pick one." [calls 006, 014]
- Meta description: 120 to 156 characters, keyword near the front and exact even if it reads less naturally ("the title and description are okay to kind of push the boundaries"); do not paste the title into it, it "is your chance to convince people to click your link" ("we are a string quartet with 25 years of experience" plus event types); spell the place out ("Washington D.C., not just D.C.... Google only knows what you tell it"). Missing metas and titles were the first fix on several homepages. [email 2025-07-03; calls 035, 061, 097, 066, 068, 086, 106]
- H1: the exact target keyword as "the very first tag" on the page ("When Google goes through your site to crawl it... it will go through the heading structure chronologically. And so having that H1 passes the most authority right off the bat"); "one heading one tag per page. And that should be the very first heading"; "it should say exactly what you do"; company name never the H1 ("That's assumed that they know the company name") and taken out of a keyword H1 ("newborn photographer - Ariel portraits" loses the name); brand in the H1 is tolerable only on the homepage of a brand-new business establishing recognition, and on a branded ecommerce homepage. [calls 050, 110, 061, 097; playbook III 2.4, IV Heading Structure, II 2.4]
- Bad H1 patterns and their fixes: taglines ("Our quality is our reputation" becomes "Residential roofing in Cincinnati," tagline demoted to H2 or text; "Leave dirt and grime behind with Prime" becomes "Same day dumpster rental in Fort Smith"; "Escape from reality" becomes "Premier boat tours and rentals"; "Lose weight, keep it off, tighten tone contour" needs "weight loss Chandler"; "Ancient artisans, modern luxury" becomes "outdoor yoga rugs"; "Messy Books" becomes "Bookkeeper in [city]"; "tough conversations at work"; "Are you ready?" at least becomes "Are you ready to hire a bookkeeper"; "Couples counseling is like going to the gym" is a metaphor, not a headline; "Best Bronze" is the site name, not the product), keyword stacks ("Los Angeles based premiere string quartet and more for weddings, corporate events, and private celebrations" becomes "the best string quartet in Los Angeles," fine even if competitors say "best"; "Residential and commercial painting experts" becomes "House painter in Springfield, Massachusetts"), and keyword-free brand lines ("road tested," "take the road less traveled"; pair personality with the keyword: "best hair pomades for guys: tools for the trade"). - H1 defects from themes: Shopify ties the H1 to the logo or injects an empty H1 (fix in Online Store > Edit code > theme.liquid, search "page.title" or "h1"; change an H3 or H2 title to H1; Sidekick can help; or add a secondary keyword H1 as a workaround while pushing support); pop-ups ("25% off your first order"), lead-magnet boxes and plugins generate extra H1s (restyle as paragraph text); a WordPress logo set as H1 on every page; 13 H1s found with the HeyTony Chrome extension on a theme; an empty H1 from a stray space; a homepage with no H1 at all. [calls 044, 055, 064, 068, 110, 041, 062, 013, 019, 054, 049, 007, 086, 098; course: every-page-1-main-topic]
- H2s support the H1: "your heading two tag should reinforce what your heading one tag says. Right. So it's a subtopic of the main topic"; test every heading with "does this support what the main page is about?"; put the keyword in 2 to 3 H2s ("Three pillars of health" becomes "Three pillars of weight loss treatment"; "Our best sellers" becomes "our best selling yoga rugs"; "What our customers say" becomes "What our customers say about our Yellowstone tours"; "Most loved by pets" becomes "Organic Dog Treats Most Loved by Pets"; "Why D.C." becomes "Why Washington D.C. residents..."; "Hi, I'm Prito" becomes "Hi, I'm Prito, a wedding photographer"; "About us" becomes "About Pets Gone Wild"); a heading without the keyword must have the keyword in the copy under it; ChatGPT can draft two or three supporting H2s. [calls 105, 014, 007, 097, 068, 061, 125; email 2025-07-03]
- Heading hygiene: headings are structure, not style ("people... use their heading tags as style versus what they're meant for"); "Inquire now," "Featured in," "Explore signature work," a testimonial line, "Items added to your cart," "limited time offer," "Ann and Derek at Lake Simcoe" are not headings; stat blocks ("+9 years in business," "3K in sales") and promo offers built as H3/H5 become larger paragraph text; 100 H2s on a retreat homepage and three H2s stacked at the top are both wrong; "Wedding photography effortlessly refined" is "way too long" for an H2; H3s like "Why choose Prime, customer testimonials" are fine; keep semantic order H1 then H2, H2, H3; clean up with the page-audit extension. [calls 014, 041, 068, 035, 096, 088, 064]
- URL: the homepage is the one page whose URL carries no keyword; it is the bare domain ("whatever your website is, dot com or ca or co") and needs no further optimizing, even when it is the money page ("totally fine"). [course: urls; book ch. 6; playbook III 2.3; calls 058, 062]
- Canonical: the homepage "should be canonicalized to itself"; a "not self-referencing" flag usually means a trailing-slash mismatch (add the slash where you edit the canonical so it matches the live URL) or a cached audit (give the page-audit tool 1 to 12 hours; the Detailed SEO extension does not cache); Squarespace/GoDaddy homepage-slug warnings can be ignored. [calls 030, 054, 059]

Matt's stance on weight: "Not schema markup. Not 47 backlinks from guest posts. Not the perfect keyword density." [playbook IV Schema; call 064; email 2026-05-09]
- Local signals in text: the address on the page ("your address is in here. ## CTAs and conversion

- First question: "what action are you trying to get people to take?" / "what is the main purpose when somebody comes to this website? What do you want them to do?" (med spa: "Book"; boudoir: "get somebody to go to the pricing page or book or inquire"; video company: send an inquiry). [calls 096, 058, 049, 063, 105]
- One primary CTA, one color, everything else demoted: "Typically, when you come to a website, there is a call to action that is a different color than everything else on the screen"; "the main thing we want people to do, we want that to stand out the most. And then the secondary thing... in the background a little bit" (solid primary, outlined/transparent secondary); "if people have too many choices, they sometimes just choose not to make any choice"; "the more options you give people, the less likely they are to take action" (Costco carries at most three variations); a magician's book now / call now / email now / WhatsApp now became one big inquire button under the hero line (number and email already live in the header); a speaker's five competing actions, a cleaning site's repeated "Book now," an organizer's green/purple/blue buttons, and "a lot of buttons up here" in a roofing header were all cut to one. Remove a big "reviews" button lower down: "we don't want people to click that. We want people to click this or this." [calls 109, 100, 055, 101, 063, 066, 096, 060]
- Primary CTA above the fold, "the button that people want to click right before scrolling. Because you can't depend on anybody scrolling anywhere"; "before anyone even has to scroll, we are guiding the eye to this clear button"; keep the CTA in the menu and add another big button near the top "if people aren't looking at the menu or don't see that for some reason." [calls 113, 067, 049]
- A CTA in every section ("I tried to include a call to action in every section here"; "somebody can be convinced by this"), under the how-it-works block, after the audience boxes, and at the bottom; buttons come after the text ("We'd read this, this, and then we want to click the button after"); more buttons through the content when a reviewer asks "where are the CTAs?" [calls 067, 083, 058, 028, 100, 043]
- CTA wording carries the keyword or the real action: "Shop Treats" beats "shop now," and "Shop Healthy Dog Treats" / "Shop Organic Dog Treats" beats both; "Book a massage appointment"; "book classes today"; "Get a quote" / "Book a free call"; "buy my book" / "get my book" instead of "About my book"; "pricing," not "investment" ("Is somebody going to Google and typing in boudoir investment? [calls 068, 083, 100, 113, 058, 049, 034]
- Conversion mechanics: phone and email clickable (even though mail-app hijacking annoys Matt); "Book your free consultation" text made clickable; a CTA can scroll to the on-page contact section rather than load another page; the button must not lead to a slow contact page; the lead form in the hero for maximum inquiries; an on-page quote form ("put in your information... get a clean for 155 bucks or whatever, or you could learn more"); a pre-footer "request a quote." [calls 058, 041, 045, 103, 113, 083]

## Links, indexing, and where it sits in the site

- Crawl depth counts from the homepage at zero: anything linked from the nav or the homepage body is one click, a post reached from the blog index or a category is two, a post linked only from another post is three; "If it takes four clicks or more to get to a page on your website, Google doesn't really care. The harder it is for a human to find your page, the less likely Google will actually care about it"; "if I can't get to it from your homepage, Google's not going to index it"; four or five clicks is "buried." "If a page matters, link to it from the home page or the main menu." [course: crawl-depth; calls 022, 073, 010, 084; book ch. 27]
- The homepage links 3 to 5 other pages inside its content, with keyword anchor text or images relating to those words (HeyTony's: "neutral vodka" to the case study, "web design" to the portfolio category, "search engine optimization," "local SEO," "Facebook ads," "Instagram ads," "TikTok ads" each to its service page). [course: dead-end-page, internal-linking, anchor-text; book ch. 9]
- The homepage links directly to individual services or top-selling products ("This puts the things people actually want to buy one click away"), to the money pages ("make sure you are internal linking from your homepage to those other service page, to those product pages, to those money pages"), to "areas we serve" location pages (footer-only links index slower), and to blog categories (10 max) plus the 5 to 10 most recent posts. Matt's homepage modules: services bar (one click), recent posts (one click), category links (every post two clicks), a clickable map to the GBP; listing all categories beats "page 10 of pagination." "I don't recommend putting everything in dropdowns like this or in toggles." [book ch. 27; calls 068, 088, 073, 015; playbook III 2.11, IV Crawl Depth, VI 2.2]
- Orphan test: "go to your homepage... try to get to that product from the homepage. If you can't do that, that means it's orphaned or it's buried"; hidden product landing pages "are invisible" and a blog on a subdomain breaks the cohesive domain; "if you get to it in two clicks, it's not indexing, there's another reason." [calls 065, 082, 017, 114, 084]

## Don'ts

- Don't put two keywords, two services, or several cities on the homepage (St. Catharines plus Niagara plus the brand with no H1 is "a really good example of a page that isn't just about one topic"). [course: every-page-1-main-topic; calls 006, 014, 113]
- Don't hide what you do in a logo, a photo, a tagline, or a metaphor; don't open with "welcome." [calls 004, 113, 060, 066, 105]
- Don't use a hero slider, carousel, autoplay video, Instagram embed, embedded catalogs, rotating strips, or scroll-catching animation. [book ch. 20; calls 049, 074, 055, 022, 124, 060]
- Don't make the company name, logo, nav items, pop-ups, stats, offers, testimonial lines, or cart notices heading tags; don't have zero, two, or 13 H1s. [calls 097, 058, 110, 035, 096, 014, 041, 049]
- Don't revise a ranking homepage, rotate the H1 seasonally, or diagnose rankings on it. [calls 021, 055, 124]
- Don't target the homepage keyword on a service page, location page, or blog post. [calls 096, 052, 037; playbook II 2.4]
- Don't stuff it to 10,000 words, cram every service in depth, or reuse the contact page FAQs; don't force FAQs onto an ecommerce homepage. [calls 061, 020, 055]
- Don't write "near me" / "near you," don't leave the address off, and don't leave title or meta empty. [calls 109, 096, 068]
- Don't send Google Ads or GBP traffic to the wrong place (homepage for a product ad; Facebook instead of the homepage). [call 016; book ch. 21; call 026]
- Don't run five CTAs in five colors, cursive fonts, text shadows, glows, box shadows, all-centered text, or a "you deserve it" button to an "investment" page. [calls 109, 049, 028, 100]
- Don't keep a one-page or squeeze-page site as the whole website, a duplicate "home 2.0," a homepage redirecting to an IDX search, or a second website for a second service. [calls 026, 061, 082, 124, 038]
- Don't delete pages while testing a homepage swap, and don't submit the homepage alone to Search Console. [call 113; book ch. 23]

## Where the sources are thin or disagree

- Title tag order on the homepage: the curriculum (Lakeport) and the calls (movers, Teton, string quartet) put the keyword first and the brand at the very end; the playbook states the homepage title as brand name + focus keyword with only other pages keyword-first; the branded ecommerce advice (Laulu) is company name + focus keyword. No call reconciles these; the safest reading is keyword-first when the homepage targets a keyword, brand-first only when it does not. [course: every-page-1-main-topic; calls 061, 097, 086; playbook III 2.1]
- Word count: the Level-0 course says 2,000+ even for the homepage; the calls say match and slightly beat the top two or three competitors (600 beats 500, "do less" when a 896-word page wins); the playbook explicitly says the calls supersede. The 2,000 figure still gets quoted to members (Chuck's Garage, Ariel). [course: word-count; calls 024, 034, 050, 055; playbook II 1.7]
- Neri's bookkeeping homepage got opposite advice a few weeks apart: keep the homepage as the "bookkeeping services" page and do not build a duplicate service page (call 052) versus keep the homepage general and build a dedicated bookkeeping service page (call 058). [calls 052, 058]
- Ecommerce homepages: the playbook, Matt and Leanne say do not rank it (branded H1), while Denroy, Denroi and Kira gave single-line stores a keyword H1 ("outdoor yoga rugs," "beard oil," "organic pet food"). The split seems to be multi-product vs single-line, but nobody states that rule. [playbook II 2.4; calls 020, 100, 007, 076, 068]
- Hidden content: the course and Leanne endorse dropdowns/accordions for word count; Matt says "I don't recommend putting everything in dropdowns like this or in toggles" (about links) and told a 2,000-word dropdown-heavy homepage it "did too much." [course: word-count; calls 055, 073, 034]
- Hero video and lead forms: the default is no hero video and introduce-first, but Leanne allowed a hero video for two brands and a hero lead form for conversion-first sites, and Matt reportedly said hero forms convert more now; no numbers given. [calls 060, 100, 045]
- Hero size: "almost full screen" for Joan versus Leanne's dislike of oversized heroes with a scroll hint; the header-as-1/6-of-page rule comes only from the playbook. [calls 008, 055; playbook IV Web Design]
- Brand in the H1: playbook and Denroi allow it on a brand-new business's homepage; Elliot and Denroi elsewhere say take the name out / never a header tag. [playbook III 2.4; calls 097, 050]
- State-level first for businesses too small to rank at city level appears only in the playbook and runs the other way from every call's "pick your localized area" advice; context (tiny-town vs big-city) is not given. [playbook VI 2.2]
- Thin everywhere: exact meta length for the homepage (only the blog checklist's 120 to 156), how many testimonials or FAQs, ecommerce homepage wireframe beyond "a section per product," mobile specifics beyond the hero stack and bottom phone bar, footer and map contents (deferred to other chapters), schema detail, and anything AI/AEO-specific for the homepage. The email archive adds almost nothing homepage-specific beyond the 35-word counter-example and the sitemap location. [email 2026-08-11, 2026-08-04]
