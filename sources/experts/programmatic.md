## 2026-09-02 | A large set of near identical pages risks being read as thin content
- Source: Ahrefs, "Programmatic SEO", 2026-09-02, https://ahrefs.com/blog/programmatic-seo/
- Quote: "Any company that publishes a huge number of very similar pages runs the risk of creating thin content: content that offers little to no value to the end user."
- Means: Publishing a large number of near identical pages risks producing thin content with little user value. Ahrefs frames the risk as one of similarity and value, not raw page count.
- Factor: thin-page-ratio | Evidence: D | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: both

## 2026-01-20 | A location page that only swaps the city name is spam, not programmatic SEO
- Source: Backlinko, "Programmatic SEO: What It Is + Tips & Examples for 2026", 2026-01-20, https://backlinko.com/programmatic-seo
- Quote: "Simply changing '[City] plumbers' to target 500 locations while offering identical generic text isn't programmatic SEO, it's spam."
- Means: Backlinko calls a location page template that only swaps the city name, with no other change to the text, spam rather than programmatic SEO.
- Factor: location-pages | Evidence: D | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic local | Stage: build

## 2026-01-20 | Proprietary data is named as the precondition for a programmatic page set
- Source: Backlinko, "Programmatic SEO: What It Is + Tips & Examples for 2026", 2026-01-20, https://backlinko.com/programmatic-seo
- Quote: "Do you have lots of proprietary data, user-generated content, or structured information at your disposal?"
- Means: Backlinko frames access to proprietary data, user generated content or structured information as the precondition for a programmatic page set worth building.
- Factor: programmatic-templates-unique-data | Evidence: D | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: build

## 2025-11-27 | Each programmatic template should target a specific need, not a generic keyword swap
- Source: Search Engine Land, "Programmatic SEO: Scale content, rankings & traffic fast", 2025-11-27, https://searchengineland.com/guide/programmatic-seo
- Quote: "Each template should target searchers with clear, specific needs. Instead of generic '[keyword] guide' pages, focus on '[specific problem] solution for [specific target audience]' patterns."
- Means: The guide advises building each template around a specific searcher need rather than a generic keyword swap, framing this as the difference between a useful template and a doorway page.
- Factor: location-pages | Evidence: D | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic local | Stage: build

## 2025-11-27 | The strongest programmatic pages carry elements competitors cannot copy
- Source: Search Engine Land, "Programmatic SEO: Scale content, rankings & traffic fast", 2025-11-27, https://searchengineland.com/guide/programmatic-seo
- Quote: "The strongest programmatic pages include elements competitors can't replicate: your own research data, custom calculators, exclusive interviews, or original graphics tailored to each page's focus."
- Means: The guide names proprietary research, calculators, interviews and original graphics as the elements that keep a programmatic page from reading as a copy of what competitors already publish.
- Factor: programmatic-templates-unique-data | Evidence: D | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: build

## 2025-05-05 | Improper programmatic SEO is tied directly to a spam policy penalty risk
- Source: Semrush, "What Is Programmatic SEO? Examples + How to Do It", 2025-05-05, https://www.semrush.com/blog/programmatic-seo/
- Quote: "When done improperly, programmatic SEO can lead to having a large number of pages that offer little or no value to users. This violates Google's spam policies and may result in a penalty."
- Means: Semrush ties improperly built programmatic pages directly to Google's spam policies and a possible penalty, not only a ranking risk.
- Factor: thin-page-ratio | Evidence: D | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: build
