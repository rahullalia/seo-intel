## 2026-08-28 | Google names scaled content abuse as a spam policy target
- Source: Google Search Central, "Spam policies for Google web search", 2026-08-28, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
- Quote: "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users."
- Means: Publishing a wave of pages outside a site's normal topic to chase traffic risks being read as scaled content abuse, which Google enforces against directly rather than just ranking it lower.
- Factor: topical-mismatch | Evidence: A | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: audit

## 2025-12-10 | Helpful content is judged on originality and depth, not topic coverage alone
- Source: Google Search Central, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content", 2025-12-10, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- Quote: "Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?"
- Means: A page competing on a well covered topic needs analysis or data the top results do not already have, not a restatement of them in different words.
- Factor: information-gain | Evidence: A | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: both

## 2025-09-11 | Google's rater guidelines put Trust at the center of E-E-A-T
- Source: Google, "General Guidelines", 2025-09-11, https://guidelines.raterhub.com/searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf
- Quote: "The most important member at the center of the E-E-A-T family is Trust."
- Means: Experience, Expertise and Authoritativeness support a Trust judgement rather than standing on their own, and the same section says an untrustworthy page has low E-E-A-T however expert the author looks. Author bios on a page that hides ownership, contact detail or a conflict of interest do not lift the rating.
- Factor: eeat | Evidence: A | Lever: trust | Scope: organic | Stage: build

## 2025-09-11 | First-hand life experience can carry high E-E-A-T on Your Money or Your Life topics
- Source: Google, "General Guidelines", 2025-09-11, https://guidelines.raterhub.com/searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf
- Quote: "Pages that share first-hand life experience on clear YMYL topics may be considered to have high E-E-A-T as long as the content is trustworthy, safe, and consistent with well-established expert consensus."
- Means: A practitioner account on a health, money or safety topic can rate high without formal credentials, provided it stays inside expert consensus. The guidelines still reserve items such as treatment options and instructions for filling out tax forms for experts.
- Factor: experience-first-person | Evidence: A | Lever: trust | Scope: organic | Stage: build

## 2024-03-05 | Google expected the March 2024 update to cut unoriginal content by 40 percent
- Source: Google, "New ways we're tackling spammy, low-quality content on Search", 2024-03-05, https://blog.google/products/search/google-search-update-march-2024/
- Quote: "we expect that the combination of this update and our previous efforts will collectively reduce low-quality, unoriginal content in search results by 40%."
- Means: Google treats its core ranking systems and spam policies as one lever against thin, unoriginal pages, and put a specific number on how much of that content it expected to remove from results.
- Factor: information-gain | Evidence: A | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: audit

## 2023-11 | Google's own rater guidelines overview names Experience the first of the four E-E-A-T criteria
- Source: Google, "Search Quality Rater Guidelines: An Overview", 2023-11, https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hsw-sqrg.pdf
- Quote: "For this task, Raters consider the quality of the webpage using criteria that we call E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trust."
- Means: This is a separate, shorter Google document from the raterhub.com guidelines already on file, and it states plainly that Experience is one of four criteria raters weigh on a page. A page with no visible creator or sign of first-hand contact with its topic fails this test regardless of how well it is written.
- Factor: eeat | Evidence: A | Lever: trust | Scope: organic | Stage: build

## 2022-06-07 | Google's information gain patent scores a page by what it adds beyond documents the user already saw
- Source: Google Patents, "Contextual estimation of link information gain" (US11354342B2), 2022-06-07, https://patents.google.com/patent/US11354342B2/en
- Quote: "An information gain score for a given document is indicative of additional information that is included in the given document beyond information contained in other documents that were already presented to the user."
- Means: This patent measures a page against what a specific user already read, not against the page's own topic coverage. It is a second grade A Google source for information-gain, and rubric rule 2 still holds: grade A alone does not restore Core.
- Factor: information-gain | Evidence: A | Lever: ranking | Scope: organic | Stage: both
